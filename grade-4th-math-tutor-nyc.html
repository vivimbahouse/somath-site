The curriculum

What 4th graders learn in NYC public and private schools

Fourth grade math extends the operational fluency built in 3rd grade to multi-digit numbers: multi-digit multiplication (up to 4-digit by 1-digit and 2-digit by 2-digit), long division, and fluent addition and subtraction of multi-digit whole numbers. Fractions expand to include addition and subtraction with like denominators, comparison of fractions with different denominators, and equivalent fractions. Decimals enter for the first time as an extension of fractions (tenths and hundredths). Angle measurement and lines of symmetry appear in geometry. This is also the second year of NY State Math testing.

Where kids get stuck

The three struggles we see most often in 4th grade math

1. The long division breakdown

Long division is the most complex procedure a 4th grader has ever learned — it requires multiplication, subtraction, place value, and estimation all working together. The most common failure is that any weakness in the underlying operations (usually multiplication facts) becomes catastrophic in long division. A student who is 85% fluent with multiplication facts hits a wall the first time division asks them to test 7 x 8, 8 x 8, 9 x 8 in sequence. The prerequisite work is fact fluency, not more division practice.

2. Equivalent fractions confusion

Understanding that 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6 = 4/8 is conceptually simple but procedurally slippery. Fourth graders often can identify equivalent fractions on a visual model but fail to apply the concept when they need to compare 3/4 vs. 5/8 or add 1/2 + 1/4. The stronger habit is always converting to a common denominator first — even for problems the student thinks they can solve mentally.

3. Decimals as separate from fractions

Most 4th grade curricula introduce decimals in a separate unit from fractions, which leads students to treat them as different things. In reality, 0.5 and 1/2 are the same number, and every operation on decimals is an operation on fractions. Students who learn decimals procedurally without connecting them to fractions consistently make errors like 0.3 + 0.4 = 0.34 (adding the way they would with whole numbers, ignoring place value).

Milestones by month

The five 4th grade math milestones every NYC parent should watch for

  • By November: Fluent multi-digit multiplication (2-digit by 2-digit and 3-digit by 1-digit).
  • By January: Long division of 3-digit or 4-digit dividends by 1-digit divisors, confidently.
  • By February: Adding and subtracting fractions with like denominators, and identifying equivalent fractions.
  • By March: Reading and writing decimals to hundredths, and understanding decimal-fraction equivalence.
  • By April: Solving multi-step word problems that combine operations, ready for NY State test.

Common questions

4th grade math in NYC: parent FAQ

What NY State Math Test score should my 4th grader be aiming for?

For families targeting selective NYC middle schools, a Level 4 (top level) is the goal — it keeps every door open including screened programs and specialized progress tracks. A Level 3 is on-grade-level and acceptable for most placement decisions. A Level 2 is a signal to look at where the gaps are before 5th grade, when the last year of elementary math consolidates before middle school.

How does 4th grade math math prep for the SHSAT?

The math on the SHSAT includes topics that first appear in 4th grade — fraction operations, decimal fluency, multi-step word problems, and basic geometry. The SHSAT itself is taken in fall of 8th grade, but the students who score highest are typically those who built solid 4th and 5th grade fluency and never had to catch up. Starting SHSAT-style problem solving in 4th grade is not too early — but it should layer on top of grade-level work, not replace it.

Why does long division seem so much harder than 3rd grade multiplication?

Because it is harder. Long division combines four skills — multiplication, subtraction, place value, estimation — that were separate in 3rd grade. It requires holding multiple partial results in working memory. Students who struggle typically struggle in one of the four skills, not in division itself. The right diagnostic is to isolate which underlying skill is weak.

Are 4th graders too young to think about middle school math placement?

Not for planning, though not for pressure. Most NYC middle school placement decisions are made based on 5th and 6th grade performance, but the foundations are built in 4th. A 4th grader who is solid on multi-digit arithmetic, fluent with fractions, and comfortable with multi-step word problems has every option open in 5th grade. A 4th grader with gaps in these areas is spending 5th grade fixing them rather than moving forward.
