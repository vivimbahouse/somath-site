Little Newtons

GoalLock in number sense, basic operations and simple problem-solving — with the focus on fluency and confidence, not just “doing the math.”
Duration24 h (16 weeks, 1 class/week × 1.5 h)
LevelChildren entering Kindergarten through 2nd grade
Weekly schedule
WeekMain focus
1Number sense up to 20. Counting forward and back, comparing, ordering, simple skip-counting (2s, 5s, 10s).
2Number sense up to 100. Place value (tens and ones), grouping in tens, hundreds chart, simple number patterns.
3Addition within 20 (with meaning). Strategies: counting up, making 10, doubles & near-doubles, hands-on manipulatives.
4Subtraction within 20 (with meaning). Counting back, “think-addition,” story problems, missing-addend puzzles.
5Addition within 100 (easy). Round tens, friendly numbers, number line jumps, mental-math drills.
6Subtraction within 100 (easy). Take-away on the number line, compare-difference problems, money examples.
7Two-step word problems. Drawing pictures, writing the equation, “does the answer make sense?” check.
8Intro to fractions (halves, thirds, fourths). Folding paper, sharing fairly, fractions of shapes and collections.
9Money and time (basics). Coins and bills up to $1, telling time to the hour and half hour, calendar.
10Measurement. Length with rulers, comparing weight, capacity with cups; standard vs. non-standard units.
112D shapes and symmetry. Triangles, squares, rectangles, circles, hexagons; lines of symmetry; tangram play.
123D shapes. Cubes, spheres, cylinders, cones; faces, edges, vertices; building with blocks.
13Patterns and early algebra thinking. Growing patterns, missing numbers, simple “what comes next” rules.
14Data and graphs (intro). Tallying, simple bar graphs and pictographs, “which has more?” questions.
15Story problems — full mixed review. Combine every idea: add/subtract, fractions, money, time, measurement.
16Math Olympics 1–2 — final session. Mini challenges, problem-solving games, certificates, and a parent show-and-tell.

Kid Einsteins A

GoalPrepare students well for SAT-style work, multiplication/division and problem-solving — without falling into “just calculate.”
Duration32 h (16 weeks, 1 class/week × 2 h)
LevelStudents entering 3rd or 4th grade
Weekly schedule
WeekMain objective
1Diagnostic + multiplication fluency (single-digit). Times tables 0–10 with strategies, arrays, equal-group story problems.
2Multi-digit multiplication. 2-digit × 1-digit and 2-digit × 2-digit using area models and the standard algorithm.
3Division fluency. Connecting to multiplication, partial quotients, simple long division with remainders.
4Word problems — multiplication & division structures. Equal groups, arrays, comparison (“3 times as many”), splitting into groups.
5Factors, multiples, primes. GCF, LCM in context, divisibility shortcuts for 2, 3, 5, 9, 10.
6Fractions — meaning & equivalence. Number line, area models, equivalent fractions, comparing fractions with unlike denominators.
7Fractions — addition & subtraction. Same denominator, then unlike denominators using equivalent fractions.
8Fractions — multiplication and a first look at division. Fraction times whole number, fraction times fraction with models.
9Decimals to hundredths. Place value, comparing, converting between common fractions and decimals.
10Decimal operations. Add/subtract decimals (lined up), multiply/divide by 10, 100, 1000.
11Percent (first pass). Percent as “out of 100,” converting between fractions, decimals, percents; simple percent of a number.
12Geometry — angles, polygons, lines. Right/acute/obtuse angles, classifying triangles and quadrilaterals.
13Perimeter, area, and an intro to volume. Rectangles, composite shapes, rectangular prisms (unit-cube counting).
14Data, mean, median, mode. Reading bar graphs, line plots, computing “the typical value.”
15Mixed word-problem strategies. Pulling tools from the whole semester: when to multiply, when to divide, when fractions help.
16Final review + Kid Einsteins Olympics. Timed mini practice test, team problem-solving game, parent showcase.

Weekly schedule
WeekMain focus

Young Fermats Pre-Algebra

GoalCover solid pre-algebra fundamentals (negative numbers, expressions, simple equations, ratio, proportion, percent) so students don’t arrive in 8th/9th grade feeling lost.
Duration32 h (16 weeks, 1 class/week × 2 h)
LevelStudents entering 5th or 6th grade
Weekly schedule
WeekMain content
1Diagnostic + integer operations. Number line, opposites, add/subtract/multiply/divide negatives, sign rules.
2Expressions & the distributive property. Combining like terms, distributing, evaluating expressions for given values.
3One-step equations. Inverse operations, isolating the variable, checking by substitution.
4Two-step and multi-step equations. Variables on one side; equations with parentheses; word-to-equation translation.
5Equations with variables on both sides. Move-and-combine strategy; recognizing “no solution” and “infinitely many.”
6Inequalities. Solving, graphing on a number line, flipping the sign when multiplying/dividing by a negative.
7Ratios and rates. Unit rate, ratio tables, comparing ratios, real-world rate problems.
8Proportions. Cross-multiplying, scale drawings, similar figures, proportion word problems.
9Percent toolkit. Percent of a number, percent change, discount, tax, simple interest.
10Exponents & powers. Positive integer exponents, product/quotient/power rules, intro to negative exponents.
11Scientific notation. Converting back and forth, multiplying and dividing in scientific notation.
12Square roots and irrational numbers. Perfect squares, estimating roots, irrational numbers on the number line.
13Coordinate plane and linear graphs (intro). Plotting, finding slope from two points, recognizing y = mx + b.
14Functions — first look. Input/output tables, function machines, mapping diagrams, function vs. relation.
15Geometry refresh. Angle relationships, triangle properties, Pythagorean theorem at a basic level.
16Final review + algebra-readiness mini-test. Timed mixed section; clear gap map for Algebra Ignite next year.

Young Fermats Algebra Ignite

GoalAssume the student already has solid pre-algebra and move from linear equations to the first topics in functions.
Duration32 h (16 weeks, 1 class/week × 2 h)
LevelStudents entering 7th or 8th grade with solid pre-algebra
Weekly schedule
WeekMain content
1Diagnostic + linear equations review. Multi-step solving, variables on both sides, equations with fractions and parentheses.
2Linear inequalities & compound inequalities. Graphing, “and” vs. “or,” word problems involving inequalities.
3Linear functions & slope. Slope from two points, from a graph, from an equation; rate-of-change in context.
4Slope-intercept and point-slope forms. Writing equations, graphing, parallel and perpendicular lines.
5Systems of equations — graphing & substitution. Solving 2-variable systems, recognizing intersections, interpreting in context.
6Systems — elimination & applications. Elimination method, mixture and rate problems, choosing the best method.
7Exponents and exponent rules. Product, quotient, power, zero and negative exponents, simplifying expressions.
8Polynomials — intro. Adding, subtracting, multiplying monomials and binomials, distributive multiplication.
9Factoring — GCF and difference of squares. Pulling out common factors, recognizing a² − b² patterns.
10Factoring quadratics. ax² + bx + c with a = 1, then a ≠ 1; box method and grouping.
11Quadratic equations — solving by factoring. Zero-product property, real-world word problems involving quadratics.
12Quadratic formula & the discriminant. When factoring fails; interpreting the discriminant; sketching parabolas.
13Exponential functions (intro). Growth vs. decay, evaluating, simple word problems (population, depreciation).
14Functions in depth. Domain/range, function notation, evaluating, transformations preview.
15Radical & rational expressions (light). Simplifying square roots, basic rational expressions, restrictions.
16Final review + Algebra I diagnostic. Honors-track check; clear roadmap for Algebra II / Geometry next year.

Frequently asked

Do I need to apply, or can I just enroll?

Every new family books a free 60-minute evaluation first. We use the time to meet the student, run a quick written diagnostic, and decide together which course is the right fit. You receive the diagnostic write-up within 48 hours, even if you don't enroll. Returning families can skip the eval — just call (646) 668-6151.

How is tuition calculated?

All SOMATH courses are billed at our standard $60/hr rate. The Weekends term runs 16 weeks, with every track meeting once per week — the full school-year pace. Little Newtons is 90 minutes/session (24 hours total → $1,440). Every other track is 120 minutes/session (32 hours total → $1,920). Payment is upfront, or in 4 monthly installments by request. Credit card adds a 5% processing fee; Zelle and Venmo are accepted without fee.

What if my child misses a session?

Make-up sessions can be arranged with the lead instructor, subject to scheduling capacity. We do not refund missed sessions, but no child loses material — every session has a written summary and home practice the student can complete to stay on pace.

How big are the classes?

Small. Little Newtons caps at 4–6 students; Kid Einsteins and Young Fermats cap at 6–8. Every cohort is small enough that the instructor knows where each student is struggling — the opposite of a 15-kid worksheet room.

What happens when the Weekends term ends?

The Weekends term wraps October 4, 2026. Students who finish the term get priority enrollment into the next Weekends cohort (starting mid-October) and a $100 returning-student credit applied toward the next term’s tuition. We’ll send a re-enrollment link in late September.

Where are you located?

SOMATH is at 226 W 79th St, 1st Floor, NY 10024 — between Broadway and Amsterdam, two blocks from the 79th St 1 train, four blocks from the 86th St B/C. Walkable from PS 87, PS 199, PS 452, PS 9, the Anderson School, and Riverside Park.
