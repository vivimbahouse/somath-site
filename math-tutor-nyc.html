Why parents pick SOMATH over a private math tutor

The best math tutor in NYC is usually not a private tutor

Every NYC parent asks the same question the first time their kid brings home a bad math grade: should we get a math tutor? The instinct is understandable — math is cumulative, gaps compound, and Manhattan has plenty of $150–$250/hour private math tutors happy to come to your apartment. But for most kids, most of the time, private math tutoring in NYC is the wrong answer.

Here is what actually happens with private one-on-one math tutoring. The tutor arrives, works through the current homework, closes a specific gap in that week's material, and leaves. Next week, a new homework, a new gap. There is no curriculum. There is no long arc. The child never builds fluency on the fundamentals underneath the homework — they just get the specific homework done. When the tutor stops, the gains stop.

Small-group math classes at a real math school do the opposite. Your child works through a curriculum designed for their grade, in a classroom with 4–6 peers, with a trained teacher watching them think. They see how other students approach the same problem. They persist through problems that private tutors would have solved for them in thirty seconds. And they do this for less than half the cost of a weekly private tutor.

SOMATH is the math school on the Upper West Side that runs this format. We built it because we could not find a math tutoring option in NYC that we would send our own children to. Small groups of six. Trained teachers, not college students. A written curriculum. Weekly, in person, year-round.

Math tutor NYC — common parent questions

How much does a math tutor cost in NYC?

Private one-on-one math tutors in Manhattan typically charge $100–$250 per hour, which works out to roughly $400–$1,000 per month for one weekly session. SOMATH's small-group math tutoring runs $246–$800 per month depending on grade level for a 90-minute weekly class capped at six students — a fraction of the private-tutor cost, with a real teacher and a real curriculum.

Is small-group math tutoring better than one-on-one?

For most kids, yes. Small groups keep students engaged, expose them to how peers solve the same problem, and cost far less. One-on-one is the right choice for a child recovering from a specific gap in a short window (a failing quarter, a make-up unit). For year-round math development in grades 1–12, small groups of 4–6 outperform one-on-one on almost every measurable outcome.

Do you tutor for the SHSAT and SAT math?

Yes. SOMATH runs dedicated SHSAT prep for rising 8th graders and SAT math for high schoolers, both as small-group tracks with the same 6-student cap. SHSAT is $587/month, SAT Math is $640/month, and both include diagnostic and practice tests aligned to the current exam format.
