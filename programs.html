First through third grade are the years that decide whether a child grows up confident with numbers or quietly convinced that math “isn’t for them.” Little Newtons is built for these foundational learners who are ready to think, not just fill in worksheets — offered in two levels so every child lands in the right cohort.

Little Newtons A (Grades 1–2): number sense up to 100, addition and subtraction fluency, an honest first look at fractions and measurement, and the language of word problems.

Little Newtons B (Grades 2–3): stronger arithmetic fluency, intro to multiplication and division, place-value into the thousands, deeper work with fractions, and richer multi-step word problems — the bridge into Kid Einsteins.

The goal isn’t to race ahead a grade; it’s to make the foundation strong enough that Kid Einsteins, Young Fermats, and eventually the SHSAT have somewhere stable to land.

  • Number sense & place value to 100+
  • Addition & subtraction fluency
  • Introduction to fractions & measurement
  • Shapes, geometry & spatial reasoning
  • Word-problem language & strategy
  • Patterns & early algebraic thinking

The upper-elementary years — 3rd through 5th grade — are where math either clicks or quietly slips away. Kid Einsteins is built for students ready to move past basic arithmetic into real problem-solving — offered in two levels so every child lands in the right cohort.

Kid Einsteins A (Grades 3–4): multiplication and division fluency, a careful first pass at fractions and decimals, intro geometry, and structured two-step word problems — the upper-elementary core.

Kid Einsteins B (Grades 4–5): the bridge to middle school. Advanced fractions and decimals, ratio and percent reasoning, proportionality, and pre-algebra readiness — setting up a strong start in Young Fermats.

The goal across both levels is mastery, not exposure — the kind of structured word-problem work that pays off later on the SHSAT, SAT, and every standardized test in between.

The middle-school years are where a quiet “I’m just not a math person” can take root for life, or where a student quietly, decisively, becomes one of the strongest math students in the grade. Young Fermats is built for middle schoolers who want depth, not just survival — offered in three sequenced levels so every student lands at the right entry point.

Young Fermats — Pre-Algebra (Grades 5–6): the algebra on-ramp. Integers, expressions, simple equations, ratio, proportion and percent — the bridge from arithmetic into symbolic math.

Young Fermats — Algebra I (Grades 7–8): linear equations and inequalities, systems, exponents and intro functions. The real start of high-school math, taught with the same rigor we’ve used for two decades preparing adults for the GMAT and GRE quantitative sections.

Young Fermats — Geometry & Trigonometry (Grades 7–8): angles, area, transformations, 3D solids, right-triangle trig and the unit circle — the conceptual approach, not formula-memorization.

The shift from arithmetic to algebra happens in a small group where no one falls behind silently.
