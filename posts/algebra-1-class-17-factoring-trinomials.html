Understanding trinomial structure

Write a quadratic trinomial in descending powers: \(ax^2+bx+c\), with \(a\ne0\). The signed number \(a\) is the leading coefficient, \(b\) is the coefficient of \(x\), and \(c\) is the constant. A missing written coefficient of \(x^2\) means 1.

Factoring reverses multiplication: it rewrites one expression as a product with the same value for every \(x\). Identifying the coefficients helps you choose a method; it does not mean the expression is already an equation to solve.

Q1

Identify a, b, and c, then factor.

\[x^{2} + 8 x + 15\]
Answer

\(\left(x + 3\right) \left(x + 5\right)\)

  1. Write the polynomial in standard form and read the coefficients with their signs.

    \[x^{2} + 8 x + 15\]

  2. The original signed coefficients are \(a=1,\quad b=8,\quad c=15\). The leading coefficient is 1.

  3. The GCF is 1, so no common numerical or variable factor needs to be removed.

  4. Find two integers with product \(15\) (the constant term) and sum \(8\). Use \(3\) and \(5\).

    \[\begin{aligned}(3)(5)&=15\\3+(5)&=8\end{aligned}\]

  5. Use the pair as the constants in the two binomial factors, retaining any outside GCF.

    \[\left(x + 3\right) \left(x + 5\right)\]

Check and justify

Multiply all the factors, including the GCF. The result must match every original coefficient.

\[\left(x + 3\right) \left(x + 5\right)\]
\[= x^{2} + 8 x + 15\]

Q2

Identify a, b, and c. State whether a equals 1, then factor.

\[2 x^{2} + 5 x + 2\]
Answer

\(\left(x + 2\right) \left(2 x + 1\right)\)

  1. Write the polynomial in standard form and read the coefficients with their signs.

    \[2 x^{2} + 5 x + 2\]

  2. The original signed coefficients are \(a=2,\quad b=5,\quad c=2\). The leading coefficient is not 1.

  3. The GCF is 1, so no common numerical or variable factor needs to be removed.

    \[\begin{aligned}(1)(4)&=4\\1+(4)&=5\end{aligned}\]

  5. Split the middle term into two terms with the selected coefficients. The following work is for the quadratic inside any outside GCF.

    \[2 x^{2} + x + 4 x + 2\]

  6. Factor each group. The binomial inside both groups must match; factor a negative coefficient when needed.

    \[x\left(2 x + 1\right) + 2\left(2 x + 1\right)\]

  7. Factor out the common binomial and restore the outside GCF, if there was one.

    \[\left(x + 2\right) \left(2 x + 1\right)\]

Check and justify

Multiply all the factors, including the GCF. The result must match every original coefficient.

\[\left(x + 2\right) \left(2 x + 1\right)\]
\[= 2 x^{2} + 5 x + 2\]

Q5

Identify the original signed coefficients. Factor out a negative factor before completing the factorization.

\[- 3 x^{2} + 10 x - 8\]
Answer

\(\left(4 - 3 x\right) \left(x - 2\right)\)

  1. Write the polynomial in standard form and read the coefficients with their signs.

    \[- 3 x^{2} + 10 x - 8\]

  2. The original signed coefficients are \(a=-3,\quad b=10,\quad c=-8\). The leading coefficient is not 1.

  3. Factor out \(-1\). Divide every term by this common factor and keep it outside.

    \[-1\left(3 x^{2} - 10 x + 8\right)\]

  4. Find two integers with product \(24\) (a × c) and sum \(-10\). Use \(-6\) and \(-4\).

    \[\begin{aligned}(-6)(-4)&=24\\-6+(-4)&=-10\end{aligned}\]

    \[3 x^{2} - 6 x - 4 x + 8\]

    \[3 x\left(x - 2\right) - 4\left(x - 2\right)\]

    \[\left(4 - 3 x\right) \left(x - 2\right)\]

Check and justify

Multiply all the factors, including the GCF. The result must match every original coefficient.

\[\left(4 - 3 x\right) \left(x - 2\right)\]
\[= - 3 x^{2} + 10 x - 8\]

Check the greatest common factor first

Find the greatest numerical factor shared by all coefficients and the smallest power of each variable present in every term. Pull that GCF outside before factoring the remaining trinomial.

Keep the GCF in the final answer. If the leading coefficient is negative, factoring out a negative common factor often makes the remaining quadratic easier to use. A polynomial of degree 3 or 4 may become quadratic after a variable GCF is removed.

Leading coefficient 1: positive constant

For \(x^2+bx+c\), look for integers \(m,n\) with \(mn=c\) and \(m+n=b\). Then the factors are \((x+m)(x+n)\). Check both the product and the sum.

When \(c>0\), the pair has the same sign. A positive \(b\) requires two positive numbers; a negative \(b\) requires two negative numbers. List factor pairs systematically rather than guessing only one pair.

Leading coefficient 1: negative constant

When \(c<0\), one member of the pair is positive and the other is negative. Their product must be \(c\), while their signed sum must be \(b\).

If \(b>0\), the positive number has the greater absolute value. If \(b<0\), the negative number has the greater absolute value. The sign of \(c\) determines opposite signs regardless of the sign of \(b\).

The ac method for a leading coefficient other than 1

After removing the GCF, find two integers whose product is \(ac\) and whose sum is \(b\). These numbers split the middle term; they are not automatically the constants in the final binomials.

Replace \(bx\) with the two matching variable terms, group the resulting four terms in pairs, and factor a common binomial. You can use this method when \(a=1\) too, but the direct sum-and-product method is shorter then.

Perfect squares and integer-irreducible trinomials

A perfect-square trinomial has the form \(u^2+2uv+v^2=(u+v)^2\) or \(u^2-2uv+v^2=(u-v)^2\). Square first and last terms are not enough: verify the middle term.

Some primitive quadratics do not factor into integer-coefficient linear factors. If no integer pair has product \(ac\) and sum \(b\), state that the quadratic is irreducible over the integers. That does not by itself rule out factors using irrational or complex numbers.

Q34

Factor over the integers, or explain why no integer factorization exists.

\[x^{2} + x - 4\]
Answer

Irreducible over the integers.

  1. The GCF is 1, so no common numerical or variable factor needs to be removed.

  2. For an integer factorization, the middle-term pair must have product -4 and sum 1.

  3. All distinct signed integer pairs with that product are: -4 and 1 (sum -3); -1 and 4 (sum 3); -2 and 2 (sum 0).

  4. None has the required sum. Therefore the quadratic is irreducible over the integers; this statement is about the allowed coefficients, not a claim about every larger number system.

Check and justify

Every possible signed integer factor pair of -4 was tested; none adds to 1.

R7

Factor over the integers, or justify why it is irreducible.

\[x^{2} + 4 x + 7\]
Answer

Irreducible over the integers.

  1. The GCF is 1, so no common numerical or variable factor needs to be removed.

  2. For an integer factorization, the middle-term pair must have product 7 and sum 4.

  3. All distinct signed integer pairs with that product are: 1 and 7 (sum 8); -7 and -1 (sum -8).

  4. None has the required sum. Therefore the quadratic is irreducible over the integers; this statement is about the allowed coefficients, not a claim about every larger number system.

Check and justify

Every possible signed integer factor pair of 7 was tested; none adds to 4.

Quick questions and answers

Why check for a GCF first?

Removing the greatest common factor simplifies the coefficients and helps you factor completely. Keep the GCF in the final product, including any variable factor.

What changes when the leading coefficient is not 1?

Use a pair whose product is ac and whose sum is b, then split the middle term and factor by grouping. The pair is not automatically the pair of final binomial constants.

How do I choose the signs?

A positive constant requires a same-sign pair; a negative constant requires opposite signs. In both cases the signed sum must equal the middle coefficient.

How do I recognize a perfect square?

Check the first and last terms and verify that the middle term is plus or minus twice the product of their square-root terms. Square end terms alone are not sufficient.

What if no integer pair works?

After removing the GCF, state that the quadratic is irreducible over the integers if no integer pair has product ac and sum b. Other coefficient systems may still allow factorization.

When can I set each factor equal to zero?

Only when the product equals zero. First move all terms to one side of an equation, then factor and apply the zero-product property. Factoring an expression alone does not solve an equation.
