1. The three teaching models, honestly

The fundamental difference between these three options is not price. It is the teaching model. If you understand the model, the price, the results, and the fit follow naturally.

SOMATH — Small-group teacher-led math school Our Program

Model: student attends a weekly 90-120 minute class in person on the Upper West Side. Class capped at six students. A real math teacher teaches, watches every student's work in real time, and gives feedback.

SOMATH operates like a school, not a service. Classes meet weekly at our Upper West Side classroom at 226 W 79th Street. Cohorts are age-and-level based (Little Newtons for K-2, Kid Einsteins for grades 2-4, Young Fermats for grades 5-8, plus SHSAT, SAT, AP, and pre-calculus tracks). Curriculum is teacher-designed and progressive across the year. New families start with a free 60-minute evaluation and a written diagnostic.

IXL — Self-paced adaptive software

Model: student practices problems on a screen, alone, at home. Algorithm adjusts difficulty based on right/wrong answers. No live teacher.

IXL is a subscription web-and-app product used by millions of families and thousands of schools. A child logs in, picks a skill (or is assigned one), and answers problems. The algorithm gives easier or harder problems based on accuracy. Right answers earn points and awards. There is no explanation delivered by a teacher — if the child gets a problem wrong, IXL shows the correct steps and moves on.

Kumon — Franchise-based worksheet repetition

Model: student completes daily worksheets at home. Twice weekly, they visit the Kumon center for 20-30 minutes to submit worksheets and take a short check-in with a center instructor.

Kumon is a Japanese-origin franchise system with over 20 centers in NYC. Every child works through the same worksheet sequence at their own pace. The Kumon center is not primarily a classroom — it is a submission point. Actual work is done at home, daily, on worksheets. The instructor's role is to grade, monitor pace, and advance the child to the next worksheet level.

IXL — all-in cost

Kumon — all-in cost (NYC)

Manhattan and Brooklyn Kumon centers typically charge $150-$200 per month per subject. Add a one-time registration fee (~$50) and a materials/testing fee (~$30). If a child does both Math and Reading, monthly cost doubles. Extra time cost: 20-30 minutes daily of worksheet completion at home, plus two brief center visits per week. Parents often underestimate the at-home time commitment — a child who skips daily worksheets falls behind quickly.

The honest comparison. IXL is a nearly-free supplement. Kumon runs $150-$400 per month depending on subjects. SOMATH runs $246-$800 per month. The three are not competing on price — they are competing on model. Comparing them on price alone is like comparing a language app, a foreign-language textbook, and a private-school foreign language class. All three “teach” the same subject; they do very different things.

4. IXL: what it does well and where it stops

Where IXL wins. For daily practice reinforcement of school-year curriculum, IXL is genuinely excellent. It has broad content coverage, works on any device, and gives a self-motivated child a way to log 15-20 minutes of math practice most days at almost no cost. For families whose child is doing well in school and just wants to stay sharp, IXL is often the right answer.

Where IXL stops. IXL teaches by exposure — if the child sees enough problems of a certain type, they get better at that type. It does not explain concepts in a satisfying way, and it does not diagnose reasoning errors. When the child gets a problem wrong, IXL shows the correct steps and moves on. If the child does not understand the correct steps, IXL cannot help. It is also purely a solo activity, which for younger children (K-5) tends to mean either constant parent supervision (which defeats the purpose) or shallow, distracted practice.

Honest verdict. IXL is a good supplementary tool for any child. It is not a full math program for a child who is struggling, targeting selective tests, or ready for genuine problem-solving. Best combined with a real teaching program.

5. Kumon: what it does well and where it stops

Where Kumon wins. Kumon builds computational fluency and a daily-work habit better than any other program available in NYC. If your child needs to build arithmetic speed, memorize multiplication tables, or get comfortable with long division and fraction operations at speed, Kumon works. The daily-worksheet discipline itself teaches something valuable — showing up, doing the work, moving forward incrementally. For families whose primary concern is these outcomes, Kumon delivers.

Where Kumon stops. Kumon is worksheet repetition. It does not teach problem-solving, does not build genuine reasoning skill, and does not prepare students for tests that reward creative thinking (SHSAT, AMC 8, top-school interviews). A bright child in Kumon often plateaus after 6-12 months, works through worksheets on autopilot, and gets bored. Parents whose child is not building anything beyond speed after a year of Kumon are often disappointed. There is also very little real teaching — the center instructor grades and files worksheets; explanation of concepts is not the model.

Honest verdict. Kumon is genuinely good for the specific job of building arithmetic fluency and daily habit. It is not a general-purpose math education, not a competition or SHSAT preparation program, and not the right fit for a child who already has good computational skill. If your child can already do arithmetic quickly and accurately, Kumon has little to add.

6. SOMATH: what it does well and where it stops

Where SOMATH wins. Small-group, teacher-led, in-person teaching is the model that builds genuine mathematical thinking. A teacher who can see six students working on a problem in real time can watch reasoning, ask the exposing question, correct process (not just answers), and adjust on the fly. This is how top math programs everywhere work — from the best private schools to specialty math circles — because it is the only model that develops problem-solving skill. SOMATH runs this model for a targeted price point on the Upper West Side. Our SHSAT, AMC 8, and AP/Honors tracks reflect this: they are hard because the student is developing reasoning, not just accumulating problems.

Where SOMATH stops. SOMATH meets once a week. We do not check on daily practice. If a family needs daily accountability on top of weekly teaching, we typically recommend they add IXL for daily reinforcement — not as a substitute, but as a complement. We are also not the right fit for a child whose primary need is arithmetic drill; a child at that stage may do better with Kumon or a math-fact app until they are ready for problem-solving work. We are honest about this at the free evaluation — if a family arrives and the child needs something we do not do, we say so.

Honest verdict. SOMATH is the right fit for families who want their child to actually get taught — by a real teacher, in a small group, on a coherent curriculum. It is meaningfully more expensive than software or worksheets, because live small-group teaching costs more. For families who value that model, the cost is proportional.

7. Which is right for your child

Pick IXL if Your child is doing well in school, is self-motivated, and you just want a low-cost daily practice tool. Or you want to supplement any of the other two options with daily reinforcement.
Pick Kumon if Your child needs to build arithmetic speed and daily-work discipline, is under 4th grade, and computational fluency is the primary gap. Not the fit for reasoning or test prep.
Pick SOMATH if You want your child taught by a real teacher in a small group, targeting either general math growth (Little Newtons through Young Fermats) or specific tests (SHSAT, AMC 8, SAT, AP). Especially for gifted-track and selective-school preparation.
Do all three if Rarely necessary. SOMATH + IXL is a common combination that works well. Kumon + SOMATH usually creates curriculum conflict.

9. Can you combine them?

  1. SOMATH + IXL — a good combination. IXL handles daily reinforcement (15 min/day) while SOMATH does the actual teaching, curriculum, and diagnostic work. Many SOMATH families run this combination. Cost: one SOMATH plan + ~$10/month IXL.
  2. SOMATH + Kumon — usually a conflict. Kumon's fixed franchise curriculum runs its own sequence which frequently pulls a child out of sync with what we are teaching. Most families who join SOMATH end their Kumon subscription within a month. If your child needs both a reasoning program and a fluency program, we can coordinate on drill work internally.
  3. IXL + Kumon — largely redundant. Both are self-paced fluency programs. Combining them mostly just doubles the at-home worksheet load without adding teaching. Rarely the right combination.
  4. All three — almost never necessary. Time and cost add up quickly, and the child ends up with three different curriculum sequences pulling different directions.
