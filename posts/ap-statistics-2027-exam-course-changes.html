AP Statistics Changes for 2026–27: A Guide to the 2027 Exam

Five units, a fully digital exam, and new free-response expectations: what to change in your AP Statistics preparation.

School of Math · Updated October 6, 2026 · 4 min read

AP Statistics changed for the 2026–27 school year, and the May 2027 exam will be fully digital. Students should use the revised course framework, practice entering clear written responses in Bluebook, and avoid treating every chapter in an older prep book as a current exam requirement (College Board revision guidance).

These changes are documented in the official revisions. They do not mean that the course has become a calculator-skills course: students still need to formulate questions, collect data, analyze data, and interpret results.

The five units to organize your notes around

  1. Exploring One-Variable Data and Collecting Data
  2. Probability, Random Variables, and Probability Distributions
  3. Inference for Categorical Data: Proportions
  4. Inference for Quantitative Data: Means
  5. Regression Analysis

These are College Board’s current unit names, not a required week-by-week timetable. Teachers may sequence instruction differently while addressing the framework (current course overview).

Which older topics should you set aside for AP exam preparation?

The removed topics include combining random variables, the geometric distribution, chi-square goodness-of-fit testing, analyzing departures from linearity, and inference for regression slopes. The removal of slope inference does not remove all regression analysis: regression is still a named unit in the revised course (revision list, current units).

Do not discard an entire older textbook. Instead, label chapters “current scope,” “extension,” or “ask the teacher.” Older problems can still teach relevant ideas, but an old full-length test is not a reliable simulation of the new exam format. What should a strong digital response include?

Start by naming the population or process, the variable, and the purpose of the analysis. Next choose a procedure and justify its conditions. Finally connect the calculation to the original question. A correct calculator output without a conclusion is unfinished reasoning.

Original example: a survey claim

A school wants to estimate the proportion of all enrolled students who would use an earlier library opening. It surveys only students who are already in the library at 8 AM. Is a larger sample from that same group enough to fix the design?

Answer

No. The sampling method can overrepresent students who already use the library early. Increasing the number sampled within the same restricted group may reduce random variation without removing selection bias. A probability sample drawn from all enrolled students would better match the target population.

Original example: what does “significant” mean?

A properly designed test produces p = 0.03. At a significance level of 0.05, what decision follows, and what does it not establish?

Answer

Because 0.03 < 0.05, reject the null hypothesis in favor of the stated alternative. The result is evidence against the null under the model and conditions; it does not prove the alternative with 97% probability or establish that the effect is large or practically important.

A practical transition checklist

  • Compare your course notes with the current five-unit framework.
  • Use revised-format practice for timing and digital response habits.
  • Keep a separate error log for design, conditions, calculation, and interpretation.
  • Practice notation on a standard keyboard without replacing explanations with symbols.
  • Review confidence intervals versus hypothesis tests before memorizing more formulas.

Questions and answers

Is the 2027 AP Statistics exam fully digital?

Yes. Both multiple-choice and free-response responses are completed in Bluebook.

How many free-response questions are there?

The revised exam has four free-response questions, each worth 10 points.

Is regression still part of AP Statistics?

Yes. Regression Analysis remains a unit, but inference for regression slopes has been removed.

Can I still use an older textbook?

Yes, selectively. Match its topics to the current framework and use current-format resources for exam simulation.

Does a bigger sample fix a biased sampling method?

Not automatically. More observations from the same unrepresentative sampling process can leave the bias in place.
