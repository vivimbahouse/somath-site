1. What is money?

According to this SOMATH poster, money is what people use to buy things. Money can be in the form of coins or bills. We use money to pay for the things we need and the things we want — and we can also save money for later.

Examples of U.S. money (from the poster)

  • Penny — 1 cent (1¢)
  • Nickel — 5 cents (5¢)
  • Dime — 10 cents (10¢)
  • Quarter — 25 cents (25¢)
  • 1-dollar bill — $1

What can money do?

The poster shows three things money can do:

  • Buy — use money to buy things you need and want.
  • Save — keep money for later to reach your goals.
  • Share — use money to help others and make a difference.
Small change, big possibilities. Money is a tool that helps people trade fairly for things they need, save toward the future, and share with others.

4. What is a piggy bank?

The poster defines a piggy bank as “a place where we keep money safe for later.” It teaches three important money words:

  • Deposit — putting money in. Example from the poster: put $1 from your allowance into your piggy bank.
  • Withdrawal — taking money out. Example: take out money to buy a toy or a treat.
  • Savings — the money we keep for the future. Example: keep money in your piggy bank to reach a goal.

Small amounts add up

The poster shows a $1-a-week example: after Week 1 you have $1, after Week 2 you have $2, after Week 3 you have $3, and after Week 4 you have $4.

Why use a piggy bank?

  1. Keep money safe — your money stays in one place and is less likely to get lost.
  2. Save for a goal — you can save for something you really want, like a toy, book, or trip.
  3. Watch your savings grow — as you add more money, your piggy bank gets fuller and your dreams get closer.
Little coins make big dreams. Save today. Brighter tomorrows ahead.

Practice — 5 questions from this poster

  1. Q1. Fill in the blank from the poster: “A piggy bank is a place where we keep money ____ for later.”
    Show explanation

  2. Q2. What word means putting money in?
    Show explanation
    Deposit.
  3. Q3. What word means taking money out?
    Show explanation
    Withdrawal.
  4. Q4. The poster shows a $1-a-week savings example. How much is in the piggy bank after Week 4?
    Show explanation
    $4. Week 1 = $1, Week 2 = $2, Week 3 = $3, Week 4 = $4.
  5. Q5. Name one reason (from the poster) to use a piggy bank.
    Show explanation
    Any one of: keep money safe (less likely to get lost), save for a goal (a toy, book, or trip), or watch your savings grow as you add more money.

5. Saving over time — reach a goal

This poster gives kids a 4-step plan for saving over time.

  1. Choose a Goal. Pick something you want to buy (the poster shows a bike).
  2. Save Regularly. Put aside a little money often. Even a small amount helps.
  3. Watch It Grow. Your savings add up over time. The poster shows: Week 1 = $5, Week 2 = $10, Week 3 = $15, Week 4 = $20.
  4. Reach Your Goal. Keep going, and you can buy it — “I did it!”

How the math works

The example on the poster is $5 saved every week. That gives:

  • Week 1: $5
  • Week 2: $5 + $5 = $10
  • Week 3: $10 + $5 = $15
  • Week 4: $15 + $5 = $20

This is skip counting by 5 — the same skip counting your child will practice again in Little Newtons Class 12.

Patience today. Bigger possibilities tomorrow. Set a goal, save a little regularly, and be proud of your progress.

Practice — 5 questions from this poster

  1. Q1. What are the four steps on the poster?

  2. Q2. In Step 1, what does the boy on the poster want?
    Show explanation
    A bike. The thought bubble says “I want a bike!”
  3. Q3. How much money is in the jar at Week 3?
    Show explanation

  4. Q4. How much money did the child save each week in the poster example?
    Show explanation

  5. Q5. If the same child kept saving $5 a week for one more week, how much would be in the jar at Week 5?
    Show explanation
    $20 + $5 = $25.

6. Counting your savings — coins & dollars

This poster teaches how to identify and count pennies, nickels, dimes, quarters, and dollar bills.

Meet the money

Coin / BillValue
Penny1 cent (1¢)
Nickel5 cents (5¢)
Dime10 cents (10¢)
Quarter25 cents (25¢)
1-Dollar Bill$1 = 100¢

How to count (poster’s 3-step method)

  1. Name the coin or bill. (“This is a quarter!”)
  2. Say its value. (“It’s worth 25 cents!”)
  3. Add the values together.

Worked examples from the poster

  • 1 quarter + 1 dime + 1 nickel = 25 + 10 + 5 = 40¢
  • 2 quarters = 25 + 25 = 50¢
  • 3 dimes + 2 pennies = 30 + 2 = 32¢
  • 1 dollar bill + 2 quarters = $1.00 + 50¢ = $1.50
Money fact: 100 cents = 1 dollar. Small coins can grow into big savings.

  3. Q3. Count: 2 quarters = ?
    Show explanation
    25 + 25 = 50¢ — that’s half a dollar.
  4. Q4. Count: 3 dimes + 2 pennies = ?
    Show explanation
    30 + 2 = 32¢.
  5. Q5. Count: 1 dollar bill + 2 quarters = ?
    Show explanation
    $1.00 + 50¢ = $1.50.

7. The 3-jar system — Save · Spend · Share

The 3-Jar System is a simple way to plan what to save, what to spend, and what to share. You divide your money into three jars.

JarMeaning (from the poster)Examples
SAVEMoney you keep for later.A bike, a book, or a game.
SPENDMoney you use now.A snack, school supplies, or a small toy.
SHAREMoney you use to help others.Donating, helping a cause, or buying a gift.

How it works (4 steps on the poster)

  1. Get money — allowance, a gift, or chore money.
  2. Split it up — decide how to divide your money.
  3. Put money in each jar.
  4. Use your plan wisely — make good choices and feel proud.

Poster example: if you have $10

  • SAVE = $5
  • SPEND = $3
  • SHARE = $2

Total: $5 + $3 + $2 = $10. Every dollar has a job. The poster reminds kids: “Different amounts are okay — find what works for you.”

A smart plan for every dollar.

  2. Q2. Which jar is for “money you keep for later”?
    Show explanation
    SAVE. Examples on the poster: a bike, a book, or a game.
  3. Q3. Which jar is for “money you use now”?
    Show explanation
    SPEND. Examples on the poster: a snack, school supplies, or a small toy.
  4. Q4. The poster shows an example with $10. How does it split up?
    Show explanation

8. Everyday money decisions

Before buying anything, this poster teaches kids to ask three questions:

  1. Can I afford it? Do I have enough money right now? How much does it cost?
  2. Do I need it? Needs help us stay healthy, safe, and alive.
  3. Should I save instead? Sometimes it’s better to save for something bigger and more special.

“Let’s Decide” — the four items on the poster

New Sneakers$60Buy now · Save instead · I don’t need it
Smart choices today, brighter tomorrow. Think before you spend. Make good choices. Save for your goals. Money helps you create a brighter future.

Practice — 5 questions from this poster

  1. Q1. Name the three questions the poster asks you to think about before buying.
    Show explanation
    1) Can I afford it? 2) Do I need it? 3) Should I save instead?
  2. Q2. You have $10 in your SPEND jar and the video game costs $50. Can you afford it?
    Show explanation
    No. $10 is less than $50, so you cannot afford it right now. You would need to save $40 more (or pick something you can afford).
  3. Q3. Your feet grew and your old sneakers don’t fit. The New Sneakers on the poster cost $60. Is this a need or a want?
    Show explanation
    A need. Shoes that fit are on the needs side (like on Poster 2). If your feet grew and old shoes don’t fit, new shoes are a need.
  4. Q4. You have $5 in your SPEND jar. The ice cream costs $3 and the new book costs $12. Which one can you afford today?
    Show explanation
    You can afford the ice cream ($3 is less than $5). The book costs $12, and $12 is more than $5, so you can’t buy the book yet — you would need to save $7 more.
  5. Q5. Fill in the poster’s ending: “Smart choices today, ______ tomorrow.”
    Show explanation
    Brighter. “Smart choices today, brighter tomorrow.”
FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is a need vs. a want (for kids)?
A need is something we must have to live, be healthy, and stay safe — food, shelter, water, clothing, healthcare, and rest. A want is something we’d like to have but can live without — video games, toys, ice cream, a bicycle, a new phone, or a vacation. The poster’s rule: Needs come first. Wants are extra. (Source: Poster 2 in this class pack.)
What are the four things you can do with money?
Earn, Spend, Save, and Share. We earn money by working, helping others, or receiving an allowance. We spend money to buy things we need or want. We save money for the future to reach our goals. We share money by giving to help others or a good cause. (Source: Poster 3.)
What is the 3-jar system for kids?
The 3-jar system splits every dollar into three jars: SAVE (money you keep for later, like a bike, book, or game), SPEND (money you use now, like a snack or a small toy), and SHARE (money to help others). The poster’s starter example, using $10, splits into SAVE $5, SPEND $3, SHARE $2. Different amounts are okay — find what works for your family. (Source: Poster 7.)
How do you count coins and dollars?
Follow the 3-step method on Poster 6: (1) name the coin or bill, (2) say its value, and (3) add the values together. Coin values: penny 1¢, nickel 5¢, dime 10¢, quarter 25¢. And 100¢ = $1. Example from the poster: 1 quarter + 1 dime + 1 nickel = 25 + 10 + 5 = 40¢.
What questions should a child ask before buying something?
Poster 8 gives three questions: (1) Can I afford it? Do I have enough money right now, and how much does it cost? (2) Do I need it? Needs help us stay healthy, safe, and alive. (3) Should I save instead? Sometimes it’s better to save for something bigger and more special.
What age is this class pack for?
This class pack is designed for Little Newtons (grades 1–2) at SOMATH, roughly ages 6–8. Vocabulary, dollar amounts, and arithmetic all stay inside what a first- or second-grader can handle. Coin arithmetic and making change get more practice later in Class 30 (Money combinations).
