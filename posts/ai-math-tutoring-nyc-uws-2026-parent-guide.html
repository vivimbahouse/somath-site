What changed in NYC: from the ChatGPT ban to AI-approved classrooms

In January 2023 the New York City Department of Education banned ChatGPT on school networks and devices. By summer 2025 that ban had been fully reversed, and during the 2025–2026 school year NYC Public Schools issued formal guidance approving AI for student learning. By the time Mayor Mamdani announced the NYC Solves math curriculum expansion in May 2026, AI was no longer treated as a threat to learning — it was treated as a tool teachers were expected to know how to use well.

That shift mirrors what families were already doing. By spring 2026, surveys of NYC private and public-school parents showed the majority were using AI — usually ChatGPT or Khanmigo — to help with math homework at least weekly. The kitchen-table experience that one NYC parent, who later founded an AI SHSAT prep platform, has described publicly is now the median experience: a parent and child sitting with a laptop, asking AI to re-explain whatever the math teacher covered that day.

For Upper West Side families specifically, AI also coincides with three other 2026 forces: the NYC Solves curriculum standardization reaching District 3 middle schools this fall, the computer-adaptive SHSAT format debuting this October, and a continuing post-pandemic appetite for enrichment that has driven Manhattan tutoring demand to a multi-year high. Parents are not just asking “is AI any good?” They are asking “how do I combine AI, my child’s school, and outside enrichment without burning my child out?” That is the right question.

What AI math tutoring actually does well in 2026

Three things AI is genuinely good at, with current 2026 capability levels:

Re-explaining a concept in a second voice. If your child’s teacher explained the unit circle one way and it did not land, AI will explain it a different way — ten different ways if you ask. For visual learners, ChatGPT-4o and Claude can describe diagrams and walk through transformations step by step. This is the single highest-leverage use of AI for math at home. A January 2026 EdWeek investigation found that AI’s strongest impact was in surfacing individual learning gaps and offering personalized re-explanations — especially for students who do not have a parent who remembers the math.

Generating unlimited practice. Pattern fluency — the kind of repetition that gets a 7th grader truly comfortable with linear equations — requires seeing the same problem type in many forms. No textbook has fifty variants of one Heart of Algebra question. AI does. For SHSAT and SAT math prep this is genuinely powerful: a child can drill the four most-tested SHSAT question types until they are reflexive, and AI will adjust difficulty in real time.

Shortening the feedback loop. Instead of you, the parent, looking at your child’s working a week later (when you both have forgotten the problem), AI can check method in seconds. A 2026 study from UC Berkeley’s School of Information (the StepWise project) found that students who got immediate AI feedback on their working — and were required to verify the AI’s reasoning — learned faster than students with delayed paper-only feedback.

Where AI math tutoring fails — and why it matters for your child

The honest list of what AI does badly, in mid-2026, is just as important.

It gives the answer instead of teaching the method. A December 2024 study described “metacognitive laziness” — students who lean on standard ChatGPT-style help retain less of what they study. This is the single biggest danger and the reason ChatGPT was banned by NYC schools in the first place. Updated research in 2026 confirms it: when AI hands over a solution, the child has not done the cognitive work that creates learning. Socratic mode (used in Khanmigo and in well-prompted ChatGPT) largely solves this problem — but only if you use it.

It cannot grade handwritten working. Real math is written by hand on paper. No 7th grader keys integration by substitution into a textbox. Yet most AI tutors are built around exactly that: a textbox plus, at best, a screenshot upload. Recent tools (StepWise, the latest Khanmigo, Photomath Pro) accept handwritten input or voice questions and close this gap. Pure chat tools do not.

It still makes confident errors. Even the strongest 2026 models will occasionally give a wrong arithmetic answer with total confidence. A child who treats AI as authoritative will absorb wrong methods. Treat every AI answer as a hypothesis to verify, not gospel.

It cannot diagnose, motivate, or know your child. This is the deepest gap. The reason families pay for tutors is rarely “explain this concept one more time” — YouTube and ChatGPT do that. Families pay because their child has shut down, lost confidence, hit a wall they cannot describe, or needs someone to notice that their hand goes up less in class. No AI in 2026 does that work.

The human-in-the-loop model the research keeps pointing to

Three independent 2026 research strands — Brookings, Bloom’s revisited two-sigma tutoring work, and EdWeek’s personalization investigation — arrived at the same conclusion: the best learning outcomes come not from AI replacing teachers, and not from teachers ignoring AI, but from a human-in-the-loop model. A teacher diagnoses, motivates, and sets the path. AI does the unlimited practice, the second-voice explanation, and the instant feedback in between sessions. The student is always in front of paper, working by hand, with a real teacher who knows their name.

This is also what families on the Upper West Side intuitively already buy. Parents who can afford SOMATH, Mathnasium, RSM, or a private tutor are not buying explanation — explanation is free. They are buying diagnosis, accountability, and the relationship that makes a child keep going.

Why this matters for SHSAT and SAT prep in particular. AI is excellent at generating endless practice items and drilling speed. The edge a human prep teacher brings — pacing strategy, test-anxiety management, when to skip a question, when to come back — is exactly the socio-emotional work AI is worst at. For the 2026 computer-adaptive SHSAT, that gap is widening, not closing.

How UWS parents should use AI at home: a 3-rule playbook

Rule 1. Hand-written first, AI second. Your child writes the working on paper, by hand, before any AI is opened. AI then checks the method. Reversing the order — AI first, child copies — is what produces the retention damage the 2024 metacognitive-laziness study documented.

Rule 2. Socratic mode on, answer-mode off. Use Khanmigo, or prompt ChatGPT with: “Act as a Socratic math tutor. Do not give the answer. Ask me one question at a time to help me find the next step.” Research is consistent: this single change protects almost all of AI’s upside without the downside.

Rule 3. AI cannot diagnose — book a human evaluation at least once a year. No AI in 2026 can tell you, “your daughter understands fractions conceptually but freezes on word problems because of test anxiety,” or “your son’s arithmetic is fine but he has a misconception about negative exponents that will block him in Algebra II.” A human teacher catches these patterns in 30 minutes. Frequently asked questions

Can ChatGPT replace a math tutor for my NYC child?

Not for most students. ChatGPT is excellent at re-explaining a concept, generating extra practice, and giving instant feedback on a specific answer. It fails at three things human tutors do best: diagnosing what your child does not yet know, building motivation, and grading handwritten working. The 2026 consensus is AI works best as a supplement, not a replacement.

What is the best AI math tutor for NYC students in 2026?

It depends on the use case. Khanmigo for homework with built-in Socratic mode; ChatGPT-4o for re-explanation and unlimited practice; Photomath for step-by-step from a photo (but use sparingly); Third Space’s Skye for younger learners. Match the tool to the task and require Socratic mode whenever possible.

Did NYC Public Schools approve AI for student learning in 2026?

Yes. NYC Public Schools reversed its ChatGPT ban and approved AI for student learning during the 2025–2026 school year. The policy permits classroom AI that supports learning and prohibits dishonest use on graded assessments.

Will AI tutors replace human math teachers on the Upper West Side?

No. AI has replaced the lowest tier of explanation-only tutoring. It has not touched the diagnosis, motivation, and trust work that small-group teachers at SOMATH do. The 2026 verdict is human-plus-AI hybrids beat either alone.

How should UWS parents use AI to support math at home?

Three rules: (1) Child writes by hand first, AI checks second. (2) Turn on Socratic mode. (3) Treat every AI answer as a hypothesis to verify. Pair AI practice with weekly human instruction — a small-group class at SOMATH or a free 60-minute diagnostic — so a teacher can spot patterns AI cannot.

Is AI tutoring safe to use with younger children (K–3)?

Use it sparingly. Younger children benefit most from concrete manipulatives, real teachers, and small-group conversation. AI can help with read-aloud practice problems and quick math facts drilling, but the developmental win for K–3 math is human relationship and physical materials.
