Understanding rational expressions

A rational expression is a quotient of two polynomials. The denominator polynomial must not be the zero polynomial, and any input that makes its value zero must be excluded. A numerator may be zero: zero divided by a nonzero number equals zero.

For \(\frac{2x+5}{x+4}\), the numerator is \(2x+5\) and the denominator is \(x+4\). The expression is undefined at \(x=-4\). At \(x=1\), it equals \(\frac{7}{5}\). Substitution is safe only after you check the denominator.

Simplifying rewrites an expression; solving an equation finds inputs that make a statement true. Here we simplify and evaluate expressions, not set every numerator equal to zero. In a word problem, state what the variable represents and include units; practical limits can be stronger than algebraic restrictions.

Q1 Practice 1 of 5

For \(F(x)=\frac{5x-2}{2x+6}\), identify the numerator and denominator, find the excluded value, and evaluate \(F(-2)\).

Answer

Numerator \(5x-2\); denominator \(2x+6\); \(x\ne-3\); \(F(-2)=-6\).

  1. Solve \(2x+6=0\), giving \(x=-3\). The proposed input \(-2\) is allowed.
  2. At \(-2\), the numerator is \(-12\) and the denominator is \(2\), so \(F(-2)=-12/2=-6\).

Q2 Practice 2 of 5

Evaluate \(\frac{3x^2-5}{2x-1}\) at \(x=-2\) and \(x=\frac12\). Explain any undefined result.

Answer

At \(-2\): \(-\frac75\). At \(\frac12\): undefined.

  1. For \(-2\), compute \((3\cdot4-5)/(-4-1)=7/(-5)=-7/5\).
  2. For \(1/2\), the denominator \(2(1/2)-1\) is zero. No quotient is defined there.

Q3 Practice 3 of 5

For \(\frac{x^2-9}{x^2-x-6}\), classify the result at \(x=-3,\ 2,\ 3\) as zero, nonzero, or undefined. Give each defined value.

Answer

At \(-3\): zero. At \(2\): nonzero, \(\frac54\). At \(3\): undefined.

  1. The denominator factors as \((x-3)(x+2)\), so \(3\) and \(-2\) are excluded.
  2. At \(-3\), the fraction is \(0/6=0\). At \(2\), it is \((-5)/(-4)=5/4\). At \(3\), it is \(0/0\), which is undefined, not zero.

Q4 Practice 4 of 5

Consider \(\frac{3x+9}{x-5}\). (a) Which value of \(x\) makes the expression undefined? (b) Which value makes it equal to zero? (c) Find its value at \(x=2\).

Answer

(a) \(x=5\). (b) \(x=-3\). (c) \(-5\).

  1. (a) An expression is undefined when its denominator is zero. Solve \(x-5=0\), giving \(x=5\). This input is not allowed.
  2. (b) A fraction equals zero when its numerator is zero and its denominator is not zero. Solve \(3x+9=0\), giving \(x=-3\). At this input the denominator is \(-8\), so the fraction is \(0/(-8)=0\).
  3. (c) Substitute \(x=2\): \(\frac{3(2)+9}{2-5}=\frac{15}{-3}=-5\). The denominator is nonzero, so this value is defined.

Q5 Practice 5 of 5

A workshop charges $108 plus $8 per student. A sponsor pays $24 of the bill. Write the average amount paid by each of \(n\) students and compare the averages for 7 and 12 students.

Answer

Average \(\frac{84+8n}{n}=8+\frac{84}{n}\) dollars. For 7 students: $20; for 12: $15, which is $5 less.

  1. Subtract the sponsor payment from the total: \(108+8n-24=84+8n\). Then divide by \(n\).
  2. Compute \(8+84/7=20\) and \(8+84/12=15\). Here \(n\) must be a positive whole number; algebraically \(n\ne0\).

Finding excluded values

Excluded values come from the original expression, before cancellation. Set each original denominator equal to zero, factor it, solve, and record all resulting values. A fraction does not become valid at an excluded input just because its simplified form happens to be defined there.

For \(\frac{x+5}{x^2-16}\), factor the denominator as \((x-4)(x+4)\). Both \(4\) and \(-4\) are excluded. For \(\frac{x-4}{x^2-16}\), cancellation gives \(\frac1{x+4}\), but the restrictions are still \(x\ne-4,4\).

We use real-number domains in this class. When the variable represents people, elapsed time, or length, also impose the practical conditions: positive whole numbers for people, or positive measurements when appropriate.

Q8 Practice 3 of 5

Find every excluded value of \(\frac{x^2-16}{x^2+2x-8}\). Which exclusion comes from a factor that cancels?

Answer

\(x\ne-4,2\); the factor \(x+4\) cancels, but \(-4\) stays excluded.

  1. The denominator factors as \((x+4)(x-2)\); the numerator is \((x-4)(x+4)\).
  2. The simplified formula \((x-4)/(x-2)\) does not restore the missing input \(-4\).

Q9 Practice 4 of 5

Find the real domain of \(\frac{2x-1}{2x^2-7x+3}\). Is \(x=\frac12\) a zero of the original expression?

Answer

\(x\ne\frac12,3\). At \(1/2\) the original is undefined, not zero.

  1. Factor \(2x^2-7x+3=(2x-1)(x-3)\). Both denominator factors must be nonzero.
  2. Although the numerator vanishes at \(1/2\), so does the denominator; \(0/0\) is undefined.

Q10 Practice 5 of 5

Find every excluded value of \(\frac{x^2-9}{2x^3-18x}\), then simplify. Which restrictions would be missed by looking only at the simplified denominator?

Answer

\(\frac1{2x}\), with \(x\ne-3,0,3\). Looking only at the final denominator misses \(-3\) and \(3\).

  1. Factor the denominator as \(2x(x-3)(x+3)\) and the numerator as \((x-3)(x+3)\).
  2. Cancel the matching quadratic factors only after recording all three exclusions.

Factoring before simplifying

Cancellation works on factors, so turn sums into products first. Start with a greatest common factor, then check for differences of squares and factorable trinomials. A difference of squares follows \(a^2-b^2=(a-b)(a+b)\).

To factor \(x^2+7x+10\), find two numbers with product 10 and sum 7: 5 and 2. Thus it is \((x+5)(x+2)\). If the denominator is \(x^2-25=(x-5)(x+5)\), the common factor is the whole group \((x+5)\).

Record \(x\ne-5,5\) first, then cancel \((x+5)\) to get \(\frac{x+2}{x-5}\). The canceled factor represented multiplication by one only where it was nonzero. Expanding everything first usually hides this structure.

Simplifying and keeping restrictions

For nonzero \(B\) and \(C\), \(\frac{AC}{BC}=\frac AB\). Canceling \(C\) means dividing both the numerator and denominator by the same nonzero quantity. It does not mean erasing any symbol that appears in both places.

For \(\frac{(x-6)(x+2)}{(x-6)(x+7)}\), exclude \(x=6,-7\) before simplifying. The result is \(\frac{x+2}{x+7}\) on that original domain. Its simpler appearance does not make \(x=6\) valid.

Reduce numerical coefficients and variable powers as factors too. For \(\frac{12x^3}{18x^2}\), reduce \(12/18\) to \(2/3\) and \(x^3/x^2\) to \(x\). The result is \(2x/3\), with \(x\ne0\).

Avoiding cancellation mistakes

A term is a part separated by addition or subtraction; a factor multiplies the entire expression. In \(x+8\), \(x\) is a term, not a factor of the whole numerator. Therefore \(\frac{x+8}{x}\) cannot simplify to 8.

You may divide each term by a common denominator: \(\frac{5x+15}{x}=5+\frac{15}{x}\), with \(x\ne0\). Or factor first: \(5x+15=5(x+3)\). Neither method permits the cancellation of \(x\) from just one term while leaving the other untouched.

A quick numerical check can disprove a proposed rule: at \(x=4\), \(\frac{x+8}{x}=3\), not 8. One matching numerical check does not prove an identity, however. To prove equivalence, use valid algebra and keep the original domain. Also remember \(7-x=-(x-7)\).

Q21 Practice 1 of 5

A student claims \(\frac{3x+12}{3x}=4\). Disprove the claim at \(x=2\), then give a correct expression and restriction.

Answer

At \(x=2\), the original is \(3\), not \(4\). Correct: \(1+\frac4x\), \(x\ne0\).

  2. The variable is not a factor of the entire sum \(3x+12\), so it cannot simply be erased.

Q22 Practice 2 of 5

A student simplifies \(\frac{6x^2+18x}{3x}\) to \(2x+18\). Correct the result and explain both cancellations.

Answer

\(2x+6\), with \(x\ne0\).

  1. Factor the numerator as \(6x(x+3)\). Divide the coefficient by \(3\) and cancel one \(x\), leaving \(2(x+3)=2x+6\).
  2. Alternatively, \(6x^2/(3x)=2x\) and \(18x/(3x)=6\); the second term is not 18.

Q23 Practice 3 of 5

Rewrite \(\frac{6x^2-9x+12}{3x}\) as separate terms. State its restriction and evaluate it at \(x=-2\).

Answer

\(2x-3+\frac4x\), \(x\ne0\); the value at \(-2\) is \(-9\).

  1. Divide every numerator term by \(3x\): \(2x-3+4/x\).
  2. At \(-2\), the expression is \(-4-3-2=-9\).

Q24 Practice 4 of 5

A student says \(\frac{x^2-4x+4}{x^2-4}=\frac{x-2}{x+2}\) for every \(x\ne-2\). Correct the domain and compare the two formulas at \(x=2\).

Answer

The equality holds only for \(x\ne-2,2\). At \(2\), the original is undefined, while the simplified formula gives \(0\).

  1. Factor to obtain \((x-2)^2/[(x-2)(x+2)]\). Both \(2\) and \(-2\) were excluded before cancellation.
  2. A canceled factor does not extend the original domain.

Q25 Practice 5 of 5

A student cancels \(4-x\) with \(x-4\) in \(\frac{(4-x)(x+3)}{x^2-16}\) and gets \(\frac{x+3}{x+4}\). Find and correct the sign error; state all exclusions.

Answer

\(-\frac{x+3}{x+4}\), with \(x\ne-4,4\).

  1. Use \(4-x=-(x-4)\) and \(x^2-16=(x-4)(x+4)\). The common factor cancels but the negative sign remains.

Multiplying rational expressions

Multiplication follows the same rule as numerical fractions: multiply the numerators and multiply the denominators. First record the exclusions from every original denominator, then factor. Cancel common factors before multiplying the remaining factors to keep the algebra small.

For \(\frac{x^2-25}{x+3}\cdot\frac{x+3}{x+5}\), the original denominators exclude \(-3\) and \(-5\). Factor \(x^2-25=(x-5)(x+5)\). Cancel \((x+3)\) and \((x+5)\) to obtain \(x-5\), still with both exclusions.

A numerator can be zero in a product, so do not automatically exclude its zeros. You also do not need a common denominator to multiply fractions. A factor may cancel across the two fractions because, after multiplication, it is a factor of the entire numerator and denominator.

Dividing rational expressions

Dividing by a fraction means multiplying by its reciprocal. Keep the first fraction unchanged and flip only the divisor. Before doing that, require both original denominators to be nonzero and the divisor itself to be nonzero.

For \(\frac{x^2-16}{x+5}\div\frac{x-4}{x+5}\), the denominators exclude \(-5\). The divisor is zero at \(4\), so exclude that input too. Rewrite as \(\frac{x^2-16}{x+5}\cdot\frac{x+5}{x-4}\). Factoring and cancellation give \(x+4\), with \(x\ne-5,4\).

The extra check on the divisor is essential: division by zero is undefined even when the reciprocal form cancels to something harmless-looking. A zero numerator in the first fraction is allowed, provided every denominator and the divisor satisfy their restrictions.

  1. Use the original denominators and the numerator of each divisor to find \(x\ne -4, -3, -1, 3\).
  2. Replace division by multiplication by the reciprocal of the divisor. Factor completely: \(\frac{3 \left(x - 3\right) \left(x + 3\right)}{\left(x + 1\right) \left(x + 3\right)}\cdot\frac{\left(x + 1\right) \left(x + 4\right)}{2 \left(x - 3\right)}\).
  3. Cancel matching nonzero factors from the numerator and denominator. The result is \(\frac{3 \left(x + 4\right)}{2}\). Keep all original exclusions, even when their factors disappear.

Combining multiplication and division

Multiplication and division have equal priority. With no grouping symbols, work from left to right, or rewrite each division as multiplication by a reciprocal while preserving the original grouping. The chain \(A\cdot B\div C\) becomes \(A\cdot B\cdot\frac1C\).

Keep a restriction list as you work: every original denominator must be nonzero, and every divisor must be defined and nonzero. Factor the full product only after you have recorded these conditions. Cancel matching factors and then simplify the remaining coefficients.

For \(\frac{x^2-16}{x}\cdot\frac{x}{x+5}\) divided by \(\frac{x-4}{x+5}\), exclude \(0,-5,4\). Rewrite the division, factor \((x-4)(x+4)\), and cancel to obtain \(x+4\). All three exclusions remain.

Finish with a check at a permitted input. At \(x=6\), the example is \(\frac{20}{6}\cdot\frac6{11}\div\frac2{11}=10\), matching \(6+4\). Checking a value helps catch mistakes but does not replace factoring or the restriction list. Parentheses matter: \(A\div(B\cdot C)\) is not generally the same as \(A\div B\cdot C\).

W1 Homework word problem 1 of 12

A pottery club pays $120 plus $9 per participant, then receives a $30 group discount. Write the average cost for \(n\) participants. How much less does each person pay with 18 participants than with 12?

Answer

\(9+\frac{90}{n}\) dollars per person; $2.50 less with 18 participants.

  1. After the discount, total cost is \(120+9n-30=90+9n\). Divide by \(n\), a positive whole number.
  2. For 12 people: \(9+90/12=16.50\). For 18: \(9+90/18=14\). The difference is \(16.50-14=2.50\). Algebraically \(n\ne0\).

W2 Homework word problem 2 of 12

A cyclist rides 24 miles to a park in \(t\) hours and returns along the same route in \(t+\frac12\) hours. Write the average speed for the entire trip and evaluate it when \(t=\frac32\). State a practical domain.

Answer

Average speed \(\frac{48}{2t+\frac12}=\frac{96}{4t+1}\) mph; \(\frac{96}{7}\) mph when \(t=\frac32\). Practical domain: \(t>0\).

  1. Total distance is 48 miles; total time is \(t+(t+1/2)=2t+1/2\). Divide total distance by total time, not by one leg’s time.
  2. At \(t=3/2\), total time is \(7/2\) hours, giving \(48/(7/2)=96/7\) mph. Algebra excludes \(t=-1/4\), already outside \(t>0\).

Quick questions and answers

What is a rational expression?

A rational expression is a quotient of polynomials. Its denominator must be nonzero for each allowed input.

How do you simplify a rational expression?

Record exclusions from the original denominator, factor completely, and cancel only common nonzero factors. Keep all original restrictions.

Why do canceled factors still give excluded values?

A canceled factor was part of the original denominator. An input that made that denominator zero was never allowed, even when the new formula is defined there.

How do you multiply rational expressions?

Factor all numerators and denominators, record original denominator restrictions, cancel common factors, and multiply what remains. A common denominator is not required.

What extra restriction matters when dividing?

The divisor must be defined and nonzero. Check both original denominators and the numerator of the divisor before multiplying by its reciprocal.
