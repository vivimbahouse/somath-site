Standard form and coefficients

The quadratic formula only works on an equation written in standard form, \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\) with \(a \neq 0\). The left side lists the terms in descending powers of \(x\) and the right side is exactly zero. The number in front of \(x^2\) is \(a\), the number in front of \(x\) is \(b\), and the term without \(x\) is the constant \(c\). If \(a\) were 0, there would be no \(x^2\) term and the equation would be linear, not quadratic.

Read each coefficient together with the sign in front of it. In \(2x^2 - 5x + 3 = 0\), the middle term is \(-5x\), so \(b = -5\), not 5. When terms are on both sides, move everything to the left first by adding or subtracting the same terms on both sides. When a term is missing, its coefficient is 0: \(x^2 - 9 = 0\) means \(x^2 + 0x - 9 = 0\), so \(b = 0\). A bare \(x^2\) means \(a = 1\), and \(-x\) means \(b = -1\).

Q1

Identify \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\).

\[4x^2 + 9x - 2 = 0\]
Answer

\(a = 4, \; b = 9, \; c = -2\)

  1. The equation is already in standard form: descending powers on the left and 0 on the right.

  2. Read each coefficient with its sign. The constant is \(-2\), not 2.

    \[a = 4, \quad b = 9, \quad c = -2\]

Q2

Write the equation in standard form. Then identify \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\).

\[5x^2 = 3x + 8\]
Answer

\(5x^2 - 3x - 8 = 0; \; a = 5, \; b = -3, \; c = -8\)

  1. Subtract \(3x\) and \(8\) from both sides so the right side is 0.

    \[5x^2 - 3x - 8 = 0\]

  2. Read each coefficient with its sign.

    \[a = 5, \quad b = -3, \quad c = -8\]

Q3

Identify \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\). Watch for a missing term.

\[x^2 + 6x = 0\]
Answer

\(a = 1, \; b = 6, \; c = 0\)

  1. There is no constant term, so write it as \(+0\).

    \[x^2 + 6x + 0 = 0\]

  2. A bare \(x^2\) has coefficient 1.

    \[a = 1, \quad b = 6, \quad c = 0\]

Q4

Write the equation in standard form. Then identify \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\).

\[7 - 2x^2 = 0\]
Answer

\(-2x^2 + 0x + 7 = 0; \; a = -2, \; b = 0, \; c = 7\)

  1. Reorder the terms in descending powers of \(x\). The sign stays with \(2x^2\). The \(x\)-term is missing, so its coefficient is 0.

    \[-2x^2 + 0x + 7 = 0\]

  2. Read the coefficients.

    \[a = -2, \quad b = 0, \quad c = 7\]

  3. Also correct: multiplying every term by \(-1\) gives \(2x^2 - 7 = 0\), with \(a = 2\), \(b = 0\), \(c = -7\). Both equations have the same solutions.

Q5

Expand and write the equation in standard form. Then identify \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\).

\[(x + 3)(x - 1) = 2x\]
Answer

\(x^2 + 0x - 3 = 0; \; a = 1, \; b = 0, \; c = -3\)

  1. Expand the left side with FOIL.

    \[x^2 - x + 3x - 3 = 2x \;\Rightarrow\; x^2 + 2x - 3 = 2x\]

  2. Subtract \(2x\) from both sides. The \(x\)-terms cancel.

    \[x^2 + 0x - 3 = 0\]

  3. Read the coefficients.

    \[a = 1, \quad b = 0, \quad c = -3\]

The quadratic formula

Completing the square on \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\) in general produces one formula that solves every quadratic equation:

\[x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}\]

Read it in parts. The \(-b\) means the opposite of \(b\): if \(b = -5\), then \(-b = 5\). The \(\pm\) sign means you calculate twice, once adding the square root and once subtracting it, which is why a quadratic can have two solutions. The expression under the radical, \(b^2 - 4ac\), is called the discriminant. The fraction bar runs under the entire numerator, so \(2a\) divides both \(-b\) and the square root, not just one of them.

A good routine is: write the equation in standard form, list \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\), compute the discriminant first, and only then fill in the formula. Computing \(b^2 - 4ac\) on its own keeps the arithmetic short and tells you in advance what kind of answers to expect.

Q6

For the equation below, find the values of \(-b\), \(b^2 - 4ac\), and \(2a\). Then solve.

\[x^2 - 7x + 10 = 0\]
Answer

\(-b = 7, \; b^2 - 4ac = 9, \; 2a = 2; \; x = 5 \text{ or } x = 2\)

  1. Identify the coefficients.

    \[a = 1, \quad b = -7, \quad c = 10\]

  2. The opposite of \(b = -7\) is 7, and \(2a = 2(1) = 2\).

    \[-b = -\left(-7\right) = 7, \qquad 2a = 2\left(1\right) = 2\]

  3. Compute the discriminant.

    \[\Delta = b^2 - 4ac = \left(-7\right)^2 - 4\left(1\right)\left(10\right) = 49 - 40 = 9\]

  4. Put the three parts into the formula and calculate both answers.

    \[x = \frac{7 \pm \sqrt{9}}{2} = \frac{7 \pm 3}{2} \;\Rightarrow\; x = \frac{10}{2} = 5 \quad\text{or}\quad x = \frac{4}{2} = 2\]

  5. Check \(x = 5\) in \(x^2 - 7x + 10 = 0\).

    \[5^2 - 75 + 10 = 25 - 35 + 10 = 0\]

  6. Check \(x = 2\) in \(x^2 - 7x + 10 = 0\).

    \[2^2 - 72 + 10 = 4 - 14 + 10 = 0\]

Solve using the quadratic formula. Check your answers.

\[x^2 + 3x - 10 = 0\]
Answer

\(x = 2 \text{ or } x = -5\)

  1. Identify the coefficients from standard form.

    \[a = 1, \quad b = 3, \quad c = -10\]

  2. Calculate the discriminant. \(49\) is positive and a perfect square, so there are two rational solutions.

    \[\Delta = b^2 - 4ac = 3^2 - 4\left(1\right)\left(-10\right) = 9 + 40 = 49\]

  3. Substitute into the quadratic formula, using parentheses for every value.

    \[x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} = \frac{-\left(3\right) \pm \sqrt{49}}{2\left(1\right)} = \frac{-3 \pm \sqrt{49}}{2}\]

  4. Simplify the square root: \(\sqrt{49} = 7\).

    \[x = \frac{-3 \pm 7}{2}\]

  5. Calculate both answers: once with \(+\) and once with \(-\).

    \[x = \frac{-3 + 7}{2} = 2 \quad\text{or}\quad x = \frac{-3 - 7}{2} = -5\]

  6. Check \(x = 2\) in \(x^2 + 3x - 10 = 0\).

    \[2^2 + 32 - 10 = 4 + 6 - 10 = 0\]

  7. Check \(x = -5\) in \(x^2 + 3x - 10 = 0\).

    \[\left(-5\right)^2 + 3\left(-5\right) - 10 = 25 - 15 - 10 = 0\]

Substituting into the formula

Most mistakes with the quadratic formula are sign mistakes, and parentheses prevent them. Replace each letter with its value inside parentheses, exactly as written, before doing any arithmetic: \(-b\) becomes \(-\left(-3\right)\), \(b^2\) becomes \(\left(-3\right)^2\), and \(4ac\) becomes \(4\left(2\right)\left(-2\right)\).

Then simplify one piece at a time. The opposite of a negative is positive: \(-\left(-3\right) = 3\). Squaring a negative number gives a positive result: \(\left(-3\right)^2 = 9\), while \(-3^2 = -9\) because the exponent applies only to the 3. When \(a\) and \(c\) have opposite signs, \(-4ac\) is positive, so the discriminant gets bigger. When \(a\) is negative, the denominator \(2a\) is negative too, so divide carefully or multiply the whole equation by \(-1\) first.

Two rational solutions

When the discriminant is a positive perfect square such as 1, 4, 9, 25, 49 or 121, its square root is a whole number. The formula then produces two rational solutions: integers or fractions with no radical left over. Calculate the \(+\) case and the \(-\) case separately, and reduce each fraction to lowest terms.

A perfect-square discriminant is also a signal that the quadratic could have been factored. For \(2x^2 - 7x + 3 = 0\), the solutions 3 and \(\frac{1}{2}\) correspond to the factors \((x - 3)(2x - 1)\). The formula reaches the same answers without having to search for the factor pair. Always check by substituting each solution into the original equation; the left side must equal 0.

Two irrational solutions

When the discriminant is positive but not a perfect square, its square root is irrational. The two solutions are still real numbers, but they cannot be written as fractions, so exact answers keep a radical. These are exactly the quadratics that cannot be solved by factoring with integers, which is where the formula is most useful.

Finish in three moves. First simplify the radical by pulling out the largest perfect-square factor: \(\sqrt{12} = \sqrt{4 \cdot 3} = 2\sqrt{3}\). Next, if every term in the numerator and the denominator shares a common factor, divide each term by it: \(\frac{4 \pm 2\sqrt{3}}{2} = 2 \pm \sqrt{3}\). You may not cancel only part of the numerator. Finally, give decimal approximations only when a problem asks for them, and round at the very end.

Understanding the discriminant

The discriminant, written \(\Delta = b^2 - 4ac\), is the part of the formula under the square root. Its sign predicts how many real solutions an equation has before you solve it. If \(\Delta > 0\), the formula adds and subtracts a positive square root, giving two different real solutions. If \(\Delta = 0\), you add and subtract zero, so both cases give the same number \(x = \frac{-b}{2a}\), called a repeated or double root. If \(\Delta < 0\), the formula asks for the square root of a negative number, which is not a real number, so there are no real solutions.

For two real solutions you can say a little more: a perfect-square discriminant gives rational solutions, while any other positive value gives irrational ones. The discriminant also works in reverse. If a problem asks for which values of an unknown coefficient an equation has one solution, set \(\Delta = 0\) and solve; for two solutions, require \(\Delta > 0\); for none, require \(\Delta < 0\).

Solutions and graphs: the discriminant and x-intercepts

An \(x\)-intercept of the parabola \(y = ax^2 + bx + c\) is a point where \(y = 0\). Setting \(y = 0\) gives the equation \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\), so the real solutions of the equation are exactly the \(x\)-coordinates of the \(x\)-intercepts. Every intercept is written as a point \((x, 0)\).

That makes the discriminant a graph predictor. If \(\Delta > 0\), the parabola crosses the \(x\)-axis at two points. If \(\Delta = 0\), it just touches the axis at its vertex, \(x = \frac{-b}{2a}\). If \(\Delta < 0\), the whole parabola stays above the axis (when \(a > 0\)) or below it (when \(a < 0\)) and never meets it. To find the intercepts, solve with the formula; when they are irrational, use decimal approximations to plot them.

Word problems add two steps before and after the formula. Before: define a variable for one unknown, write the other quantities in terms of it, build an equation from the facts, and rearrange it into standard form.   8. Check \(x = 1\) in \(x^2 + 9x - 10 = 0\).

    \[1^2 + 91 - 10 = 1 + 9 - 10 = 0\]

  9. Check \(x = -10\) in \(x^2 + 9x - 10 = 0\).

    \[\left(-10\right)^2 + 9\left(-10\right) - 10 = 100 - 90 - 10 = 0\]

  10. Reject \(x = -10\). The frame is 1 in wide. Check: \(10 \times 12 = 120\).

W7

A theater sells \(200 - 10p\) tickets when the price is \(p\) dollars. What ticket price brings in $750 in revenue?

Answer

\(p = \$5 \text{ or } p = \$15\)

  1. Revenue is price times tickets sold.

    \[p(200 - 10p) = 750 \;\Rightarrow\; -10p^2 + 200p - 750 = 0\]

  2. Divide every term by \(-10\).

    \[p^2 - 20p + 75 = 0\]

  3. Identify the coefficients from standard form.

    \[a = 1, \quad b = -20, \quad c = 75\]

  4. Calculate the discriminant. \(100\) is positive and a perfect square, so there are two rational solutions.

    \[\Delta = b^2 - 4ac = \left(-20\right)^2 - 4\left(1\right)\left(75\right) = 400 - 300 = 100\]

  5. Substitute into the quadratic formula, using parentheses for every value.

    \[p = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} = \frac{-\left(-20\right) \pm \sqrt{100}}{2\left(1\right)} = \frac{20 \pm \sqrt{100}}{2}\]

  6. Simplify the square root: \(\sqrt{100} = 10\).

    \[p = \frac{20 \pm 10}{2}\]

  7. Calculate both answers: once with \(+\) and once with \(-\).

    \[p = \frac{20 + 10}{2} = 15 \quad\text{or}\quad p = \frac{20 - 10}{2} = 5\]

  8. Check \(p = 15\) in \(p^2 - 20p + 75 = 0\).

    \[15^2 - 2015 + 75 = 225 - 300 + 75 = 0\]

  9. Check \(p = 5\) in \(p^2 - 20p + 75 = 0\).

    \[5^2 - 205 + 75 = 25 - 100 + 75 = 0\]

  10. Both prices are positive and make sense. Check: \(5 \times 150 = 750\) and \(15 \times 50 = 750\).

W8

A ball is kicked from 6 ft above the ground. Its height after \(t\) seconds is \(h = -16t^2 + 40t + 6\). When does it land? Give the exact time and round to the nearest hundredth.

Answer

\(t = \frac{5 + \sqrt{31}}{4} \approx 2.64 \text{ seconds}\)

  1. Set \(h = 0\) and divide every term by \(-2\).

    \[-16t^2 + 40t + 6 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; 8t^2 - 20t - 3 = 0\]

  2. Identify the coefficients from standard form.

    \[a = 8, \quad b = -20, \quad c = -3\]

  3. Calculate the discriminant. \(496\) is positive but not a perfect square, so there are two irrational solutions.

    \[\Delta = b^2 - 4ac = \left(-20\right)^2 - 4\left(8\right)\left(-3\right) = 400 + 96 = 496\]

  4. Substitute into the quadratic formula, using parentheses for every value.

    \[t = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} = \frac{-\left(-20\right) \pm \sqrt{496}}{2\left(8\right)} = \frac{20 \pm \sqrt{496}}{16}\]

  5. Simplify the radical: \(\sqrt{496} = \sqrt{16 \cdot 31} = 4\sqrt{31}\).

    \[t = \frac{20 \pm 4\sqrt{31}}{16}\]

  6. Every term in the numerator and the denominator shares a factor of 4. Divide each by 4.

    \[t = \frac{5 \pm \sqrt{31}}{4}\]

  7. Write both exact solutions, then approximate only if asked.

    \[t = \frac{5 + \sqrt{31}}{4} \approx 2.642 \quad\text{or}\quad t = \frac{5 - \sqrt{31}}{4} \approx -0.142\]

  8. Check: the two solutions should add to \(-\frac{b}{a}\) and multiply to \(\frac{c}{a}\). The radicals cancel when you add, and the product is a difference of squares.

    \[\text{sum} = \frac{5}{2} = -\frac{\left(-20\right)}{8}, \qquad \text{product} = - \frac{3}{8} = \frac{-3}{8}\]

  9. \(\frac{5 - \sqrt{31}}{4} \approx -0.14\) is a negative time, so reject it. The ball lands at \(t = \frac{5 + \sqrt{31}}{4} \approx 2.64\) s.

W9

A ball's height after \(t\) seconds is \(h = -16t^2 + 48t + 4\). Can it ever reach 50 feet? Use the discriminant.

Answer

\(\text{No: } \Delta = -160 < 0; \text{ the maximum height is } 40 \text{ ft}\)

  1. Set the height equal to 50 and rearrange.

    \[-16t^2 + 48t + 4 = 50 \;\Rightarrow\; -16t^2 + 48t - 46 = 0\]

  2. Divide every term by \(-2\).

    \[8t^2 - 24t + 23 = 0\]

  3. Identify the coefficients from standard form.

    \[a = 8, \quad b = -24, \quad c = 23\]

  4. Calculate the discriminant. \(-160\) is negative, so there are no real solutions.

    \[\Delta = b^2 - 4ac = \left(-24\right)^2 - 4\left(8\right)\left(23\right) = 576 - 736 = -160\]

  5. The formula would need \(\sqrt{-160}\), and no real number squared is negative.

    \[t = \frac{24 \pm \sqrt{-160}}{16} \quad \text{(not real)}\]

  6. A negative discriminant means no time \(t\) gives a height of 50 ft. The highest point is at \(t = \frac{-48}{2(-16)} = 1.5\) s, where \(h = -36 + 72 + 4 = 40\) ft.

W10

The diagonal of a rectangle is 13 m. The length is 7 m more than the width. Find the dimensions.

Answer

\(5 \text{ m by } 12 \text{ m}\)

  1. Let \(w\) be the width; the length is \(w + 7\). The diagonal is the hypotenuse.

    \[w^2 + (w + 7)^2 = 169 \;\Rightarrow\; 2w^2 + 14w + 49 = 169\]

  2. Subtract 169 and divide every term by 2.

    \[2w^2 + 14w - 120 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; w^2 + 7w - 60 = 0\]

  3. Identify the coefficients from standard form.

    \[a = 1, \quad b = 7, \quad c = -60\]

  4. Calculate the discriminant. \(289\) is positive and a perfect square, so there are two rational solutions.

    \[\Delta = b^2 - 4ac = 7^2 - 4\left(1\right)\left(-60\right) = 49 + 240 = 289\]

  5. Substitute into the quadratic formula, using parentheses for every value.

    \[w = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} = \frac{-\left(7\right) \pm \sqrt{289}}{2\left(1\right)} = \frac{-7 \pm \sqrt{289}}{2}\]

  6. Simplify the square root: \(\sqrt{289} = 17\).

    \[w = \frac{-7 \pm 17}{2}\]

  7. Calculate both answers: once with \(+\) and once with \(-\).

    \[w = \frac{-7 + 17}{2} = 5 \quad\text{or}\quad w = \frac{-7 - 17}{2} = -12\]

  8. Check \(w = 5\) in \(w^2 + 7w - 60 = 0\).

    \[5^2 + 75 - 60 = 25 + 35 - 60 = 0\]

  9. Check \(w = -12\) in \(w^2 + 7w - 60 = 0\).

    \[\left(-12\right)^2 + 7\left(-12\right) - 60 = 144 - 84 - 60 = 0\]

  10. Reject \(w = -12\). The rectangle is 5 m by 12 m. Check: \(5^2 + 12^2 = 169 = 13^2\).

W11

A 12 m by 16 m garden is surrounded by a path of uniform width. The garden and path together cover 320 m². How wide is the path?

Answer

\(x = 2 \text{ m}\)

  1. Let \(x\) be the path width. The outer dimensions are \(12 + 2x\) and \(16 + 2x\).

    \[(12 + 2x)(16 + 2x) = 320 \;\Rightarrow\; 4x^2 + 56x + 192 = 320\]

  2. Subtract 320 and divide every term by 4.

    \[4x^2 + 56x - 128 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; x^2 + 14x - 32 = 0\]

  3. Identify the coefficients from standard form.

    \[a = 1, \quad b = 14, \quad c = -32\]

  4. Calculate the discriminant. \(324\) is positive and a perfect square, so there are two rational solutions.

    \[\Delta = b^2 - 4ac = 14^2 - 4\left(1\right)\left(-32\right) = 196 + 128 = 324\]

  5. Substitute into the quadratic formula, using parentheses for every value.

    \[x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} = \frac{-\left(14\right) \pm \sqrt{324}}{2\left(1\right)} = \frac{-14 \pm \sqrt{324}}{2}\]

  6. Simplify the square root: \(\sqrt{324} = 18\).

    \[x = \frac{-14 \pm 18}{2}\]

  7. Calculate both answers: once with \(+\) and once with \(-\).

    \[x = \frac{-14 + 18}{2} = 2 \quad\text{or}\quad x = \frac{-14 - 18}{2} = -16\]

  8. Check \(x = 2\) in \(x^2 + 14x - 32 = 0\).

    \[2^2 + 142 - 32 = 4 + 28 - 32 = 0\]

  9. Check \(x = -16\) in \(x^2 + 14x - 32 = 0\).

    \[\left(-16\right)^2 + 14\left(-16\right) - 32 = 256 - 224 - 32 = 0\]

  10. Reject \(x = -16\). The path is 2 m wide. Check: \(16 \times 20 = 320\).

W12

Challenge: Two numbers have a sum of 10 and a product of 20. Find them exactly. Then use the discriminant to find the largest possible product of two real numbers whose sum is 10.

Answer

\(5 + \sqrt{5} \text{ and } 5 - \sqrt{5}; \text{ largest product } 25\)

  1. Let one number be \(x\); the other is \(10 - x\).

    \[x(10 - x) = 20 \;\Rightarrow\; x^2 - 10x + 20 = 0\]

  2. Identify the coefficients from standard form.

    \[a = 1, \quad b = -10, \quad c = 20\]

  3. Calculate the discriminant. \(20\) is positive but not a perfect square, so there are two irrational solutions.

    \[\Delta = b^2 - 4ac = \left(-10\right)^2 - 4\left(1\right)\left(20\right) = 100 - 80 = 20\]

  4. Substitute into the quadratic formula, using parentheses for every value.

    \[x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} = \frac{-\left(-10\right) \pm \sqrt{20}}{2\left(1\right)} = \frac{10 \pm \sqrt{20}}{2}\]

  5. Simplify the radical: \(\sqrt{20} = \sqrt{4 \cdot 5} = 2\sqrt{5}\).

    \[x = \frac{10 \pm 2\sqrt{5}}{2}\]

  6. Every term in the numerator and the denominator shares a factor of 2. Divide each by 2.

    \[x = 5 \pm \sqrt{5}\]

  7. Write both exact solutions, then approximate only if asked.

    \[x = 5 + \sqrt{5} \approx 7.236 \quad\text{or}\quad x = 5 - \sqrt{5} \approx 2.764\]

  8. Check: the two solutions should add to \(-\frac{b}{a}\) and multiply to \(\frac{c}{a}\). The radicals cancel when you add, and the product is a difference of squares.

    \[\text{sum} = 10 = -\frac{\left(-10\right)}{1}, \qquad \text{product} = 20 = \frac{20}{1}\]

  9. The two numbers are \(5 + \sqrt{5}\) and \(5 - \sqrt{5}\): together they add to 10, and their product is \(25 - 5 = 20\).

  10. For a general product \(P\), the equation is \(x^2 - 10x + P = 0\). Real numbers exist only when \(\Delta \ge 0\).

    \[\Delta = 100 - 4P \ge 0 \;\Rightarrow\; P \le 25\]

  11. The largest product is 25, reached when \(\Delta = 0\): both numbers equal 5.

Quick questions and answers

When should I use the quadratic formula instead of factoring?

Factoring is fastest when you can spot an integer factor pair. The quadratic formula works on every quadratic equation, including ones with irrational solutions or no real solutions, so it is the reliable choice when factoring is not obvious.

Why must the equation equal zero first?

The formula is derived from \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\). If terms are on both sides, the values you read as \(a\), \(b\) and \(c\) are wrong, so move everything to one side before identifying coefficients.

What is the discriminant?

It is \(\Delta = b^2 - 4ac\), the expression under the square root. Positive means two real solutions, zero means one repeated solution, and negative means no real solutions.

How do I know if the answers will be rational or irrational?

If the discriminant is a positive perfect square, the solutions are rational. If it is positive but not a perfect square, the solutions are irrational and the exact answers keep a radical.

Why do I need parentheses when I substitute?

Parentheses keep signs attached to their numbers. For \(b = -3\), \(\left(-3\right)^2 = 9\), but typing \(-3^2\) gives \(-9\), which changes the discriminant and the answers.

Can I cancel part of the numerator with the denominator?

Only if every term in the numerator shares the factor. In \(\frac{4 \pm 2\sqrt{3}}{2}\), both 4 and \(2\sqrt{3}\) are divisible by 2, giving \(2 \pm \sqrt{3}\). Dividing only the 4 is an error.

What do the solutions mean on a graph?

The real solutions of \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\) are the \(x\)-coordinates of the points where the parabola \(y = ax^2 + bx + c\) meets the \(x\)-axis.
