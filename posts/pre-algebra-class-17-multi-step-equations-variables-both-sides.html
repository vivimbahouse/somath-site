Understanding balance and equivalent equations

An equation says that its two sides have the same value. Equivalent equations have exactly the same solutions. To preserve that balance, add or subtract the same quantity on both sides, or multiply or divide both sides by the same nonzero constant.

The aim is to isolate the variable, not to “move” symbols without a reason. If you subtract \(x\) from the left, subtract \(x\) from the right too. Check the final value by replacing the variable in the original equation.

Practice: try these five questions

Q1

Solve for x. Show the operation applied to both sides.

\[2 x + 7 = x + 12\]
Answer

\(x = 5\)

  1. Subtract \(x\) on both sides to collect the variable terms on the left.

    \[x + 7 = 12\]

  2. Subtract \(7\) on both sides to isolate the variable term.

    \[x = 5\]

Check in the original equation

Substitute \(x = 5\) into each side of the original equation.

Left side

\[2 \left(5\right) + 7 = 17\]

Right side

\[ \left(5\right) + 12 = 17\]

Both sides equal \(17\), so the value checks.

Q4

\[6 x + 5 = 2 x + 29\]

  4. Divide both sides by \(4\). Keep the result exact.

    \[x = 6\]

Check in the original equation

Left side

Right side

\[ 2 \left(6\right) + 29 = 41\]

Both sides equal \(41\), so the value checks.

Q5

Solve. Explain why it is valid to collect the variables on either side.

\[2 x + 15 = 5 x - 6\]
Answer

\(x = 7\)

  1. Either side works if every operation is applied to both sides. This solution collects variables on the left; its negative coefficient is not an error.

  2. Subtract \(5 x\) on both sides to collect the variable terms on the left.

    \[15 - 3 x = -6\]

  3. Subtract \(15\) on both sides to isolate the variable term.

    \[- 3 x = -21\]

  4. Divide both sides by \(-3\). Keep the result exact.

    \[x = 7\]

Check in the original equation

Substitute \(x = 7\) into each side of the original equation.

Left side

\[2 \left(7\right) + 15 = 29\]

Right side

\[ 5 \left(7\right) - 6 = 29\]

Both sides equal \(29\), so the value checks.

Combining like terms on each side

Like terms contain the same variable to the same power. Add their coefficients: \(2x+5x=7x\). Constants combine with constants, but \(7x+4\) cannot become \(11x\).

Simplify each side separately before collecting variables across the equals sign. Keep the sign attached to each term: \(8x-3x\) means \(8x+(-3x)\), so it becomes \(5x\).

Collecting variables on one side

Add or subtract a variable term on both sides so that only one side contains the variable. Then undo the constant term and divide by the remaining coefficient.

It is often convenient to keep a positive variable coefficient, but a negative coefficient is equally valid. For example, from \(-4x=20\), dividing both sides by \(-4\) gives \(x=-5\).

Solving equations with parentheses

The distributive property multiplies every term in a group: \(a(b+c)=ab+ac\). After distributing on both sides, combine like terms before isolating the variable.

Do not forget constants outside parentheses. In \(2(x+3)+5\), distribution gives \(2x+6+5=2x+11\). You may sometimes divide out a common outside factor first instead.

Working with negative signs

A minus sign before parentheses is a factor of \(-1\). It changes the sign of every term: \(-(x-7)=-x+7\). A factor such as \(-3\) must also multiply every term.

Keep negative terms intact while combining. After simplifying, use the usual balance operations; dividing by a negative number is allowed in an equation. The equals sign remains an equals sign.

Solving equations with fractions and decimals

For constant denominators, multiply every term on both sides by the least common denominator (LCD). This clears the fractions while preserving equality. A fraction bar groups the entire numerator.

For decimals, use a power of 10 large enough to make every coefficient and constant a whole number. Use 10 for tenths and 100 for hundredths. Keep exact values throughout the calculation instead of rounding.

How many solutions?

After simplifying, a linear equation can have one solution, no solution, or infinitely many solutions. A nonzero variable coefficient leads to one value after division.

If all variable terms cancel, inspect the remaining statement. A false statement such as \(4=9\) means no solution. A true statement such as \(4=4\) means every real value works. Neither outcome means \(x=0\).

\[50 + 6 \left(\left(6\right) - 2\right) = 74\]

Right side

\[ 20 + 9 \left(6\right) = 74\]

Both sides equal \(74\), so the value checks.

In context: At 6 hours, both packages cost $74. Six is a whole number at least 2.

W10

A school orders three boxes containing x + 2 notebooks each and two packs containing x notebooks each. Another order has four boxes containing x + 5 notebooks each plus one loose notebook. The totals match. Find x and the total notebooks in each order.

Answer

\(x = 15\)

  1. Let x be the number in each packing rule. Add all contributions in each order before setting the totals equal.

    \[3 \left(x + 2\right) + 2 x = 4 \left(x + 5\right) + 1\]

  2. Distribute any grouped factors to every term, then combine like terms on each side separately.

    \[5 x + 6 = 4 x + 21\]

  3. Subtract \(4 x\) on both sides to collect the variable terms on the left.

    \[x + 6 = 21\]

  4. Subtract \(6\) on both sides to isolate the variable term.

    \[x = 15\]

Check in the original equation

Substitute \(x = 15\) into each side of the original equation.

Left side

\[3 \left(\left(15\right) + 2\right) + 2 \left(15\right) = 81\]

Right side

\[ 4 \left(\left(15\right) + 5\right) + 1 = 81\]

Both sides equal \(81\), so the value checks.

In context: x = 15. The first order contains 3 × 17 + 2 × 15 = 81 notebooks; the second contains 4 × 20 + 1 = 81 notebooks.

W11

Print Shop A charges an $18 setup fee plus $2.40 per poster, then applies a $6 discount to the entire bill. Shop B charges $8 plus $2.80 per poster. Find the number of posters at which the final bills match and the cost.

Answer

\(x = 10\)

  1. Let x be the number of posters. Subtract the discount once, not once per poster.

    \[18 + 2.4 x - 6 = 8 + 2.8 x\]

  2. Multiply every term by 10 to clear the decimals.

    \[180 + 24 x - 60 = 80 + 28 x\]

  3. Distribute any grouped factors to every term, then combine like terms on each side separately.

    \[24 x + 120 = 28 x + 80\]

  4. Subtract \(28 x\) on both sides to collect the variable terms on the left.

    \[120 - 4 x = 80\]

  5. Subtract \(120\) on both sides to isolate the variable term.

    \[- 4 x = -40\]

  6. Divide both sides by \(-4\). Keep the result exact.

    \[x = 10\]

Check in the original equation

Substitute \(x = 10\) into each side of the original equation.

Left side

\[18 + 2.4 \left(10\right) - 6 = 36\]

Right side

\[ 8 + 2.8 \left(10\right) = 36\]

Both sides equal \(36\), so the value checks.

In context: At 10 posters, both final bills are $36. The discount has been applied exactly once.

W12

A workshop offers Plan A: $60 covers two participants, each additional participant costs $7, and a $4 coupon reduces the whole bill. Plan B charges a $24 booking fee plus $9 for every participant. For a whole-number group of at least two people, find the group size at which costs match. Then determine which plan is cheaper for 15 participants and by how much.

Answer

\(x = 9\)

  1. Let x be the total participants, with x at least 2. Plan A charges for x − 2 additional participants and subtracts one $4 coupon.

    \[60 + 7 \left(x - 2\right) - 4 = 24 + 9 x\]

  2. Distribute any grouped factors to every term, then combine like terms on each side separately.

    \[7 x + 42 = 9 x + 24\]

  3. Subtract \(9 x\) on both sides to collect the variable terms on the left.

    \[42 - 2 x = 24\]

  4. Subtract \(42\) on both sides to isolate the variable term.

    \[- 2 x = -18\]

  5. Divide both sides by \(-2\). Keep the result exact.

    \[x = 9\]

Check in the original equation

Substitute \(x = 9\) into each side of the original equation.

Left side

\[60 + 7 \left(\left(9\right) - 2\right) - 4 = 105\]

Right side

\[ 24 + 9 \left(9\right) = 105\]

Both sides equal \(105\), so the value checks.

In context: For 9 participants, both plans cost $105. For 15 participants, Plan A costs 60 + 7 × 13 − 4 = $147 and Plan B costs 24 + 9 × 15 = $159. Plan A is cheaper by $12.

Quick questions and answers

What does it mean to keep an equation balanced?

Apply the same addition, subtraction, or multiplication or division by a nonzero constant to both sides. This preserves the solution set.

Must the variable end up on the left?

No. You can collect variables on either side. Keeping a positive coefficient can make arithmetic easier, but a negative coefficient is also valid.

How do I distribute a negative sign?

Treat the sign as part of the factor. Multiply that factor by every term in the group, including the sign of each term. A negative times a negative gives a positive.

How do I clear fractions or decimals?

Multiply every term on both sides by a suitable nonzero number: the least common denominator for constant fractions, or an appropriate power of 10 for decimals.

What if all variable terms cancel?

A remaining true statement means infinitely many solutions; a false statement means no solution. Neither case means that the variable equals zero.
