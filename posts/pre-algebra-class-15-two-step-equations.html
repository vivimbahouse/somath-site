Understanding two-step equations

A solution is a value that makes both sides of an equation equal. In this lesson, one side contains a variable affected by two operations, such as multiplication followed by addition. Isolating the variable means finding its value by undoing those operations.

Read the variable expression in its usual order of operations. In \(\displaystyle ax+b=c\), multiply x by a, then add b. To solve, work backward: undo the addition first, then undo the multiplication. For \(\displaystyle \frac{x}{a}+b=c\), the first operation applied to x is division by a. The multiplier or divisor must be nonzero.

Try it yourself: five questions

Q1 Practice 1 of 5

Name the two operations applied to x, then solve \(\displaystyle 2x + 3 = 17\).

Answer

\(\displaystyle x = 7\).

  1. The operations applied to x are: multiply by \(\displaystyle 2\), then add 3. Undo them in reverse order.
  2. Subtract \(\displaystyle 3\) from both sides: \(\displaystyle 2x = 14\).
  3. Divide both sides by \(\displaystyle 2\): \(\displaystyle x = 7\).
  4. Check in the original equation: \(\displaystyle 2\cdot(7) + 3 = 14 + 3 = 17\). Both sides equal \(\displaystyle 17\).

Using inverse operations

An equation is a statement of equality, so keep it balanced by doing the same operation to both sides. Subtraction undoes addition, addition undoes subtraction, division undoes multiplication, and multiplication undoes division.

For the forms in this lesson, remove the added or subtracted constant first. Then undo the multiplication or division involving x. Write a new equality after each step rather than saying a number “moves across” the equals sign. That habit makes the reason for each sign change visible.

Equations with multiplication

The coefficient is the number multiplying the variable. After you remove the constant, divide both sides by that nonzero coefficient. Division acts on the whole side of the equation, not just one convenient term.

Read subtraction carefully: to undo “minus 11,” add 11 to both sides. When you obtain a value for x, substitute it into the original expression, including the constant, rather than checking only the final simplified line.

Equations with division

A fraction bar can mean division. If x is divided by a number, first remove the added or subtracted constant, then multiply both sides by the divisor. This restores the whole value of x.

Watch the fraction bar’s grouping. The expression \(\displaystyle \frac{x}{7}-4\) means divide x by 7, then subtract 4. It is not the same as \(\displaystyle \frac{x-4}{7}\), where the subtraction happens before division. The questions in this section have the constant outside the fraction.

Working with negative numbers

The negative sign is part of the coefficient. If the variable term is \(\displaystyle -4x\), divide by −4, not by 4. Same-sign division gives a positive quotient; different-sign division gives a negative quotient.

A negative answer can be completely valid. Decide by substituting it into the original equation. Use parentheses around a negative value when substituting so that, for example, a negative coefficient multiplied by a negative solution is clearly a positive product.

Working with fractions and decimals

Fractions and decimals follow the same balance rules as whole numbers. Remove the constant, then undo the coefficient. To undo multiplication by a nonzero fraction, multiply by its reciprocal, or equivalently divide by that fraction.

Keep values exact. Align decimal places when adding or subtracting, and use a common denominator when subtracting fractions. You do not need to change every fraction into a decimal. Before checking your answer, return to the original equation so that no constant is left out.

Variable on the right

Equality works in both directions: if \(\displaystyle a=b\), then \(\displaystyle b=a\). You can solve with the variable on the right or rewrite the equation with the two complete sides exchanged. Swapping the sides does not change any signs.

Apply the same inverse operation to both sides and keep their order consistent. If the final line says \(\displaystyle 7=x\), you may write \(\displaystyle x=7\). Do not turn a positive coefficient into a negative one merely because it is on the right.

Solving word problems

Define x as the quantity you need to find, including its unit. Translate each part of the story into an expression: a fixed fee is a constant, a cost per item is a coefficient, and equal sharing can be represented by division.

Write an equation for the stated total, solve it, and interpret the value in context. A count of tickets or people should be a nonnegative whole number. A length, time, or price may be fractional. Check both the calculation and whether the result answers the actual question.

Quick questions and answers

How do you solve a two-step equation?

For the forms in this lesson, undo the added or subtracted constant first, then undo the multiplication or division involving the variable. Do the same operation to both sides and check by substitution.

Why do you undo the operations in reverse order?

You are working backward from the final value to the starting variable. If the expression multiplies x and then adds a constant, subtract the constant before dividing by the coefficient.

What if the coefficient is negative?

Keep the sign with the coefficient. After removing the constant, divide both sides by the signed coefficient. A negative solution is valid if it makes the original equality true.

Can the variable be on the right?

Yes. Equality works in both directions. You may leave the variable on the right while solving or exchange the two complete sides without changing signs.

Do fractions and decimals need different rules?

No. Use the same inverse operations and keep the arithmetic exact. A nonzero fractional coefficient can be undone by multiplying both sides by its reciprocal.
