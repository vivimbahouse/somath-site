Introduction to decimals

A decimal point separates whole units from parts of a unit. The first place to its right is tenths; the second is hundredths. A zero can hold a place so another digit has the correct value.

Try it yourself: five questions

Q1 Practice 1 of 5 · Start

Which number is written with a decimal point: 8, 24, 6.32, or 150?

Answer

6.32.

  1. The point in 6.32 separates 6 whole units from 32 hundredths. Whole numbers can also be written in decimal notation, for example 8.0.

Q2 Practice 2 of 5 · Build

In 0.8, which place does the 8 occupy, and what fraction does it represent?

Answer

The tenths place; 8/10.

  1. The first digit after the point counts tenths. Eight tenths is 8 out of 10 equal parts.

Q3 Practice 3 of 5 · Apply

In 4.06, which digit is in the hundredths place? What is its value?

Answer

6; its value is 0.06.

  1. The 0 holds the tenths place. The 6 is two places to the right of the point, so it represents 6/100.

Q4 Practice 4 of 5 · Explain

Separate 8.24 into its whole-number part and fractional part. Write an addition sentence.

Answer

8 + 0.24 = 8.24.

  1. The whole-number part is 8. The remaining part is 24 hundredths, written 0.24.

Q5 Practice 5 of 5 · Challenge

A student writes 6.4 for six whole units and four hundredths. Correct the number and explain the role of zero.

Answer

6.04.

  1. Four hundredths must be two places after the point. A zero in the tenths place keeps the 4 in the hundredths place; 6.4 would mean six and four tenths.

Tenths: ten equal parts

One tenth is one of ten equal parts of the same whole. Count tenths with a denominator of 10, and write the count in the tenths place. Ten tenths make one whole.

Try it yourself: five questions

Q6 Practice 1 of 5 · Start

Write 6/10 as a decimal.

Answer

0.6.

  1. Six tenths places the digit 6 immediately to the right of the decimal point.

Q7 Practice 2 of 5 · Build

Write 0.8 as a fraction with denominator 10.

Answer

8/10.

  1. The 8 is in the tenths place, so it counts eight equal parts out of ten.

Q8 Practice 3 of 5 · Apply

A strip has 10 equal sections. Five are colored. What decimal of the strip is uncolored?

Answer

0.5.

  1. There are 10 − 5 = 5 uncolored sections. Five out of ten is 5/10 = 0.5.

Q9 Practice 4 of 5 · Explain

A container holds 1.0 liter when full. It contains 0.6 liter. How many tenths of a liter are needed to fill it?

Answer

4 tenths of a liter, or 0.4 liter.

  1. A full liter is 10 tenths. Ten tenths minus six tenths leaves four tenths.

Q10 Practice 5 of 5 · Challenge

A path is divided into 10 equal sections. A child walks 2 sections, then 4 more. What decimal of the path remains, and what decimal was walked?

Answer

0.4 remains; 0.6 was walked.

  1. The child walks 2 + 4 = 6 of the 10 sections, or 0.6. Four sections remain, so the remaining part is 0.4.

Hundredths: one hundred equal parts

One hundredth is one of 100 equal parts of a whole. Ten hundredths equal one tenth. Use two places after the decimal point to show hundredths, including a zero in the tenths place when needed.

Try it yourself: five questions

Q11 Practice 1 of 5 · Start

Write 46/100 as a decimal.

Answer

0.46.

  1. Forty-six hundredths uses two decimal places: four tenths and six hundredths.

Q12 Practice 2 of 5 · Build

Write 0.72 as a fraction with denominator 100.

Answer

72/100.

  1. The last digit is in the hundredths place, so 0.72 means seventy-two hundredths.

Q13 Practice 3 of 5 · Apply

Write 9/100 as a decimal. Explain why 0.9 would be different.

Answer

0.09.

  1. Nine hundredths needs a zero in the tenths place. By contrast, 0.9 = 90/100, which is ninety hundredths.

Q14 Practice 4 of 5 · Explain

A hundred-square grid has 52 shaded squares. Express the shaded amount in hundredths, as a decimal, and as tenths plus hundredths.

Answer

52/100 = 0.52 = 5 tenths + 2 hundredths.

  1. Fifty shaded squares make five tenths. The two remaining shaded squares are two hundredths.

Q15 Practice 5 of 5 · Challenge

A hundred-square grid has 64 shaded squares. How many more squares must be shaded to show 0.80? What decimal of the grid is added?

Answer

16 squares; 0.16 of the grid.

  1. The target 0.80 is 80 hundredths. Subtract 64 from 80 to get 16, so shade 16 more squares.

Decimal place value

Name a digit's place before finding its value. In expanded form, add the value of each digit. Adding a zero at the end of a decimal does not change its value, but moving digits between places does.

Try it yourself: five questions

Q16 Practice 1 of 5 · Start

What is the value of the 6 in 2.64?

Answer

0.6, or six tenths.

  1. The 6 is the first digit after the point, so it counts tenths.

Q17 Practice 2 of 5 · Build

Write 0.92 in expanded form.

Answer

0.9 + 0.02.

  1. Nine tenths is 0.9 and two hundredths is 0.02. Together they make 0.92.

Q18 Practice 3 of 5 · Apply

Write 14.08 in expanded form, showing the tens, ones, and hundredths.

Answer

10 + 4 + 0.08.

  1. The 1 is worth 10, the 4 is worth 4, and the 8 is worth 0.08. There are zero tenths.

Q19 Practice 4 of 5 · Explain

Are 6.5 and 6.50 equal? Explain using hundredths.

Answer

Yes, 6.5 = 6.50.

  1. Five tenths is fifty hundredths. Both numbers mean six whole units and fifty hundredths.

Q20 Practice 5 of 5 · Challenge

Use the digits 2, 5, and 8 once each to make the greatest number with one digit before the decimal point and two after it. Write its expanded form.

Answer

8.52 = 8 + 0.5 + 0.02.

  1. Use the largest digit, 8, in the ones place. Of the two digits left, place 5 in tenths and 2 in hundredths.

Reading and writing decimals

Read the whole-number part, say 'and' for the decimal point, and name the decimal part using the last digit's place. Four and six hundredths is 4.06, not 4.6. Fractions with denominators 10 or 100 name the same parts.

Try it yourself: five questions

Q21 Practice 1 of 5 · Start

Write 0.4 in word form.

Answer

Four tenths.

  1. The 4 is in the tenths place, so name four equal parts out of ten.

Q22 Practice 2 of 5 · Build

Write 0.56 in word form.

Answer

Fifty-six hundredths.

  1. Read the digits 56 together, then use the name of the last place: hundredths.

Q23 Practice 3 of 5 · Apply

Write 'eight and two tenths' in standard decimal form.

Answer

8.2.

  1. Eight is the whole-number part. Put 2 in the tenths place.

Q24 Practice 4 of 5 · Explain

Write 'six and four hundredths' in standard decimal form and expanded form.

Answer

6.04 = 6 + 0.04.

  1. There are no tenths, so use a zero to hold that place. Four hundredths is 0.04.

Q25 Practice 5 of 5 · Challenge

Maya writes 9.7 for 'nine and seven hundredths.' Correct her number, write it as a whole number plus a fraction, and explain her mistake.

Answer

9.07 = 9 + 7/100.

  1. Her 9.7 means nine and seven tenths. The correction needs zero tenths so the 7 is in hundredths.

Comparing and ordering decimals

Compare the whole-number parts first, then tenths, then hundredths. You may add trailing zeros to line up the places: 0.4 = 0.40. The number with more written digits is not automatically greater.

Try it yourself: five questions

Q26 Practice 1 of 5 · Start

Fill the blank with <, >, or =: 0.8 __ 0.5.

Answer

0.8 > 0.5.

  1. Eight tenths is greater than five tenths.

Q27 Practice 2 of 5 · Build

Fill the blank with <, >, or =: 0.46 __ 0.5.

Answer

0.46 < 0.5.

  1. Write 0.5 as 0.50. Forty-six hundredths is less than fifty hundredths.

Q28 Practice 3 of 5 · Apply

Compare 2.09 and 2.04. Explain which place decides the comparison.

Answer

2.09 > 2.04; the hundredths place decides.

  1. Both numbers have two ones and zero tenths. Nine hundredths is greater than four hundredths.

Q29 Practice 4 of 5 · Explain

Order these decimals from least to greatest: 0.62, 0.7, 0.09, 0.65.

Answer

0.09 < 0.62 < 0.65 < 0.7.

  1. Write 0.7 as 0.70 and compare 9, 62, 65, and 70 hundredths.

Q30 Practice 5 of 5 · Challenge

Find every digit that makes 3.4□ greater than 3.46 but less than 3.50.

Answer

7, 8, and 9.

  1. The hundredths digit must be greater than 6. The numbers 3.47, 3.48, and 3.49 all remain below 3.50.

Decimals on a number line

Equal intervals on a number line represent equal changes in value. Ten equal intervals from 0 to 1 each have length 0.1; one hundred equal intervals each have length 0.01. Greater numbers lie farther right.

Try it yourself: five questions

Q31 Practice 1 of 5 · Start

A number line runs from 0 to 1 in jumps of 0.1. How many jumps from 0 reach 0.5?

Answer

5 jumps.

  1. Each jump adds one tenth. Five jumps add five tenths, or 0.5.

Q32 Practice 2 of 5 · Build

Which point lies farther right on a number line: 0.42 or 0.48?

Answer

0.48.

  1. Forty-eight hundredths is greater than forty-two hundredths, so it lies farther right.

Q33 Practice 3 of 5 · Apply

Between which two consecutive tenths does 0.63 lie? Which tenth is closer?

Answer

Between 0.6 and 0.7; closer to 0.6.

  1. It is 0.03 from 0.60 and 0.07 from 0.70. The smaller distance is to 0.6.

Q34 Practice 4 of 5 · Explain

Starting at 0.24, move three jumps of 0.1 to the right. Where do you land?

Answer

0.54.

  1. Three jumps add 0.30. The landings are 0.34, 0.44, and 0.54.

Q35 Practice 5 of 5 · Challenge

A point halfway between 0.52 and 0.60 is marked on a number line. What number is it? Explain in hundredths.

Answer

0.56.

  1. The endpoints are 8 hundredths apart. Half of 8 is 4, so move four hundredths from 0.52 to reach 0.56.

Decimals in money and measurement

Keep the units the same when comparing or combining decimal amounts. Line up decimal points so ones meet ones, tenths meet tenths, and hundredths meet hundredths. In dollars, a hundredth is one cent.

Try it yourself: five questions

Q36 Practice 1 of 5 · Start

Write 'four dollars and sixty-two cents' as a decimal amount of dollars.

Answer

$4.62.

  1. Four dollars is the whole-number part; 62 cents is 62 hundredths of a dollar.

Q37 Practice 2 of 5 · Build

A ribbon measures 2.56 meters. How many whole meters and how many additional hundredths of a meter is that?

Answer

2 whole meters and 56 hundredths of a meter.

  1. Separate the number as 2.56 = 2 + 0.56.

Q38 Practice 3 of 5 · Apply

In a pretend shop, a notebook costs $2.34 and an eraser costs $1.25. What is the total cost?

Answer

$3.59.

  1. Line up the decimal points and add: 2.34 + 1.25 = 3.59.

Q39 Practice 4 of 5 · Explain

A cord is 2.86 meters long. A student cuts off 1.24 meters. How much remains?

Answer

1.62 meters.

  1. Subtract with matching place values: 2.86 − 1.24 = 1.62. Keep meters as the unit.

Q40 Practice 5 of 5 · Challenge

A jug contains 1.50 liters. Two cups each use 0.35 liter. How much liquid remains?

Answer

0.80 liter.

  1. The cups use 0.35 + 0.35 = 0.70 liter. Subtract 1.50 − 0.70 = 0.80 liter.

Whole-number part: 7; the 2 is worth 0.2.

A strip has 10 equal pieces and 8 are colored. Write the uncolored part as a fraction and a decimal.

2/10 = 0.2.

Homework word problems

These twelve homework word problems increase in difficulty, from interpreting parts of a whole to solving multi-step decimal stories. Together with the eight review questions, they make 20 homework questions. Keep track of the units and use place value to explain your work.

W1 Homework word problem 1 of 12

A garden row is divided into 10 equal sections. Lina plants flowers in 3 sections. What fraction and decimal of the row has flowers?

Answer

3/10 = 0.3 of the row.

  1. Count the planted sections out of the ten equal sections. Three tenths is written 0.3.

W2 Homework word problem 2 of 12

A class mosaic has 100 equal tiles. Students color 58 tiles blue. What decimal of the mosaic is blue, and what decimal is not blue?

Answer

0.58 is blue; 0.42 is not blue.

  1. Fifty-eight tiles out of one hundred is 0.58. The other 42 tiles represent 0.42.

W3 Homework word problem 3 of 12

A toy boat travels 2.07 meters. Write the distance in words and explain the value of the 7.

Answer

Two and seven hundredths meters; the 7 is worth 0.07 meter.

  1. The zero holds the tenths place, so the 7 counts hundredths, not tenths.

W4 Homework word problem 4 of 12

Three seedlings measure 0.36 meter, 0.4 meter, and 0.29 meter. Order them from shortest to tallest and find the difference between the tallest and shortest.

Answer

0.29 m < 0.36 m < 0.4 m; the difference is 0.11 m.

  1. Write 0.4 as 0.40. The tallest minus the shortest is 0.40 − 0.29 = 0.11 meter.

W5 Homework word problem 5 of 12

A robot starts at the 0.18-meter mark on a track. It moves forward two jumps of 0.1 meter and then four jumps of 0.01 meter. At what mark does it stop?

Answer

0.42 meter.

  1. Two tenth-jumps add 0.20, reaching 0.38. Four hundredth-jumps add 0.04, reaching 0.42.

W6 Homework word problem 6 of 12

In a pretend shop, a snack costs $1.65 and a drink costs $0.85. You pay with $5.00. What is the total cost and how much change should you receive?

Answer

Total: $2.50; change: $2.50.

  1. Add 1.65 + 0.85 = 2.50. Then subtract 5.00 − 2.50 = 2.50.

W7 Homework word problem 7 of 12

A bottle contains 1.20 liters of water. A student pours out 0.35 liter, then adds 0.18 liter. How much water is in the bottle now?

Answer

1.03 liters.

  1. After pouring, 1.20 − 0.35 = 0.85 liter remains. Adding 0.18 gives 0.85 + 0.18 = 1.03 liters.

W8 Homework word problem 8 of 12

Two students complete the same course. Ava takes 14.26 seconds and Ben takes 14.6 seconds. Ava's time is 0.18 second faster than her previous attempt. Who wins, by how much, and what was Ava's previous time?

Answer

Ava wins by 0.34 second; her previous time was 14.44 seconds.

  1. Write Ben's time as 14.60 seconds. A smaller time is faster, and 14.60 − 14.26 = 0.34. Ava's previous, slower time was 14.26 + 0.18 = 14.44 seconds.

W9 Homework word problem 9 of 12

A craft team has 2.50 meters of ribbon. It uses 0.65 meter for one project and 0.80 meter for another. It needs 1.20 meters for a third project. How much ribbon is left, and how much more must the team get?

Answer

1.05 meters remain; the team needs 0.15 meter more.

  1. The first two projects use 0.65 + 0.80 = 1.45 meters. Subtract 2.50 − 1.45 = 1.05. The shortfall is 1.20 − 1.05 = 0.15 meter.

W10 Homework word problem 10 of 12

In a pretend shop, each craft kit needs a $1.35 notebook and a $0.65 pencil. A teacher buys supplies for three kits and pays $10.00. Find the cost of one kit, the total cost, and the change.

Answer

One kit: $2.00; total: $6.00; change: $4.00.

  1. One kit costs 1.35 + 0.65 = 2.00. Three kits cost 2.00 + 2.00 + 2.00 = 6.00. Change is 10.00 − 6.00 = 4.00.

W11 Homework word problem 11 of 12

Four students need a pretend museum ticket and a snack each. Plan A charges $2.45 per ticket and $1.20 per snack. Plan B charges $13.80 for all four tickets and snacks together. Which plan costs less, by how much, and what change is left from $15.00 with that plan?

Answer

Plan B costs $0.80 less and leaves $1.20 change.

  1. Plan A costs 2.45 + 1.20 = 3.65 per student. Four students cost 3.65 + 3.65 + 3.65 + 3.65 = 14.60. Plan B saves 14.60 − 13.80 = 0.80; change is 15.00 − 13.80 = 1.20.

W12 Homework word problem 12 of 12

A class joins three 1.25-meter ribbons end to end without overlap. It cuts four pieces of 0.65 meter and loses 0.05 meter in total to trimming. How much ribbon remains? Can it make two more 0.60-meter pieces, and if not, how much is it short?

Answer

1.10 meters remain. Two more pieces need 1.20 meters, so it is short by 0.10 meter.

  1. The joined length is 1.25 + 1.25 + 1.25 = 3.75 meters. Four pieces use 0.65 + 0.65 + 0.65 + 0.65 = 2.60 meters. After trimming, 3.75 − 2.60 − 0.05 = 1.10 meters remain. Two new pieces require 0.60 + 0.60 = 1.20 meters, leaving a shortfall of 1.20 − 1.10 = 0.10 meter.

Quick questions and answers

What are tenths and hundredths?

A tenth is one of ten equal parts of a whole, written 1/10 or 0.1. A hundredth is one of one hundred equal parts, written 1/100 or 0.01. Ten hundredths equal one tenth.

Why is 0.5 equal to 0.50?

Five tenths and fifty hundredths name the same amount. A trailing zero does not move the other digits to different places, so it does not change the number's value.

How do you compare decimals with different numbers of digits?

Line up the decimal points, add trailing zeros if helpful, and compare from left to right: whole-number parts, tenths, then hundredths. For example, 0.46 is less than 0.5 because 46 hundredths is less than 50 hundredths.
