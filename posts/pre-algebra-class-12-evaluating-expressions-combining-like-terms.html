Evaluating Expressions and Combining Like Terms

To evaluate an expression, substitute the given values and follow the order of operations. To combine like terms, add or subtract their coefficients while keeping the variables and exponents unchanged. The distributive property connects these skills when parentheses are involved.

Parts of an algebraic expression

An expression represents a value. In an expanded sum, a term includes its sign; a coefficient multiplies a variable part, and a constant has no variable. An unwritten coefficient is still present: a lone variable has coefficient one, and its negative has coefficient negative one.

Try it yourself: five questions, increasing difficulty

Q1 Practice 1 of 5 · Foundation

List the terms in \(7m+2\). Which term is constant?

Answer

Terms: \(7m\) and \(2\). Constant: \(2\).

  1. The plus sign separates the two terms. Only \(7m\) contains a variable.

Q2 Practice 2 of 5 · Build

In \(-4a+9\), identify the variable, its coefficient, and the constant.

Answer

Variable: \(a\). Coefficient: \(-4\). Constant: \(9\).

  1. The coefficient includes the minus sign because \(-4\) multiplies \(a\).

Q3 Practice 3 of 5 · Apply

List the three terms and the two variable coefficients in \(8p-3q+6\).

Answer

Terms: \(8p,-3q,6\). Coefficients: \(8,-3\).

  1. Keep the sign attached to each term. The number six is the constant, not a variable coefficient.

Q4 Practice 4 of 5 · Reason

Identify all variable coefficients in \(-x+5y-11\). How many terms are there?

Answer

Coefficients: \(-1,5\). There are three terms.

  1. Rewrite \(-x\) as \(-1x\). The terms are \(-x,5y,-11\).

Q5 Practice 5 of 5 · Challenge

For \(4a^2-2ab+b-9\), list all terms, their variable coefficients, and the distinct variables.

Answer

Terms: \(4a^2,-2ab,b,-9\). Variable coefficients: \(4,-2,1\). Variables: \(a,b\).

  1. The term \(-2ab\) is one term containing a product of two variables.
  2. The exponent two belongs to the variable part of \(4a^2\), not to its coefficient. The constant is \(-9\).

Evaluating expressions by substitution

To evaluate, replace every occurrence of each variable with its given value, then calculate. A coefficient next to a variable means multiplication, not a two-digit number. In a fraction, the entire numerator is grouped together.

Try it yourself: five questions, increasing difficulty

Q6 Practice 1 of 5 · Foundation

Evaluate \(6x-5\) when \(x=4\).

Answer

\(19\).

  1. \(6(4)-5=24-5=19\).

Q7 Practice 2 of 5 · Build

Evaluate \(2a+3b\) when \(a=5\) and \(b=2\).

Answer

\(16\).

  1. Substitute both values: \(2(5)+3(2)=10+6=16\).

Q8 Practice 3 of 5 · Apply

Evaluate \(\frac m4+2\) when \(m=12\).

Answer

\(5\).

  1. \(\frac{12}{4}+2=3+2=5\). Divide before adding.

Q9 Practice 4 of 5 · Reason

Evaluate \(3u^2-v\) when \(u=2\) and \(v=5\).

Answer

\(7\).

  1. Square first: \(3(2^2)-5=3(4)-5=12-5=7\).

Q10 Practice 5 of 5 · Challenge

Evaluate \(\frac{2p+q}{3}\) when \(p=4\) and \(q=7\).

Answer

\(5\).

  1. The numerator is \(2(4)+7=15\).
  2. Divide the whole numerator by three: \(15/3=5\), not \(8+7/3\).

Following the order of operations

Work inside grouping symbols, then evaluate exponents. Multiplication and division share priority and are performed left to right; addition and subtraction also share priority and proceed left to right. Parentheses can change the value of an expression, so do not remove them casually.

Try it yourself: five questions, increasing difficulty

Q11 Practice 1 of 5 · Foundation

Evaluate \(4+3x\) when \(x=5\).

Answer

\(19\).

  1. \(4+3(5)=4+15=19\). Multiply before adding.

Q12 Practice 2 of 5 · Build

Evaluate \(5(x-2)\) when \(x=6\).

Answer

\(20\).

  1. First calculate inside parentheses: \(5(6-2)=5(4)=20\).

Q13 Practice 3 of 5 · Apply

Evaluate \(3x^2-2\) when \(x=4\).

Answer

\(46\).

  1. The square comes before multiplication: \(3(16)-2=48-2=46\).

Q14 Practice 4 of 5 · Reason

Evaluate \(24\div x\times2+1\) when \(x=3\).

Answer

\(17\).

  1. Division and multiplication have equal priority. Work left to right: \(24\div3=8\), then \(8\times2=16\).
  2. Finally add one. Do not treat this as \(24\div(3\times2)+1\).

Q15 Practice 5 of 5 · Challenge

Evaluate \(2[3+(x-1)^2]-12\div3\) when \(x=4\).

Answer

\(20\).

  1. Inside the brackets, \(x-1=3\), so \(3+(x-1)^2=3+9=12\).
  2. Then \(2(12)-12\div3=24-4=20\).

Substituting negative numbers

Put a negative value inside parentheses whenever you substitute it. Squaring the entire negative number produces a positive result, while a minus sign outside a square is applied after the square. Subtracting a negative is the same as adding its opposite.

Try it yourself: five questions, increasing difficulty

Q16 Practice 1 of 5 · Foundation

Evaluate \(x+9\) when \(x=-5\).

Answer

\(4\).

  1. \((-5)+9=4\).

Q17 Practice 2 of 5 · Build

Evaluate \(-3x+2\) when \(x=-4\).

Answer

\(14\).

  1. \(-3(-4)+2=12+2=14\). Two negative factors give a positive product.

Q18 Practice 3 of 5 · Apply

Evaluate \(x^2+3x\) when \(x=-6\).

Answer

\(18\).

  1. \((-6)^2+3(-6)=36-18=18\).

Q19 Practice 4 of 5 · Reason

Evaluate \(-x^2+2x\) when \(x=-3\). Explain the first minus sign.

Answer

\(-15\).

  1. The first term is \(-[(-3)^2]=-9\), not \(9\).
  2. The second term is \(2(-3)=-6\), so the total is \(-9-6=-15\).

Q20 Practice 5 of 5 · Challenge

Evaluate \(2a^2-3ab+b\) when \(a=-2\) and \(b=3\).

Answer

\(29\).

  1. \(2(-2)^2=8\). Also, \(-3(-2)(3)=18\).
  2. Add all terms: \(8+18+3=29\).

Identifying like and unlike terms

Like terms have exactly the same variable part, including every exponent. Their coefficients can differ. All constants are like terms. Reordering factors does not change their product, but changing which variable is squared changes the term.

Try it yourself: five questions, increasing difficulty

Q21 Practice 1 of 5 · Foundation

Are \(3m\) and \(8m\) like terms? Explain.

Answer

Yes.

  1. Both have variable part \(m\), with exponent one. The coefficients do not need to match.

Q22 Practice 2 of 5 · Build

From the list t, 2t², 7t², and −3, select all terms that are like −5t². Explain your choices.

Answer

2t² and 7t².

  1. Like terms must have the same variable raised to the same power. The target term −5t² and both selected terms have variable part t².
  2. The coefficients −5, 2, and 7 may differ in value and sign; this does not prevent the terms from being like terms.
  3. The term t has exponent 1, not 2, and −3 is a constant. Neither is like −5t².

Q23 Practice 3 of 5 · Apply

Group the like terms in \(4a-7b+6+9a+2b-1\), without combining them yet.

Answer

\((4a+9a)+(-7b+2b)+(6-1)\).

  1. Make an \(a\)-group, a \(b\)-group, and a constant group. Preserve every sign.

Q24 Practice 4 of 5 · Reason

Are \(3ab\) and \(-8ba\) like terms? What about \(3ab\) and \(3a\)?

Answer

The first pair is like; the second pair is unlike.

  1. Multiplication can be reordered, so \(ba=ab\).
  2. The term \(3a\) lacks the factor \(b\), so its variable part differs.

Q25 Practice 5 of 5 · Challenge

Among \(2x^2y\), \(-6xy^2\), and \(4yx^2\), identify the like terms. Explain why all three cannot be combined.

Answer

\(2x^2y\) and \(4yx^2\) are like terms.

  1. Reorder \(yx^2\) as \(x^2y\).
  2. The term \(-6xy^2\) squares \(y\), not \(x\), so it has a different variable part.

Combining like terms

Add or subtract the coefficients of like terms and keep their variable part unchanged. Combining terms is addition, not multiplication, so exponents do not increase. Once all like groups have been combined, unlike groups remain separate.

Try it yourself: five questions, increasing difficulty

Q26 Practice 1 of 5 · Foundation

Simplify \(9x-4x\).

Answer

\(5x\).

  1. Subtract coefficients: \((9-4)x=5x\).

Q27 Practice 2 of 5 · Build

Simplify \(6a+7+2a-3\).

Answer

\(8a+4\).

  1. Combine \(6a+2a=8a\) and \(7-3=4\).

Q28 Practice 3 of 5 · Apply

Simplify \(5m-2n-8m+7n\).

Answer

\(-3m+5n\).

  1. The \(m\)-coefficient is \(5-8=-3\); the \(n\)-coefficient is \(-2+7=5\).

Q29 Practice 4 of 5 · Reason

Simplify \(4x^2+6x-x^2-9x+5\).

Answer

\(3x^2-3x+5\).

  1. Combine the square terms: \((4-1)x^2=3x^2\).
  2. Combine the first-power terms: \((6-9)x=-3x\). The constant stays five.

Q30 Practice 5 of 5 · Challenge

Simplify \(\frac12p+\frac34q-\frac32p+\frac14q+6-9\).

Answer

\(-p+q-3\).

  1. The \(p\)-coefficient is \(1/2-3/2=-1\). The \(q\)-coefficient is \(3/4+1/4=1\).
  2. The constants give \(6-9=-3\).

Using the distributive property

Multiply every term inside parentheses by the factor outside. An outside negative multiplies each sign as well as each number. After expanding, combine any like terms across the whole expression.

Try it yourself: five questions, increasing difficulty

Q31 Practice 1 of 5 · Foundation

Expand \(4(x+6)\).

Answer

\(4x+24\).

  1. Multiply both terms by four: \(4x+4(6)\).

Q32 Practice 2 of 5 · Build

Expand \(-2(3y+5)\).

Answer

\(-6y-10\).

  1. Multiply \(-2\) by \(3y\) and by \(5\). Both products are negative.

Q33 Practice 3 of 5 · Apply

Simplify \(-(2a-7)+3a\).

Answer

\(a+7\).

  1. The leading minus means multiply by \(-1\): \(-2a+7+3a\).
  2. Combine the \(a\)-terms to get \(a+7\).

Q34 Practice 4 of 5 · Reason

Simplify \(3(2x-5)-2(x+4)\).

Answer

\(4x-23\).

  1. Expand both products: \(6x-15-2x-8\).
  2. Combine to get \(4x-23\).

Q35 Practice 5 of 5 · Challenge

Simplify \(-\frac12(6p-8q+10)+2(p-q)\).

Answer

\(-p+2q-5\).

  1. The first product is \(-3p+4q-5\); the second is \(2p-2q\).
  2. Combine each variable group separately.

Simplifying before evaluating

Simplifying writes an equivalent expression that works for every allowed input. Evaluating finds its value for particular inputs. Distribute, combine like terms, and only then substitute when that makes the arithmetic easier; substituting first should give the same result.

Try it yourself: five questions, increasing difficulty

Q36 Practice 1 of 5 · Foundation

Simplify \(3x+2x+4\), then evaluate at \(x=3\).

Answer

\(5x+4\); value \(19\).

  1. Combine \(3x+2x\) to get \(5x\).
  2. Then \(5(3)+4=19\).

Q37 Practice 2 of 5 · Build

Simplify \(3(y+2)+2y\), then evaluate at \(y=4\).

Answer

\(5y+6\); value \(26\).

  1. Expand to \(3y+6+2y\), then combine.
  2. Substitute four: \(5(4)+6=26\).

Q38 Practice 3 of 5 · Apply

Simplify \(4(a-3)-2a+5\), then evaluate at \(a=-2\).

Answer

\(2a-7\); value \(-11\).

  1. \(4a-12-2a+5=2a-7\).
  2. Then \(2(-2)-7=-11\).

Q39 Practice 4 of 5 · Reason

Simplify \(2(3m-n)-3(m+2n)+4\), then evaluate at \(m=2,\ n=-1\).

Answer

\(3m-8n+4\); value \(18\).

  1. Expand to \(6m-2n-3m-6n+4\). Combine like terms to get \(3m-8n+4\).
  2. Substitute: \(6-8(-1)+4=6+8+4=18\).

Q40 Practice 5 of 5 · Challenge

Simplify \(5-2[3x-(x-4)]+x\), then evaluate at \(x=-3\).

Answer

\(-3x-3\); value \(6\).

  1. Inside brackets, \(3x-(x-4)=2x+4\).
  2. Now the expression is \(5-2(2x+4)+x\). Distribute to get \(5-4x-8+x\), then combine to obtain \(-3x-3\).
  3. At \(-3\), the value is \(9-3=6\).

Expressions versus equations

An expression names a value; an equation states that two expressions are equal. You evaluate an expression at a given input, but solve an equation to find the inputs that make it true. An equation can also be an identity, true for every allowed input.

Try it yourself: five questions, increasing difficulty

Q41 Practice 1 of 5 · Foundation

Classify \(5n-8\) and \(5n-8=17\) as an expression or an equation.

Answer

\(5n-8\) is an expression; \(5n-8=17\) is an equation.

  1. Only the second statement asserts an equality between two expressions.

Q42 Practice 2 of 5 · Build

Evaluate \(7t+2\) at \(t=3\). Are you finding a value or solving for an unknown?

Answer

\(23\); you are finding a value.

  1. The input is already supplied. Calculate \(7(3)+2=23\).

Q43 Practice 3 of 5 · Apply

Does \(x=5\) make \(4x-7=13\) true? Explain using substitution.

Answer

Yes.

  1. Substitute five on the left: \(4(5)-7=20-7=13\), which equals the right side.

Q44 Practice 4 of 5 · Reason

Solve \(6y+5=29\), then check the solution.

Answer

\(y=4\).

  1. Subtract five from both sides: \(6y=24\). Divide both sides by six.
  2. Check: \(6(4)+5=29\).

Q45 Practice 5 of 5 · Challenge

Is \(2(x+5)+x=3x+10\) true for one value of \(x\), no values, or all values? Explain without guessing.

Answer

It is true for all real values of \(x\).

  1. Simplify the left side to \(2x+10+x=3x+10\).
  2. Both sides are the same expression, so the equation is an identity, not an equation with just one solution.

Writing and evaluating expressions from stories

Define what your variable measures, then translate the story. Separate a one-time charge from a per-item charge. Write an expression, simplify it if useful, and substitute the requested value. Finish with units and an interpretation that fits the story.

Try it yourself: five questions, increasing difficulty

Q46 Practice 1 of 5 · Foundation

A ribbon costs $3 per meter. Write the cost of \(m\) meters, then find the cost of 7 meters.

Answer

\(3m\) dollars; $21.

  1. Multiply the cost per meter by the number of meters: \(3(7)=21\).

Q47 Practice 2 of 5 · Build

A skating rink charges $8 admission plus $5 per hour of skate rental. Write the cost of \(h\) hours and evaluate it for 3 hours.

Answer

\(8+5h\) dollars; $23.

  1. The admission fee is paid once. Rental for three hours costs \(5(3)=15\).
  2. The total is \(8+15=23\).

Q48 Practice 3 of 5 · Apply

One club makes \(4k+3\) cards and another makes \(2k+7\) cards. Write a simplified expression for their combined total, then evaluate at \(k=5\).

Answer

\(6k+10\) cards; 40 cards.

  1. Add the two expressions: \(4k+3+2k+7=6k+10\).
  2. At five, \(6(5)+10=40\).

Q49 Practice 4 of 5 · Reason

Three craft kits each contain \(2n+4\) beads. Five beads from the combined supply are set aside. Simplify the usable total and evaluate at \(n=6\).

Answer

\(6n+7\) beads; 43 beads.

  1. Write \(3(2n+4)-5\), then distribute: \(6n+12-5=6n+7\).
  2. At six, \(36+7=43\).

Q50 Practice 5 of 5 · Challenge

A robot’s battery-change model is \(4t-3(t+2)\) percentage points after \(t\) minutes. Simplify and evaluate at \(t=4\). What does the sign of the answer mean?

Answer

\(t-6\); \(-2\) percentage points, a decrease of 2 percentage points.

  1. Distribute the subtraction: \(4t-3t-6=t-6\).
  2. At four, the result is \(-2\). This is a change in charge, not a negative battery level.

Ten final word problems

Define the variable and write an expression before calculating. The first problems use one operation; later problems require distribution, subtraction of a whole expression, negative values, and an equation. Give a complete sentence interpreting your result.

W1 Buying pencils

Pencils cost $2 each. Maya buys \(p\) pencils. Write an expression for the cost, then evaluate it when \(p=9\).

Answer

\(2p\) dollars; $18.

  1. Each pencil contributes two dollars, so multiply \(2\) by \(p\).
  2. For nine pencils, \(2(9)=18\).

W2 A bicycle rental

A bicycle rental costs $6 to unlock the bike and $4 per hour. Write the cost for \(h\) hours and find the cost of 5 hours.

Answer

\(6+4h\) dollars; $26.

  1. The unlock fee is paid once, not once per hour.
  2. \(6+4(5)=6+20=26\).

W3 Temperature changes

At dawn the temperature is \(-3^\circ\mathrm C\). It rises by \(2^\circ\mathrm C\) each hour for \(h\) hours. Write the modeled temperature and evaluate it for \(h=4\).

Answer

\(-3+2h\); \(5^\circ\mathrm C\).

  1. Start with the initial temperature and add the rise.
  2. \(-3+2(4)=-3+8=5\).

W4 Combining fundraiser sales

A morning fundraiser sells \(3n+5\) tickets and the afternoon fundraiser sells \(4n-2\). Write a simplified expression for total ticket sales and evaluate at \(n=6\).

Answer

\(7n+3\) tickets; 45 tickets.

  1. Add the totals: \(3n+5+4n-2=7n+3\).
  2. At six, \(7(6)+3=45\).

W5 A garden border

A rectangular garden has length \(2x+3\) meters and width \(x+1\) meters. Write and simplify its perimeter, then evaluate at \(x=4\).

Answer

\(6x+8\) meters; 32 meters.

  1. A rectangle has two lengths and two widths: \(2(2x+3)+2(x+1)\).
  2. Distribute and combine: \(4x+6+2x+2=6x+8\). At four, \(24+8=32\).

W6 Identical supply boxes

A teacher buys 4 supply boxes. Each contains \(3b+2\) pencils, and 6 pencils from the combined supply are saved for another class. Write a simplified expression for the pencils available now, then evaluate at \(b=3\).

Answer

\(12b+2\) pencils; 38 pencils.

  1. The available total is \(4(3b+2)-6\).
  2. Expand to \(12b+8-6=12b+2\). At three, \(36+2=38\).

W7 Two workshop groups

Each student in Group A pays $7 and each student in Group B pays $5. The groups have \(2k+1\) and \(k+3\) students respectively. Write a simplified expression for the combined fees, then evaluate at \(k=4\).

Answer

\(19k+22\) dollars; $98.

  1. Write \(7(2k+1)+5(k+3)\).
  2. Distribute to get \(14k+7+5k+15=19k+22\). At four, \(76+22=98\).

W8 Comparing printing plans

Printer A charges \(12+2p\) dollars for \(p\) posters. Printer B charges \(5+3p\) dollars. Write and simplify “A’s cost minus B’s cost.” Evaluate it for 9 posters and explain which printer is cheaper.

Answer

\(7-p\); \(-2\) dollars at \(p=9\). Printer A is $2 cheaper.

  1. Subtract the whole second cost: \((12+2p)-(5+3p)\). Remove parentheses to get \(12+2p-5-3p\), then combine to obtain \(7-p\).
  2. At nine the difference is \(-2\), so A costs less. Directly, A costs $30 and B costs $32.

W9 A game-score rule

A game awards a score modeled by \(3r^2+2r-5\), where \(r\) is a signed round result. Find the score when \(r=-2\). A player claims the square contributes \(-12\); explain the error.

Answer

The score is \(3\) points. The square term contributes \(12\), not \(-12\).

  1. Substitute with parentheses: \(3(-2)^2+2(-2)-5\).
  2. Since \((-2)^2=4\), the score is \(12-4-5=3\). The minus sign is part of the number being squared.

W10 A school-fair budget

A club collects $8 from each of \(n\) students and receives a $25 donation. It buys 3 supply packs costing \(2n+4\) dollars each and spends another $7 on signs. Write a simplified expression for the money left, evaluate it for \(n=14\), and find how many students are needed to have exactly $40 left.

Answer

\(2n+6\) dollars left; $34 for 14 students; 17 students for $40 left.

  1. Income is \(8n+25\). Total expense is \(3(2n+4)+7=6n+19\).
  2. Subtract all expenses: \(8n+25-(6n+19)=2n+6\). At fourteen, \(28+6=34\).
  3. For exactly forty dollars, solve \(2n+6=40\). Then \(2n=34\), so \(n=17\). Check: income $161 minus expenses $121 leaves $40.

Quick questions and answers

What does it mean to evaluate an algebraic expression?

Replace every variable with its given value, then calculate using the order of operations. Evaluation produces a value for those particular inputs.

What are like terms?

Like terms have the same variable part with the same exponents. Their coefficients may be different. Constants are like terms with other constants.

How do you combine like terms?

Add or subtract their coefficients and keep the variable part unchanged. Combining terms does not increase exponents.

How do you substitute negative values safely?

Put each negative value in parentheses. Distinguish squaring the whole negative number from applying a minus sign after a square.

How does the distributive property work?

Multiply every term inside parentheses by the outside factor, including its sign, then combine like terms if possible.

What is the difference between an expression and an equation?

An expression represents a value. An equation asserts that two expressions are equal. Evaluate an expression at supplied inputs; solve an equation to find the inputs that make it true.
