Read the equation: slope and y-intercept

An equation written as \(y = mx + b\) is in slope-intercept form. It tells you two things at a glance. The number multiplying \(x\) is the slope \(m\): how much \(y\) changes each time \(x\) increases by 1. The constant term is \(b\), and the point \((0, b)\) is the y-intercept, where the line crosses the y-axis.

In the poster, \(y = 2x + 3\) has \(m = 2\) and \(b = 3\). The slope 2 means the line moves up 2 units for every 1 unit to the right. The y-intercept is \((0, 3)\): when \(x = 0\), \(y = 2(0) + 3 = 3\).

Read the signs with the numbers. In \(y = 5x - 4\) the constant is \(-4\), so \(b = -4\) and the y-intercept is \((0, -4)\). When no number is written in front of \(x\), the slope is 1 (\(y = x + 2\)) or \(-1\) (\(y = -x + 2\)). If the terms are in a different order, such as \(y = 3 - 2x\), rewrite the equation as \(y = -2x + 3\) before reading \(m\) and \(b\).

Practice: try these five questions

Q1

Find the slope and the y-intercept of \(y = 4x + 1\). Describe what the slope tells you.

Answer

\(m = 4\); y-intercept \((0, 1)\)

  1. Match \(y = 4x + 1\) to \(y = mx + b\): the number multiplying \(x\) is \(m = 4\) and the constant is \(b = 1\).

  2. The y-intercept is the point \((0, b) = (0, 1)\). Check: \(y = 4(0) + 1 = 1\).

  3. The slope 4 means the line moves up 4 units for every 1 unit to the right.

Q2

Find the slope and the y-intercept of \(y = -5x + 7\). Does the line rise or fall from left to right?

Answer

\(m = -5\); y-intercept \((0, 7)\); the line falls

  1. The coefficient of \(x\) is \(-5\), so \(m = -5\). Keep the negative sign with the number.

  2. The constant is 7, so the y-intercept is \((0, 7)\).

  3. A negative slope means the line goes down 5 units for every 1 unit to the right, so it falls from left to right.

Q3

Find the slope and the y-intercept of \(y = x - 6\).

Answer

\(m = 1\); y-intercept \((0, -6)\)

  1. No number is written in front of \(x\), and \(x\) means \(1x\), so \(m = 1\).

  2. Subtracting 6 is the same as adding \(-6\): \(y = 1x + (-6)\), so \(b = -6\).

  3. The y-intercept is \((0, -6)\). Check: \(y = 0 - 6 = -6\).

Q4

A classmate says the slope of \(y = 3 - 2x\) is 3. Rewrite the equation in the form \(y = mx + b\) and give the correct slope and y-intercept.

Answer

\(y = -2x + 3\); \(m = -2\); y-intercept \((0, 3)\)

  1. The slope is the number multiplying \(x\), not the first number written. Here \(x\) is multiplied by \(-2\).

  2. Reorder the terms: \(y = 3 - 2x = -2x + 3\).

  3. So \(m = -2\) and \(b = 3\); the y-intercept is \((0, 3)\). The classmate read the constant as the slope.

Q5

Write the equation of the line with slope \(\dfrac{3}{4}\) and y-intercept \((0, -2)\).

Answer

\(y = \dfrac{3}{4}x - 2\)

  1. Substitute \(m = \dfrac34\) and \(b = -2\) into \(y = mx + b\).

  2. \(y = \dfrac{3}{4}x + (-2) = \dfrac{3}{4}x - 2\).

  3. Check the intercept: when \(x = 0\), \(y = 0 - 2 = -2\), so the line passes through \((0, -2)\).

Plot the starting point

Graphing a line from slope-intercept form begins with the starting point, the y-intercept. Every point on the y-axis has \(x = 0\), so substitute 0 for \(x\). For the poster's line, \(y = 2(0) - 4 = -4\), giving the point \((0, -4)\).

To plot it, start at the origin \((0, 0)\) and move along the y-axis only: up for a positive \(b\), down for a negative \(b\). For \((0, -4)\) you move down 4 units. You never move left or right to plot a y-intercept, because its x-coordinate is 0.

Two common slips: writing the y-intercept as \((b, 0)\), which is a point on the x-axis, and dropping the sign of \(b\). If an equation has no constant term, such as \(y = 6x\), then \(b = 0\) and the line starts at the origin.

Q6

Find the y-intercept of \(y = 3x + 5\) by substituting \(x = 0\). Then plot it.

Answer

\((0, 5)\)

  1. Every point on the y-axis has \(x = 0\): \(y = 3(0) + 5 = 0 + 5 = 5\).

  2. The y-intercept is \((0, 5)\).

  3. Start at the origin and move up 5 units along the y-axis; plot the point there.

Q7

Find the y-intercept of \(y = -4x - 2\). Describe how to move from the origin to plot it, then plot it.

Answer

\((0, -2)\); move down 2

  1. Substitute \(x = 0\): \(y = -4(0) - 2 = 0 - 2 = -2\).

  2. The y-intercept is \((0, -2)\).

  3. From the origin, move down 2 units along the y-axis. The slope \(-4\) does not change where the line starts.

Q8

Find the y-intercept of \(y = 6x\).

Answer

\((0, 0)\), the origin

  1. Substitute \(x = 0\): \(y = 6(0) = 0\).

  2. There is no constant term, so \(b = 0\): \(y = 6x + 0\).

  3. The y-intercept is \((0, 0)\). The line starts at the origin, so no move along the y-axis is needed.

Q9

For \(y = -x + 8\), Ana says the y-intercept is \((8, 0)\). Is she right? Give the correct y-intercept and explain what \((8, 0)\) is.

Answer

No; the y-intercept is \((0, 8)\)

  1. Substitute \(x = 0\): \(y = -0 + 8 = 8\), so the y-intercept is \((0, 8)\).

  2. A y-intercept always has x-coordinate 0. Ana reversed the coordinates.

  3. The point \((8, 0)\) is on the line too (\(-8 + 8 = 0\)), but it lies on the x-axis: it is where the line crosses the x-axis, not the y-axis.

Q10

Which two of these lines cross the y-axis at the same point? \(y = 2x - 3\), \(y = -5x - 3\), \(y = 3x + 2\).

Answer

\(y = 2x - 3\) and \(y = -5x - 3\), both at \((0, -3)\)

  1. Substitute \(x = 0\) in each: \(2(0) - 3 = -3\), \(-5(0) - 3 = -3\), \(3(0) + 2 = 2\).

  2. The y-intercepts are \((0, -3)\), \((0, -3)\) and \((0, 2)\).

  3. The first two share the starting point \((0, -3)\). Different slopes send them in different directions after they leave it.

Graph a positive slope

Once the starting point is plotted, the slope tells you how to move to the next point. Write the slope as rise over run. A whole-number slope such as 2 is \(\dfrac{2}{1}\): run 1 to the right, rise 2 up.

For \(y = 2x + 1\) in the poster, start at \((0, 1)\), move right 1 and up 2 to reach \((1, 3)\), then repeat the same move to reach \((2, 5)\). Draw a straight line through all three points and extend it in both directions with arrows.

Plot at least three points: two determine the line, and the third is a check. If the three points do not line up, recheck a move. You can also check any point by substituting: for \((2, 5)\), \(2(2) + 1 = 5\). A positive slope always makes the line rise from left to right.

Q11

Graph \(y = 3x - 2\). Start at the y-intercept and use the slope to find two more points.

Answer

Points \((0, -2)\), \((1, 1)\), \((2, 4)\)

  1. The y-intercept is \((0, -2)\). Plot it.

  2. Slope \(3 = \dfrac{3}{1}\): from \((0, -2)\) move right 1, up 3 to \((1, 1)\); repeat to \((2, 4)\).

  3. Check \((2, 4)\): \(3(2) - 2 = 4\). Draw the line through the three points.

Q12

Graph \(y = x + 2\). List the points for \(x = 0, 1, 2, 3\).

Answer

\((0, 2)\), \((1, 3)\), \((2, 4)\), \((3, 5)\)

  1. The slope is 1 (no number is written in front of \(x\)) and the y-intercept is \((0, 2)\).

  2. Each move is right 1, up 1: \((0, 2) \to (1, 3) \to (2, 4) \to (3, 5)\).

  3. Check \((3, 5)\): \(3 + 2 = 5\). Draw the line through the points.

Q13

Graph \(y = 2x - 3\). Where does the line cross the y-axis, and what is \(y\) when \(x = 3\)?

Answer

Crosses the y-axis at \((0, -3)\); \(y = 3\) when \(x = 3\)

  1. Start at the y-intercept \((0, -3)\).

  2. Slope 2: right 1, up 2 each time: \((1, -1)\), \((2, 1)\), \((3, 3)\).

  3. So when \(x = 3\), \(y = 3\). Check: \(2(3) - 3 = 3\).

Q14

Graph \(y = 4x - 5\) using two moves from the y-intercept.

Answer

Points \((0, -5)\), \((1, -1)\), \((2, 3)\)

  1. Plot the y-intercept \((0, -5)\): from the origin, down 5.

  2. Slope \(4 = \dfrac41\): right 1, up 4 to \((1, -1)\); again to \((2, 3)\).

  3. Check \((2, 3)\): \(4(2) - 5 = 3\). The line is steep because the slope is large.

Q15

Graph \(y = 3x + 1\). Then decide whether \((2, 7)\) and \((3, 9)\) are on the line.

Answer

\((2, 7)\) is on the line; \((3, 9)\) is not

  1. Start at \((0, 1)\); right 1, up 3 to \((1, 4)\); again to \((2, 7)\).

  2. \((2, 7)\) is reached by the slope moves, and \(3(2) + 1 = 7\), so it is on the line.

  3. For \((3, 9)\): \(3(3) + 1 = 10 \ne 9\). The next point on the line is \((3, 10)\), so \((3, 9)\) is just below the line.

Graph a negative slope

A negative slope uses the same three steps, but the rise is negative, so you move down. Write \(-3\) as \(\dfrac{-3}{1}\): run 1 to the right, rise \(-3\), meaning down 3.

In the poster, \(y = -3x + 2\) starts at \((0, 2)\). Moving right 1 and down 3 gives \((1, -1)\); repeating the move gives \((2, -4)\). The line through these points falls from left to right.

You may also move to the left. Moving left 1 and up 3 is the same slope, \(\dfrac{3}{-1} = -3\), and it is useful when the points to the right run off the grid. The larger the number without its sign, the steeper the line: \(y = -5x\) is steeper than \(y = -x\).

Q16

Graph \(y = -2x + 4\). List the points for \(x = 0, 1, 2, 3\). Where does the line cross the x-axis?

Answer

\((0, 4)\), \((1, 2)\), \((2, 0)\), \((3, -2)\); crosses the x-axis at \((2, 0)\)

  1. Start at the y-intercept \((0, 4)\).

  2. Slope \(-2\): right 1, down 2 each time: \((1, 2)\), \((2, 0)\), \((3, -2)\).

  3. The point with \(y = 0\) is \((2, 0)\), so the line crosses the x-axis there. Check: \(-2(2) + 4 = 0\).

Q17

Graph \(y = -x + 3\).

Answer

Points \((0, 3)\), \((1, 2)\), \((2, 1)\), \((3, 0)\)

  1. The slope is \(-1\) and the y-intercept is \((0, 3)\).

  2. Each move is right 1, down 1: \((1, 2)\), \((2, 1)\), \((3, 0)\).

  3. Draw the line through the points; it falls from left to right.

Q18

Graph \(y = -4x + 5\) using two moves from the y-intercept.

Answer

Points \((0, 5)\), \((1, 1)\), \((2, -3)\)

  1. Plot \((0, 5)\).

  2. Slope \(-4\): right 1, down 4 to \((1, 1)\); again to \((2, -3)\).

  3. Check \((2, -3)\): \(-4(2) + 5 = -8 + 5 = -3\).

Q19

Graph \(y = -3x - 1\). Find one point to the right of the y-intercept and one point to the left.

Answer

\((0, -1)\), \((1, -4)\) and \((-1, 2)\)

  1. Plot the y-intercept \((0, -1)\).

  2. To the right: right 1, down 3 gives \((1, -4)\).

  3. To the left: reverse both directions, left 1 and up 3, giving \((-1, 2)\). Check: \(-3(-1) - 1 = 3 - 1 = 2\).

Q20

Without graphing, decide whether each line rises or falls from left to right, and name the steepest: \(y = -5x + 2\), \(y = 5x - 2\), \(y = -x\).

Answer

Falls, rises, falls; \(y = -5x + 2\) and \(y = 5x - 2\) are equally steep, and both are steeper than \(y = -x\)

  1. Read each slope: \(-5\), \(5\) and \(-1\).

  2. Negative slope falls; positive slope rises. So: falls, rises, falls.

  3. Steepness depends on the size of the slope without its sign: 5, 5 and 1. The first two are equally steep (in opposite directions); \(y = -x\) is the least steep.

Use a fractional slope

When the slope is a fraction, read it directly as rise over run: the numerator is the rise and the denominator is the run. For \(y = \dfrac{2}{3}x - 1\), start at \((0, -1)\), then move right 3 and up 2 to land exactly on \((3, 1)\).

Moving the full run keeps every point on a grid corner. Moving right 1 would require going up \(\dfrac23\) of a unit, which is hard to plot accurately. To find a point on the other side, reverse both directions: left 3 and down 2 gives \((-3, -3)\).

For a negative fraction such as \(-\dfrac{3}{4}\), move right 4 and down 3 (or left 4 and up 3). Check a point by substituting: for \((3, 1)\), \(\dfrac23(3) - 1 = 2 - 1 = 1\). Choosing x-values that are multiples of the denominator keeps the arithmetic whole.

Q21

Graph \(y = \dfrac{1}{2}x + 1\). Find two points to the right of the y-intercept and one to the left.

Answer

\((0, 1)\), \((2, 2)\), \((4, 3)\) and \((-2, 0)\)

  1. Plot the y-intercept \((0, 1)\).

  2. Slope \(\dfrac12\): rise 1, run 2. Right 2, up 1 gives \((2, 2)\); again gives \((4, 3)\).

  3. Left 2, down 1 gives \((-2, 0)\). Check: \(\dfrac12(-2) + 1 = -1 + 1 = 0\).

Q22

Graph \(y = \dfrac{3}{4}x - 2\) using two moves to the right.

Answer

\((0, -2)\), \((4, 1)\), \((8, 4)\)

  1. Plot \((0, -2)\).

  2. Slope \(\dfrac34\): right 4, up 3 gives \((4, 1)\); again gives \((8, 4)\).

  3. Check \((8, 4)\): \(\dfrac34(8) - 2 = 6 - 2 = 4\).

Q23

Graph \(y = -\dfrac{2}{3}x + 4\). Where does the line cross the x-axis?

Answer

\((0, 4)\), \((3, 2)\), \((6, 0)\); crosses the x-axis at \((6, 0)\)

  1. Plot \((0, 4)\).

  2. Slope \(-\dfrac23\): right 3, down 2 gives \((3, 2)\); again gives \((6, 0)\).

  3. \((6, 0)\) has \(y = 0\), so the line crosses the x-axis there. Check: \(-\dfrac23(6) + 4 = -4 + 4 = 0\).

Q24

Graph \(y = \dfrac{5}{2}x - 4\). Is this line steeper or less steep than \(y = 2x - 4\)?

Answer

\((0, -4)\), \((2, 1)\), \((4, 6)\); steeper, because \(\dfrac52 \gt 2\)

  1. Plot \((0, -4)\).

  2. Slope \(\dfrac52\): right 2, up 5 gives \((2, 1)\); again gives \((4, 6)\).

  3. \(\dfrac52 = 2.5\), which is greater than 2, so this line rises faster and is steeper than \(y = 2x - 4\).

Q25

Graph \(y = -\dfrac{1}{3}x - 1\). Then check whether \((6, -3)\) is on the line.

Answer

\((-3, 0)\), \((0, -1)\), \((3, -2)\); yes, \((6, -3)\) is on the line

  1. Plot \((0, -1)\). Slope \(-\dfrac13\): right 3, down 1 gives \((3, -2)\); left 3, up 1 gives \((-3, 0)\).

  2. One more move right from \((3, -2)\) gives \((6, -3)\).

  3. Check: \(-\dfrac13(6) - 1 = -2 - 1 = -3\). The point is on the line.

Zero slope or zero intercept?

Two equations that look alike can describe very different lines. In \(y = 3\), there is no \(x\)-term, so the slope is 0: \(y = 0x + 3\). Every point has height 3, and the graph is a horizontal line through \((0, 3)\).

In \(y = 3x\), there is no constant term, so \(b = 0\): \(y = 3x + 0\). The line passes through the origin \((0, 0)\) and rises 3 units for every 1 unit to the right. In the poster the two lines meet at \((1, 3)\), where \(3x = 3\).

A quick test: a missing \(x\)-term means zero slope; a missing constant means a zero y-intercept. The x-axis itself is the line \(y = 0\), which has both slope 0 and y-intercept 0.

Q26

Graph \(y = -2\) and \(y = -2x\) on the same grid. Where do they meet?

Answer

They meet at \((1, -2)\)

  1. \(y = -2\) has slope 0: a horizontal line through \((0, -2)\).

  2. \(y = -2x\) has \(b = 0\): it starts at \((0, 0)\) and moves right 1, down 2 to \((1, -2)\).

Q27

Give the slope \(m\) and the y-intercept for each line: \(y = 5\), \(y = 5x\), \(y = x\), \(y = 0\).

Answer

\(y = 5\): \(m = 0\), \((0, 5)\). \(y = 5x\): \(m = 5\), \((0, 0)\). \(y = x\): \(m = 1\), \((0, 0)\). \(y = 0\): \(m = 0\), \((0, 0)\)

  1. \(y = 5\) is \(y = 0x + 5\): slope 0, y-intercept \((0, 5)\), a horizontal line.

  2. \(y = 5x\) is \(y = 5x + 0\): slope 5, y-intercept \((0, 0)\).

  3. \(y = x\) is \(y = 1x + 0\): slope 1, y-intercept \((0, 0)\).

  4. \(y = 0\) is \(y = 0x + 0\): slope 0 and y-intercept \((0, 0)\). This line is the x-axis.

Q28

Write the equation of (a) the horizontal line through \((4, -3)\), and (b) the line through the origin with slope \(-\dfrac12\).

Answer

(a) \(y = -3\); (b) \(y = -\dfrac12 x\)

  1. (a) A horizontal line has slope 0, so every point has the same y-value as \((4, -3)\): \(y = -3\).

  2. (b) Through the origin means \(b = 0\). With \(m = -\dfrac12\): \(y = -\dfrac12 x + 0 = -\dfrac12 x\).

  3. Check (b): \((2, -1)\) is on it, since \(-\dfrac12(2) = -1\).

Q29

Which of these lines pass through the origin? \(y = 7x\), \(y = x + 7\), \(y = -x\), \(y = 7\).

Answer

\(y = 7x\) and \(y = -x\)

  1. A line passes through the origin when \(x = 0\) gives \(y = 0\), which means \(b = 0\).

  2. \(y = 7x\): \(7(0) = 0\). Yes. \(y = -x\): \(-0 = 0\). Yes.

  3. \(y = x + 7\) gives \(y = 7\) at \(x = 0\), and \(y = 7\) is always 7. Neither passes through the origin.

Q30

Graph \(y = \dfrac12 x\) and \(y = 4\) on the same grid. At what point do they meet?

Answer

\((8, 4)\)

  1. \(y = 4\) is a horizontal line through \((0, 4)\).

  2. \(y = \dfrac12 x\) starts at \((0, 0)\); right 2, up 1 each move: \((2, 1)\), \((4, 2)\), \((6, 3)\), \((8, 4)\).

  3. They meet where \(\dfrac12 x = 4\), so \(x = 8\): the point \((8, 4)\).

Spot the slope mistake

Most graphing errors come from one of four slips: using the wrong sign (up instead of down), swapping rise and run, plotting the y-intercept on the x-axis, or reading the slope from the wrong term. The poster shows the sign slip: for \(y = -\dfrac12 x + 3\), the student moved right 2 and up 1, but a negative slope moving right must go down.

The corrected move from \((0, 3)\) is right 2 and down 1, reaching \((2, 2)\). Then check by substituting the x-coordinate: \(-\dfrac12(2) + 3 = -1 + 3 = 2\), which matches the y-coordinate.

Make substitution your habit. If the result does not match the y-coordinate of the point you plotted, a move is wrong. The check takes seconds and catches every one of the four slips.

Q31

For \(y = -2x + 5\), a student went from \((0, 5)\) right 1 and up 2 to \((1, 7)\). Find the mistake, correct the point, and check it.

Answer

The move should be down 2; correct point \((1, 3)\)

  1. The slope \(-2\) is negative, so moving right 1 must go down 2, not up.

  2. Correct move: \((0, 5)\) right 1, down 2 gives \((1, 3)\).

  3. Check: \(-2(1) + 5 = 3\). The student's point fails: \(-2(1) + 5 = 3 \ne 7\).

Q32

For \(y = \dfrac{3}{2}x - 1\), a student moved from \((0, -1)\) right 3 and up 2 to \((3, 1)\). What went wrong? Give the correct point.

Answer

Rise and run were swapped; correct move right 2, up 3 to \((2, 2)\)

  1. In \(\dfrac32\) the numerator is the rise (3) and the denominator is the run (2).

  2. The student used run 3 and rise 2, which is the slope \(\dfrac23\).

  3. Correct move: right 2, up 3 from \((0, -1)\) gives \((2, 2)\). Check: \(\dfrac32(2) - 1 = 3 - 1 = 2\).

Q33

For \(y = 4x - 3\), a student plotted the starting point at \((-3, 0)\). Explain the error and give the correct starting point and the next point.

Answer

The y-intercept is \((0, -3)\); next point \((1, 1)\)

  1. The starting point is on the y-axis, so its x-coordinate is 0. The student plotted it on the x-axis instead.

  2. Substitute \(x = 0\): \(y = 4(0) - 3 = -3\), so the starting point is \((0, -3)\).

  3. Slope 4: right 1, up 4 gives \((1, 1)\). Check: \(4(1) - 3 = 1\).

Q34

A student says \(y = 2 + 5x\) has slope 2 and y-intercept \((0, 5)\). Correct the statement.

Answer

Slope 5; y-intercept \((0, 2)\)

  1. The slope is the coefficient of \(x\), wherever that term appears. Here \(x\) is multiplied by 5.

  2. Rewrite: \(y = 5x + 2\), so \(m = 5\) and \(b = 2\).

  3. Check the intercept: when \(x = 0\), \(y = 2 + 0 = 2\).

Q35

For \(y = -\dfrac{3}{4}x + 6\), a student moved from \((0, 6)\) left 4 and down 3 to \((-4, 3)\). Is the point correct? If not, give two correct points, one on each side of the intercept.

Answer

No. Correct points: \((4, 3)\) and \((-4, 9)\)

  1. Moving left and down describes a positive slope, \(\dfrac{-3}{-4} = \dfrac34\), not \(-\dfrac34\).

  2. Check the student's point: \(-\dfrac34(-4) + 6 = 3 + 6 = 9 \ne 3\).

  3. Correct moves: right 4, down 3 gives \((4, 3)\); left 4, up 3 gives \((-4, 9)\). Check: \(-\dfrac34(4) + 6 = 3\).

When is the tank empty?

Slope-intercept form fits any situation with a starting amount and a constant rate. The starting amount is \(b\) and the rate is \(m\); a quantity that decreases has a negative rate. The poster's tank starts with 12 gallons and drains 2 gallons per minute, so \(y = -2x + 12\), where \(x\) is minutes and \(y\) is gallons.

Each intercept answers a question. The y-intercept \((0, 12)\) says the tank holds 12 gallons at time 0. The tank is empty when \(y = 0\): \(0 = -2x + 12\), so \(2x = 12\) and \(x = 6\). The point \((6, 0)\), where the line meets the horizontal axis, says the tank is empty after 6 minutes.

In real situations the graph usually stops at the axes: time cannot be negative, and the tank cannot hold less than 0 gallons. Always label the axes with the quantities and units, and state the meaning of each intercept in a sentence.

Quick questions and answers

What is slope-intercept form?

Slope-intercept form is y = mx + b. The number m multiplying x is the slope, and b gives the y-intercept (0, b), where the line crosses the y-axis. In y = 2x + 3 the slope is 2 and the y-intercept is (0, 3).

How do I graph a line from y = mx + b?

Plot the y-intercept (0, b) first. Write the slope as rise over run, then move right by the run and up by the rise (down if the slope is negative) to find a second point. Repeat the move for a third point, check it by substituting, and draw a straight line through the points.

How do I graph a fractional slope like 2/3?

The numerator is the rise and the denominator is the run. For a slope of 2/3, move right 3 and up 2 from the y-intercept so the next point lands on a grid corner. To go the other way, move left 3 and down 2.

What is the difference between y = 3 and y = 3x?

y = 3 has zero slope: it is a horizontal line where every point has height 3. y = 3x has a zero y-intercept: it passes through the origin and rises 3 units for every 1 unit to the right. The two lines meet at (1, 3).

How do I check that a point is on my line?

Substitute the point's x-coordinate into the equation. If the result equals the point's y-coordinate, the point is on the line. For y = −(1/2)x + 3 and the point (2, 2): −(1/2)(2) + 3 = 2, so it is on the line.

What do the intercepts mean in a word problem?

The y-intercept is the starting amount when x = 0. The point where the line meets the x-axis is when the quantity reaches 0. For a tank with y = −2x + 12, the tank starts with 12 gallons and is empty at (6, 0), after 6 minutes.
