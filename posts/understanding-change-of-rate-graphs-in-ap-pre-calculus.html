Understanding Change of Rate Graphs in AP Pre-Calculus

1. Let f be a decreasing function where the rate of change of f is increasing.

School of Math April 28, 2026 1 min read

1. Let f be a decreasing function where the rate of change of f is increasing. Which of the following could be the graph of f?

(pre calculus rate of change)

Solution:

If f is decreasing, then the graph must go down from left to right. That means the slope is negative.

If the rate of change of f is increasing, then the slope is becoming less negative.

Example:

The slope may change from -5 to -3 to -1.

So the function is still decreasing, but it is decreasing more slowly. This means the graph is: decreasing and concave up

Now check the choices:

  • A is increasing, so it is not correct.
  • B is increasing, so it is not correct.
  • C is decreasing and concave up, so it is correct.
  • D is decreasing, but it is concave down because the slope becomes more negative.

Final answer: C

AP Precalculus tip:

When a problem says “decreasing function,” look for a graph that moves downward from left to right. When it says “rate of change is increasing,” look for slopes that are becoming larger. For a decreasing function, that means the slopes are negative but becoming closer to zero. This is why the correct graph is decreasing and concave up.
