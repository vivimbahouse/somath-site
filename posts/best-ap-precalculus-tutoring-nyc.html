1. What the AP Precalculus exam actually tests in 2026

Before choosing a tutor, understand what your student is preparing for. The 2026 AP Precalculus exam is a 3-hour College Board exam administered in a hybrid digital format (Bluebook for multiple choice, paper for free response). The score is 62.5% multiple choice and 37.5% free response.

4Free-response questions
3 hrsTotal exam time
82%Scored 3 or higher in 2026

Multiple choice structure. Part A is 28 questions in 80 minutes with no calculator. Part B is 12 questions in 40 minutes with a graphing calculator. The no-calculator section rewards algebraic fluency and symbolic manipulation; the calculator section tests modeling and numerical reasoning.

Free response structure. Four questions, each targeted at a specific exam skill:

  • FRQ 1 — Function Concepts (calculator). Interpreting function behavior from graphs, tables, and equations.
  • FRQ 2 — Modeling a Non-Periodic Context (calculator). Polynomial, rational, exponential, or logarithmic modeling of a real-world scenario.
  • FRQ 3 — Modeling a Periodic Context (no calculator). Trigonometric modeling of a periodic scenario.
  • FRQ 4 — Symbolic Manipulations (no calculator). Algebraic and symbolic manipulation without a calculator. Historically the strongest differentiator between 3, 4, and 5 scores.

Units tested. Only Units 1 through 3 are assessed on the exam:

Unit 2Exponential & Logarithmic Functions27–40%

Key insight from the 2026 exam. Trevor Packer (College Board VP of AP) noted after the 2026 exam that students found exponential and logarithmic problems more accessible than trigonometric and polar problems, and that FRQ 4 (no-calculator symbolic manipulation) was the strongest differentiator between 3s, 4s, and 5s. Any AP Precalculus tutor worth hiring will drill trig/polar and no-calculator symbolic manipulation harder than the rest of the curriculum. If they do not, they are not preparing your student for the exam.

Coming in 2027. The multiple choice section expands from 40 to 42 questions and the timing structure shifts. Any tutor claiming to prepare students for the 2027 exam should already have curriculum updated for the new format.

2. What to look for in an AP Precalculus tutor in NYC

Not every “precalculus tutor” in NYC actually teaches AP Precalculus. The College Board framework is new (launched 2023–24) and many tutors are still teaching pre-College-Board precalculus material. Six things to check before you hire anyone in NYC:

  1. Do they know the current framework by unit? Ask them to name the three tested units and their approximate weights. If they cannot, they have not read the current framework.
  2. Do they drill no-calculator work? More than half the exam (Part A of multiple choice + FRQ 3 + FRQ 4) is no-calculator. A tutor who lets your student use a calculator on every problem is not preparing them for the exam.
  3. Do they run timed practice? The exam is time-constrained. Untimed tutoring builds understanding but not exam performance.
  4. Do they have graded free-response practice with feedback? FRQ scoring rubrics are specific. A tutor who does not grade FRQ work with a rubric is missing the biggest exam skill.
  5. What is their teacher training? Ivy League, Northwestern, MIT, top-STEM PhD, or licensed math teacher — not “a college student who did well in math.” The AP Precalculus content is graduate-level in places (especially trig identities and polar-to-Cartesian conversion). A less-trained tutor gets exposed fast.
  6. Do they have a curriculum, or just a stack of practice problems? A serious AP Precalculus program is a 15–20 week teacher-designed curriculum with a diagnostic, a topic sequence, and progression checkpoints. Ad-hoc “work through past AP exams” sessions are not a program.
