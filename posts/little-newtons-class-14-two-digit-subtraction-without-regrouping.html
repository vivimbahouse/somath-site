Understanding subtraction

Subtraction can tell us how many are left after we take some away. Start with the whole amount, subtract the part taken away, and find the remaining part. The answer to a subtraction question is called the difference.

Read the subtraction sign as “minus” or “take away.” Keep the numbers in the order the story gives: starting amount minus amount taken away. You can draw a picture to show what stays and what is removed.

Reviewing tens and ones

A two-digit number has a tens digit on the left and a ones digit on the right. For example, 62 is 6 tens and 2 ones. The 6 is worth 60, not 6.

Subtract tens from tens and ones from ones. Saying the place values aloud helps: “tens minus tens” and “ones minus ones.” We will not trade a ten for ten ones in this lesson.

Subtracting with models

A tens counter represents 10 and a ones counter represents 1. Draw the starting number with these two kinds of counters. Cross out the tens and ones you are taking away, then count what remains.

A picture and a subtraction sentence should describe the same action. Keep the tens counters separate from the ones counters so you do not count a ten as a single one.

Aligning numbers

When you write subtraction vertically, keep the place values lined up. The ones digit of the bottom number goes below the ones digit of the top number. The tens digits belong in the tens column.

A one-digit number has no tens. If you subtract 7, place the 7 in the ones column, not the tens column. You may think of it as 0 tens and 7 ones.

Subtracting the ones

Start in the ones column on the right. Subtract the bottom ones digit from the top ones digit and write that result in the ones place. Leave the tens in their own column.

Every subtraction in this class has enough ones in the starting number. If the ones digits are equal, the answer has zero ones. Keep that zero when you write a number such as 30.

Q25 Practice 5 of 5

Answer

The missing digit is 6. The starting number is 96.

  1. The ones must satisfy ? − 4 = 2, so ? = 6. The tens check is 9 − 5 = 4. Thus 96 − 54 = 42.

Subtracting the tens

After the ones, subtract the tens. A result of 4 in the tens column means 4 tens, or 40. Read the tens and ones together to name the whole answer.

If you take away a number ending in zero, you take away whole tens and no ones. The ones digit stays the same. If the tens digits are equal, no tens remain.

Checking with addition

To check subtraction, add the difference to the amount you took away. The sum should equal the starting amount. This works because subtraction takes a part away and addition puts that part back.

If your check does not return to the starting amount, look at the columns again. Check the ones, then the tens. An incorrect check is a clue to revise your answer, not a reason to guess.

Solving word problems

Before choosing an operation, decide what the question asks. Taking away can mean finding what is left. Comparing can mean finding how many more one group has than another. Both situations can use subtraction.

Write a number sentence and label the answer with the thing you counted. A question asking “how many more” needs the difference, not the larger total. For a two-step story, work through one action at a time.

Quick questions and answers

How do you subtract two-digit numbers without regrouping?

Line up tens and ones. Subtract the ones, then subtract the tens. Read the two place values together and check by adding the difference to the amount subtracted.

What does “without regrouping” mean?

You already have enough ones and tens to take away the required amount. You do not need to exchange a ten for ten ones. Every subtraction in this lesson stays within that rule.

What if the ones digits are the same?

Their difference is zero. Write 0 in the ones place; for example, three tens and no ones is written 30, not 3.

Where does a one-digit number go in vertical subtraction?

It goes in the ones column, under the top number’s ones digit. It represents zero tens and that many ones.

How can addition check a subtraction answer?

Add the difference to the number subtracted. If you get the starting number, the subtraction checks. If not, inspect the ones and tens again.
