What factoring polynomials means

Factoring rewrites a sum or difference as a product with exactly the same value. Expanding and factoring are opposite directions: distribution opens a product, while factoring exposes the pieces being multiplied.

An expression and an equation are different. Factoring an expression does not mean setting its factors equal to zero. You may use the zero-product property only after an equation has been written with zero on one side.

Begin by checking for a greatest common factor. Then choose a pattern that fits the remaining terms. Finish by multiplying back: a convincing-looking factorization is not enough unless it expands to the original expression.

Practice: five questions, increasing difficulty

Q1

Factor completely over the integers: \(5 x + 35\).

Answer

\(5 \left(x + 7\right)\)

  1. Both terms are divisible by 5; divide each term by 5.
  2. The complete factorization is \(5 \left(x + 7\right)\).
  3. Expand the factors to check that they reproduce \(5 x + 35\).

Q2

Factor completely over the integers: \(x^{2} + 11 x + 28\).

Answer

\(\left(x + 4\right) \left(x + 7\right)\)

  1. For a monic trinomial, find numbers with product 28 and sum 11: 4 and 7.
  2. The complete factorization is \(\left(x + 4\right) \left(x + 7\right)\).
  3. Expand the factors to check that they reproduce \(x^{2} + 11 x + 28\).

Q3

Does \((x+2)(x+7)\) equal \(x^2+9x+12\)? Expand and, if needed, correct the constant term.

Answer

No. The correct expansion is \(x^2+9x+14\).

  1. Distribute to get \(x^2+7x+2x+14\).
  2. Combine the middle terms. The constant is \(2\cdot7=14\), not 12.

Answer

\(3 \left(x - 3\right) \left(x + 3\right)\)

  1. First remove the GCF 3; then factor the difference of squares.
  2. The complete factorization is \(3 \left(x - 3\right) \left(x + 3\right)\).
  3. Expand the factors to check that they reproduce \(3 x^{2} - 27\).

Q5

Factor completely over the integers: \(2 x^{3} + 14 x^{2} + 24 x\).

Answer

\(2 x \left(x + 3\right) \left(x + 4\right)\)

  1. Remove 2x first, then factor the remaining monic quadratic.
  2. The remaining quadratic is \(x^2+7x+12\); the pair 3 and 4 has the needed sum and product.
  3. The complete factorization is \(2 x \left(x + 3\right) \left(x + 4\right)\).
  4. Expand the factors to check that they reproduce \(2 x^{3} + 14 x^{2} + 24 x\).

Greatest common factor

The GCF uses the greatest positive common divisor of the coefficients and the lowest exponent of each variable present in every term. If a variable is missing from one term, it is not part of the GCF.

Divide every term by the GCF to obtain the expression inside parentheses. A term equal to the GCF leaves 1 inside, not an empty space. Removing a negative common factor can also make the remaining leading coefficient positive.

Factoring out the GCF may only be the first step. Inspect the parentheses for another pattern, and keep the extracted factor attached to the final answer.

Factoring trinomials

For a monic trinomial \(x^2+bx+c\), look for integers \(u,v\) satisfying \(u+v=b\) and \(uv=c\). Then the factors are \((x+u)(x+v)\). A positive product gives matching signs; a negative product gives opposite signs.

The simple sum-and-product rule printed in the image applies directly when the leading coefficient is 1. For \(ax^2+bx+c\) with \(a\ne1\), use the product \(ac\): split the middle term into two terms whose coefficients add to \(b\) and multiply to \(ac\), then group.

Not every trinomial factors into integer binomials. If no integer pair works, say it is irreducible over the integers rather than inventing factors. Always check for a GCF before applying a trinomial method.

Difference of squares

The identity \(A^2-B^2=(A-B)(A+B)\) works because the two middle terms cancel when the factors are expanded. Check for exactly two squared terms separated by subtraction after removing any GCF.

The square roots may be monomials rather than single variables. Take the square root of the coefficient and halve the even exponent of each variable.

A factor can itself be another difference of squares, so inspect your answer again. Do not apply this pattern to a sum of squares over the real numbers. In this class, factor over the integers unless a question asks otherwise.

Perfect-square trinomials

The patterns are \(A^2+2AB+B^2=(A+B)^2\) and \(A^2-2AB+B^2=(A-B)^2\). The first and last terms being squares is necessary but not sufficient: the middle term must be exactly twice their square-root product.

The sign of the middle term determines the sign inside the repeated binomial. The constant term in either perfect-square pattern is positive because it comes from squaring the second term.

Check for an outside GCF and for variables inside both square terms. When asked for an unknown coefficient, identify the two square roots first, then calculate the required middle term.

Factoring by grouping

Grouping is useful when pairs of terms can produce the same remaining factor. For four terms, try two pairs, remove each pair’s GCF, and look for a shared binomial.

If the remaining binomials have opposite signs, factor a negative out of one group. Reordering terms can reveal a useful grouping, but keep each sign attached to its term.

After removing the common binomial, check the other factor too. It may be a difference of squares or another factorable expression. Grouping does not guarantee that every four-term polynomial factors.

Finding zeros from factors

Graph clarification: a real zero is where the graph meets the x-axis. It can cross or touch and turn. Nonreal zeros are not x-intercepts on a real coordinate graph.

A zero is an input \(r\) with \(f(r)=0\). Factoring lets us use the zero-product property: a product equals zero only when at least one factor equals zero.

Zeros are numbers; x-intercepts are ordered pairs. A real zero r corresponds to the point (r,0). A nonzero constant multiplier affects the graph’s scale but does not add a zero.

A repeated linear factor still contributes a zero, but with multiplicity. Even multiplicity produces a touch-and-turn; odd multiplicity produces a crossing. Do not confuse a zero with the y-intercept, which is found by substituting x = 0.

The Remainder Theorem

When \(P(x)\) is divided by \(x-a\), polynomial division gives \(P(x)=(x-a)Q(x)+R\), where the remainder \(R\) is a constant. Substitute \(x=a\): the product becomes zero, leaving \(P(a)=R\).

This gives a shortcut for the remainder, not for the entire quotient. Evaluate carefully with parentheses around negative substitutions and compute powers before multiplying.

For a nonmonic linear divisor, solve the divisor equal to zero to determine the substitution value. The remainder is still the polynomial evaluated there, but the quotient differs from the quotient for the corresponding monic divisor.

Q36

Find the remainder when \(P(x)=x^{2} + 4 x - 7\) is divided by \(x - 3\).

Answer

  1. Set the divisor equal to zero: \(x - 3=0\), so evaluate at \(x=3\).

The Factor Theorem

The Factor Theorem is the zero-remainder case: \(x-a\) is a factor of \(P(x)\) if and only if \(P(a)=0\). Either direction is useful: a verified zero gives a factor, and a known factor gives a zero.

A nonzero remainder is decisive: the tested expression is not a factor. Being close to zero is not sufficient when exact arithmetic is available.

If a polynomial contains an unknown coefficient, a given factor supplies an equation. Substitute the factor’s zero, set the resulting expression equal to zero, and solve for the coefficient. Then check your result.

Q45

Find \(k\) so that \(2x-3\) is a factor of \(P(x)=2x^3+kx^2-5x+3\).

Answer

\(k=-1\).

  1. The divisor is zero at \(x=\frac32\). Set \(P(\frac32)=0\).
  2. \(\frac{27}{4}+\frac94k-\frac{15}{2}+3=0\).
  3. This simplifies to \(\frac94(k+1)=0\), so \(k=-1\).

Choosing the correct substitution sign

The safest rule is to solve the divisor equal to zero. For \(x-a\), use \(a\); for \(x+a\), use \(-a\). This explains the sign rather than relying on a memorized switch.

For a divisor such as \(3x+2\), simply reversing the sign of 2 is not enough: solve \(3x+2=0\), giving \(-\frac23\). Keep this extra division in mind for nonmonic factors.

Write parentheses around the substituted negative number. Odd powers stay negative and even powers become positive. A sign mistake in the first step can turn a real factor into a false rejection.

\(8,\;-9,\;-\frac23\).

  1. Solve each divisor equal to zero: \(x=8\), \(x=-9\), and \(3x=-2\).

Q48

Find the remainder when \(P(x)=x^{3} - 2 x^{2} + 4 x + 5\) is divided by \(x + 3\).

Answer

\(-52\)

  1. Set the divisor equal to zero: \(x + 3=0\), so evaluate at \(x=-3\).
  2. Substitute into the entire polynomial: \(P\left(-3\right)=-52\).
  3. By the Remainder Theorem, this value is the remainder. It need not be zero.

Q49

A student tests \(P(4)\) to decide whether \(x+4\) is a factor of \(P(x)=x^2+3x-4\). Correct the test and decide.

Answer

Test \(P(-4)=0\); \(x+4\) is a factor.

  1. \(P(-4)=16-12-4=0\).
  2. The student tested the zero of \(x-4\), which is a different divisor.

Q50

Use the Factor Theorem to decide whether \(3 x + 2\) is a factor of \(P(x)=9 x^{2} + 3 x - 2\).

Answer

Yes. \(P\left(- \frac{2}{3}\right)=0\).

  1. Set the divisor equal to zero: \(3 x + 2=0\), so evaluate at \(x=- \frac{2}{3}\).
  2. Substitute into the entire polynomial: \(P\left(- \frac{2}{3}\right)=0\).
  3. The Factor Theorem says a linear divisor is a factor exactly when the remainder is zero. Here the remainder is zero.

Using a verified factor to finish a polynomial

A verified zero is a starting point, not the end of a factoring problem. Divide out its corresponding factor to lower the degree, then apply familiar quadratic or special-product methods to the quotient.

For synthetic division, list every coefficient in descending powers. A missing power requires a zero placeholder. Bring down the first coefficient, multiply by the test value, add to the next coefficient, and repeat.

Only a zero remainder supports a factorization without a remainder term. Keep the original linear factor attached to the factored quotient, and list every zero, including repeated ones when relevant.

Connecting expressions, factors, zeros, and graphs

Graph clarification: only real zeros produce x-intercepts. A graph may touch the axis at an even-multiplicity zero rather than cross it.

Expanded form, factored form, and a graph show different features of the same polynomial. Expanded form makes the leading term and y-intercept easy to identify; factored form reveals zeros and their multiplicities.

To build a polynomial from zeros, include a factor for each zero, with any required repeated power, and multiply by an unknown scale factor. A supplied point determines that scale factor. Zeros alone do not usually determine a unique polynomial.

For a sketch, label the real intercepts, calculate the y-intercept, identify crossing or touching, and use the leading term for the tails. In a word problem, also restrict the input to meaningful times, counts, or dimensions.

R1

A student factors \(x^2+8x+15\) as \((x+1)(x+15)\). Check the answer and give the correct factorization.

Answer

The correct answer is \((x+3)(x+5)\).

  2. Use 3 and 5 because their sum is 8 and product is 15.

R2

Factor completely over the integers: \(28 x^{3} y - 42 x^{2} y^{2}\).

Answer

\(14 x^{2} y \left(2 x - 3 y\right)\)

  1. Remove the GCF 14x squared y.
  2. The complete factorization is \(14 x^{2} y \left(2 x - 3 y\right)\).
  3. Expand the factors to check that they reproduce \(28 x^{3} y - 42 x^{2} y^{2}\).

R3

Factor completely over the integers: \(3 x^{2} + 10 x + 8\).

Answer

\(\left(x + 2\right) \left(3 x + 4\right)\)

  1. Use ac = 24 and split \(10x\) into \(6x+4x\), then group.
  2. The complete factorization is \(\left(x + 2\right) \left(3 x + 4\right)\).
  3. Expand the factors to check that they reproduce \(3 x^{2} + 10 x + 8\).

R4

Answer

\(\left(7 x - 10\right) \left(7 x + 10\right)\)

Homework word problems

Apply the ideas to dimensions, areas, profit, calibration, and motion. These eight problems build from choosing a factorization to combining several class topics. Together with the twelve review questions, they make 20 homework questions. Respect each situation’s domain and include units where appropriate.

W1

A rectangular display has area \(x^2+10x+21\) square meters and one side \(x+3\) meters, where \(x>0\). Factor the area to find the other side. Give both dimensions when \(x=2\).

Answer

The other side is \(x+7\) m. At \(x=2\), the dimensions are 5 m by 9 m.

  1. Factor \(x^2+10x+21=(x+3)(x+7)\).
  2. Divide by the given nonzero side, then substitute \(x=2\). The area is \(5\cdot9=45\) square meters.

W2

A square courtyard has side \(x\) meters. A square flower bed of side 6 meters is removed from its usable area. Factor the remaining area and find it when \(x=10\), assuming \(x>6\).

Answer

\(A(x)=x^2-36=(x-6)(x+6)\). At \(x=10\), the usable area is 64 square meters.

  1. Subtract the flower bed’s area \(6^2=36\) from the courtyard’s \(x^2\).
  2. Apply the difference of squares, then calculate \(4\cdot16=64\).

W3

A square art panel has area \(9x^2+30x+25\) square centimeters, where \(x\ge0\). Factor the expression to find the panel’s side length. What is its perimeter when \(x=3\)?

Answer

The side is \(3x+5\) cm, and the perimeter is 56 cm at \(x=3\).

  1. The area is \((3x+5)^2\). Since \(x\ge0\), \(3x+5\) is positive and is the physical side length.
  2. At \(x=3\), the side is \(14\) cm, so the perimeter is \(4(14)=56\) cm.

W4

A fundraiser models profit by \(P(n)=-n^2+18n-65\) dollars when \(n\) bundles are sold, where \(n\) is a whole number from 0 to 18. Find both break-even sales levels and the whole-number sales levels with positive profit.

Answer

Break-even occurs at 5 and 13 bundles. Profit is positive for 6 through 12 bundles.

  1. Factor \(P(n)=-(n-5)(n-13)\). Set each factor equal to zero for break-even.
  2. Between the zeros, the two factors have opposite signs. The leading negative makes profit positive. Whole-number counts give 6–12.

W5

A calibration model is \(E(s)=s^3-5s^2+2s+8\), where \(0\le s\le5\). A technician uses division by \(s-2\) to test whether setting 2 has zero error. Find the remainder, then factor the model to find every allowed zero-error setting.

Answer

The remainder is 0. The allowed settings are 2 and 4.

  1. \(E(2)=8-20+4+8=0\), so \(s-2\) is a factor.
  2. Division gives \(s^2-3s-4=(s-4)(s+1)\). Thus the mathematical zeros are \(2,4,-1\).
  3. Reject \(-1\) because it is outside the device’s permitted settings.

W6

A rectangular storage box has positive inside dimensions \(x\), \(x+2\), and \(x+4\) centimeters and volume 105 cubic centimeters. Form a polynomial equation, verify that \(x=3\) is a solution, and show why it is the only real solution.

Answer

\(x^3+6x^2+8x-105=0\). The dimensions are 3 cm by 5 cm by 7 cm.

  1. Use \(x(x+2)(x+4)=105\). At \(x=3\), \(3\cdot5\cdot7=105\).
  2. The volume-difference polynomial factors as \((x-3)(x^2+9x+35)\).
  3. The quadratic discriminant is \(81-140=-59<0\), so it contributes no real roots.

W7

Over \(0\le t\le6\) seconds, a moving marker’s signed displacement from a reference line is \(d(t)=t^3-7t^2+11t-5\) centimeters. Verify that \(t=1\) is a zero, then find all times it meets the line. Decide whether it crosses or only touches at each time, and find \(d(3)\).

Answer

It touches at 1 second and crosses at 5 seconds. At 3 seconds, it is 8 cm below the line.

  1. \(d(1)=1-7+11-5=0\). Divide by \(t-1\) to get \(t^2-6t+5=(t-1)(t-5)\).
  2. Thus \(d(t)=(t-1)^2(t-5)\). The double zero at 1 touches, and the simple zero at 5 crosses.
  3. \(d(3)=(2)^2(-2)=-8\). A negative displacement means below the reference line, not necessarily below the ground.

W8

A designer wants a least-degree polynomial profile that crosses a reference line at \(x=-2\) and \(x=5\), touches it at \(x=1\), and passes through \((0,20)\). Negative horizontal coordinates are allowed. Find its factored equation, its y-intercept, and its end behavior.

Answer

\(f(x)=-2(x+2)(x-1)^2(x-5)\). The y-intercept is \((0,20)\), and both mathematical ends fall.

  1. Use multiplicity 1 at each crossing and 2 at the touch: \(f(x)=a(x+2)(x-1)^2(x-5)\).
  2. Substitute \(x=0\): \(20=a(2)(1)(-5)=-10a\), so \(a=-2\).
  3. The degree is 4 with negative leading coefficient. Both ends fall, although the physical design uses only a finite part of the graph.

Quick questions and answers

What does factoring a polynomial mean?

Factoring rewrites a polynomial as a product of simpler expressions whose expansion reproduces the original polynomial.

What should I check first when factoring?

Look for a greatest common factor. After removing it, inspect the remaining expression for trinomial, difference-of-squares, perfect-square, or grouping patterns.

What is the difference between the Remainder and Factor Theorems?

The Remainder Theorem gives the remainder P(a) for division by x−a. The Factor Theorem says x−a is a factor exactly when that remainder is zero.

What value do I use for a divisor with a plus sign?

Solve the divisor equal to zero. For x+a, substitute −a. For a nonmonic divisor such as 3x+2, substitute −2/3.

Does every zero make a graph cross the x-axis?

No. Real zeros with odd multiplicity cross; real zeros with even multiplicity touch and turn. Nonreal zeros are not x-intercepts on a real graph.
