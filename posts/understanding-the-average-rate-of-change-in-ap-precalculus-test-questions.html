What Is Average Rate of Change?

The average rate of change measures how much a function changes between two points.

m = f(b) - f(a)/b - a

This formula represents the slope of the secant line between two points on a graph.

How to Solve Average Rate of Change Problems

Let’s go step by step:

  1. Identify the function ( f(x) )
  2. Choose the interval ([a, b])
  3. Compute ( f(a) ) and ( f(b) )
  4. Apply the formula

Example

Find the average rate of change of f(x) = x^2 from ( x = 1 ) to ( x = 4 )

Step 1: f(4) = 16 and f(1) = 1

Step 2: 16 - 1 / 4 - 1 = 5

Final Answer: 5

Graph Interpretation

The average rate of change is the slope of a line connecting two points on a curve.

This is called a secant line, and it shows how the function behaves over an interval — not just at a single point.

Why This Matters for Pre Calculus Exams

You’ll commonly see questions like:

  • Find the average rate of change from a table
  • Interpret a graph and estimate slope
  • Compare rates of change between functions
  • Word problems (velocity, cost, growth)

This topic is heavily tested in:

  • AP Precalculus exams
  • Regents exams
  • SHSAT advanced math sections

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Mixing up ( f(a) ) and ( f(b) )
  • Subtracting in the wrong order
  • Forgetting units in word problems
  • Confusing average rate of change with instantaneous rate

Pro Tips from School of Math New York

  • Always write the formula first
  • Label your values clearly
  • Double-check subtraction signs
  • Practice with graphs, tables, and equations
