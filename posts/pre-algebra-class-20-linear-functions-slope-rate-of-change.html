Understanding linear functions

A linear function is a function whose output changes by the same amount every time the input increases by the same amount. In the poster, every extra minute of walking adds 60 meters: 0, 60, 120, 180. Those equal jumps are the signature of a linear function, and the size of the jump per one unit of input is the rate of change, here 60 meters per minute.

Because the walker starts at 0 meters and adds 60 meters per minute, the rule is \(d = 60t\), where \(t\) is time in minutes and \(d\) is distance in meters. A rule lets you jump to any input without building the whole table: the “your turn” question asks for 5 minutes, and \(d = 60(5) = 300\) meters.

To test a table for linearity, first make sure the inputs go up in equal steps (or account for unequal steps, as in the next topic), then look at the changes in the output. Equal output changes mean linear; changes that grow, shrink or switch direction mean not linear. A decreasing quantity can also be linear, as long as it decreases by the same amount each step.

A printer prints 25 pages each minute. Make a table for 0, 1, 2, 3 and 4 minutes, state the rate of change, and write a rule for the number of pages \(p\) after \(t\) minutes.

Answer

Pages: 0, 25, 50, 75, 100; rate 25 pages per minute; \(p = 25t\)

  1. Start at 0 pages at 0 minutes and add 25 for each minute: 0, 25, 50, 75, 100.

  3. Multiply the time by the rate: \(p = 25t\). Check: \(t = 4\) gives \(25(4) = 100\) pages, matching the table.

Rate of change from tables

When the inputs in a table do not go up by 1, you cannot read the rate of change from a single jump. Instead divide: rate of change = change in output ÷ change in input. In the poster the hours go up by 2 and the earnings go up by 30, so the rate is \(30 \div 2 = 15\) dollars per hour. Using rows 4 and 2 hours gives the same \((60 - 30) \div (4 - 2) = 15\).

For a linear function, every pair of rows gives the same rate, so it is wise to check a second interval. The poster's check \(90 \div 6 = 15\) works because the table starts at (0, 0); when a table does not start at 0, dividing a single output by its input is a mistake, and you must use the changes.

For the “your turn” table, the hours go 1, 3, 5 (up by 2) and the earnings go 12, 36, 60 (up by 24), so the rate is \(24 \div 2 = 12\) dollars per hour. If two intervals give different rates, the table is not linear.

Slope: rise over run

On a graph, the rate of change of a line is called its slope, written \(m\). Pick two points on the line and move from the left point to the right point. The run is the horizontal change and the rise is the vertical change. Then \(m = \dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}\).

In the poster, moving from A(1, 1) to B(4, 4) means running 3 to the right and rising 3, so \(m = \dfrac{3}{3} = 1\): for every 1 unit right, the line rises 1 unit. Any two points on the same line give the same slope, so choose points where the line crosses grid corners exactly.

Rise can be negative. If the line goes down as you move right, the rise is negative and so is the slope. The “your turn” line rises 4 and runs 2, so \(m = \dfrac{4}{2} = 2\). Always simplify the fraction; a slope like \(\dfrac{2}{4}\) is written \(\dfrac{1}{2}\).

Q13

A line rises 5 units for every 10 units of run. What is its slope? How much does it rise over a run of 4 units?

Answer

\(m = \dfrac{1}{2}\); it rises 2 units

  1. \(m = \dfrac{5}{10} = \dfrac{1}{2}\).

  2. Rise = slope × run, so over a run of 4 the rise is \(\dfrac{1}{2} \times 4 = 2\) units.

  3. Check: \(\dfrac{2}{4}\) also simplifies to \(\dfrac{1}{2}\).

Q14

\(m = -1\)

  1. Move from the left point (1, 5) to the right point (4, 2).

  2. Run: \(4 - 1 = 3\) to the right. Rise: the line goes down from 5 to 2, so the rise is \(-3\).

Q15

A wheelchair ramp rises 2 feet over a horizontal distance of 24 feet. What is the slope of the ramp? How many feet of run are needed for each 1 foot of rise?

Answer

\(m = \dfrac{1}{12}\); 12 feet of run per foot of rise

  1. \(m = \dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} = \dfrac{2}{24}\).

  2. Simplify by dividing top and bottom by 2: \(\dfrac{1}{12}\).

  3. A slope of \(\dfrac{1}{12}\) means 1 foot up for every 12 feet across, so 12 feet of run are needed per foot of rise.

Slope between two points

When you know two points but have no graph, use the slope formula \(m = \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}\). The top is the change in \(y\) (the rise) and the bottom is the change in \(x\) (the run). Label one point \((x_1, y_1)\) and the other \((x_2, y_2)\) first, then substitute.

In the poster, A(2, 3) and B(6, 11) give \(m = \dfrac{11 - 3}{6 - 2} = \dfrac{8}{4} = 2\). Starting from B instead gives \(\dfrac{3 - 11}{2 - 6} = \dfrac{-8}{-4} = 2\), the same slope. The rule is to keep the same point order on top and bottom; mixing the order flips the sign and gives a wrong answer.

Watch the signs when coordinates are negative: subtracting a negative means adding. For the “your turn” points (1, 4) and (5, 12), \(m = \dfrac{12 - 4}{5 - 1} = \dfrac{8}{4} = 2\).

\(m = -2\)

  2. Change in \(x\): \(4 - (-2) = 4 + 2 = 6\).

  3. \(m = \dfrac{-12}{6} = -2\). The line falls 2 units for each unit to the right.

Q18

Find the slope of the line through (0, −3) and (6, 1).

Answer

\(m = \dfrac{2}{3}\)

  1. Change in \(y\): \(1 - (-3) = 1 + 3 = 4\).

  2. Change in \(x\): \(6 - 0 = 6\).

  3. \(m = \dfrac{4}{6} = \dfrac{2}{3}\).

Q19

For the points (5, 9) and (1, 1), a student wrote \(m = \dfrac{9 - 1}{1 - 5} = -2\). Find the mistake and give the correct slope.

Answer

The point order was mixed; \(m = 2\)

  1. The numerator \(9 - 1\) starts with the point (5, 9), but the denominator \(1 - 5\) starts with the point (1, 1). The order was switched.

  2. Keep the same order: \(m = \dfrac{9 - 1}{5 - 1} = \dfrac{8}{4} = 2\).

  3. Check with the other order: \(\dfrac{1 - 9}{1 - 5} = \dfrac{-8}{-4} = 2\). Both give 2.

Q20

Find the slope of the line through (−3, 4) and (5, 4). What kind of line is it?

Answer

\(m = 0\); a horizontal line

  1. Change in \(y\): \(4 - 4 = 0\). Change in \(x\): \(5 - (-3) = 8\).

  2. \(m = \dfrac{0}{8} = 0\).

  3. Both points have \(y = 4\), so the line is the horizontal line \(y = 4\). It has no rise at all.

Types of slope

Read every line from left to right. A line that goes up has a positive slope (\(m \gt 0\)): as \(x\) increases, \(y\) increases. A line that goes down has a negative slope (\(m \lt 0\)): as \(x\) increases, \(y\) decreases.

A horizontal line such as \(y = 2\) has rise 0 for any run, so \(m = \dfrac{0}{\text{run}} = 0\): zero slope. A vertical line such as \(x = 2\) has run 0, and division by zero is not defined, so its slope is undefined. Zero slope and undefined slope are different: “zero” is a number, “undefined” means no number works.

A vertical line fails the vertical-line test, because one input (like \(x = 2\)) has infinitely many outputs, so it is not a function of \(x\). For the “your turn” list: \(y = 3x\) is positive, \(y = -2x\) is negative, \(y = 4\) is zero and \(x = 4\) is undefined. In an equation of the form \(y = mx\) or \(y = mx + b\), the number multiplying \(x\) is the slope.

Classify the slope of each line as positive, negative, zero or undefined: (a) a line that goes up from left to right (b) a line that goes down from left to right (c) a horizontal line (d) a vertical line

Q25

Which of the lines \(y = 7\) and \(x = 7\) is a function of \(x\)? Explain with slope and the vertical-line test.

Answer

\(y = 7\) is a function; \(x = 7\) is not

  1. \(y = 7\) is horizontal with zero slope. Every vertical line crosses it exactly once, so each input has one output (7): it is a function.

  2. \(x = 7\) is vertical with undefined slope. The vertical line at \(x = 7\) lies on top of it, so the input 7 has infinitely many outputs.

  3. Therefore \(x = 7\) fails the vertical-line test and is not a function of \(x\).

Graphing lines using slope

A slope is a set of directions: the top tells how far to move up (or down, if negative) and the bottom tells how far to move right. To graph a line from a starting point and a slope, plot the start, apply rise and run, plot the new point, and repeat; then join the points with a straight line and add arrows.

In the poster, the line starts at (0, 1) with \(m = \dfrac{2}{1}\): up 2, right 1 each time, giving (1, 3), (2, 5), (3, 7). Every move uses the same slope, which is why the points line up. The line is \(y = 2x + 1\); the starting value 1 is where the line crosses the \(y\)-axis.

For a fractional slope, use the denominator as the run so you land on grid corners. The “your turn” line starts at (0, 2) with slope \(\dfrac{3}{2}\): right 2 and up 3 lands on (2, 5). A negative slope such as \(-\dfrac{3}{4}\) means down 3 for every 4 right (or, equivalently, up 3 for every 4 left).

Interpreting slope and its units

In a real situation, slope is not just a number: it carries units, and the units are always output units per input unit. For the cyclist in the poster, time (hours) is the input and distance (miles) is the output, so \(m = \dfrac{24 - 12}{2 - 1} = 12\) miles per hour.

To interpret a slope, write a sentence of the form “for each additional input unit, the output increases (or decreases) by slope output units.” A slope of 18 dollars per hour means each extra hour adds $18; a slope of \(-3\) liters per minute means the tank loses 3 liters every minute. The sign tells the direction; the units tell what is changing and against what.

The “your turn” candle has slope \(-2\) centimeters per hour: each hour it burns, the candle gets 2 cm shorter. Slope also lets you predict: change in output = slope × change in input.

Applying and comparing rates

Rates can be given in different forms: a table, an equation, a graph or a sentence. To compare them, find each slope, write both in the same units, then compare. From a table or graph, compute change in output ÷ change in input; in an equation like \(d = 15t\), the coefficient of the input variable is the rate.

In the poster, Cyclist A's table gives \(\dfrac{24 - 0}{2 - 0} = 12\) miles per hour and Cyclist B's equation gives 15 miles per hour, so B is \(15 - 12 = 3\) miles per hour faster. Over 3 hours the gap grows to \(3 \times 3 = 9\) miles (45 versus 36).

If the units do not match (miles per minute versus miles per hour, or meters versus centimeters), convert first. For the “your turn” walkers, Walker A goes \(8 \div 2 = 4\) miles per hour and Walker B goes 3 miles per hour, so A is faster by 1 mile per hour.

Train A, by 20 miles per hour

  1. Convert Train A to miles per hour: 1 hour = 60 minutes, so \(2 \times 60 = 120\) miles per hour.

  2. Train B: 100 miles per hour.

  3. \(120 - 100 = 20\), so Train A is faster by 20 miles per hour. Comparing “2” with “100” directly would have been misleading.

  4. The lines cross at (4, 60): after 4 weeks each has $60. Check: \(15(4) = 60\) and \(20 + 10(4) = 60\).

W1

A taxi charges $3 to start plus $2 per mile. Make a table of the total cost for 1, 2, 3 and 4 miles. What is the rate of change, and what does a 10-mile ride cost?

Answer

Costs $5, $7, $9, $11; rate $2 per mile; $23

  1. Cost = 3 + 2 × miles: 1 mile → $5, 2 → $7, 3 → $9, 4 → $11.

  3. 10 miles: \(3 + 2(10) = 23\) dollars.

A candle 24 cm tall burns down 1.5 cm per hour. What is the slope of height versus time? How tall is it after 6 hours, and when does it burn out?

Answer

Slope −1.5 cm per hour; 15 cm; after 16 hours

  1. The height decreases, so the slope is \(-1.5\) centimeters per hour.

  2. After 6 hours: \(24 - 1.5(6) = 24 - 9 = 15\) cm.

  3. Burn out when the whole 24 cm is gone: \(24 \div 1.5 = 16\) hours. Check: \(1.5 \times 16 = 24\).

Answer

3 °F per hour; 57 °F at 10 a.m.

  1. From 7 a.m. to 1 p.m. is 6 hours. The temperature changes \(66 - 48 = 18\) °F.

  2. Rate \(= 18 \div 6 = 3\) degrees Fahrenheit per hour.

  3. 10 a.m. is 3 hours after 7 a.m.: \(48 + 3(3) = 57\) °F.

  1. \(m = \dfrac{2600 - 1400}{5 - 2} = \dfrac{1200}{3} = 400\) feet per hour.

  2. Work back 2 hours from 1,400 feet: \(1400 - 2(400) = 600\) feet at time 0.

  3. Check at 5 hours: \(600 + 400(5) = 2600\). Correct.

Quick questions and answers

What is a linear function?

A linear function is a function with a constant rate of change: every time the input increases by the same amount, the output changes by the same amount. Its graph is a straight line.

How do I find the rate of change from a table?

Pick two rows and divide the change in output by the change in input. For a linear table every pair of rows gives the same answer, so check a second interval. Do not divide a single output by its input unless the table starts at (0, 0).

What does slope mean?

Slope is the rate of change of a line: rise over run, the vertical change divided by the horizontal change between two points, read from left to right. A slope of 2 means the line rises 2 units for every 1 unit to the right.

What is the slope formula?

For points (x1, y1) and (x2, y2), m = (y2 − y1) ÷ (x2 − x1). Keep the same point order in the numerator and denominator; for (2, 3) and (6, 11), m = 8 ÷ 4 = 2.

What is the difference between zero slope and undefined slope?

A horizontal line has zero slope because its rise is 0. A vertical line has undefined slope because its run is 0 and division by zero is not defined. A vertical line is not a function of x.

What are the units of slope?

Slope units are output units per input unit, such as miles per hour, dollars per hour or liters per minute. A slope of −3 liters per minute means the amount decreases by 3 liters each minute.
