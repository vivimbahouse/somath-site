Recognize radical equations and restrictions

A radical equation has a variable inside a radical. For real square roots, the radicand must be nonnegative, and the principal square-root value is nonnegative. These are two different restrictions to track.

Our workflow is: state restrictions, isolate a radical, raise both sides to the appropriate power, solve for candidates, and check each candidate in the original equation. Do not assume every root of the squared equation is valid.

Practice: try these five questions

Q1

Which equation is a radical equation? Explain the role of the variable.

\[\sqrt{x+8}=6,\qquad x+\sqrt{8}=6\]
Answer

Only \(\sqrt{x+8}=6\) is a radical equation.

  1. In the first equation, the unknown x appears under a square-root sign.

  2. In the second, only the fixed number 8 is under the radical. The equation is linear in x.

Check in the original equation

A constant radical in a coefficient or constant term does not by itself make an equation radical in the unknown.

Q2

State the real-domain restriction. Also state the additional sign restriction imposed by the equation.

\[\sqrt{3x-6}=x-4\]
Answer

\(x\ge4\) is necessary for any solution.

  1. The radicand must be nonnegative.

    \[3x-6\ge0\quad\Rightarrow\quad x\ge2\]

  2. The principal square root is nonnegative, so the opposite side must be nonnegative.

    \[x-4\ge0\quad\Rightarrow\quad x\ge4\]

  3. Intersect the restrictions: keep values meeting both.

    \[x\ge4\]

Check in the original equation

These are necessary restrictions, not a claim that every x ≥ 4 solves the equation. A solution must also satisfy equality.

Q4

Why is a candidate obtained by squaring not automatically a solution? Use 4 and −4 as an example.

Answer

Squaring erases sign information.

  1. The two numbers are unequal.

    \[4\ne-4\]

  2. Their squares are equal.

    \[4^2=(-4)^2=16\]

  3. Thus a value may satisfy a squared equation while failing the original sign-sensitive equation. Substitute each candidate back into the original.

Check in the original equation

For example, squaring \(\sqrt{x}=-4\) gives x = 16, but the original becomes 4 = −4, which is false.

Basic square-root equations

When the isolated square root equals a nonnegative constant, squaring removes the radical: \(\sqrt{A}=b\) implies \(A=b^2\). Solve the remaining equation and check the result.

Do not attach a plus-or-minus sign to a principal square root. The equation \(\sqrt{x}=7\) has \(x=49\), not \(x=\pm49\). The plus-or-minus belongs to solving an equation such as \(x^2=49\).

Isolate the radical before squaring

Undo operations outside the radical first. Subtract or add outside constants, then divide by the radical’s coefficient. This prevents unnecessary binomial squares.

Keep the signs when dividing by a negative coefficient. After isolation, a square root equaling a negative number proves no real solution immediately. Squaring such an equation can still produce a misleading candidate.

A square root equal to a variable expression

When the other side contains x, squaring often creates a quadratic equation. Expand the whole binomial: \((x-c)^2=x^2-2cx+c^2\). Omitting the middle term changes the equation.

A quadratic may produce two candidates, one candidate, or none. Some equations have two valid solutions; others have an extraneous root. Only the original equation decides which candidates survive.

Understand extraneous solutions

An extraneous candidate solves a transformed equation but not the original one. Squaring is a common source because positive and negative quantities can have the same square.

A candidate can fail because a radicand is negative, because an isolated square root would need to be negative, or because substitution simply gives unequal sides. Report valid solutions separately from rejected candidates.

When radicals appear on both sides, isolate one radical, square the entire other side, then isolate the remaining radical and square again if necessary. The middle term of a binomial square still matters.

Quick questions and answers

What is an extraneous solution?

It is a candidate that satisfies a transformed equation but not the original equation. Squaring can introduce such candidates because unequal opposite numbers have equal squares.

Why isolate a radical first?

Isolating avoids unnecessary binomial expansions and makes the square-root sign restriction visible. Undo operations outside the radical before squaring.

Does a square root have a plus-or-minus sign?

The principal square root is nonnegative. Solving x² = 25 produces two values of x, but the expression √25 itself equals 5, not plus or minus 5.

Can a negative value of x be valid?

Yes. Check the radicand and both original sides. A negative x can still make a nonnegative radicand and satisfy the equation.

How do I solve equations with two radicals?

State both radicand restrictions, isolate one radical, square carefully with the middle term, then isolate and square again if needed. Check all candidates in the original equation.

Can a cube root be negative?

Yes. The real cube root of a negative number is negative. Cubing is one-to-one over the reals, unlike squaring, so it does not lose sign information.
