Two-Digit Addition Without Regrouping

To add two-digit numbers without regrouping, add the ones to the ones and the tens to the tens. Each column in this lesson totals less than ten, so you do not need to carry. Blocks, expanded form, vertical columns, and number-line jumps all show the same place-value idea.

Tens and ones: read each place

A two-digit number has a tens place on the left and a ones place on the right. One ten is a group of ten ones. The position of a digit tells you its value, so a 4 in the tens place means 40, not 4.

Answer

  1. The left-hand digit is 1. It represents one ten, or 10.

Tens and ones: break a number apart

Writing a number as a tens part plus a ones part is called expanded form. Keep the value of each part: seven tens means 70. A plus sign shows that the parts join to make the whole number.

Join numbers with models

Model each addend, which is a number being added. Slide the tens rods together and collect the ones cubes together. When the ones total is less than ten and the tens total is less than ten, you can read the answer without trading any pieces.

Comparison: what regrouping would mean

Comparison only. This supplied poster shows addition with regrouping. It is a comparison, not the method being practiced in Class 13. Our questions below do not require carrying. The Little Newtons syllabus studies two-digit addition with regrouping later, in Class 17.

Regrouping means trading ten ones for one ten. To decide whether a trade is needed, check the ones before you begin. If their sum is less than ten, keep them as ones; do not add an extra ten. In this class, totals also stay below 100, so no group of ten tens needs trading for a hundred.

Homework review

This homework reviews tens and ones, expanded form, place-value models, vertical addition, and number-line jumps. Use these ideas to add two-digit numbers without regrouping and explain your thinking.

R1 Review image 1 of 11

In 57, what is the value of the 5? Explain using tens and ones.

Answer

The 5 is worth 50.

  1. The 5 is in the tens place, so it represents 5 tens.
  2. Five tens are 50; the 7 represents 7 ones. Therefore 57 = 50 + 7.

R2 Review image 2 of 11

Write 86 in expanded form. Explain why 8 + 6 is not the correct expanded form.

Answer

86 = 80 + 6.

  1. The 8 means 8 tens, worth 80. The 6 means 6 ones.
  2. Adding 8 + 6 gives 14 because it treats the tens digit as only 8 ones.

R3 Review image 3 of 11

Draw a model for 48 using rods for tens and dots for ones. How many rods and dots do you need?

Answer

4 tens rods and 8 ones dots.

  1. Each rod represents 10. Four rods represent 40.
  2. Draw eight separate dots for the remaining 8 ones: 40 + 8 = 48.

R4 Review image 4 of 11

Two models show 14 and 22. Join them. How many tens and ones are there altogether, and what is the total?

Answer

3 tens and 6 ones; 36 altogether.

  1. The first model has 1 ten and 4 ones. The second has 2 tens and 2 ones.
  2. Add the ones: 4 + 2 = 6. No new ten is needed.
  3. Add the tens: 1 + 2 = 3 tens, worth 30.
  4. Combine the parts: 30 + 6 = 36.

R5 Review image 5 of 11

Find 33 + 16 by adding the tens values and ones values separately.

49.

  3. Add the ones values: 3 + 6 = 9. Then 40 + 9 = 49.

Quick questions and answers

How do you add two-digit numbers without regrouping?

Add ones to ones and tens to tens. For the problems in this lesson, each column totals less than ten, so no carrying is needed. You can show the same addition with blocks, expanded form, columns, or number-line jumps.

What does without regrouping mean?

It means you do not need to trade ten ones for a ten or ten tens for a hundred. All addition practice here has a ones total below ten and an overall total below one hundred.

Do you always have to add the ones first?

Start with the ones when using the standard vertical method. When adding separate tens and ones values or making number-line jumps, you may add the tens first and then the ones. Keep the place values correct either way.

Why is there a regrouping image in this class?

One of the supplied images shows carrying as a contrast. It is clearly labeled as a comparison. The questions beneath it ask students to recognize when carrying is not needed; the class does not require solving regrouping problems.

How many practice questions are included?

Little Newtons Class 13 includes eleven images in order, five original questions after each image, eleven homework review questions (one per image), and nine homework word problems in increasing difficulty. There are seventy-five questions, each with a hidden worked answer.

How can a parent help without giving away the answer?

Ask the child to name the tens and ones, draw a model, or explain each number-line jump. Read a word problem aloud if needed, but let the child choose the operation and try a solution before opening the Answer button.
