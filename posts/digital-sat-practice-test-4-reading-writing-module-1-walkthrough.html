Theory refresher · What R&W Module 1 tests

The Digital SAT Reading & Writing section has two 32-minute modules, each with 27 scored questions plus embedded pretest questions (33 total questions appear in this module). Module 1 is not adaptive — every student sees the same items. Every question is a short, self-contained passage (roughly 25–150 words) followed by one question and four answer choices — there are no multi-question passage sets. The four content domains are:

1 · Craft & Structure (roughly 13–15% of the section)

Words in Context. Pick the word or phrase that most precisely fits the logic of the sentence. Watch for signal words like “although,” “however,” and “therefore” that tell you whether the blank should agree with or contrast the surrounding idea.

Text Structure & Purpose. Identify how a text (or a single sentence within it) is organized — e.g., does it move from description to reflection, from criticism to ambition, from a claim to a supporting example?

Cross-Text Connections. Two short texts are given on the same topic. You must determine how the author/idea of Text 2 would most likely respond to, or relate to, a claim made in Text 1.

2 · Information & Ideas (roughly 26–28% of the section)

Central Ideas & Details. Identify the main point of a text as a whole — not a detail, not a partial truth, but the idea every sentence in the passage supports.

Command of Evidence (Textual). Choose the quotation (or piece of the text) that best supports, illustrates, or exemplifies a given claim.

Command of Evidence (Quantitative). Use a table or graph to complete a statement logically — the correct choice must be numerically accurate and serve the logical role the sentence needs (e.g., completing a pattern or contrast).

Inferences. Draw the most logically supported conclusion from the given information — usually phrased as which finding would most support a hypothesis, or what a passage most strongly suggests.

3 · Standard English Conventions (roughly 11–15% of the section)

Boundaries. Punctuation between and within sentences — periods, semicolons, commas, colons, and dashes. A period or semicolon joins two independent clauses; a comma alone between two independent clauses is a comma splice (incorrect); no punctuation at all between two independent clauses is a run-on (incorrect).

Form, Structure & Sense. Grammar and usage — subject-verb agreement, verb tense consistency, pronoun-antecedent agreement, plural vs. possessive nouns, and modifier placement (a modifying phrase must sit next to the noun it logically describes, or you get a dangling modifier).

4 · Expression of Ideas (roughly 12–19% of the section)

Transitions. Choose the logical connector (however, therefore, in addition, finally, etc.) that correctly signals the relationship between two ideas — contrast, cause-and-effect, addition, or sequence/conclusion.

Rhetorical Synthesis. You are given bulleted notes and a specific rhetorical goal (e.g., “describe X to an audience unfamiliar with Y” or “emphasize a difference between X and Y”). Pick the single sentence that best accomplishes that exact goal using only the given notes — accuracy alone isn’t enough; the sentence must match the stated purpose.

Question 1 · Words in Context

The spacecraft OSIRIS-REx briefly made contact with the asteroid 101955 Bennu in 2020. NASA scientist Daniella DellaGiustina reports that despite facing the unexpected obstacle of a surface mostly covered in boulders, OSIRIS-REx successfully _______ a sample of the surface, gathering pieces of it to bring back to Earth.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?

  • A) attached
  • B) collected
  • C) followed
  • D) replaced

Answer: B) collected

One-line summary: OSIRIS-REx gathered and took away pieces of the asteroid, which is exactly what “collected” means.

✓ B) collected
“Collected” means acquired and took away. The text indicates that despite the unforeseen obstacle of boulders on the surface, OSIRIS-REx was able to gather a sample to return to Earth — it successfully collected a sample of 101955 Bennu.

✗ A) attached
“Attached” means connected or affixed. The text says OSIRIS-REx gathered pieces of the asteroid to bring to Earth; it doesn’t suggest the spacecraft attached anything to the asteroid.

✗ C) followed
“Followed” means tracked or traveled behind. The text describes a brief encounter during which the spacecraft gathered a sample — it doesn’t suggest the spacecraft tracked the sample, and it’s unclear what it would even mean to travel behind a sample it collected.

✗ D) replaced
“Replaced” means put back or returned. The text indicates OSIRIS-REx gathered pieces to bring to Earth, not that anything was returned to the asteroid.

Question 2 · Words in Context

Research conducted by planetary scientist Katarina Miljkovic suggests that the Moon’s surface may not accurately _______ early impact events. When the Moon was still forming, its surface was softer, and asteroid or meteoroid impacts would have left less of an impression; thus, evidence of early impacts may no longer be present.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?

  • A) reflect
  • B) receive
  • C) evaluate
  • D) mimic

Answer: A) reflect

One-line summary: The Moon's surface may not accurately “show” signs of early impacts, since softer early terrain would have preserved less evidence.

✓ A) reflect
In this context “reflect” means show or make apparent. The text states that because the Moon’s surface was softer when it was still forming, early impacts “would have left less of an impression,” so evidence may no longer exist — the surface may not accurately show signs of early impact events.

✗ B) receive
It wouldn’t make sense to say the surface may not accurately “receive” (acquire/experience) early impacts — the impacts have already occurred, and a surface can’t be accurate or inaccurate in experiencing them.

✗ C) evaluate
“Evaluate” means determine the significance or condition of something, which would require the Moon’s surface to make a decision — that doesn’t make sense here.

✗ D) mimic
“Mimic” means to deliberately simulate or imitate. It wouldn’t make sense to say the surface may not accurately “deliberately imitate” early impacts.

Question 3 · Words in Context

Handedness, a preferential use of either the right or left hand, typically is easy to observe in humans. Because this trait is present but less _______ in many other animals, animal-behavior researchers often employ tasks specially designed to reveal individual animals’ preferences for a certain hand or paw.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?

  • A) recognizable
  • B) intriguing
  • C) significant
  • D) useful

Answer: A) recognizable

One-line summary: Handedness is easy to observe (recognize) in humans but less apparent in other animals — hence special tasks are needed.

✓ A) recognizable
“Recognizable” means apparent or identifiable. The text says handedness is “easy to observe in humans,” but researchers need special tasks to determine handedness in other animals. Combined with “less” before the blank, this indicates handedness in other animals is less recognizable than in humans.

✗ B) intriguing
Nothing in the text suggests handedness is less fascinating in nonhuman animals — the text focuses on how easy it is to observe, not on how interesting it is.

✗ C) significant
Nothing in the text suggests handedness is less important or meaningful in animals — again, the contrast is about ease of observation, not importance.

✗ D) useful
“Useful” doesn’t fit — the text is about how easily researchers can determine handedness, not about how functional or helpful it is.

Question 4 · Words in Context

It is by no means _______ to recognize the influence of Dutch painter Hieronymus Bosch on Ali Banisadr’s paintings; indeed, Banisadr himself cites Bosch as an inspiration. However, some scholars have suggested that the ancient Mesopotamian poem Epic of Gilgamesh may have had a far greater impact on Banisadr’s work.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?

  • A) substantial
  • B) satisfying
  • C) unimportant
  • D) appropriate

Answer: C) unimportant

One-line summary: “By no means” signals a contradiction that’s resolved by “indeed” — Bosch's influence is significant, so recognizing it is by no means unimportant (trivial).

✓ C) unimportant
“Unimportant” means trivial or lacking value. “It is by no means” signals the blank word is contradicted by later information; “indeed” introduces that contradiction — Banisadr himself cites Bosch as an inspiration. So Bosch’s influence is significant, meaning recognizing it is by no means unimportant.

✗ A) substantial
It wouldn’t make sense to say recognizing Bosch’s influence isn’t “substantial” (meaningful) — the text states Banisadr himself cites Bosch as an influence, so it is substantial.

✗ B) satisfying
It wouldn’t make sense to say it isn’t “satisfying” (pleasing) to recognize Bosch’s influence — nothing in the text relates to pleasure.

✗ D) appropriate
It wouldn’t make sense to say recognizing Bosch’s influence isn’t “appropriate” (suitable) — the text indicates Banisadr himself notes the effect Bosch has had on him.

Question 5 · Text Structure & Purpose

The following text is adapted from Susan Glaspell’s 1912 short story “‘Out There.’” An elderly shop owner is looking at a picture that he recently acquired and hopes to sell.

It did seem that the picture failed to fit in with the rest of the shop. A persuasive young fellow who claimed he was closing out his stock let the old man have it for what he called a song. It was only a little out-of-the-way store which subsisted chiefly on the framing of pictures. The old man looked around at his views of the city, his pictures of cats and dogs, his flaming bits of landscape. “Don’t belong in here,” he fumed.

And yet the old man was secretly proud of his acquisition. There was a hidden dignity in his scowling as he shuffled about pondering the least ridiculous place for the picture.

Which choice best states the main purpose of the text?

  • A) To reveal the shop owner’s conflicted feelings about the new picture
  • B) To convey the shop owner’s resentment of the person he got the new picture from
  • C) To describe the items that the shop owner most highly prizes
  • D) To explain differences between the new picture and other pictures in the shop

Answer: A) To reveal the shop owner's conflicted feelings about the new picture

One-line summary: The shop owner is furious that the picture doesn't fit in, yet secretly proud of owning it — the text's purpose is to reveal that inner conflict.

✓ A) To reveal the shop owner's conflicted feelings about the new picture
The text begins by stating the picture “failed to fit in” with the shop’s other items, and the owner fumes that it doesn’t belong. Yet the second paragraph reveals he is “secretly proud of his acquisition.” The main purpose is to reveal the shop owner’s conflicted feelings.

✗ B) To convey the shop owner's resentment of the person he got the new picture from
The text gives no indication of the owner’s feelings about the young man at all.

✗ C) To describe the items that the shop owner most highly prizes
There's no suggestion the owner prizes the new picture or the other pictures more than any other items — only that the new picture is different.

✗ D) To explain differences between the new picture and other pictures in the shop
The text never describes what the new picture actually looks like; it names some other kinds of images in the shop without explaining how they differ from the new one.

Question 6 · Text Structure & Purpose

The following text is from the 1923 poem “Black Finger” by Angelina Weld Grimké, a Black American writer. A cypress is a type of evergreen tree.

I have just seen a most beautiful thing, Slim and still, Against a gold, gold sky, A straight black cypress, Sensitive, Exquisite, A black finger Pointing upwards. Why, beautiful still finger, are you black? And why are you pointing upwards?

Which choice best describes the overall structure of the text?

  • A) The speaker assesses a natural phenomenon, then questions the accuracy of her assessment.
  • B) The speaker describes a distinctive sight in nature, then ponders what meaning to attribute to that sight.
  • C) The speaker presents an outdoor scene, then considers a human behavior occurring within that scene.
  • D) The speaker examines her surroundings, then speculates about their influence on her emotional state.

Answer: B) The speaker describes a distinctive sight in nature, then ponders what meaning to attribute to that sight.

One-line summary: The speaker first describes the striking image of the black cypress against the gold sky, then wonders why it's black and pointing upward.

✓ B) The speaker describes a distinctive sight in nature, then ponders what meaning to attribute to that sight.
The speaker first describes a “most beautiful” sight — a cypress standing out against the gold sky like a finger “pointing upwards.” Then she wonders about the image’s meaning, asking why the finger is black and why it points upward. The text moves from description to pondering meaning.

✗ A) The speaker assesses a natural phenomenon, then questions the accuracy of her assessment.
The speaker wonders about the tree's meaning but never questions whether her assessment (that it resembles a finger) is accurate.

✗ C) The speaker presents an outdoor scene, then considers a human behavior occurring within that scene.
No people are present in the scene at all — only the tree.

✗ D) The speaker examines her surroundings, then speculates about their influence on her emotional state.
The speaker never addresses her own emotional state or how it's affected by her surroundings.

Question 7 · Text Structure & Purpose

The following text is from Walt Whitman’s 1860 poem “Calamus 24.”

I HEAR it is charged against me that I seek to   destroy institutions; But really I am neither for nor against   institutions (What indeed have I in common with them?—   Or what with the destruction of them?), Only I will establish in the Mannahatta   [Manhattan] and in every city of These States,   inland and seaboard, And in the fields and woods, and above every   keel [ship] little or large, that dents the water, Without edifices, or rules, or trustees, or any   argument, The institution of the dear love of comrades.

Which choice best describes the overall structure of the text?

  • A) The speaker questions an increasingly prevalent attitude, then summarizes his worldview.
  • B) The speaker regrets his isolation from others, then predicts a profound change in society.
  • C) The speaker concedes his personal shortcomings, then boasts of his many achievements.
  • D) The speaker addresses a criticism leveled against him, then announces a grand ambition of his.

Answer: D) The speaker addresses a criticism leveled against him, then announces a grand ambition of his.

One-line summary: The speaker rebuts an accusation, then declares his real goal: instilling "the institution of the dear love of comrades" everywhere.

✓ D) The speaker addresses a criticism leveled against him, then announces a grand ambition of his.
The speaker begins by noting others accuse him of seeking to destroy institutions. He addresses this by saying he is “neither for nor against institutions,” then states his real goal: to instill “the institution of the dear love of comrades” everywhere. This is criticism addressed, then a grand ambition announced.

✗ A) The speaker questions an increasingly prevalent attitude, then summarizes his worldview.
The speaker doesn't indicate the criticism has become increasingly prevalent, and he concludes with a future goal, not a summary of his current worldview.

✗ B) The speaker regrets his isolation from others, then predicts a profound change in society.
The text doesn't portray the speaker as isolated or regretful, and he expresses a hope, not an explicit prediction.

✗ C) The speaker concedes his personal shortcomings, then boasts of his many achievements.
The speaker addresses a criticism he believes is false — he admits no personal shortcomings, and he states a future goal rather than boasting of achievements.

Question 8 · Text Structure & Purpose

The mimosa tree evolved in East Asia, where the beetle Bruchidius terrenus preys on its seeds. In 1785, mimosa trees were introduced to North America, far from any B. terrenus. But evolutionary links between predators and their prey can persist across centuries and continents. Around 2001, B. terrenus was introduced in southeastern North America near where botanist Shu-Mei Chang and colleagues had been monitoring mimosa trees. Within a year, 93 percent of the trees had been attacked by the beetles.

Which choice best describes the function of the third sentence (underlined) in the overall structure of the text?

  • A) It states the hypothesis that Chang and colleagues had set out to investigate using mimosa trees and B. terrenus.
  • B) It presents a generalization that is exemplified by the discussion of the mimosa trees and B. terrenus.
  • C) It offers an alternative explanation for the findings of Chang and colleagues.
  • D) It provides context that clarifies why the species mentioned spread to new locations.

Answer: B) It presents a generalization that is exemplified by the discussion of the mimosa trees and B. terrenus.

One-line summary: The third sentence states a general rule about predator-prey links persisting across time and distance, which the mimosa/beetle story then illustrates.

✓ B) It presents a generalization that is exemplified by the discussion of the mimosa trees and B. terrenus.
The third sentence asserts that evolutionary links between predators and prey can persist across great time and distance. This is exemplified by the mimosa/beetle relationship: disrupted when trees were introduced to North America in 1785 without the beetle, then confirmed when the beetle arrived 200+ years later and quickly attacked the trees — illustrating the generalization.

✗ A) It states the hypothesis that Chang and colleagues had set out to investigate using mimosa trees and B. terrenus.
Chang and colleagues were simply monitoring mimosa trees when the beetles happened to be introduced — the third sentence doesn't describe a hypothesis they were testing.

✗ C) It offers an alternative explanation for the findings of Chang and colleagues.
The third sentence offers a generalization, not an alternative explanation differing from one presented elsewhere.

✗ D) It provides context that clarifies why the species mentioned spread to new locations.
The third sentence doesn't discuss any particular species spreading to new locations — that's not its function.

Question 9 · Cross-Text Connections

Text 1
Conventional wisdom long held that human social systems evolved in stages, beginning with hunter-gatherers forming small bands of members with roughly equal status. The shift to agriculture about 12,000 years ago sparked population growth that led to the emergence of groups with hierarchical structures: associations of clans first, then chiefdoms, and finally, bureaucratic states.

Text 2
In a 2021 book, anthropologist David Graeber and archaeologist David Wengrow maintain that humans have always been socially flexible, alternately forming systems based on hierarchy and collective ones with decentralized leadership. The authors point to evidence that as far back as 50,000 years ago some hunter-gatherers adjusted their social structures seasonally, at times dispersing in small groups but also assembling into communities that included esteemed individuals.

Based on the texts, how would Graeber and Wengrow (Text 2) most likely respond to the “conventional wisdom” presented in Text 1?

  • A) By conceding the importance of hierarchical systems but asserting the greater significance of decentralized collective societies
  • B) By disputing the idea that developments in social structures have followed a linear progression through distinct stages
  • C) By acknowledging that hierarchical roles likely weren’t a part of social systems before the rise of agriculture
  • D) By challenging the assumption that groupings of hunter-gatherers were among the earliest forms of social structure

Answer: B) By disputing the idea that developments in social structures have followed a linear progression through distinct stages

One-line summary: Text 2's view that social systems were always flexible directly contradicts Text 1's staged, linear-progression model.

✓ B) By disputing the idea that developments in social structures have followed a linear progression through distinct stages
Text 1's conventional wisdom is that social systems progressed through fixed stages (bands → clans → chiefdoms → states). Text 2 says Graeber and Wengrow believe systems have been flexible, shifting between hierarchical and collective structures, sometimes seasonally — directly disputing a linear staged progression.

✗ A) By conceding the importance of hierarchical systems but asserting the greater significance of decentralized collective societies
Text 2 is about flexibility between structures, not about ranking the relative importance of hierarchical vs. collective systems.

✗ C) By acknowledging that hierarchical roles likely weren't a part of social systems before the rise of agriculture
Text 2 actually indicates the opposite: some hunter-gatherer groups had hierarchical elements ("esteemed individuals") 50,000 years ago, long before agriculture arose (~12,000 years ago per Text 1).

✗ D) By challenging the assumption that groupings of hunter-gatherers were among the earliest forms of social structure
Text 2 doesn't dispute that hunter-gatherer groups were among the earliest social systems — it says only that some hunter-gatherer groups used different structures at different times.

Question 10 · Central Ideas & Details

The following text is adapted from Frances Hodgson Burnett’s 1911 novel The Secret Garden. Mary, a young girl, recently found an overgrown hidden garden.

Mary was an odd, determined little person, and now she had something interesting to be determined about, she was very much absorbed, indeed. She worked and dug and pulled up weeds steadily, only becoming more pleased with her work every hour instead of tiring of it. It seemed to her like a fascinating sort of play.

Which choice best states the main idea of the text?

  • A) Mary hides in the garden to avoid doing her chores.
  • B) Mary is getting bored with pulling up so many weeds in the garden.
  • C) Mary is clearing out the garden to create a space to play.
  • D) Mary feels very satisfied when she’s taking care of the garden.

Answer: D) Mary feels very satisfied when she's taking care of the garden.

One-line summary: Mary is "very much absorbed," grows "more pleased... every hour," and finds garden work "a fascinating sort of play" — all signaling satisfaction.

✓ D) Mary feels very satisfied when she's taking care of the garden.
The text describes Mary as "very much absorbed" and becoming "more pleased with her work every hour" rather than tiring of it, and thinking of garden activities as "a fascinating sort of play." The main idea is that Mary feels very satisfied caring for the garden.

✗ A) Mary hides in the garden to avoid doing her chores.
The text never mentions Mary's chores at all.

✗ B) Mary is getting bored with pulling up so many weeds in the garden.
The text indicates Mary finds pulling weeds fascinating, not boring — the opposite of what this choice claims.

✗ C) Mary is clearing out the garden to create a space to play.
Mary thinks of the garden activities themselves as play, not as work done to create a separate space to play later.

Question 11 · Inferences

The following text is from Ezra Pound’s 1909 poem “Hymn III,” based on the work of Marcantonio Flaminio.

As a fragile and lovely flower unfolds its gleaming   foliage on the breast of the fostering earth, if   the dew and the rain draw it forth; So doth my tender mind flourish, if it be fed with the   sweet dew of the fostering spirit, Lacking this, it beginneth straightway to languish,   even as a floweret born upon dry earth, if the   dew and the rain tend it not.

Based on the text, in what way is the human mind like a flower?

  • A) It becomes increasingly vigorous with the passage of time.
  • B) It draws strength from changes in the weather.
  • C) It requires proper nourishment in order to thrive.
  • D) It perseveres despite challenging circumstances.

Answer: C) It requires proper nourishment in order to thrive.

One-line summary: Both the flower and the "tender mind" need to be "fed" (nourished) or else they "languish" — the comparison is about needing nourishment.

✓ C) It requires proper nourishment in order to thrive.
The text compares the needs of a "fragile and lovely flower" to the speaker's "tender mind": both need to be fed to survive. Without such feeding, they'll "beginneth straightway to languish" (weaken). The mind is like a flower in that both need proper nourishment to thrive.

✗ A) It becomes increasingly vigorous with the passage of time.
The text doesn't address the passage of time or describe the mind/flower as becoming increasingly vigorous.

✗ B) It draws strength from changes in the weather.
The rain references pertain to a flower's need for water, not to drawing strength from changing weather generally.

✗ D) It perseveres despite challenging circumstances.
The text says the opposite: without nourishment, the mind/flower will languish right away, not persist despite hardship.

Question 12 · Central Ideas & Details

The following text is adapted from Jack London’s 1903 novel The Call of the Wild. Buck is a sled dog living with John Thornton in Yukon, Canada.

Thornton alone held [Buck]. The rest of mankind was as nothing. Chance travellers might praise or pet him; but he was cold under it all, and from a too demonstrative man he would get up and walk away. When Thornton’s partners, Hans and Pete, arrived on the long-expected raft, Buck refused to notice them till he learned they were close to Thornton; after that he tolerated them in a passive sort of way, accepting favors from them as though he favored them by accepting.

Which choice best states the main idea of the text?

  • A) Buck has become less social since he began living with Thornton.
  • B) Buck mistrusts humans and does his best to avoid them.
  • C) Buck has been especially well liked by most of Thornton’s friends.
  • D) Buck holds Thornton in higher regard than any other person.

Answer: D) Buck holds Thornton in higher regard than any other person.

One-line summary: Buck treats "the rest of mankind" as nothing and only reluctantly tolerates people close to Thornton — Thornton has a uniquely special status in Buck's mind.

✓ D) Buck holds Thornton in higher regard than any other person.
After establishing Buck views most people "as nothing," the text explains Buck won't acknowledge anyone but Thornton unless they seem friendly toward Thornton, and even then he's only reluctantly accepting. The main idea is that Thornton has special status in Buck's mind above all others.

✗ A) Buck has become less social since he began living with Thornton.
The text doesn't address Buck's temperament before living with Thornton, so there's no basis for "since."

✗ B) Buck mistrusts humans and does his best to avoid them.
Buck is aloof toward others, but there's no indication he mistrusts and avoids people generally — he does accept Thornton, a human.

✗ C) Buck has been especially well liked by most of Thornton's friends.
The text mentions travelers praising Buck and Thornton's partners giving him favors, but there's no indication these are Thornton's friends or that they have particular fondness for Buck.

Question 13 · Command of Evidence (Quantitative)

See bar chart ("US States with the Greatest Number of Organic Farms in 2016") in the original College Board PDF above.

Organic farming is a method of growing food that tries to reduce environmental harm by using natural forms of pest control and avoiding fertilizers made with synthetic materials. Organic farms are still a small fraction of the total farms in the United States, but they have been becoming more popular. According to the US Department of Agriculture, in 2016 California had between 2,600 and 2,800 organic farms and _______

Which choice most effectively uses data from the graph to complete the text?

  • A) Washington had between 600 and 800 organic farms.
  • B) New York had fewer than 800 organic farms.
  • C) Wisconsin and Iowa each had between 1,200 and 1,400 organic farms.
  • D) Pennsylvania had more than 1,200 organic farms.

Answer: A) Washington had between 600 and 800 organic farms.

One-line summary: Only choice A's numbers match the bar chart — Washington's bar falls between 600 and 800.

✓ A) Washington had between 600 and 800 organic farms.
The graph shows: California 2,600–2,800; Wisconsin 1,200–1,400; New York 1,000–1,200; Pennsylvania ~800; Iowa and Washington 600–800. The sentence needs an accurate completion continuing the pattern started with California — Washington's 600–800 range is correct.

✗ B) New York had fewer than 800 organic farms.
The graph shows New York had between 1,000 and 1,200 organic farms, not fewer than 800.

✗ C) Wisconsin and Iowa each had between 1,200 and 1,400 organic farms.
Wisconsin had 1,200–1,400, but Iowa had only 600–800 — they don't match.

✗ D) Pennsylvania had more than 1,200 organic farms.

Digital SAT FAQ

What is on Module 1 of the Digital SAT Reading & Writing section?

Module 1 is a 32-minute, 27 scored + embedded pretest question module (33 total questions) that every student sees in the same fixed form. It draws from four content domains: Craft & Structure, Information & Ideas, Standard English Conventions, and Expression of Ideas. Each question is a short, self-contained passage with one question and four answer choices.

How is the Digital SAT Reading & Writing section scored?

The Reading & Writing section is scored from 200 to 800. Module 1 is the same for every student; Module 2 is stage-adaptive — students who perform well on Module 1 are routed to a harder Module 2 with access to the full 800, and students who did not are routed to an easier Module 2 that typically caps in the mid-600s.

How long is the Digital SAT Reading & Writing section?

64 minutes total, split into two 32-minute modules. Together with the Math section (70 minutes), the full Digital SAT is 2 hours 14 minutes.

What skills are tested on Digital SAT Reading & Writing Module 1?

Craft & Structure (Words in Context, Text Structure & Purpose, Cross-Text Connections), Information & Ideas (Central Ideas & Details, Command of Evidence, Inferences), Standard English Conventions (Boundaries, Form/Structure/Sense), and Expression of Ideas (Transitions, Rhetorical Synthesis).

What is a good Digital SAT Reading & Writing score?

As a general reference: 600 ≈ 70th percentile, 700 ≈ 90th percentile, 750+ ≈ top 5%. For MIT, Stanford, Harvard, and the Ivy League, competitive applicants score 750+ on Reading & Writing. For SUNY Binghamton or UNC, 700+. For most CUNY campuses, 580–660.
