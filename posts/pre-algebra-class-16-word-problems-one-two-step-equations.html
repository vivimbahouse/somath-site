Identifying the unknown

Read what the problem asks before doing arithmetic. The unknown might be a starting amount, an amount removed, the price of one item, or a number of groups.

Define a variable as a quantity with units. “Let p be the price of one ticket, in dollars” is clearer than “p is tickets.” Keep that meaning unchanged throughout the solution.

Separate the known facts from the missing quantity. You can define the unknown even before enough information is available to solve for it.

Try it yourself: five questions

Q1 Practice 1 of 5

A jar has some marbles. After 9 are added, it has 31. Define m for the question “How many marbles were in the jar at first?” Do not solve yet.

Answer

Let m be the number of marbles in the jar at first.

The unknown is the starting count, not the 9 added or the final count of 31.

Q2 Practice 2 of 5

Five identical folders cost $20. Define a variable for the price of one folder, and include its units.

Answer

Let f be the price of one folder, in dollars.

The count of folders is known. The unknown is a price, not the number of folders.

Q3 Practice 3 of 5

A rope is cut into 6 equal pieces. Each piece is 7 cm long. Define L for the original rope length.

Answer

Let L be the original length of the rope, in centimeters.

The unknown is the whole rope length. Each piece has a known length of 7 cm.

Q4 Practice 4 of 5

A box contains 52 cards. Some are given away, leaving 37. Define c for the amount given away and list the two known counts.

Answer

Let c be the number of cards given away. Known counts: 52 at first and 37 left.

The unknown is the removed amount, not the starting amount.

Q5 Practice 5 of 5

A club pays a $12 booking fee plus $4 per player. Its bill is $48. A student writes “p is the price.” Replace this with a precise variable definition for finding how many players attended.

Answer

Let p be the number of players who attended.

The fee and price per player are already known. The question asks for a count of players.

Translating words into equations

An equation states that two quantities are equal. Build each side from the relationships in the story, then use an equals sign to connect quantities with the same units.

Order matters in subtraction and division. “Seven less than n” means n − 7, while “seven minus n” means 7 − n. A keyword alone does not identify which quantity comes first.

Parentheses preserve a grouped quantity. Twice the sum of n and 4 is 2(n + 4); four more than twice n is 2n + 4. These describe different calculations.

Try it yourself: five questions

Q6 Practice 1 of 5

Write an equation using n: a number increased by 11 equals 29. Do not solve.

Answer

n + 11 = 29.

The unknown number is increased by 11. The result is equal to 29.

Q7 Practice 2 of 5

Write an equation using n: 7 less than a number is 18. Do not solve.

Answer

n − 7 = 18.

Start with the number, then remove 7. Reversing it to 7 − n changes the meaning.

Q8 Practice 3 of 5

Write an equation using n: 26 minus a number is 9. Do not solve.

Answer

26 − n = 9.

This time 26 is the starting amount and the unknown is removed.

Q9 Practice 4 of 5

A shop charges $6 for delivery plus $4 for each box. The total is $34. Define b and write an equation; do not solve.

Answer

Let b be the number of boxes. Then 4b + 6 = 34.

The boxes cost 4b dollars altogether. Add the single delivery charge, not a delivery charge for every box.

Q10 Practice 5 of 5

Write an equation for each statement: (a) twice the sum of n and 5 equals 24; (b) five more than twice n equals 24. Do not solve.

Answer

(a) 2(n + 5) = 24. (b) 2n + 5 = 24.

In (a), the entire sum is doubled. In (b), only n is doubled before 5 is added.

Addition: finding the missing part

A useful model is starting amount + amount added = final amount. You can use addition to describe the situation even when subtraction is needed to find the unknown.

If x + a = b, subtract a from both sides. Keep the equation balanced; an inverse operation is applied to both sides, not just to the variable side.

Addition also joins parts of a whole. The unknown may be the amount added rather than the starting amount. Define it clearly and check the completed sum.

Try it yourself: five questions

Q11 Practice 1 of 5

A shelf gains 7 books and now holds 26. Write and solve an equation for the number it held before.

Answer

The shelf held 19 books.

Let b be the starting number of books.

Model: b + 7 = 26.

Subtract 7 from both sides: b = 26 − 7 = 19.

Check: 19 + 7 = 26 books.

Q12 Practice 2 of 5

Lena adds $16 to her savings and then has $61. Find her starting savings using an equation.

Answer

Lena started with $45.

Let m be the starting savings, in dollars.

Model: m + 16 = 61.

Subtract 16: m = 61 − 16 = 45.

Check: 45 + 16 = 61 dollars.

Q13 Practice 3 of 5

A team has 28 points and finishes with 47 after one round. How many points did it earn in that round? Write and solve an equation.

Answer

The team earned 19 points.

Let p be the points earned in the round.

Model: 28 + p = 47.

Subtract the known starting score: p = 47 − 28 = 19.

Check: 28 + 19 = 47 points.

Q14 Practice 4 of 5

After 2.75 liters of water are added, a container holds 8.5 liters. Find the original amount using an equation.

Answer

The original amount was 5.75 liters.

Let v be the original volume, in liters.

Model: v + 2.75 = 8.5.

Subtract 2.75 from both sides: v = 5.75.

Check: 5.75 + 2.75 = 8.5 liters.

Q15 Practice 5 of 5

An afternoon temperature is 9°C higher than the morning temperature. The afternoon temperature is 4°C. Find the morning temperature.

Answer

The morning temperature was −5°C.

Let t be the morning temperature, in degrees Celsius.

Model: t + 9 = 4.

Subtract 9: t = 4 − 9 = −5. A temperature can be negative.

Check: −5 + 9 = 4°C.

Subtraction: start or amount removed

A removal model is starting amount − amount removed = amount left. Locate the unknown before selecting the operation that will isolate it.

For x − a = b, add a to both sides to recover the start. For a − x = b, the removed amount is a − b. Do not treat the two equations as if the unknown were in the same position.

A subtraction model can also describe a decrease in a measurement. Use consistent units and check that the starting, removed, and remaining quantities fit the context.

Q16 Practice 1 of 5

A basket loses 11 apples and has 24 left. How many apples were in it at first? Write and solve an equation.

Answer

There were 35 apples.

Let a be the starting number of apples.

Model: a − 11 = 24.

Add 11 to both sides: a = 35.

Check: 35 − 11 = 24 apples.

Q17 Practice 2 of 5

A class begins with 63 stickers and gives some away, leaving 38. Find the number given away.

Answer

The class gave away 25 stickers.

Let g be the number given away.

Model: 63 − g = 38.

The removed part is 63 − 38 = 25. Equivalently, add g and subtract 38 on both sides.

Check: 63 − 25 = 38 stickers.

Q18 Practice 3 of 5

After a $27 purchase, Omar has $46 left. Find how much money he had before the purchase.

Answer

Omar had $73.

Let m be the starting amount, in dollars.

Model: m − 27 = 46.

Add 27: m = 46 + 27 = 73.

Check: 73 − 27 = 46 dollars.

Q19 Practice 4 of 5

A 12.5-meter cable is shortened to 8.75 meters. Write and solve an equation for the length removed.

Answer

3.75 meters were removed.

Let r be the length removed, in meters.

Model: 12.5 − r = 8.75.

Subtract the remaining length from the original: r = 12.5 − 8.75 = 3.75.

Check: 12.5 − 3.75 = 8.75 meters.

Q20 Practice 5 of 5

A freezer temperature falls by 8°C and becomes −3°C. What was its temperature before the change?

Answer

The original temperature was 5°C.

Let t be the original temperature, in degrees Celsius.

Model: t − 8 = −3.

Add 8 to both sides: t = −3 + 8 = 5.

Check: 5 − 8 = −3°C.

Multiplication: equal groups

Equal groups connect three quantities: number of groups × amount per group = total. Name which factor is unknown and which quantities are given.

The notation 5p means 5 × p. If 5p = 40, dividing both sides by 5 finds the unknown factor. An equation can use multiplication even though division solves it.

For prices, state whether p is a price per item or the number of items. Whole objects need whole-number counts, but a price or length can be a decimal.

Q21 Practice 1 of 5

Each bag contains 9 beads.

Q22 Practice 2 of 5

A tray holds 7 cupcakes. A baker fills identical trays with 56 cupcakes in total. How many trays are filled?

Answer

8 trays are filled.

Let t be the number of trays.

Model: 7t = 56.

Divide 56 by 7 to find the missing number of groups.

Check: 7 × 8 = 56 cupcakes.

Q23 Practice 3 of 5

Six identical sketchpads cost $51 altogether. Find the price of one sketchpad.

Answer

One sketchpad costs $8.50.

Let p be the price of one sketchpad, in dollars.

Model: 6p = 51.

Divide both sides by 6: p = 8.5. A decimal is valid for a price.

Check: 6 × 8.50 = 51 dollars.

Q24 Practice 4 of 5

A fabric strip is 3 times as long as a 4.2-meter strip. Define L as the longer length and write and solve an equation.

Answer

The longer strip is 12.6 meters.

Let L be the longer length, in meters.

Model: L = 3 × 4.2.

Multiply the known length by 3. Here the total, not a factor, is unknown.

Check: 12.6 = 3 × 4.2 meters.

Q25 Practice 5 of 5

A rectangle has area 84 square centimeters and width 7 centimeters. Find its length using an equation.

Answer

The length is 12 cm.

Let L be the length, in centimeters.

Model: 7L = 84.

Area equals width times length. Divide both sides by 7.

Check: 7 cm × 12 cm = 84 square centimeters.

Division: equal sharing and grouping

Equal sharing asks for the amount in each group: total ÷ number of groups = amount per group. Grouping asks how many equal groups fit: total ÷ amount per group = number of groups.

When the original total is unknown, an equation such as t ÷ 4 = 9 is solved by multiplying both sides by 4. When the total is known, the quotient may be the unknown.

A variable can also represent the divisor. For 48 ÷ g = 6 with a positive group count, the equivalent multiplication equation is 6g = 48. Check that division uses a nonzero divisor.

The original ribbon was 42 cm long.

Q27 Practice 2 of 5

Each student receives 9 counters.

Q28 Practice 3 of 5

A gardener places 54 seedlings into pots, with 6 seedlings per pot. How many pots are used?

Answer

9 pots are used.

Let p be the number of pots.

Model: 54 ÷ 6 = p.

Divide by the amount in each group, not by the number of groups.

Check: 9 × 6 = 54 seedlings.

Q29 Practice 4 of 5

A 45-liter supply is shared equally among some tanks, giving each tank 5 liters. Find the number of tanks.

Answer

There are 9 tanks.

Let t be the positive number of tanks.

Model: 45 ÷ t = 5.

Multiply by t to get 45 = 5t, then divide by 5.

Check: 45 ÷ 9 = 5 liters per tank.

Q30 Practice 5 of 5

A bag of rice is shared equally among 4 families. Each receives 1.75 kg. Find the original mass of rice.

Answer

The bag contained 7 kg of rice.

Let m be the original mass, in kilograms.

Model: m ÷ 4 = 1.75.

Multiply both sides by 4: m = 7.

Check: 7 ÷ 4 = 1.75 kg per family.

Modeling two-step situations

A two-step model combines two operations. A fixed cost plus repeated equal costs often gives ax + b = c; repeated lengths followed by a cut often give ax − b = c.

Undo the operations in reverse order. For ax + b = c, subtract b before dividing by a. For x ÷ a + b = c, subtract b before multiplying by a.

Read where the change occurs. Sharing a total and then adding to each share is different from changing the total before sharing. Parentheses show when an entire sum or difference must be divided.

Q32 Practice 2 of 5

Five equal pieces are joined end to end. After 9 cm is trimmed off, the result is 46 cm long. Find the length of each original piece.

Answer

Each original piece was 11 cm long.

Let L be the length of each original piece, in centimeters.

Q33 Practice 3 of 5

A bag of counters is shared equally among 6 students. Each then receives 3 extra counters and has 11. Find the number in the original bag.

Answer

The bag originally held 48 counters.

Let c be the original total number of counters.

Model: c ÷ 6 + 3 = 11.

Subtract 3 to get c ÷ 6 = 8, then multiply by 6.

Check: 48 ÷ 6 + 3 = 11 counters per student.

Q34 Practice 4 of 5

A roll of fabric has 8 meters removed. The rest is cut into 4 equal pieces of 7 meters each. Find the original length.

Answer

The original roll was 36 meters long.

Let L be the original length, in meters.

Model: (L − 8) ÷ 4 = 7.

Multiply by 4 first: L − 8 = 28. Then add 8. The subtraction happened before sharing.

Check: (36 − 8) ÷ 4 = 7 meters per piece.

Q35 Practice 5 of 5

A bicycle rental costs $4.50 to start plus $2.75 per hour. The charge is $21, with no other fees. Find the rental time.

Answer

The bicycle was rented for 6 hours.

Let h be the rental time, in hours.

Model: 2.75h + 4.50 = 21.

Subtract 4.50 to get 2.75h = 16.50. Divide by 2.75.

Check: 2.75 × 6 + 4.50 = 21 dollars.

Checking and interpreting solutions

Substitute a candidate into the original equation. Calculate both sides independently. If they are different, the candidate is not a solution.

Then return to the story. Does the equation actually model it? Are the units right? Is the result a price, a length, a count, or a starting amount? A correct calculation cannot rescue an incorrect model.

Check restrictions without rounding away contradictions. A length may be 2.5 meters, but a count of indivisible teams cannot be 2.5. If the information is inconsistent, say so rather than inventing a whole-number answer.

Q36 Practice 1 of 5

A ticket booth charges $5 plus $2 per game. A player spends $19. Check the claim that the player chose 7 games, and answer in a sentence.

Answer

Yes. The player chose 7 games.

Let g be the number of games.

Model: 2g + 5 = 19.

Substitute g = 7 into the original equation.

Q37 Practice 2 of 5

Three identical plants and a $4 pot cost $31. A student says each plant costs $11. Check and correct the answer.

Answer

Each plant costs $9, not $11.

Let p be the price of one plant, in dollars.

Model: 3p + 4 = 31.

At p = 11, the total is 37, not 31. Subtract 4 and divide by 3 to get p = 9.

Check: 3 × 9 + 4 = 31 dollars.

Q38 Practice 3 of 5

A 20-meter cord is cut into 8 equal pieces. A student solves 8L = 20 and writes “L = 2.5 pieces.” Correct the interpretation.

Answer

Each piece is 2.5 meters long.

Let L be the length of each piece, in meters.

Model: 8L = 20.

The value is correct but the units are not. L represents a length, not a number of pieces.

Check: 8 × 2.5 = 20 meters.

Q39 Practice 4 of 5

An organizer says 3 equal teams plus 2 extra players total 24 players. Solve 3p + 2 = 24 and explain whether a whole-number team size fits.

Answer

No whole-number team size fits the information. Algebra gives p = 22/3.

Let p be the number of players per team.

Model: 3p + 2 = 24.

Subtract 2 and divide by 3. A team cannot contain 7⅓ players; rounding to 7 would give only 23 players in total.

Check: 3 × (22/3) + 2 = 24 algebraically, but 22/3 violates the whole-player condition.

Q40 Practice 5 of 5

A service charges a $9 setup fee plus $5 per hour, totaling $34. A student solves 9h + 5 = 34. Explain the modeling error and find the correct time.

Answer

The correct time is 5 hours.

Let h be the number of service hours.

Model: 5h + 9 = 34.

The $5 hourly rate is repeated; the $9 setup fee occurs once. The student reversed these roles. Subtract 9, then divide by 5.

Check: 5 × 5 + 9 = 34 dollars.

Quick questions and answers

How do I choose a variable for a word problem?

Choose a letter for the quantity the question asks you to find. State exactly what it measures and include units, such as a starting number of objects or the price of one item in dollars.

Does the operation in the story have to match the operation used to solve?

No. An addition equation such as x + 8 = 20 is solved by subtraction. A multiplication equation such as 4x = 28 is solved by division. First model the relationship, then use inverse operations.

How do I distinguish an unknown start from an unknown amount removed?

For an unknown start, use x − amount removed = amount left. For an unknown removed amount, use starting amount − x = amount left. The position of the variable changes how you isolate it.

What is the difference between equal sharing and grouping?

Equal sharing finds how much goes in each of a known number of groups. Grouping finds how many groups of a known size fit into the total. Identify the total, group count, and amount per group.

How do I solve a two-step equation?

Undo operations in reverse order while keeping both sides balanced. For ax + b = c, subtract b and divide by a. For (x − b) ÷ a = c, multiply by a and then add b.

What makes a word-problem answer valid?

It must satisfy an equation that correctly models the story and obey the context. Check by substitution, state units, and respect whole-number or nonnegative restrictions when they apply.
