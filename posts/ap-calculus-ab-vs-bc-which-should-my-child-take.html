AP Calculus AB vs BC: Which Should My Child Take?

Compare scope, readiness, and workload before choosing AB or BC. Strong foundations matter more than taking the more advanced label.

School of Math · Updated October 6, 2026 · 4 min read

Choose AP Calculus AB or BC based on readiness, the school’s placement rules, and the student’s overall workload. BC includes AB material plus additional topics; choosing it should reflect capacity to learn and explain the mathematics, not a belief that AB is an inadequate course (College Board BC framework).

Readiness concernCan the student work confidently with algebra, functions, and trigonometry?Can the student sustain that fluency while learning the additional material?

The official BC framework includes ten units. The extra material is not only something added at the end: BC-specific content also appears within shared areas, so compare your school’s actual syllabus rather than assuming one timetable fits everyone (course framework).

What should be secure before either course?

Students should be comfortable factoring, preserving restrictions when simplifying rational expressions, composing functions, working with exponential and logarithmic models, and using radians and trigonometric values. Limits and derivatives are taught in Calculus; prior mastery of Calculus is not a prerequisite for beginning AB.

Use our five-question readiness check to identify gaps. The purpose is not to time a student against an arbitrary cutoff. It is to see whether the student can choose a method and explain it independently.

A decision framework for families

  1. Check school policy: Does the school permit direct entry to BC, require AB first, or use a placement test?
  2. Review actual work: Look at unfamiliar problems and written explanations, not only a final course grade.
  3. Consider workload: Labs, essays, activities, and other advanced courses all use the same available hours.
  4. Ask about support: Know how the teacher handles gaps and whether the student seeks help early.
  5. Revisit the plan: Set a check-in after the first assessments rather than waiting for a crisis.

When AB may be the more sensible choice

AB may fit a student who is ready for Calculus but needs more time to consolidate algebra, is balancing a demanding schedule, or would benefit from a narrower scope. That is not a prediction of a particular AP score, nor a claim about what every college prefers. Credit and placement policies vary, so check the student’s prospective colleges directly when that decision becomes relevant.

When BC may fit

BC may fit a student whose prior course and school pathway prepare them for the added scope, who enjoys working through unfamiliar problems, and who can keep earlier material active while learning new topics. Interest matters, but it should be paired with evidence of readiness and sustainable work habits.

For a taste of the distinction, compare a related-rates problem, which belongs to the shared Calculus foundation, with our BC series practice. They require different decisions, not merely different amounts of arithmetic.

What changes for the 2027 exams?

Both May 2027 exams have 42 multiple-choice questions in 100 minutes and six free-response questions in 90 minutes. They remain hybrid digital: multiple-choice answers are entered in Bluebook, while free-response answers are handwritten (AB exam format, BC exam format).

Questions and answers

Does BC include AB content?

Yes. BC includes the AB foundation and additional content, including parametric, polar, vector-valued functions, and infinite sequences and series.

Must a student already know derivatives before starting AB?

No. Derivatives are part of the Calculus course; algebra, functions, and trigonometry are the key foundations.

Is AB always easier for a particular student?

Not necessarily. Readiness, school pacing, teaching, and workload influence the experience.
