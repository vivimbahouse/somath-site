1. Introduction to Polynomials

Definition

A polynomial is an algebraic expression made of variables, coefficients, and constants combined only by addition, subtraction, and multiplication — and every variable exponent is a whole number (0, 1, 2, 3, …).

Example

In 3x2 + 2x − 5:

  • Variable: x.
  • Coefficients: 3 (of x2) and 2 (of x).
  • Constant: −5.
  • Exponents: 2 and 1 — both whole numbers, so this is a polynomial.

What is NOT a polynomial

Expressions with variables in a denominator (1/x), under a radical (√x), or with negative or fractional exponents (x−2, x1/2) are not polynomials.

Why it matters

Every algebra topic that follows — factoring, quadratics, rational expressions, functions — starts with fluent polynomial operations.

5 Practice Questions — Introduction to Polynomials

Q1. Is −(3/2)x³ + 0.4x² − x + 6 a polynomial? Identify its degree, variable-term coefficients, and constant.
Answer

Yes; degree 3; coefficients −3/2, 0.4, and −1; constant 6.

  1. The variable exponents are 3, 2, and 1, all nonnegative integers. Fractional or decimal coefficients are allowed.
  2. The unwritten coefficient of −x is −1. The largest exponent is 3.
Q2. Which expressions are polynomials in x: A = 5x² − 3/x + 1, B = (√5)x² − 2x + 7, and C = 2√x + x − 4? Explain each choice.
Answer

Only B is a polynomial in x.

  1. In A, 1/x is x to the power −1, so a variable exponent is negative.
  2. In B, √5 is a constant coefficient, not a variable exponent. In C, √x has exponent 1/2, so C is not a polynomial.
Q3. Simplify 4x³ − 2x + 5 − 4x³ + 7x − 9. Is the result a polynomial, and what are its degree and constant?
Answer

5x − 4; yes, degree 1 and constant −4.

  1. The cubic terms cancel: 4x³ − 4x³ = 0.
  2. Combine −2x + 7x = 5x and 5 − 9 = −4. Judge the degree after simplifying.
Q4. A student says −2a²b + (1/3)ab² − 7 is not a polynomial because it has two variables and a fraction. Is the student correct? Give the total degree.
Answer

The student is incorrect. It is a polynomial of total degree 3.

  1. Polynomials may contain more than one variable, and coefficients may be fractions.
  2. The term degrees are 2 + 1 = 3, 1 + 2 = 3, and 0. Every variable exponent is a nonnegative integer.
Q5. For n chosen from 0, 1, 2, and 3, which values make 4xn−1 + 2 a polynomial? Give the simplified expression for each allowed value.
Answer

n = 1, 2, or 3; the expressions are 6, 4x + 2, and 4x² + 2.

  1. A polynomial requires n − 1 to be a nonnegative integer. n = 0 gives exponent −1 and is not allowed.
  2. For n = 1, x⁰ = 1, so 4x⁰ + 2 = 6. For n = 2 and n = 3, the exponents are 1 and 2.

2. Identifying Terms, Coefficients, and Constants

Terms

A term is a piece of a polynomial separated by + or − signs. The sign in front belongs to the term.

Coefficient

The coefficient is the number multiplying the variable in a term. If a variable has no visible number, the coefficient is 1 (or −1 if the term is negative).

Constant

The constant is the term with no variable at all.

Worked example: 4x2 − 7x + 9

  • Terms: 4x2, −7x, 9.
  • Coefficients: 4 (of x2) and −7 (of x).
  • Constant: 9.

5 Practice Questions — Terms, Coefficients, and Constants

Q1. Write 9 − x³ + 5x − 2x² in descending powers of x. List all signed terms and identify the leading coefficient.
Answer

−x³ − 2x² + 5x + 9; terms −x³, −2x², 5x, 9; leading coefficient −1.

  1. Reorder the terms without changing any signs.
  2. The leading term is −x³. Its coefficient is −1, even though the numeral 1 is not written.
Q2. In −7x⁴ + 3x² − x + 8, give the coefficients of x⁴, x³, x², and x, then the constant.
Answer

−7, 0, 3, −1; constant 8.

  1. Include a zero coefficient for the missing cubic term: 0x³.
  2. Keep each sign with its coefficient. The final 8 has no variable.
Q3. For −3a²b + 5ab² − 4ab + 12, list the signed variable terms and their numerical coefficients. How many terms are there altogether?
Answer

Variable terms: −3a²b, 5ab², −4ab; coefficients −3, 5, −4. There are 4 terms, including 12.

  1. Separate terms at the addition and subtraction signs, retaining each term's sign.
  2. The variable parts a²b, ab², and ab are different. The constant 12 is a fourth term.
Q4. Simplify 6x² − 4x + 3 − 2x² + x − 9, then identify its leading coefficient, coefficient of x, and constant.
Answer

4x² − 3x − 6; leading coefficient 4, x-coefficient −3, constant −6.

  1. Combine equal powers: (6 − 2)x² = 4x² and (−4 + 1)x = −3x.
  2. Combine constants: 3 − 9 = −6. Read the requested coefficients from the simplified polynomial.
Q5. In P(x) = (3k − 2)x² − (k + 1)x + 7, the coefficient of x² is 10. Find k, then write P(x) with numerical coefficients.
Answer

k = 4; P(x) = 10x² − 5x + 7.

  1. Set the quadratic coefficient equal to 10: 3k − 2 = 10, so 3k = 12 and k = 4.
  2. The coefficient of x is −(4 + 1) = −5, not 5.

3. Classifying Polynomials by Number of Terms

The three named types

# of termsNameExample
1Monomial6x2
2Binomialx + 5
3Trinomial2x2 − 3x + 4
4+Polynomial (no special name)x3 + 2x2 − x + 7

Simplify first

Always combine like terms before classifying. 3x + 5x + 2 looks like 3 terms but simplifies to 8x + 2 — a binomial.

5 Practice Questions — Classifying Polynomials

Q1. Simplify and classify by number of terms: 7x² − 3x + 2x² + 8x.
Answer

9x² + 5x, a binomial.

  1. Combine the two quadratic terms and the two linear terms separately.
  2. Two nonzero terms remain; do not count the four terms in the original expression.
Q2. Simplify and classify: 5y³ + 2y² − 4y − 5y³ + 7y.
Answer

2y² + 3y, a binomial.

  1. The y³ terms cancel. The linear terms combine to −4y + 7y = 3y.
  2. The remaining two unlike terms cannot be combined.
Q3. Simplify and classify: 4a²b − 3ab² + 5 + 2ab² − a²b.
Answer

3a²b − ab² + 5, a trinomial.

  1. Combine 4a²b − a²b = 3a²b and −3ab² + 2ab² = −ab².
  2. a²b and ab² are unlike terms because their variable exponents do not match.
Q4. Find c so that (c − 4)x² + 6x − 2x + 9 − 9 simplifies to a monomial. State that monomial.
Answer

c = 4; the monomial is 4x.

  1. First simplify to (c − 4)x² + 4x. The linear term cannot disappear.
  2. For exactly one nonzero term, set c − 4 = 0. This leaves 4x.
Q5. For which values of a is (a − 2)x² + 3x + (a + 1) a binomial? State the polynomial for each value.
Answer

a = 2 gives 3x + 3; a = −1 gives −3x² + 3x.

  1. The term 3x is always nonzero. Exactly one of the other two coefficients must be zero.
  2. a − 2 = 0 gives a = 2; a + 1 = 0 gives a = −1. These cannot happen together, so each choice leaves exactly two terms.

4. Degree of a Polynomial

Degree of a monomial

Add the exponents of all variables in that term. 5x3y2 has degree 3 + 2 = 5.

Degree of a polynomial

The largest degree among all its terms. 4x4 − 2x2 + 7 has degree 4.

Special cases

  • A nonzero constant has degree 0 (because c = c · x0).
  • The zero polynomial (just 0) has an undefined degree — it’s a special case.

Why degree matters

Degree tells you how the polynomial behaves and how many roots it can have. Degree 1 = linear, 2 = quadratic, 3 = cubic, and so on.

5 Practice Questions — Degree of a Polynomial

Q1. Find the degree and leading coefficient of 8 − 3x⁴ + 5x² − x.
Answer

Degree 4; leading coefficient −3.

  1. In standard form it is −3x⁴ + 5x² − x + 8.
  2. The highest exponent is 4. Its coefficient is −3, not the first number shown in the original order.
Q2. Find the total degree of −4a³b² + 6a²b⁴ − 7ab + 10. Show each term's degree.
Answer

The term degrees are 5, 6, 2, and 0; the polynomial has total degree 6.

  1. Add exponents within each term: 3 + 2 = 5, 2 + 4 = 6, and 1 + 1 = 2.
  2. Take the largest term degree, not the sum of the degrees of all terms.
Q3. Simplify 9x⁵ − 4x³ + 2x − 9x⁵ + 7x³ − 6 before finding its degree and leading coefficient.
Answer

3x³ + 2x − 6; degree 3 and leading coefficient 3.

  1. The fifth-degree terms cancel completely.
  2. Combine −4x³ + 7x³ = 3x³. The greatest surviving power is now x³.
Q4. Choose k so that (k − 3)x⁴ + (2k + 1)x² − 5x + 7 has degree exactly 2. Explain why the quadratic term does not also disappear.
Answer

k = 3; the polynomial becomes 7x² − 5x + 7.

  1. The x⁴ coefficient must be zero: k − 3 = 0.
  2. With k = 3, the x² coefficient is 2(3) + 1 = 7, which is nonzero. The degree is therefore exactly 2.
Q5. The polynomial 3xny² + 5x²y⁴ − 8 has total degree exactly 7, where n is a nonnegative integer. Find n.
Answer

n = 5.

  1. The term degrees are n + 2, 2 + 4 = 6, and 0.
  2. The maximum must be 7. Since the second term has degree only 6, n + 2 must equal 7, giving n = 5.

5. Combining Like Terms

What are like terms?

Like terms have the same variables raised to the same powers. Only the coefficients can differ. Examples of like terms: 3x2 and 5x2. 2x and 2x2 are not like terms — the exponents differ.

How to combine

Add (or subtract) the coefficients. Keep the variable and exponent unchanged: 3x2 + 5x2 = 8x2.

Worked example

Simplify 4x2 + 3x − 2x2 + 5x − 7:

  • Group: (4x2 − 2x2) + (3x + 5x) − 7.
  • Combine: 2x2 + 8x − 7.

Common pitfalls

  • Don’t add exponents when combining like terms — you only add coefficients.
  • The sign in front belongs to the term. −2x2 stays negative when you group it.

5 Practice Questions — Combining Like Terms

Q1. Simplify 8x − 3 + 5x − 7 − 4x.
Answer

9x − 10.

  1. The x-coefficient is 8 + 5 − 4 = 9.
  2. The constants total −3 − 7 = −10.
Q2. Simplify and write in standard form: 7x² − 4x + 6 − 3x² + 9x − 11.
Answer

4x² + 5x − 5.

  1. Quadratic terms: (7 − 3)x² = 4x². Linear terms: (−4 + 9)x = 5x.
  2. Constants: 6 − 11 = −5. Keep unlike powers separate.
Q3. Simplify 5a²b − 2ab² − 7a²b + 6ab² − 3ab.
Answer

−2a²b + 4ab² − 3ab.

  1. Group matching variable parts: (5 − 7)a²b and (−2 + 6)ab².
  2. The term −3ab has no like term, so it remains unchanged.
Q4. Simplify (3/2)x² − (1/4)x + 5 − (1/2)x² + (5/4)x − 8.
Answer

x² + x − 3.

  1. Quadratic coefficient: 3/2 − 1/2 = 1. Linear coefficient: −1/4 + 5/4 = 1.
  2. Constants: 5 − 8 = −3. Fractional coefficients combine in the same way as integer coefficients.
Q5. Simplify 4x² − 3x + 8 − 6x² + 7x − 5 + x², then evaluate your result at x = −2.
Answer

−x² + 4x + 3; at x = −2, the value is −9.

  1. Combine coefficients: (4 − 6 + 1)x² + (−3 + 7)x + (8 − 5) = −x² + 4x + 3.
  2. Substitute with parentheses: −(−2)² + 4(−2) + 3 = −4 − 8 + 3 = −9.

6. Adding Polynomials

The rule

To add polynomials, combine only like terms. Signs stay the same — you’re just removing parentheses and grouping.

Worked example (horizontal)

Add (3x2 + 2x − 5) + (2x2 − x + 7):

  1. Group like terms: (3x2 + 2x2) + (2x − x) + (−5 + 7).
  2. Add coefficients: 5x2 + x + 2.

Vertical method

Line up like terms in columns, then add each column:

    3x² + 2x − 5
  + 2x² −  x + 7
  ————————
    5x² +  x + 2

Tip

Line up terms of the same degree even when a degree is missing — leave a blank space so the columns stay clean.

5 Practice Questions — Adding Polynomials

Q1. Add and simplify: (4x² − 7x + 3) + (−2x² + 5x − 8).
Answer

2x² − 2x − 5.

  1. Combine like terms: (4 − 2)x² + (−7 + 5)x + (3 − 8).
  2. This gives 2x² − 2x − 5. Adding a polynomial does not change its existing signs.
Q2. Add (3x³ − 5x + 4) + (−x³ + 2x² + 7x − 9).
Answer

2x³ + 2x² + 2x − 5.

  1. The first polynomial has no quadratic term, so treat its x² coefficient as 0.
  2. Combine cubic, quadratic, linear, and constant terms: 3 − 1 = 2; 0 + 2 = 2; −5 + 7 = 2; 4 − 9 = −5.
Q3. Add all three polynomials: (2x² − 3x + 5) + (−5x² + x − 7) + (4x² + 6x + 2).
Answer

x² + 4x.

  1. The quadratic coefficient is 2 − 5 + 4 = 1; the linear coefficient is −3 + 1 + 6 = 4.
  2. The constants cancel: 5 − 7 + 2 = 0. Do not keep an extra constant term.
Q4. Find the missing polynomial P(x): (3x² − 4x + 6) + P(x) = 8x² + x − 2.
Answer

P(x) = 5x² + 5x − 8.

  1. Match coefficients: 3 + ? = 8, −4 + ? = 1, and 6 + ? = −2.
  2. The missing coefficients are 5, 5, and −8. Add the result back to check.
Q5. A triangle has side lengths 2x + 5, 3x − 1, and x + 4 centimeters, with x > 1. Write and simplify its perimeter, then find the perimeter when x = 3.
Answer

P(x) = 6x + 8 cm; at x = 3, the perimeter is 26 cm.

  1. Add the three sides: (2x + 5) + (3x − 1) + (x + 4) = 6x + 8.
  2. Evaluate 6(3) + 8 = 26. The side lengths are then 11, 8, and 7 cm.

7. Subtracting Polynomials

The rule

Subtracting = adding the opposite. Distribute the minus sign to every term in the second polynomial — flip every sign — then combine like terms.

Worked example

Compute (5x2 + 3x − 4) − (2x2 − x + 6):

  1. Distribute the minus: 5x2 + 3x − 4 − 2x2 + x − 6.
  2. Group like terms: (5x2 − 2x2) + (3x + x) + (−4 − 6).
  3. Combine: 3x2 + 4x − 10.

Common pitfall

Students flip only the first sign of the second polynomial. You must flip every sign inside those parentheses. Watch double negatives carefully: −(−x) = +x.

5 Practice Questions — Subtracting Polynomials

Q1. Subtract and simplify: (7x² − 4x + 6) − (3x² + 2x − 5).
Answer

4x² − 6x + 11.

  1. Distribute the subtraction: 7x² − 4x + 6 − 3x² − 2x + 5.
  2. Combine like terms. Subtracting −5 contributes +5 to the constant.
Q2. Simplify (2x³ − 5x + 1) − (−3x³ + 4x² − x + 8).
Answer

5x³ − 4x² − 4x − 7.

  1. Reverse every sign in the second polynomial: +3x³ − 4x² + x − 8.
  2. Combine with the first polynomial: 2 + 3 = 5, −5 + 1 = −4, and 1 − 8 = −7.
Q3. Simplify (5x² + 2x − 6) − (3x² − 4x + 1) − (x² + x − 8).
Answer

x² + 5x + 1.

  1. Remove both subtracted parentheses: 5x² + 2x − 6 − 3x² + 4x − 1 − x² − x + 8.
  2. Combine coefficients: 5 − 3 − 1 = 1, 2 + 4 − 1 = 5, and −6 − 1 + 8 = 1.
Q4. Simplify (6x² − x + 4) − [(2x² + 3x − 5) − (x² − 2x + 1)].
Answer

5x² − 6x + 10.

  1. First simplify inside the square brackets: 2x² + 3x − 5 − x² + 2x − 1 = x² + 5x − 6.
  2. Now subtract the entire bracket: 6x² − x + 4 − x² − 5x + 6 = 5x² − 6x + 10.
Q5. For a school fundraiser, revenue from x batches is modeled by R(x) = 5x² + 18x dollars and cost by C(x) = 2x² + 7x + 24 dollars. Write the profit polynomial R(x) − C(x), then find profit for 4 batches.
Answer

P(x) = 3x² + 11x − 24; profit for 4 batches is $68.

  1. Subtract every cost term: 5x² + 18x − 2x² − 7x − 24.
  2. Evaluate 3(4²) + 11(4) − 24 = 48 + 44 − 24 = 68.

8. Multiplying Monomials

Two-step rule

  1. Multiply the coefficients (just numbers).
  2. Add the exponents of matching bases: xm · xn = xm+n.

Example 1

(3x2)(4x3) = (3 · 4)(x2+3) = 12x5.

Example 2 — two variables

(−2a3b)(5a2b4): Coefficients: (−2)(5) = −10. a-exponents: 3 + 2 = 5. b-exponents: 1 + 4 = 5. Answer: −10a5b5.

Remember

A variable with no visible exponent has exponent 1 (so b = b1). Only add exponents when the bases are the same.

5 Practice Questions — Multiplying Monomials

Q1. Multiply (−3x²)(5x³), then state the degree of the product.
Answer

−15x⁵; degree 5.

  1. Multiply coefficients: (−3)(5) = −15.
  2. Add exponents of the same base: x² · x³ = x⁵.
Q2. Multiply (−2a³b²)(−4ab³). Give the total degree of the product.
Answer

8a⁴b⁵; total degree 9.

  1. Two negative coefficients make a positive: (−2)(−4) = 8.
  2. Add exponents by variable: a³ · a = a⁴ and b² · b³ = b⁵. Total degree is 4 + 5 = 9.
Q3. Multiply all three factors: (−2x²y)(3xy³)(−4x³).
Answer

24x⁶y⁴.

  1. Coefficients: (−2)(3)(−4) = 24.
  2. For x, add 2 + 1 + 3 = 6. For y, add 1 + 3 = 4; the third factor contributes no y.
Q4. Simplify ((3/4)a²b³)(−8ab²)(−(1/2)a³).
Answer

3a⁶b⁵.

  1. Multiply numerical factors first: (3/4)(−8)(−1/2) = (−6)(−1/2) = 3.
  2. The exponents are a: 2 + 1 + 3 = 6; b: 3 + 2 = 5.
Q5. Find c and the nonnegative integer n so that (cxny²)(−3x²y³) = 18x⁷y⁵ for every x and y.
Answer

c = −6 and n = 5.

  1. Match coefficients: −3c = 18, so c = −6.
  2. Match x-exponents: n + 2 = 7, so n = 5. The y-exponents already match because 2 + 3 = 5.

9. Multiplying a Monomial by a Polynomial (Distributive Property)

The rule

Use the distributive property: a(b + c) = ab + ac. Multiply the monomial by every term inside the parentheses.

Example 1

3x(2x2 − 5x + 4):

  • 3x · 2x2 = 6x3
  • 3x · (−5x) = −15x2
  • 3x · 4 = 12x

Answer: 6x3 − 15x2 + 12x.

Example 2 — negative monomial

−2y2(3y3 + y − 6):

  • −2y2 · 3y3 = −6y5
  • −2y2 · y = −2y3
  • −2y2 · (−6) = 12y2

Answer: −6y5 − 2y3 + 12y2.

Warning about signs

A negative monomial flips the sign of every term. Apply the sign rules of multiplication carefully — missing a sign is the #1 error on this topic.

5 Practice Questions — Monomial × Polynomial

Q1. Expand and simplify −3x(2x² − 5x + 4).
Answer

−6x³ + 15x² − 12x.

  1. Multiply −3x by each of the three terms.
  2. The middle product is positive: (−3x)(−5x) = 15x².
Q2. Expand (1/2)x²(6x³ − 8x + 10) and write the result in standard form.
Answer

3x⁵ − 4x³ + 5x².

  1. Multiply each coefficient by 1/2: 6 becomes 3, −8 becomes −4, and 10 becomes 5.
  2. Add the outer x-exponent 2 to each inner x-exponent: 3 + 2 = 5, 1 + 2 = 3, and 0 + 2 = 2.
Q3. Expand and simplify −2ab(3a² − 4ab + 5b²).
Answer

−6a³b + 8a²b² − 10ab³.

  1. Distribute −2ab to all three terms.
  2. Keep each variable's exponent separate: (ab)(ab) = a²b² and (ab)(b²) = ab³.
Q4. Simplify 3x(2x² − x + 4) − 2x(x² + 5x − 3).
Answer

4x³ − 13x² + 18x.

  1. The first product is 6x³ − 3x² + 12x. Distributing the outer −2x gives −2x³ − 10x² + 6x.
  2. Add the expanded results: (6 − 2)x³ + (−3 − 10)x² + (12 + 6)x.
Q5. A rectangle has width 3x meters and length 2x + 5 meters. A smaller rectangle of width x and length x + 2 is removed. For x > 0, write the remaining area as a polynomial, then find it when x = 2.
Answer

A(x) = 5x² + 13x m²; when x = 2, the remaining area is 46 m².

  1. Subtract the two areas: 3x(2x + 5) − x(x + 2) = 6x² + 15x − x² − 2x.
  2. Combine to get 5x² + 13x. Evaluate 5(2²) + 13(2) = 20 + 26 = 46.

10. Multiplying Two Binomials — The FOIL Method

FOIL — First, Outer, Inner, Last

FOIL is a memory aid for the distributive property applied to two binomials. You get four products:

  • First — the first term of each binomial.
  • Outer — the two outer terms.
  • Inner — the two inner terms.
  • Last — the last term of each binomial.

Worked example: (x + 3)(x + 5)

  • First: x · x = x2
  • Outer: x · 5 = 5x
  • Inner: 3 · x = 3x
  • Last: 3 · 5 = 15

Sum: x2 + 5x + 3x + 15. Combine like terms: x2 + 8x + 15.

Why FOIL works

FOIL is just the distributive property in disguise. It only works for two binomials. For a binomial times a trinomial — or any bigger product — use the distributive property directly and multiply every term of the first by every term of the second.

Sign patterns worth memorizing

  • (x + a)(x + b) = x2 + (a+b)x + ab
  • (x + a)(x − a) = x2 − a2 — the difference of squares.
  • (x + a)2 = x2 + 2ax + a2 — the perfect square.

5 Practice Questions — FOIL / Multiplying Binomials

Q1. Expand and simplify (2x + 3)(x + 5).
Answer

2x² + 13x + 15.

  1. The four products are 2x², 10x, 3x, and 15.
  2. Combine the middle terms: 10x + 3x = 13x.
Q2. Expand and simplify (3x − 4)(2x + 5).
Answer

6x² + 7x − 20.

  1. The four products are 6x², 15x, −8x, and −20.
  2. Combine 15x − 8x = 7x. The constant is negative because (−4)(5) = −20.
Q3. A student writes (2x − 3)² = 4x² + 9. Identify the missing term and give the correct expansion.
Answer

(2x − 3)² = 4x² − 12x + 9; the missing term is −12x.

  1. Write the square as (2x − 3)(2x − 3), not as a square of each term separately.
  2. The two cross-products are −6x and −6x. Their sum is −12x.
Q4. Expand both products and simplify (2x − 5)(2x + 5) − (x − 3)(x + 4).
Answer

3x² − x − 13.

  1. The first product is 4x² − 25 because its middle terms cancel. The second is x² + x − 12.
  2. Subtract the entire second result: 4x² − 25 − x² − x + 12 = 3x² − x − 13.
Q5. A rectangular garden measures 2x + 5 meters by x + 3 meters, where x > 0. A path 1 meter wide surrounds the garden on all four sides. Write the path's area as a simplified polynomial, then find it when x = 3.
Answer

Apath(x) = 6x + 20 m²; at x = 3, the path covers 38 m².

  1. A 1-meter border adds 2 meters to each full dimension. The outside dimensions are 2x + 7 and x + 5.
  2. Outside area: (2x + 7)(x + 5) = 2x² + 17x + 35. Garden area: (2x + 5)(x + 3) = 2x² + 11x + 15.
  3. Subtract the garden from the outside: 6x + 20. At x = 3, this is 18 + 20 = 38 m².
