1. What Fox News covered

On the September 14, 2026 broadcast of America Reports, Fox News profiled the rise of homeschooling in New York City and New York State under the headline “Homeschooling reportedly soars in New York City and state.” The segment featured two Manhattan mothers — SOMATH co-founder Vivianne Wright, who homeschools her 9-year-old daughter Violetta, and Upper West Side mother Adrienne Jensen, who is currently homeschooling three of her four children after her oldest daughter (also homeschooled) enrolled at Cornell. Both mothers were also profiled in the New York Post’s September 9, 2026 exclusive on NYC homeschooling that broke the same trend nationally a week earlier.

The Fox News segment framed the story around one number: the New York State homeschool population has more than tripled since 2009–10, reaching 53,905 students in 2025–26. It is the fastest-growing school sector in the state, outpacing charter school growth (~3.1% since 2023 vs ~8% for homeschooling).

Why this matters for NYC parents. Two years ago, homeschooling in New York was often described as a niche or fringe choice. In 2026 it is a mainstream option that both national television and the New York Post are covering as a legitimate response to public-school challenges — and one that competitive families on the Upper West Side, Harlem, Astoria, and Brooklyn are increasingly considering.

3. Why NYC parents are choosing to homeschool

The Fox News segment surfaces the same reasons parents named in the New York Post reporting a week earlier — and the same reasons SOMATH hears from families on the Upper West Side every week.

“In the beginning, our reasons for homeschooling were mainly about rigor and being able to give our kids a quality education without having to hope that they got ‘good’ teachers [and] without strategizing to live in a ‘good’ school district.” Adrienne Jensen, Upper West Side mother of four, to the New York Post (Sept 9, 2026)
“To me, the most important habit to nourish is curiosity.” Vivianne Wright, SOMATH co-founder, to the New York Post (Sept 9, 2026)

The five reasons that came up most often in the reporting:

  1. Class-size rigor gap. One New York homeschool advocate quoted in the Post put it plainly: “You have 30 or 35 kids in a classroom, all at different levels. You’re failing the whole class.” Small groups make individualized pacing possible.
  2. Academic outcomes on the state exams. A Success Academy analysis referenced in the Post found that in 906 of NYC’s 1,500+ public schools, fewer than half of students passed math, reading, or both on state exams last year. About a third of those 906 have been on state accountability lists since 2012.
  3. Cost-benefit on public spending. The Empire Center told the Post that New York taxpayers spend $37,000 per student — the most in the world — yet the state trails Mississippi (the poorest state) in reading and math.
  4. Time and freedom. Jensen described spending “at least half our time outside or at museums and other city institutions, really living while we learn.” NYC as a giant classroom is a real advantage.
  5. Fit for accelerated learners. Vivianne’s daughter Violetta — who works through math for up to 90 minutes at a time and practices chess daily — is an example of a child whose pace and interests are hard to accommodate in a 30-student classroom on a state-pacing calendar.

The national survey data lines up. The National Center for Education Statistics’ 2019 homeschool survey found that 80.3% of homeschool parents cited concerns about the school environment, 72.6% pointed to dissatisfaction with academic instruction, and 54.2% wanted more family time. In a 2025 New York City DOE survey, 21% of NYC homeschool parents said their children got a more rigorous education at home than was possible in public school.

5. FAQ

Who was on Fox News America Reports on September 14, 2026 talking about NYC homeschooling?
Fox News America Reports interviewed two New York mothers — Vivianne Wright, co-founder of SOMATH (School of Math) in Manhattan, and Adrienne Jensen, an Upper West Side mother of four. How many kids are homeschooled in New York in 2026?
Per state education data cited in the New York Post and Newsmax (Sept 9, 2026), New York State had 53,905 homeschooled students in the 2025–26 school year, with 14,000+ in New York City. That is a 200%+ increase over 15 years and about +8% since 2023 — the highest growth of any K–12 school sector in the state. The Empire Center for Public Policy ranks New York State second in the nation for homeschool growth, behind only Washington, D.C.

Why are so many NYC parents pulling their kids out of public school?
The most commonly cited reasons: academic rigor, large class sizes (30–35 students), the ability to tailor instruction, and flexibility for family time. A Success Academy analysis referenced by the Post found that in 906 of NYC’s 1,500+ public schools, fewer than half of students passed math, reading, or both on state exams last year.

What are the New York homeschooling requirements?
New York State requires: (1) a notice of intent to the local school district, (2) an Individualized Home Instruction Plan (IHIP) naming curriculum and materials for each required subject, (3) four quarterly progress reports per year, (4) at least 900 hours of instruction for grades K–6 or 990 hours for grades 7–12 across 180 days, and (5) an annual assessment (a state-approved standardized test or approved alternative). Required subjects include English, math, science, social studies, health, physical education, art, and music. Parents do not need a teaching certification or a college degree.

Can a homeschooled NYC student still take the SAT, SHSAT, or AP exams?
Yes. Homeschooled students take the same SAT, ACT, AP, and SHSAT as any other NYC student and apply to public high schools, private schools, and colleges on the same timeline. SOMATH’s enrichment ladder feeds directly into SHSAT prep, Digital SAT prep, and AP Calculus AB/BC and AP Statistics.
