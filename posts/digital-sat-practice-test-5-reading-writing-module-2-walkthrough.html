Theory refresher · What R&W Module 2 tests

The Digital SAT Reading & Writing section has two 32-minute modules, each with 33 questions. Module 2 is stage-adaptive — your Module 1 performance determines whether you see the harder or the easier version of Module 2. Every question is a short, self-contained passage (roughly 25–150 words) followed by one question and four answer choices — there are no multi-question passage sets. The tested skills are identical to Module 1; only the average difficulty of the items shifts depending on which version you are routed into. The four content domains are:

1 · Craft & Structure (roughly 13–15% of the section)

Words in Context. Pick the word or phrase that most precisely fits the logic of the sentence. Watch for signal words like “although,” “however,” and “therefore” that tell you whether the blank should agree with or contrast the surrounding idea.

Text Structure & Purpose. Identify how a text (or a single sentence within it) is organized — e.g., does it move from description to reflection, from criticism to ambition, from a claim to a supporting example?

Cross-Text Connections. Two short texts are given on the same topic. You must determine how the author/idea of one text would most likely respond to, or relate to, a claim made in the other.

2 · Information & Ideas (roughly 26–28% of the section)

Central Ideas & Details. Identify the main point of a text as a whole — not a detail, not a partial truth, but the idea every sentence in the passage supports.

Command of Evidence (Textual). Choose the quotation (or piece of the text) that best supports, illustrates, or exemplifies a given claim.

Command of Evidence (Quantitative). Use a table or graph to complete a statement logically — the correct choice must be numerically accurate and serve the logical role the sentence needs (e.g., completing a pattern or contrast).

Inferences. Draw the most logically supported conclusion from the given information — usually phrased as which finding would most support a hypothesis, or what a passage most strongly suggests.

3 · Standard English Conventions (roughly 11–15% of the section)

Boundaries. Punctuation between and within sentences — periods, semicolons, commas, colons, and dashes. A period or semicolon joins two independent clauses; a comma alone between two independent clauses is a comma splice (incorrect); no punctuation at all between two independent clauses is a run-on (incorrect).

Form, Structure & Sense. Grammar and usage — subject-verb agreement, verb tense consistency, pronoun-antecedent agreement, plural vs. possessive nouns, and modifier placement (a modifying phrase must sit next to the noun it logically describes, or you get a dangling modifier).

4 · Expression of Ideas (roughly 12–19% of the section)

Transitions. Choose the logical connector (however, therefore, in addition, finally, etc.) that correctly signals the relationship between two ideas — contrast, cause-and-effect, addition, or sequence/conclusion.

Rhetorical Synthesis. You are given bulleted notes and a specific rhetorical goal (e.g., “describe X to an audience unfamiliar with Y” or “emphasize a difference between X and Y”). Pick the single sentence that best accomplishes that exact goal using only the given notes — accuracy alone isn’t enough; the sentence must match the stated purpose.

Question 1 · Words in Context

The following text is adapted from Elizabeth von Arnim’s 1922 novel The Enchanted April. Mrs. Wilkins and her friend Rose are traveling in Italy.

“I’m going to have one of these gorgeous oranges,” said Mrs. Wilkins, staying where she was and reaching across to a black bowl piled with them. “Rose, how can you resist them. Look—have this one. Do have this beauty—” And she held out a big one.

As used in the text, what does the phrase “reaching across to” most nearly mean?

  • A) Joining with
  • B) Gaining on
  • C) Stretching toward
  • D) Arriving at

Correct answer: C) Stretching toward

✗ A) Joining with
“Joining with” would mean becoming attached to the bowl, which makes no sense here — she’s reaching for a piece of fruit, not merging with the bowl.

✗ B) Gaining on
“Gaining on” means catching up to something in a chase or competition. The bowl isn’t moving and there’s no race; she’s simply extending her arm toward it.

✓ C) Stretching toward
“Stretching toward” fits perfectly — Mrs. Wilkins “stays where she was” while extending her arm across to the bowl of oranges, then holds one out to Rose. That’s a physical reach, not a change of location.

✗ D) Arriving at
“Arriving at” is contradicted directly by the text, which says she is “staying where she was” — she never moves toward or arrives at the bowl at all.

Question 2 · Words in Context

A team of paleontologists has found a rich fossil deposit near Gulgong, Australia. The fossils are so well preserved that the team has been able to _______ detailed information about the life forms that left them behind, such as color patterns and how they interacted with other species.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?

  • A) occupy
  • B) hoard
  • C) reserve
  • D) obtain

Correct answer: D) obtain

✗ A) occupy
“Occupy” means to inhabit or fill a space, which doesn’t fit information at all; you don’t occupy facts.

✗ B) hoard
“Hoard” means to stockpile selfishly. There’s no suggestion the team is hiding or accumulating information rather than simply gathering it from the fossils.

✗ C) reserve
“Reserve” means to set aside for later use. The text is about extracting information now, not withholding it for future use.

✓ D) obtain
“Obtain” means to gain or acquire. Because the fossils are so well preserved, the team has been able to gain detailed information — such as color patterns — directly from them.

Question 3 · Words in Context

According to a team of neuroeconomists from the University of Zurich, ease of decision making may be linked to communication between two brain regions, the prefrontal cortex and the parietal cortex. Individuals tend to be more decisive if the information flow between the regions is intensified, whereas they make choices more slowly when information flow is _______.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?

  • A) reduced
  • B) evaluated
  • C) determined
  • D) acquired

Correct answer: A) reduced

✓ A) reduced
“Reduced” means decreased. The sentence sets up a contrast with “intensified”: people decide faster when information flow is strengthened, and more slowly when it is instead decreased.

✗ B) evaluated
“Evaluated” means assessed. The passage is about the rate of information flow between brain regions, not about someone judging or assessing that information.

✗ C) determined
“Determined” means influenced or decided. This doesn’t describe a decrease in flow, which is what the contrast with “intensified” requires.

✗ D) acquired
“Acquired” means obtained. The sentence needs a word that is the opposite of “intensified,” and acquired doesn’t signal a decrease in anything.

Question 4 · Words in Context

The War of 1812 has _______ place in historical memory in Britain, partly because it is overshadowed by the much larger concurrent conflict against Napoleonic France and partly because it essentially maintained the geopolitical status quo for Britain: the country neither gained nor lost significant territory or position as a result of its participation in the war.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?

  • A) a tenuous
  • B) an enduring
  • C) a contentious
  • D) a conspicuous

Correct answer: A) a tenuous

✓ A) a tenuous
“Tenuous” means weak or uncertain. Being overshadowed by a bigger war and producing no real territorial change both point to the War of 1812 having a shaky, uncertain foothold in British historical memory.

✗ B) an enduring
“Enduring” means lasting. This contradicts the point of the sentence, which is that the war is overshadowed and easily forgotten, not that its memory has persisted strongly.

✗ C) a contentious
“Contentious” means causing disagreement. Nothing in the text suggests people argue about the war’s memory; the issue is that it’s largely overlooked.

✗ D) a conspicuous
“Conspicuous” means easily noticed. This is the opposite of being overshadowed — the text explains why the war fades into the background, not why it stands out.

Question 5 · Words in Context

Claims about the original significance of Minoan bull-leaping rituals—depicted in paintings and sculptures from the second millennium BCE—are difficult to successfully _______. We know so little about the people archaeologists call the Minoans that assertions about what bull-leaping meant to them will almost inevitably rely on significant speculation and guesswork.

Which choice completes the text with the most logical and precise word or phrase?

  • A) imagine
  • B) summarize
  • C) defend
  • D) adjust

Correct answer: C) defend

✗ A) imagine
“Imagine” means to picture mentally. The text isn’t about visualizing what the rituals looked like; it’s about the difficulty of supporting claims regarding their meaning.

✗ B) summarize
“Summarize” means to recap briefly. The problem described isn’t condensing information, it’s a lack of evidence to justify any claim in the first place.

✓ C) defend
“Defend” means to support against argument. Because so little is known about the Minoans, any claim about the rituals’ meaning rests on speculation and is therefore hard to defend, or back up, convincingly.

✗ D) adjust
“Adjust” means to modify or correct. The text never discusses revising a claim based on new evidence; it discusses the basic difficulty of ever supporting one.

Question 6 · Text Structure & Purpose

The following text is adapted from Jerome K. Jerome’s 1889 novel Three Men in a Boat (To Say Nothing of the Dog). The narrator is traveling by boat with Harris and another friend.

[Harris] told us anecdotes of how he had gone across the [English] Channel when it was so rough that the passengers had to be tied into their [beds], and he and the captain were the only two living souls on board who were not ill. Sometimes it was he and the second mate who were not ill; but it was generally he and one other man. If not he and another man, then it was he by himself.

Which choice best describes the function of the underlined sentence in the text as a whole?

  • A) It indicates the reason for Harris’s eagerness to resume traveling.
  • B) It hints at Harris’s feeling that during an earlier boat trip, others didn’t include him in activities.
  • C) It emphasizes that Harris always boasts about his own constitution when speaking of a previous boat trip.
  • D) It reveals that although Harris claims to prefer solitary activities when traveling, he actually enjoys having company.

Correct answer: C) It emphasizes that Harris always boasts about his own constitution when speaking of a previous boat trip.

✗ A) It indicates the reason for Harris’s eagerness to resume traveling.
Nothing in the passage connects this anecdote to any eagerness on Harris’s part to travel again; it’s purely about how his story about the past keeps changing.

✗ B) It hints at Harris’s feeling that during an earlier boat trip, others didn’t include him in activities.
The sentence is about Harris shrinking the number of people who avoided seasickness in his story, not about him feeling excluded by others during the trip.

✓ C) It emphasizes that Harris always boasts about his own constitution when speaking of a previous boat trip.
The underlined sentence shows the number of unaffected passengers in Harris’s story steadily shrinking — from him and the captain, to him and one other, to just himself — a comic exaggeration that highlights Harris repeatedly bragging about his own hardiness whenever he retells this story.

✗ D) It reveals that although Harris claims to prefer solitary activities when traveling, he actually enjoys having company.
There is no indication in the text that Harris prefers solitude generally or that he secretly enjoys company; the humor is entirely about his exaggerated, shifting claims of endurance.

Question 7 · Text Structure & Purpose

Several studies have found negligible electoral consequences for governments that impose fiscal austerity measures, yet some European governments recently suffered electorally due to their austerity programs. Evelyne Huebscher and colleagues attribute this incongruity to governments’ tendency—not followed in the recent European cases—to implement austerity programs strategically to avoid electoral costs (e.g., setting spending cuts to take effect only after the next election), which has obscured the inherent political risks of austerity measures in the election data scholars have examined.

Which choice best describes the function of the underlined sentence in the text as a whole?

  • A) It explains a discrepancy between what has been observed in study settings and what has been observed in real-world settings that the text goes on to assert is attributable to the studies not using real-world data.
  • B) It identifies a conflict between research findings and recent events that the text goes on to suggest is a consequence of a complicating factor in the data used to generate those findings.
  • C) It presents a long-standing divergence in research findings that the text goes on to say is due to different groups of researchers using data that derive from different electoral circumstances.
  • D) It describes a recent exception to a general pattern in research findings that the text goes on to explain is a result of researchers underestimating the significance of inconsistencies in the data they’ve analyzed.

Correct answer: B) It identifies a conflict between research findings and recent events that the text goes on to suggest is a consequence of a complicating factor in the data used to generate those findings.

✗ A) It explains a discrepancy between what has been observed in study settings and what has been observed in real-world settings that the text goes on to assert is attributable to the studies not using real-world data.
The issue isn’t studies-versus-real-world data; the text never says the earlier studies failed to use real elections. It says those elections behaved differently because governments there used strategic timing that the recent cases didn’t.

✓ B) It identifies a conflict between research findings and recent events that the text goes on to suggest is a consequence of a complicating factor in the data used to generate those findings.
The underlined sentence sets up a conflict — studies say austerity has little electoral cost, yet recent European governments suffered electorally — and the text goes on to attribute this to a complicating factor: past data mostly came from governments that timed cuts strategically to avoid costs, which masked the real political risk. That’s a conflict explained by a data-generation quirk, matching choice B exactly.

✗ C) It presents a long-standing divergence in research findings that the text goes on to say is due to different groups of researchers using data that derive from different electoral circumstances.
The text doesn’t describe a “long-standing divergence” between separate groups of researchers; it describes one body of research findings clashing with one set of recent events.

✗ D) It describes a recent exception to a general pattern in research findings that the text goes on to explain is a result of researchers underestimating the significance of inconsistencies in the data they’ve analyzed.
The text never claims researchers underestimated inconsistencies in their data; it says the underlying data itself reflected a governmental strategy that obscured austerity’s true political risk.

Question 8 · Cross-Text Connections

Text 1
Graphic novels are increasingly popular in bookstores and libraries, but they shouldn’t be classified as literature. By definition, literature tells a story or conveys meaning through language only; graphic novels tell stories through illustrations and use language only sparingly, in captions and dialogue. Graphic novels are experienced as series of images and not as language, making them more similar to film than to literature.

Text 2
Graphic novels present their stories through both language and images. Without captions and dialogue, readers would be unable to understand what is depicted in the illustrations: the story results from the interaction of text and image. Moreover, Alison Bechdel’s Fun Home and many other graphic novels feature text that is as beautifully written as the prose found in many standard novels. Therefore, graphic novels qualify as literary texts.

Based on the texts, how would the author of Text 2 most likely respond to the overall argument presented in Text 1?

  • A) By asserting that language plays a more important role in graphic novels than the author of Text 1 recognizes
  • B) By acknowledging that the author of Text 1 has identified a flaw that is common to all graphic novels
  • C) By suggesting that the story lines of certain graphic novels are more difficult to understand than the author of Text 1 claims
  • D) By agreeing with the author of Text 1 that most graphic novels aren’t as well crafted as most literary works are

Correct answer: A) By asserting that language plays a more important role in graphic novels than the author of Text 1 recognizes

✓ A) By asserting that language plays a more important role in graphic novels than the author of Text 1 recognizes
Text 1 says graphic novels use language “only sparingly” and are experienced mainly as images. Text 2 directly counters this, arguing captions and dialogue are essential to understanding the story and that graphic-novel writing can be as accomplished as standard prose — i.e., language matters far more than Text 1 gives it credit for.

✗ B) By acknowledging that the author of Text 1 has identified a flaw that is common to all graphic novels
Text 2 never agrees that Text 1 has identified a real flaw; it disputes Text 1’s core premise that language is minor in graphic novels.

✗ C) By suggesting that the story lines of certain graphic novels are more difficult to understand than the author of Text 1 claims
Text 2 never discusses story lines being hard to understand; it argues the opposite, that text and image work together to make the story clear.

✗ D) By agreeing with the author of Text 1 that most graphic novels aren’t as well crafted as most literary works are
Text 2 argues graphic novels can be as well-written as standard novels (citing Fun Home), the opposite of agreeing they’re less well-crafted.

Question 9 · Central Ideas & Details

The following text is adapted from Ann Petry’s 1946 novel The Street. Lutie lives in an apartment in Harlem, New York.

The glow from the sunset was making the street radiant. The street is nice in this light, [Lutie] thought. It was swarming with children who were playing ball and darting back and forth across the sidewalk in complicated games of tag. Girls were skipping double dutch rope, going tirelessly through the exact center of a pair of ropes, jumping first on one foot and then the other.

Which choice best describes what is happening in the text?

  • A) Lutie is observing the appearance of the street at a particular time of day and the events occurring on it.
  • B) Lutie is annoyed by the noise of children playing games on her street.
  • C) Lutie is puzzled by the rules of certain children’s games.
  • D) Lutie is spending time alone in her apartment because she doesn’t want to interact with her neighbors.

Correct answer: A) Lutie is observing the appearance of the street at a particular time of day and the events occurring on it.

✓ A) Lutie is observing the appearance of the street at a particular time of day and the events occurring on it.
Lutie notices the sunset’s glow making the street look nice, then watches children playing ball, tag, and jumping rope — a straightforward observation of the street’s appearance at that moment and the activity happening on it.

✗ B) Lutie is annoyed by the noise of children playing games on her street.
The text says Lutie thinks the street looks “nice” in this light — nothing suggests she finds the children’s noise annoying.

Question 10 · Central Ideas & Details

Since its completion in 2014, Bosco Verticale (Vertical Forest)—a pair of residential towers in Milan, Italy, covered by vegetation—has become a striking symbol of environmental sustainability in architecture. Stefano Boeri intended his design, which features balconies that are home to hundreds of trees, to serve as a model for promoting urban biodiversity. However, the concept has faced skepticism: critics note that although the trees used in Bosco Verticale were specifically cultivated for the project, it’s too early to tell if they can thrive in this unusual setting.

According to the text, why are some critics skeptical of the concept behind Bosco Verticale?

  • A) Some essential aspects of Bosco Verticale’s design are difficult to adapt to locations other than Milan.
  • B) The plant life on Bosco Verticale ended up being less varied than Boeri had envisioned it would be.
  • C) The construction of Bosco Verticale was no less environmentally damaging than the construction of more conventional buildings is.
  • D) It is unclear whether Bosco Verticale can support the plant life included in its design.

Correct answer: D) It is unclear whether Bosco Verticale can support the plant life included in its design.

✗ A) Some essential aspects of Bosco Verticale’s design are difficult to adapt to locations other than Milan.
The text never discusses adapting the design to cities other than Milan; the concern is whether the current trees can survive where they already are.

✗ B) The plant life on Bosco Verticale ended up being less varied than Boeri had envisioned it would be.
There’s no comparison in the text between the variety Boeri wanted and what was actually planted; the critique is about survival, not diversity of species.

✗ C) The construction of Bosco Verticale was no less environmentally damaging than the construction of more conventional buildings is.
The text says nothing about the environmental impact of constructing the towers themselves, only about whether the vegetation can thrive after the fact.

✓ D) It is unclear whether Bosco Verticale can support the plant life included in its design.

Question 11 · Central Ideas & Details

Many literary theorists distinguish between fabula, a narrative’s content, and syuzhet, a narrative’s arrangement and presentation of events. In the film The Godfather Part II, the fabula is the story of the Corleone family, and the syuzhet is the presentation of the story as it alternates between two timelines in 1901 and 1958. But literary theorist Mikhail Bakhtin maintained that fabula and syuzhet are insufficient to completely describe a narrative—he held that systematic categorizations of artistic phenomena discount the subtle way in which meaning is created by interactions between the artist, the work, and the audience.

Which choice best states the main idea of the text?

  • A) Literary theorist Mikhail Bakhtin argued that there are important characteristics of narratives that are not fully encompassed by two concepts that other theorists have used to analyze narratives.
  • B) Literary theorist Mikhail Bakhtin claimed that meaning is not inherent in a narrative but is created when an audience encounters a narrative so that narratives are interpreted differently by different people.
  • C) The storytelling methods used in The Godfather Part II may seem unusually complicated, but they can be easily understood when two concepts from literary theory are utilized.
  • D) Narratives that are told out of chronological order are more difficult for audiences to understand than are narratives presented chronologically.

Correct answer: A) Literary theorist Mikhail Bakhtin argued that there are important characteristics of narratives that are not fully encompassed by two concepts that other theorists have used to analyze narratives.

✓ A) Literary theorist Mikhail Bakhtin argued that there are important characteristics of narratives that are not fully encompassed by two concepts that other theorists have used to analyze narratives.
The text introduces fabula and syuzhet as tools other theorists use, illustrates them with The Godfather Part II, then pivots to Bakhtin’s core claim that these two concepts alone can’t fully capture a narrative — which is precisely the main idea this choice states.

✗ B) Literary theorist Mikhail Bakhtin claimed that meaning is not inherent in a narrative but is created when an audience encounters a narrative so that narratives are interpreted differently by different people.
This overstates Bakhtin’s claim: the text says meaning arises from interactions between artist, work, and audience together, not solely from the audience’s encounter with the text, and it never discusses differing individual interpretations.

✗ C) The storytelling methods used in The Godfather Part II may seem unusually complicated, but they can be easily understood when two concepts from literary theory are utilized.
The film is used only to illustrate fabula and syuzhet; the text’s point is Bakhtin’s critique of those concepts, not that the film becomes “easily understood” once you apply them.

✗ D) Narratives that are told out of chronological order are more difficult for audiences to understand than are narratives presented chronologically.
The text never compares how difficult chronological versus non-chronological narratives are to understand; it uses the film only to define two theoretical terms before critiquing them.

Question 12 · Command of Evidence (Quantitative)

See figure (“Total Science Research Submissions by Topic, 2016–2019”) in the original College Board PDF above.

A student is researching the trends in the topics submitted to a national science fair for high school students. The graph shows the number of submissions by topic that were made each year. Based on the data in the graph, the student claims that there were more medicine and health research topics submitted in 2019 than in any other year.

Which choice most effectively uses data from the graph to support the underlined claim?

  • A) In 2016, the number of cellular and molecular biology topic submissions was the same as the number of animal science topic submissions.
  • B) In 2019, there were more physics and space science topic submissions than there were medicine and health topic submissions.
  • C) The lowest number of animal science topic submissions in a year was approximately 95 in 2016.
  • D) The highest number of medicine and health topic submissions during the period shown is approximately 285 in 2019.

Correct answer: D) The highest number of medicine and health topic submissions during the period shown is approximately 285 in 2019.

✗ A) In 2016, the number of cellular and molecular biology topic submissions was the same as the number of animal science topic submissions.
This compares two unrelated categories (cellular/molecular biology and animal science) in 2016 — it says nothing about medicine and health in 2019, so it can’t support the claim.

✗ B) In 2019, there were more physics and space science topic submissions than there were medicine and health topic submissions.
This compares physics/space science to medicine and health in 2019, which doesn’t address whether 2019 was medicine and health’s highest year — and it also misreads the graph, since medicine and health submissions were higher than physics and space science in 2019.

✗ C) The lowest number of animal science topic submissions in a year was approximately 95 in 2016.
This is about the lowest point for a completely different category (animal science), not about medicine and health reaching its peak in 2019.

✓ D) The highest number of medicine and health topic submissions during the period shown is approximately 285 in 2019.
The graph shows medicine and health submissions staying below roughly 250 in 2016, 2017, and 2018, then rising to approximately 285 in 2019 — the highest point on that line across the whole period, which is exactly what the underlined claim needs.

Question 13 · Command of Evidence (Textual)

Fish whose DNA has been modified to include genetic material from other species are known as transgenic. Some transgenic fish have genes from jellyfish that result in fluorescence (that is, they glow in the dark). Although these fish were initially engineered for research purposes in the 1990s, they were sold as pets in the 2000s and can now be found in the wild in creeks in Brazil. A student in a biology seminar who is writing a paper on these fish asserts that their escape from Brazilian fish farms into the wild may have significant negative long-term ecological effects.

Which quotation from a researcher would best support the student’s assertion?

  • A) “In one site in the wild where transgenic fish were observed, females outnumbered males, while in another the numbers of females and males were equivalent.”
  • B) “Though some presence of transgenic fish in the wild has been recorded, there are insufficient studies of the impact of those fish on the ecosystems into which they are introduced.”
  • C) “The ecosystems into which transgenic fish are known to have been introduced may represent a subset of the ecosystems into which the fish have actually been introduced.”
  • D) “Through interbreeding, transgenic fish might introduce the trait of fluorescence into wild fish populations, making those populations more vulnerable to predators.”

Correct answer: D) “Through interbreeding, transgenic fish might introduce the trait of fluorescence into wild fish populations, making those populations more vulnerable to predators.”

✗ A) “In one site in the wild where transgenic fish were observed, females outnumbered males, while in another the numbers of females and males were equivalent.”
This only compares ratios of females to males at two sites; it doesn’t mention any negative or long-term ecological consequence of the fish’s presence.

✗ B) “Though some presence of transgenic fish in the wild has been recorded, there are insufficient studies of the impact of those fish on the ecosystems into which they are introduced.”

“Having published” is also a nonfinite form; it can’t stand as the sentence’s only verb and leaves the clause incomplete.

  • D) In first-class levers, the fulcrum is located between the effort and the load, but in second-class levers, the load is located between the effort and the fulcrum.

Correct answer: D) In first-class levers, the fulcrum is located between the effort and the load, but in second-class levers, the load is located between the effort and the fulcrum.

✗ A) In levers, the effort is the force applied to the lever; the load, in contrast, is the force that the lever exerts on another object.
This contrasts effort and load as general concepts, not first-class levers with second-class levers specifically.

✗ B) In first-class and second-class levers, the fulcrum and the load are in different locations.
This vaguely states the fulcrum and load are in “different locations” without specifying what those different arrangements actually are, so it fails to give a real contrast.

✗ C) First-class levers are simple machines consisting of a rigid beam and a fulcrum, but then again, the same is true of second-class levers.
This states a similarity (both are simple machines with a beam and fulcrum) rather than a contrast, so it doesn’t accomplish the stated goal.

✓ D) In first-class levers, the fulcrum is located between the effort and the load, but in second-class levers, the load is located between the effort and the fulcrum.
This choice states the specific, contrasting arrangement for each lever type — fulcrum-between-effort-and-load for first-class versus load-between-effort-and-fulcrum for second-class — giving a precise, direct contrast between the two.

Question 33 · Rhetorical Synthesis

While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes:

  • Earthquakes start at a point called a “focus” and spread out from there as seismic waves.
  • The two types of seismic waves that travel beneath Earth’s surface are primary waves (P waves) and secondary waves (S waves).
  • P waves travel more quickly beneath Earth’s surface than do S waves.
  • P waves compress and expand the ground, causing it to move backward and forward.
  • S waves cause the ground to move from side to side.

The student wants to emphasize a similarity between P waves and S waves. Which choice most effectively uses relevant information from the notes to accomplish this goal?

  • A) P waves and S waves both travel beneath Earth’s surface, causing the ground to move.
  • B) P waves travel away from an earthquake’s starting point at a higher rate of speed than do S waves.
  • C) Spreading out from the focus of an earthquake, P waves move the ground backward and forward.
  • D) Although P waves and S waves start at the same point, they behave very differently.

Correct answer: A) P waves and S waves both travel beneath Earth’s surface, causing the ground to move.

✓ A) P waves and S waves both travel beneath Earth’s surface, causing the ground to move.
This states a feature both wave types share — traveling beneath Earth’s surface and causing ground motion — directly emphasizing a similarity between P waves and S waves, exactly as the goal requires.

✗ B) P waves travel away from an earthquake’s starting point at a higher rate of speed than do S waves.
This highlights that P waves are faster than S waves, which is a difference, not a similarity.

✗ C) Spreading out from the focus of an earthquake, P waves move the ground backward and forward.
This describes only P waves’ motion pattern; it says nothing about S waves, so it can’t emphasize a similarity between the two.

✗ D) Although P waves and S waves start at the same point, they behave very differently.
This explicitly emphasizes that the two wave types “behave very differently,” the opposite of the requested similarity.

Digital SAT FAQ

What is on Module 2 of the Digital SAT Reading & Writing section?

Module 2 is a 32-minute, 33-question module with the same four content domains as Module 1: Craft & Structure, Information & Ideas, Standard English Conventions, and Expression of Ideas. Unlike Module 1, Module 2 is stage-adaptive: the difficulty of the questions you see depends on how you performed on Module 1.

How is the Digital SAT Reading & Writing section scored?

The Reading & Writing section is scored from 200 to 800. Module 1 is the same for every student; Module 2 is stage-adaptive — students who performed well on Module 1 are routed to a harder Module 2 (the upper module) with access to the full 800, and students who did not are routed to an easier Module 2 that typically caps in the mid-600s.

How long is the Digital SAT Reading & Writing section?

64 minutes total, split into two 32-minute modules of 33 questions each. Together with the Math section (70 minutes), the full Digital SAT is 2 hours 14 minutes.

Is Module 2 harder than Module 1 on the Digital SAT?

It depends on your Module 1 performance. If you score well on Module 1, you are routed into the harder version of Module 2, which unlocks access to the top of the 200–800 scale — and that’s the version reflected in this walkthrough. If you don’t score as well, you are routed into an easier version of Module 2 that caps out in the mid-600s. Both versions test the identical four domains and skills; only the difficulty of individual questions differs.

What is a good Digital SAT Reading & Writing score?

As a general reference: 600 ≈ 70th percentile, 700 ≈ 90th percentile, 750+ ≈ top 5%. For MIT, Stanford, Harvard, and the Ivy League, competitive applicants score 750+ on Reading & Writing. For SUNY Binghamton or UNC, 700+. For most CUNY campuses, 580–660.
