Why summer math actually matters on the Upper West Side

UWS public school students are some of the strongest in the city — but the school-year math experience is uneven. Some classrooms are accelerated; some hold back the top half of the room to keep the middle moving; some teach a brilliant curriculum but with thirty kids per teacher; some have rotating staff. The result, year after year, is the same pattern: students who do nothing over the summer arrive in September having quietly lost three to six weeks of math. Students who do too much — drill packets, online platforms without a teacher — arrive bored, faster but not deeper. The right summer math program does two things at once: locks in what was learned, and opens up what is coming next.

That is the entire design behind SOMATH summer. Every track has a "review and lock" phase in June and a "preview and stretch" phase in July and August. By Labor Day, students walk into their UWS public school classrooms not just caught up, but a half-step ahead.

What a typical SOMATH summer week looks like

Each cohort meets two to three times per week, usually 60 to 90 minutes per session depending on the track. Cohorts are six to eight students. Every session has the same shape: a 5-minute warm-up that activates last session's work, a 20–30 minute new-topic block with whiteboard problems, a 20-minute guided practice in mixed-ability pairs, and a 5–10 minute wrap that previews the next session. No worksheet stacks. No screens unless the topic genuinely benefits from Desmos or GeoGebra.

Parents get a short written update at the end of each two-week module — what was covered, what the student did well, where the gap is, and the homework for the next two weeks. Honest, specific, and short. The same way we communicate during the school year.

FAQ

What grades do SOMATH summer programs serve?

Kindergarten through 12th grade, with tracks for every level from foundational number sense to AP Calc BC and college-prep statistics. Most rising 4th–10th graders see the biggest jump — the years when the curriculum gets dense fastest.

Do you offer in-person or online?

Summer is in-person at 226 W 79th Street, between Broadway and Amsterdam. We find that in-person small groups produce a deeper jump in confidence and problem-solving than any online option we have tested. We do offer year-round online for families who travel and want continuity.

My child is at an independent school, not a UWS public school. Can they still join?

Absolutely. About 40% of our summer roster is independent school students (Dalton, Trinity, Collegiate, Riverdale, Horace Mann, Hunter College HS, Brearley, Spence, and others). The tracks are built around math content, not school affiliation. The free evaluation places every student precisely.

What is the free 60-minute evaluation?

Every new family books a free, no-obligation 60-minute evaluation in our classroom. Your student works through a diagnostic with a SOMATH teacher; we follow up within 48 hours with a written diagnostic — strengths, gaps, recommended track, and recommended cohort. Yours to keep whether you enroll or not. Book the evaluation.

Is SHSAT prep too late if my child starts in summer?

Not at all — summer is the ideal start. Most students who score above 650 on SHSAT math start prep four to six months before the test, and summer is exactly that window for the fall sitting. Starting in September is also possible but tighter; we tell families honestly when timing is the bigger constraint than ability.
