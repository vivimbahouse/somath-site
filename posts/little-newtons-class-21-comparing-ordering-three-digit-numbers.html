Comparing and Ordering Three-Digit Numbers

Compare the hundreds

To compare two three-digit numbers, start on the left, in the hundreds place, because hundreds are the biggest groups. In the image, 398 has 3 hundreds flats and 402 has 4 hundreds flats. Four hundreds is more than three hundreds, so 402 is greater.

The hundreds decide even when the other digits look big. 398 has a 9 and an 8, but 398 is still less than 400, and 402 is more than 400. So 398 < 402. The symbol always opens toward the greater number, like a hungry mouth: < means “is less than” and > means “is greater than.”

Practice: try these five questions

Q1

Which number is greater, 512 or 489?

Answer

512

Compare the hundreds first: 512 has 5 hundreds and 489 has 4 hundreds. 5 hundreds > 4 hundreds, so 512 > 489.

Q2

Which number is less, 297 or 301?

Answer

297

297 has 2 hundreds and 301 has 3 hundreds. 2 hundreds < 3 hundreds, so 297 < 301. 297 is less than 300 and 301 is more than 300.

Q3

Fill in > or <: 634 ___ 721

Answer

634 < 721

634 has 6 hundreds and 721 has 7 hundreds. 6 hundreds < 7 hundreds, so 634 < 721.

Q4

Ben says 199 is greater than 200 because 9 is bigger than 2. Is he right? Explain.

Answer

No; 199 < 200

Ben compared the wrong places. Start with the hundreds: 199 has 1 hundred and 200 has 2 hundreds. 1 hundred < 2 hundreds, so 199 < 200.

Q5

Which of these numbers is greater than 600: 589, 607, or 596?

Answer

607

607 is the only number with 6 hundreds. 589 and 596 have only 5 hundreds, so they are less than 600. 607 > 600.

Compare the tens

When the hundreds digits are the same, move one place to the right and compare the tens. In the image, 563 and 536 both have 5 hundreds, so the hundreds cannot decide. Look at the tens: 563 has 6 tens and 536 has 3 tens.

6 tens is more than 3 tens, so 563 > 536. Once the tens are different, we stop. The ones do not matter, even though 536 has more ones. A place value chart helps: line up the hundreds, tens, and ones, then find the first column where the digits are different.

Practice: try these five questions

Q6

Which number is greater, 472 or 427?

Answer

472

Both numbers have 4 hundreds, so compare the tens: 472 has 7 tens and 427 has 2 tens. 7 tens > 2 tens, so 472 > 427.

Q7

Fill in > or <: 358 ___ 385

Answer

358 < 385

The hundreds are the same: 3 and 3. Compare the tens: 5 tens < 8 tens. So 358 < 385.

Q8

Which number is less, 916 or 961?

Answer

916

Both have 9 hundreds. Compare the tens: 916 has 1 ten and 961 has 6 tens. 1 ten < 6 tens, so 916 < 961.

Q9

Which number is greater, 245 or 219?

Answer

245

Both have 2 hundreds. Compare the tens: 4 tens > 1 ten, so 245 > 219. The 9 ones in 219 do not matter, because the tens already decided.

Q10

What digit can go in the blank to make this true? 6_2 > 682

Answer

9 (692 > 682)

Both numbers have 6 hundreds and 2 ones, so the tens must decide. The missing tens digit must be greater than 8, and the only digit greater than 8 is 9. Check: 692 > 682.

Compare the ones

If the hundreds and the tens are the same, the ones decide. In the image, 741 and 748 both have 7 hundreds and 4 tens. The only place that is different is the ones place: 1 one and 8 ones.

1 is less than 8, so 741 < 748. This is the last place we can check. If the ones are also the same, then every digit matches and the numbers are equal. So the rule for comparing is: hundreds first, then tens, then ones, and the first place that is different tells you the answer.

Practice: try these five questions

Q11

Which number is greater, 523 or 527?

Answer

527

Both numbers have 5 hundreds and 2 tens. Compare the ones: 3 ones < 7 ones. So 527 > 523.

Q12

Fill in > or <: 839 ___ 834

Answer

839 > 834

The hundreds match (8) and the tens match (3). Compare the ones: 9 > 4. So 839 > 834.

Q13

Which number is less, 290 or 296?

Answer

290

Both have 2 hundreds and 9 tens. Compare the ones: 0 ones < 6 ones. So 290 < 296.

Q14

Write a number with 4 hundreds and 6 tens that is greater than 465.

Answer

466, 467, 468, or 469

The number must start with 4 hundreds and 6 tens, just like 465, so the ones must decide. The ones digit must be greater than 5: 6, 7, 8, or 9. Any of 466, 467, 468, or 469 is correct.

Q15

Maya looked at 718 and 715. She saw that both have 7 hundreds and said, “They are equal.” What should she do next?

Answer

Keep comparing; 718 > 715

Matching hundreds do not make the numbers equal. Maya should compare the tens next: 1 ten and 1 ten match too. Then compare the ones: 8 > 5. So 718 > 715.

Choose the correct symbol

There are three comparison symbols. < means “is less than,” > means “is greater than,” and = means “is equal to.” The open side of < or > always faces the greater number, and the point faces the smaller number. Read the sentence from left to right: 405 < 450 says “405 is less than 450.”

In the image, 405 and 450 have the same hundreds, and 0 tens < 5 tens, so 405 < 450. For 610 and 601, 1 ten > 0 tens, so 610 > 601. Sometimes one side is written in expanded form. First add the parts: 300 + 20 = 320. Both sides show the same value, so 320 = 300 + 20.

Practice: try these five questions

Q16

Fill in <, >, or =: 509 ___ 590

Answer

509 < 590

Both have 5 hundreds. Compare the tens: 0 tens < 9 tens. So 509 < 590.

Q17

Fill in <, >, or =: 874 ___ 847

Answer

874 > 847

Both have 8 hundreds. Compare the tens: 7 tens > 4 tens. So 874 > 847.

Q18

Fill in <, >, or =: 460 ___ 400 + 60

Answer

460 = 400 + 60

Add the expanded form first: 400 + 60 = 460. Both sides are 460, so they are equal: 460 = 400 + 60.

Q19

Fill in <, >, or =: 700 + 5 ___ 750

Answer

700 + 5 < 750

700 + 5 = 705. Now compare 705 and 750. Both have 7 hundreds; 0 tens < 5 tens. So 705 < 750, which means 700 + 5 < 750.

Q20

Fill in <, >, or =: 300 + 90 + 2 ___ 300 + 20 + 9

Answer

300 + 90 + 2 > 300 + 20 + 9

Write each side as one number: 300 + 90 + 2 = 392 and 300 + 20 + 9 = 329. Both have 3 hundreds; 9 tens > 2 tens. So 392 > 329.

Use a number line

On a number line, numbers get greater as you move to the right and less as you move to the left. In the image, the line goes from 350 to 400 and each mark is 10 more than the one before: 350, 360, 370, 380, 390, 400.

The dots show 360, 370, and 390. 360 is farthest left, so it is the least. 390 is farthest right, so it is the greatest. Reading the dots from left to right puts the numbers in order: 360 < 370 < 390. To place a number, count by tens from the first mark until you reach it.

Practice: try these five questions

Q21

A number line goes from 500 to 600 with a mark every 10. Which number is farther to the right, 540 or 580? Which is greater?

Answer

580; 580 is greater

Counting by tens from 500: 510, 520, 530, 540, … 580. 580 comes after 540, so it is farther right. Farther right means greater: 580 > 540.

Q22

A number line goes from 800 to 900 by tens. Where would you put 820, 850, and 810? Which one is farthest left?

Answer

810

810 is 1 mark after 800, 820 is 2 marks after 800, and 850 is 5 marks after 800. 810 is closest to 800, so it is farthest left and is the least.

Q23

Is 630 to the left or to the right of 660 on a number line? Write a comparison with < or >.

Answer

Left; 630 < 660

Both have 6 hundreds, and 3 tens < 6 tens, so 630 comes before 660. Numbers that come first are to the left. So 630 is to the left, and 630 < 660.

Q24

Three dots are on a number line from 200 to 300: 290, 240, and 270. Write them in order from left to right.

Answer

240, 270, 290

Count by tens from 200: 210, 220, 230, 240 comes first, then 270, then 290. From left to right the order is 240 < 270 < 290.

Q25

A number line from 100 to 200 has a mark every 10. What number is at the mark between 160 and 180?

Answer

170

Counting by tens: 160, 170, 180. The mark between 160 and 180 is 170, and 160 < 170 < 180.

Order from least to greatest

Least to greatest means start with the smallest number and end with the biggest. In the image, the numbers 632, 623, 362, and 326 use the same three digits, so we must look carefully at each place.

First, group by hundreds. 362 and 326 have 3 hundreds; 632 and 623 have 6 hundreds. The 3-hundreds group comes first. Inside each group, compare the tens: 2 tens < 6 tens, so 326 < 362; 2 tens < 3 tens, so 623 < 632. Put the groups together: 326 < 362 < 623 < 632. Cross out each number as you write it so none is missed.

Q26

Order from least to greatest: 451, 415, 514

Answer

415, 451, 514

Group by hundreds: 451 and 415 have 4 hundreds; 514 has 5 hundreds. In the 4-hundreds group, 1 ten < 5 tens, so 415 < 451. Order: 415 < 451 < 514.

Q27

Order from least to greatest: 278, 728, 287

Answer

278, 287, 728

278 and 287 have 2 hundreds; 728 has 7 hundreds, so it is last. Compare 278 and 287: 7 tens < 8 tens. Order: 278 < 287 < 728.

Q28

Order from least to greatest: 904, 490, 409, 940

Answer

409, 490, 904, 940

4-hundreds group: 409 and 490. 0 tens < 9 tens, so 409 < 490. 9-hundreds group: 904 and 940. 0 tens < 4 tens, so 904 < 940. Order: 409 < 490 < 904 < 940.

Q29

Order from least to greatest: 645, 654, 564, 546

Answer

546, 564, 645, 654

5-hundreds group: 546 and 564; 4 tens < 6 tens, so 546 < 564. 6-hundreds group: 645 and 654; 4 tens < 5 tens, so 645 < 654. Order: 546 < 564 < 645 < 654.

Q30

Zoe wrote these numbers from least to greatest: 213, 231, 223, 312. Which two numbers are in the wrong spots? Fix her list.

Answer

231 and 223; 213, 223, 231, 312

Three numbers have 2 hundreds: 213, 231, and 223. Compare their tens: 1 ten < 2 tens < 3 tens, so the order is 213, 223, 231. Zoe swapped 231 and 223. 312 has 3 hundreds and goes last: 213 < 223 < 231 < 312.

Order from greatest to least

Greatest to least is the opposite order: start with the biggest number and end with the smallest. The steps are the same. In the image, 809, 890, 908, and 980 are grouped by hundreds: 908 and 980 have 9 hundreds, and 809 and 890 have 8 hundreds. The 9-hundreds group comes first.

If the hundreds match, compare the tens. 980 has 8 tens and 908 has 0 tens, so 980 > 908. 890 has 9 tens and 809 has 0 tens, so 890 > 809. The final order is 980 > 908 > 890 > 809. When we write greatest to least, every symbol is >.

Q31

Order from greatest to least: 736, 763, 673

Answer

763, 736, 673

736 and 763 have 7 hundreds; 673 has 6 hundreds, so it goes last. Compare 763 and 736: 6 tens > 3 tens. Order: 763 > 736 > 673.

Q32

Order from greatest to least: 182, 821, 218, 812

Answer

821, 812, 218, 182

8-hundreds group: 821 and 812; 2 tens > 1 ten, so 821 > 812. Then the 2-hundreds number 218, then the 1-hundred number 182. Order: 821 > 812 > 218 > 182.

Q33

Order from greatest to least: 505, 550, 455

Answer

550, 505, 455

550 and 505 have 5 hundreds; 5 tens > 0 tens, so 550 > 505. 455 has only 4 hundreds and goes last. Order: 550 > 505 > 455.

Q34

Order from greatest to least: 990, 909, 999, 900

Answer

999, 990, 909, 900

All four have 9 hundreds, so compare the tens. 999 and 990 have 9 tens; 909 and 900 have 0 tens. Then compare the ones: 999 > 990 and 909 > 900. Order: 999 > 990 > 909 > 900.

Q35

Use the digits 3, 7, and 1 once each. Make the greatest three-digit number and the least three-digit number.

Answer

Greatest 731; least 137

For the greatest number, put the biggest digit in the hundreds place, then the next biggest: 7, 3, 1 makes 731. For the least number, put the smallest digit first: 1, 3, 7 makes 137. Check: 731 > 137.

Compare and order in stories

Comparing and ordering help us answer real questions: Who has the most? Who has the fewest? In the image, three classes collected cans: Class A 284, Class B 248, and Class C 304. To order the totals, use the same steps. 248 and 284 have 2 hundreds, and 4 tens < 8 tens, so 248 < 284. 304 has 3 hundreds, so it is greatest: 248 < 284 < 304. That means Class B, Class A, Class C.

“How many more” asks for the difference. To find how many more cans Class A needs to tie Class C, count up from 284 to 304 or subtract: 304 − 284 = 20. After ordering, always go back to the story and answer with the names and labels it asks for.

Practice: try these five questions

Q36

The library lent 342 books on Monday and 324 books on Tuesday. On which day did it lend more books?

Answer

Monday

Both numbers have 3 hundreds. Compare the tens: 4 tens > 2 tens, so 342 > 324. The library lent more books on Monday.

Q37

In a reading game, Ava scored 615 points, Ben scored 561, and Cleo scored 651. Order the scores from least to greatest. Who won?

Answer

561, 615, 651; Cleo won

561 has 5 hundreds, so it is least. 615 and 651 have 6 hundreds; 1 ten < 5 tens, so 615 < 651. Order: Ben 561 < Ava 615 < Cleo 651. Cleo has the greatest score, so Cleo won.

Q38

A zoo had 408 visitors on Saturday and 480 visitors on Sunday. Write a comparison using < and tell which day was busier.

Answer

408 < 480; Sunday

Both have 4 hundreds. Compare the tens: 0 tens < 8 tens, so 408 < 480. Sunday had more visitors, so Sunday was busier.

Q39

Class A read 230 pages and Class B read 250 pages. How many more pages does Class A need to read to tie Class B?

Answer

20 more pages

Count up by tens from 230: 240, 250. That is 2 tens, or 20. Check: 250 − 230 = 20. Class A needs 20 more pages.

Q40

Jo has 375 stickers, Kim has 357 stickers, and Lu has 537 stickers. Order them from greatest to least.

Answer

Lu 537, Jo 375, Kim 357

537 has 5 hundreds, so it is greatest. 375 and 357 have 3 hundreds; 7 tens > 5 tens, so 375 > 357. Order: Lu 537 > Jo 375 > Kim 357.

Homework review

These eight questions revisit the eight ideas in this lesson, in the same order as the images. Compare from left to right, and keep the answers closed until you have tried.

R1

Which number is greater, 823 or 794?

Answer

823

Compare the hundreds: 8 hundreds > 7 hundreds. So 823 > 794.

R2

Fill in > or <: 461 ___ 416

Answer

461 > 416

Both have 4 hundreds. Compare the tens: 6 tens > 1 ten. So 461 > 416.

R3

Which number is less, 358 or 352?

Answer

352

Both have 3 hundreds and 5 tens. Compare the ones: 2 < 8. So 352 < 358.

R4

Fill in <, >, or =: 600 + 40 ___ 604

Answer

600 + 40 > 604

600 + 40 = 640. Compare 640 and 604: same hundreds, and 4 tens > 0 tens. So 640 > 604.

R5

On a number line, which number is farthest left: 910, 940, or 920?

Answer

910

All three have 9 hundreds. 910 has the fewest tens, so it comes first on the line and is farthest left: 910 < 920 < 940.

R6

Order from least to greatest: 372, 237, 327, 273

Answer

237, 273, 327, 372

2-hundreds group: 237 < 273 (3 tens < 7 tens). 3-hundreds group: 327 < 372 (2 tens < 7 tens). Order: 237 < 273 < 327 < 372.

R7

Order from greatest to least: 486, 468, 648, 684

Answer

684, 648, 486, 468

6-hundreds group: 684 > 648 (8 tens > 4 tens). 4-hundreds group: 486 > 468 (8 tens > 6 tens). Order: 684 > 648 > 486 > 468.

R8

The Red Team collected 527 bottles and the Blue Team collected 572 bottles. Which team collected more?

Answer

The Blue Team

Both have 5 hundreds. Compare the tens: 7 tens > 2 tens, so 572 > 527. The Blue Team collected more bottles.

Homework word problems

Work from the easier stories toward the challenge. Write the numbers in a place value chart or on a number line, compare or order them, and answer in a sentence with the names and labels the story asks for.

W1

Ella has 245 stickers. Max has 254 stickers. Who has more stickers?

Answer

Max

Both numbers have 2 hundreds. Compare the tens: 5 tens > 4 tens, so 254 > 245. Max has more stickers.

W2

A bakery made 318 muffins and 381 cookies. Did it make fewer muffins or fewer cookies?

Answer

Fewer muffins

Both have 3 hundreds. Compare the tens: 1 ten < 8 tens, so 318 < 381. The bakery made fewer muffins.

W3

A school garden has 136 tulips, 163 daisies, and 116 roses. Order the flowers from least to greatest.

Answer

Roses 116, tulips 136, daisies 163

All three have 1 hundred. Compare the tens: 1 ten < 3 tens < 6 tens. Order: 116 < 136 < 163, so roses, then tulips, then daisies.

W4

Three towers are 406 feet, 460 feet, and 346 feet tall. Which tower is tallest? Which is shortest?

Answer

Tallest 460 feet; shortest 346 feet

346 has 3 hundreds, so it is shortest. 406 and 460 have 4 hundreds; 6 tens > 0 tens, so 460 is tallest. Order: 346 < 406 < 460.

W5

In a video game, Noah scored 709, Ivy scored 790, and Sam scored 779. Make a scoreboard from greatest to least.

Answer

Ivy 790, Sam 779, Noah 709

All three have 7 hundreds. Compare the tens: 9 tens > 7 tens > 0 tens. Order: 790 > 779 > 709, so Ivy, Sam, Noah.

W6

Mystery number: I am between 520 and 530. My ones digit is 7. What number am I?

Answer

527

Between 520 and 530 means 5 hundreds and 2 tens. The ones digit is 7, so the number is 527. Check: 520 < 527 < 530.

W7

Lena read 260 pages and Omar read 290 pages. Who read more, and how many more pages?

Answer

Omar; 30 more pages

Same hundreds; 9 tens > 6 tens, so Omar read more. Count up by tens from 260: 270, 280, 290 is 3 tens. 290 − 260 = 30 more pages.

W8

A bake sale raised $435 for Class A, $453 for Class B, $345 for Class C, and $354 for Class D. Order the classes from least to greatest money raised.

Answer

C $345, D $354, A $435, B $453

3-hundreds group: 345 < 354 (4 tens < 5 tens). 4-hundreds group: 435 < 453 (3 tens < 5 tens). Order: 345 < 354 < 435 < 453, so Class C, Class D, Class A, Class B.

W9

Store 1 has 580 blocks. Store 2 has 5 hundreds, 9 tens, and 0 ones blocks. Which store has more blocks?

Answer

Store 2

Store 2 has 500 + 90 + 0 = 590 blocks. Compare 590 and 580: same hundreds, and 9 tens > 8 tens. 590 > 580, so Store 2 has more.

W10

Use the digits 4, 0, and 8 once each. Make the greatest and the least three-digit numbers. (A three-digit number cannot start with 0.)

Answer

Greatest 840; least 408

Greatest: put the biggest digits first, 8 then 4 then 0, to make 840. Least: 0 cannot go first, so use the smallest other digit, 4, in the hundreds place, then 0, then 8: 408. Check: 840 > 408.

W11

The goal for a can drive is 500 cans. Class A has 470 cans and Class B has 490 cans. Which class is closer to the goal? How many more cans does it need?

Answer

Class B; 10 more cans

490 > 470, so Class B has more cans and is closer to 500. Count up by tens from 490: 500 is 1 more ten. Class B needs 10 more cans (Class A would need 30).

W12

Challenge: Numbers A, B, and C each have 6 hundreds. A has 4 tens and 2 ones. B has the same tens as A but more ones than A. C has fewer tens than A. Order A, B, and C from least to greatest.

Answer

C, A, B

A is 642. B has 6 hundreds and 4 tens with more than 2 ones, so B is greater than 642 (for example 645). C has 6 hundreds and fewer than 4 tens, so C is less than 640 and less than 642. Order: C < A < B.

Quick questions and answers

How do you compare three-digit numbers?

Compare from left to right. Start with the hundreds; if they match, compare the tens; if those match too, compare the ones. The first place that is different decides, so 563 > 536 because 6 tens > 3 tens.

How can children remember the < and > symbols?

The open side of the symbol always faces the greater number, and the point faces the smaller number. Read from left to right: 398 < 402 says “398 is less than 402,” and 563 > 536 says “563 is greater than 536.”

When are two three-digit numbers equal?

Two numbers are equal when every place has the same digit. Expanded form can show the same value in a different way, so 320 = 300 + 20.

How does a number line help with comparing?

Numbers get greater as you move right on a number line. On a line from 350 to 400, 360 is to the left of 390, so 360 < 390.

How do you order three-digit numbers from least to greatest?

Group the numbers by their hundreds digit, put the groups in order, then compare the tens inside each group (and the ones if needed). For 632, 623, 362, and 326 the order is 326 < 362 < 623 < 632. Greatest to least is the same list backward.

Does a student need to attend an earlier class first?

No. This is a self-contained Little Newtons lesson in a rolling rotation. It reviews the hundreds, tens, and ones ideas it uses; class numbers identify topics, not attendance prerequisites.
