Math Tutoring in NYC: SOMATH vs Russian Math vs Kumon vs Mathnasium Compared

Most NYC families looking for math tutoring end up choosing between the same four programs — SOMATH, Russian School of Math, Kumon, and Mathnasium — and the marketing makes them sound interchangeable. They are not. Here is a category-by-category side-by-side comparison of the four NYC math tutoring options across teaching style, group size, instructor background, diagnostics, program length, homework load, and SHSAT / SAT / AP readiness, so you can match the program to your child instead of the other way around.

School of Math May 12, 2026 8 min read

The short answer: if you are shopping for math tutoring in NYC and choosing among SOMATH, Russian School of Math (RSM), Kumon, and Mathnasium, the right choice depends on what your child actually needs from a math tutor. SOMATH is built for families who want concept-first, taught-live small-group math tutoring aimed explicitly at SHSAT, SAT, AP Pre-Calculus, AP Statistics, and AP Calculus. RSM is a strong fit for competition-math-focused families willing to commit to a year-long, fast-paced, drill-heavy program. Kumon is a self-paced worksheet system with no live teaching. Mathnasium is a center-based drill model with mixed-level rooms.

The rest of this post walks through the eight dimensions every NYC parent should compare before signing up — and what each one actually means once your child is sitting in the classroom.

NYC math tutoring compared, category by category

1. Teaching style

This is the single biggest difference, and the one parents most often overlook on a tour. Each program has a fundamentally different theory of how children learn math.

  • SOMATH: Concept-first, taught live. A SOMATH instructor stands at the whiteboard and develops the idea — why a method works, where it breaks, and how to choose the right approach — before any practice happens. Students participate live, ask questions, and watch the reasoning unfold.
  • Russian School of Math (RSM): Fast-paced, drill-heavy. RSM is famously rigorous but leans on volume and pace. Students work through demanding problem sets quickly, often with limited time per concept.
  • Kumon: Self-paced worksheets. Kumon is not a class. Students arrive, pick up a worksheet packet at their current level, work independently, and have a tutor check answers. There is no live teaching.
  • Mathnasium: Center-based drilling. Mathnasium uses a drop-in model where children work through assigned drill exercises at the center, with instructors circulating to answer questions in a mixed-level room.

If your child needs to understand why — which is the prerequisite for SHSAT, SAT, and AP success — they need live teaching. Worksheets do not explain reasoning. A mixed-level room cannot pause to develop a concept.

2. Group size

Group size determines how much the instructor can actually adapt to your child during a single session.

  • SOMATH: Small cohorts of same-level students. The instructor knows each child by name, watches their pencil, and adjusts the lesson in real time.
  • RSM: Large classrooms. Strong students thrive; struggling students can be left behind.
  • Kumon: Independent work — no group instruction at all.
  • Mathnasium: Mixed-level rooms, with instructors rotating among children working on different material.

3. Teacher background

Who is actually teaching your child math matters more than most parents realize. The difference between an Ivy-trained educator and a franchise instructor is not snobbery — it is the difference between teaching a concept the way a research mathematician thinks about it versus the way a script says to.

  • SOMATH: Instructors are Harvard- and Northwestern-trained, with 20+ years of GMAT and GRE teaching experience. The same teachers who prepare adults for graduate-school math also build the foundation for our middle and high school students.
  • RSM, Kumon, Mathnasium: Franchise instructors, hired and trained at the local level.

4. Diagnostic / evaluation

Every program will tell you they assess your child. What that means in practice varies sharply.

  • SOMATH: Free 60-minute evaluation with a SOMATH instructor at 226 W 79th Street. A written diagnostic is sent within 48 hours, even if you do not enroll.
  • RSM: Placement test.
  • Kumon: Placement test.
  • Mathnasium: Assessment — paid.

The free diagnostic exists because we believe every NYC parent deserves an honest read on where their child stands before spending a dollar on tutoring. If a program charges you to find out whether they can help your child, that is a signal worth noticing.

5. Program length

How long are you committing for, and how is the curriculum structured?

  • SOMATH: 5-month structured semesters with a clear arc from diagnostic to mastery. Two semesters per year align with the school calendar.
  • RSM: Year-long, rigid commitment.
  • Kumon: Open-ended — students stay on the worksheet conveyor belt indefinitely.
  • Mathnasium: Open-ended — pay monthly and drop in.

The 5-month structure exists because that is how long it takes to genuinely change a child's relationship with a category of math — say, fractions, or word problems, or proportional reasoning — without dragging into a year of diminishing returns.

6. Homework load

The point of homework is not volume. It is whether the practice is the right practice.

  • SOMATH: Focused, purposeful. Each problem set is built around the live lesson and targets the specific reasoning pattern the student needs to practice.
  • RSM: Heavy — often overwhelming. Many parents report homework taking longer than the class itself.
  • Kumon: Daily worksheets. Volume is the model.
  • Mathnasium: Light.

7. SHSAT, SAT, and AP coverage

This is where the comparison stops being academic and starts being practical. NYC families have specific exams to prepare for, and not every program teaches them.

  • SOMATH: Yes — SHSAT, SAT, AP Pre-Calculus, AP Statistics, and AP Calculus are all explicit tracks inside the standard curriculum.
  • RSM: Competition-track focused (AMC, AIME). SHSAT and SAT are not the focus.
  • Kumon: Limited — Kumon does not teach to standardized exams.
  • Mathnasium: No SHSAT / SAT / AP track.

For a NYC family aiming at Stuyvesant, Bronx Science, Brooklyn Tech, the other specialized high schools, or a competitive college math application, this distinction is the one that matters most.

The hidden cost of the wrong fit

Parents tend to think of math programs as a flat cost decision — monthly tuition compared to monthly tuition. The real cost is almost always time. A child enrolled in the wrong program for 18 months emerges with the same gaps they started with, only now they are 18 months older and the SHSAT, SAT, or AP exam is closer.

Three patterns we see at SOMATH after a family switches over from another program:

  • Procedural fluency without conceptual understanding. Students can execute a method but cannot explain it. They fail the moment a problem is rephrased — exactly what the SHSAT and SAT are designed to test.
  • Speed-trained anxiety. Drill-heavy programs reward fast answers. Students learn to guess and check rather than reason carefully. On any timed exam where the wrong answer trap is the obvious answer, this backfires.
  • Worksheet exhaustion. By 7th or 8th grade, students who have spent years on daily worksheets often lose interest in math entirely. The worst outcome of all — not because they got worse at arithmetic, but because they never got to do anything interesting with it.

The goal of a real math program is not to fill a child's afternoon. It is to build the mathematical maturity that pays off when the SHSAT arrives, then the SAT, then the AP exam, then college, then whatever comes after. Every other outcome — better grades, more confidence, a less stressful homework hour — falls out of that one decision.

What to ask on a tour

If you are touring any of these four programs, the answers to four questions will tell you most of what you need to know.

  1. Who specifically will be teaching my child, and what is their background? Vague answers are answers. "Our trained instructors" is not a credential.
  2. What does a single lesson look like, minute by minute? A real teaching program can describe the arc: how a concept is introduced, how students participate, how practice is integrated, how the lesson closes.
  3. How will I know in 12 weeks whether this is working? A serious program names specific, observable changes — not "your child will gain confidence."
  4. What happens to my child if they get stuck? In a worksheet model, the answer is that they wait until the next visit. In a small live cohort, the answer is that the instructor sees it on their face and adjusts that lesson.

Which program is right for your child?

Use this short decision guide.

  • Choose SOMATH if: You want live, small-group, concept-first teaching with Ivy-trained instructors, an explicit path through SHSAT / SAT / AP, and a free honest diagnostic to start.
  • Choose RSM if: Your child already loves math, thrives on volume, and is competition-focused. The homework load is real.
  • Choose Kumon if: You want low-cost daily worksheet practice for arithmetic fluency in elementary years, and you do not need exam prep.
  • Choose Mathnasium if: Your child needs casual drill support and you are not preparing for SHSAT, SAT, or AP.
