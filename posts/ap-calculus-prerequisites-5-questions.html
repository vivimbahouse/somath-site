AP Calculus Prerequisites: Five Readiness Questions

Check algebra, functions, logarithms, trigonometry, and average rate of change before starting Calculus. Worked answers included.

School of Math · Updated October 6, 2026 · 4 min read

Before AP Calculus, focus on algebra, functions, exponential and logarithmic reasoning, trigonometry, and interpreting rates of change. You do not need to arrive already knowing derivatives or integrals: those are Calculus content, not entry requirements.

Question 1: factor and preserve restrictions

Simplify (x² − 9)/(x² + x − 6), giving all original restrictions.

Answer

The numerator factors as (x − 3)(x + 3); the denominator factors as (x + 3)(x − 2). Therefore the simplified expression is (x − 3)/(x − 2), with x ≠ −3 and x ≠ 2. Canceling a factor does not make the original expression defined at −3.

Why this matters: simplifying a formula and describing its domain are different jobs. The distinction becomes important when interpreting continuity and limits later.

Question 2: compose functions

Let f(x) = √(x − 1) and g(x) = 2x + 3. Find f(g(x)) and its real domain.

Answer

Substitute the entire expression for g: f(g(x)) = √(2x + 3 − 1) = √(2x + 2). A real square root requires 2x + 2 ≥ 0, so x ≥ −1. The domain is [−1, ∞).

Why this matters: recognizing an inside and an outside function prepares students for the chain rule without requiring them to know that rule in advance. Review function domains if the restriction was unclear.

Question 3: interpret an exponential model

A model is P(t) = 200(1.05)t, with t measured in years. State its initial value and annual percentage growth. Then solve P(t) = 300 exactly using logarithms.

Answer

P(0) = 200. The factor 1.05 represents 5% growth each year. Divide 300 = 200(1.05)t by 200 to get 1.5 = 1.05t. Taking logarithms gives t = ln(1.5)/ln(1.05), approximately 8.31 years.

Why this matters: a correct expression should still be interpreted in its original units. See our logarithm guide for more practice.

Question 4: use radians and an exact value

Find sin(7π/6) and explain the sign.

Answer

The angle lies in quadrant III, where the vertical coordinate on the unit circle is negative. Its reference angle is π/6, whose sine is 1/2. Therefore sin(7π/6) = −1/2.

Why this matters: radians and exact trigonometric values recur throughout Calculus. If this took a diagram, use the diagram and practice deliberately; needing a diagram is information, not a verdict about your ability.

Question 5: calculate an average rate of change

A particle’s position is s(t) = t² + 2t meters. Find its average velocity from t = 1 to t = 4 seconds.

Answer

s(4) = 24 and s(1) = 3. Average velocity is [s(4) − s(1)]/(4 − 1) = 21/3 = 7 meters per second. This is an interval average, not an assertion about velocity at every instant.

Why this matters: Calculus later asks how an interval rate relates to an instantaneous rate. You can understand the average before learning the derivative.

What comes later, not before entry?

Limits, differentiation, integration, and their applications belong to the Calculus curriculum. Programming can be useful enrichment, but it is not presented here as an AP Calculus entry requirement. The College Board framework shows how the Calculus topics fit into the course.

Questions and answers

Do I need Calculus before taking AP Calculus?

No. Algebra, functions, and trigonometry provide the foundation; limits, derivatives, and integrals are taught in Calculus.

Is programming a prerequisite?

No. Programming can be enrichment, but it is not a prerequisite in this readiness check.

Does canceling a factor remove an original restriction?

No. The original denominator determines the excluded values.

Should this check be timed?

Not initially. First focus on independent reasoning and clear explanations, then build fluency.

Does one wrong answer mean I am not ready?

No. Look at the type and consistency of the gaps, then practice and reassess.
