2. Why our family chose to homeschool

We didn’t set out to homeschool. Vivianne said it plainly in the Post: “Homeschooling wasn’t our original plan.” What changed was a series of small realizations that added up:

  • Violetta was ahead of her grade in math and reading. Her school did what most schools do — taught to the middle of the class. That’s the right call for a public school teacher with 25 students, but it meant Violetta was rehearsing skills she had already mastered.
  • The schedule didn’t fit our life. Running SOMATH means we’re in the classroom evenings and weekends. A rigid 8:20 to 3:00 public-school day cut into the hours we could actually be present with her.
  • We wanted room for depth. Violetta loves math, world folklore, judo, art, chess, and piano. Fitting all of that around a full school day plus homework was starting to squeeze out the things she cared most about.
  • We already run a school. As educators, we knew what a strong curriculum looks like and knew we could build one at home. That’s a specific advantage that most homeschool parents don’t start with — and it’s the reason SOMATH now shares its curriculum and classroom with other homeschool families in the neighborhood.

4. The one question every homeschool parent asks: “What about math?”

We meet a lot of homeschool parents on the Upper West Side. Every single one of them, without exception, raises math within the first two minutes of the conversation. Not because they don’t feel capable — most of them are perfectly capable. But because math is the one subject where a bad early foundation is very hard to fix later, and every parent knows it.

Here’s what usually happens:

  • Grades 1–3 feel manageable at home. Counting, addition, subtraction, simple word problems — a parent can absolutely teach these.
  • Around grade 4, fractions arrive, and the “wait, was that the right way to explain it?” questions start.
  • By grade 6, most parents want a second set of eyes. Pre-algebra and integer operations get technical fast.
  • By grade 8, if the plan includes SHSAT or a specialized-high-school application, the family needs a real curriculum and a real coach. Algebra, geometry, and problem-solving are non-negotiable.
  • Grades 9–12: algebra II, precalculus, AP Calculus, SAT, AP Statistics. Very few parents want to shoulder this alone, and most shouldn’t have to.

This is where SOMATH fits in. We’re a real math school — a physical classroom on 79th Street, with a 24-class curriculum ladder taught by cofounder Marcelo Ambrozio and the SOMATH team. Homeschool families use us the same way school-day families use us: as the math component. The difference is when and how often.

5. How SOMATH supports homeschool families

Homeschool students at SOMATH get the same small-group classes as school-day students, structured a little differently to fit a home schedule:

  • Morning and midday slots. Most homeschool families prefer to do their core academic work in the morning, so we hold homeschool math classes during school hours — not just after 4:00 PM like traditional after-school enrichment.
  • Small groups of 4–8 students at the same level. Not by age — by actual skill. Two homeschooled 8-year-olds can be years apart in math, and we place based on the written diagnostic, not the birthday.
  • One-hour weekly classes plus optional 1-on-1 tutoring. The class is the long-term engine (curriculum, problem-solving, competition prep). Tutoring layers on top for anything specific — a test date, a hard unit, a competition.
  • Written diagnostic before enrollment. Every child starts with a free evaluation. We tell you honestly if we’re the right fit for your family, and if we’re not, we’ll point you to who is.
  • IHIP-ready documentation. Curriculum outlines, quarterly progress reports, and attendance records — formatted for the New York State homeschool submission process (more on that in section 7 below).
  • Full grade-1-to-AP-Calculus ladder. Little Newtons (K–2), Kid Einsteins (3–5), Young Fermats (6–9 including Algebra I / Algebra II / Geometry), and Digital SAT / AP Calculus / AP Statistics at the top of the ladder. You don’t have to change programs when your child changes grade.

6. How we teach Violetta math at SOMATH

People sometimes ask if Violetta gets special treatment because her parents run the school. She doesn’t — because “special treatment” at SOMATH is just the standard treatment: a written diagnostic, placement into a small group at the right level, a real curriculum, and honest reporting.

Here’s the specific structure we use for her:

  • Weekly small-group math class at SOMATH, in the group that matches her level (currently a mixed 3rd–4th grade Kid Einsteins section). She sits with 5–7 other kids at the same math level, most of whom are not homeschooled.
  • Two shorter home practice sessions each week, using SOMATH’s worksheets and problem sets. Marcelo reviews her work; Vivianne handles the scheduling.
  • Cross-subject integration. When Violetta is doing world folklore, we look for the math — the base-20 Maya number system, tessellations in Islamic geometric art, the arithmetic in African string games. Homeschooling makes this kind of integration easy in a way school schedules rarely allow.
  • Quarterly written progress report, the same format we use for every SOMATH student, filed with the school district as part of her IHIP.
  • Competition track when she’s ready. Right now that means Math Kangaroo and Noetic. Later it will mean AMC 8, MATHCOUNTS, and eventually SHSAT prep — the same competition ladder every SOMATH middle-schooler follows.

This is the model we open to other homeschool families in the neighborhood. You don’t have to build the math component of your homeschool curriculum from scratch. We already built ours.

7. IHIP and quarterly progress reports

New York State requires every homeschool family to file an Individualized Home Instruction Plan (IHIP) with their local school district, plus quarterly progress reports and an annual assessment. Math must be taught every year, K–12. Parents don’t need a teaching certification or a college degree to homeschool, but the paperwork is real. Here’s how SOMATH fits into that process for the families we teach:

None of this replaces the parent’s IHIP submission — you’re still the responsible party with the district. But it means the math part of your paperwork is essentially done for you.

9. Frequently asked questions

Does SOMATH work with homeschool families in NYC?

Yes. We run an in-person math track for homeschool students grades 1–12 from our Upper West Side classroom. Homeschool families use SOMATH as the full math component of their IHIP, or as a supplement to whatever they already do at home. Book a free evaluation to get placed.

What are the NYC homeschooling requirements for math?

New York State requires an IHIP that names the curriculum, quarterly progress reports, attendance records, and an annual assessment. Math must be taught every year, K–12. Parents don’t need a certification or a degree. SOMATH provides IHIP-ready curriculum outlines and quarterly written progress reports for the families we teach.

Can a homeschooled child still take the SAT, SHSAT, and AP exams?

Yes — on exactly the same footing as any other NYC student. Our enrichment ladder feeds directly into SHSAT prep, Digital SAT prep, and AP Calculus AB and BC. Homeschooling doesn’t close any doors; it just changes the shape of the school day.

Isn’t homeschooling bad for socialization?

This is the most common concern parents raise, and it’s a fair one to think about. The honest answer is: it depends entirely on what a family does with the time. Homeschool families who structure regular group activities — math class, sports, art class, chess club, book clubs, playdates — get plenty of socialization. Families who don’t, don’t. In our experience, a weekly small-group class like SOMATH is one of the healthier social settings a child can have: 4–8 kids at the same skill level, an adult in the room, working on hard problems together.
