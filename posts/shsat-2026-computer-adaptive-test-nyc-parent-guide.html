The 2026 SHSAT Is Now Computer-Adaptive: A NYC Parent's Guide

Starting fall 2026, the SHSAT moves to a computer-adaptive test — the biggest format change in decades. What it means for your 7th and 8th grader, how prep needs to shift, and what stays exactly the same. Written by SOMATH (School of Math), 226 W 79th Street, Upper West Side.

2026-06-16 · By the SOMATH team · 226 W 79th St, UWS · (646) 668-6151

Short answer. On June 4, 2026, NYC Public Schools confirmed that the Specialized High Schools Admissions Test (SHSAT) becomes a computer-adaptive test (CAT) beginning fall 2026. Every student will answer the same number of questions — 50 ELA and 50 Math, 180 minutes total — but the test will now adjust question difficulty in real time based on a child's responses. Math is forward-only (no skip-and-return), ELA allows movement only within a passage set, and the skill base being tested has not changed. Cutoff scores were unchanged for 2026 admissions (561 Stuyvesant, 525 Bronx Science, 506 Brooklyn Tech, per the NYCPS-published list). What changes is how the test is delivered and how families should prepare. Below is what UWS families need to know — with what to actually do about it.

1. What is actually changing in 2026

The 2024 SHSAT was paper. The 2025 SHSAT was digital but linear — every student saw the same questions in the same order, just on a screen. The 2026 SHSAT is digital and adaptive. As Education Roundup #141 summarized from the updated NYCPS page: "Beginning in fall 2026, the SHSAT will move to a computer-adaptive test. A computer-adaptive test adjusts its content based on student responses. At the start of the test, students will see an item or passage set of average difficulty. After the student responds, an initial score estimate will be set. As students answer more questions, the testing system re-estimates their score in real time based on all of their responses until that point."

In plain English: get a question right, the next one may be harder; get one wrong, the next one may be easier. Every test-taker answers the same number of questions, but no two students see the same test.

2. What is staying the same

This part matters as much as the change — because most of the work a child does to prepare is identical to last year.

  • Content. ELA still tests revising and editing plus reading comprehension. Math still tests pre-algebra, algebra, ratios and proportions, geometry, probability and statistics.
  • Total time. 180 minutes, no scheduled break.
  • Total questions. 100 across the two sections (50 ELA + 50 Math), matching the 2025 digital portal practice tests.
  • Scoring. Raw score per section, converted to a scaled score, then ELA and Math scaled scores are added into a composite. No penalty for wrong answers.
  • Admissions logic. Composite score plus rank-order seat allocation across the eight test schools. Cutoffs for 2026 entry remain 561 Stuyvesant, 539 HSMSE, 531 Queens Sciences, 525 Bronx Science, 517 Staten Island Tech, 507 HSAS Lehman, 506 Brooklyn Tech, 495 Brooklyn Latin (per the 2026 cutoff list compiled by Bright Kids from NYCPS data).

This means: if your child has been doing topic-by-topic content mastery, do not throw that work out. The skills are the same. What is changing is the test-day experience.

3. The new navigation rules (the biggest practical change)

For paper SHSAT veterans, the "two-pass" strategy was canon — do the easy questions first, mark the hard ones, come back. That strategy is dead on the 2026 Math section. Per the updated SHSATlab navigation rules and Kaplan's 2026 strategy guide, this is what families actually need to teach their kids:

Math: forward-only

No skip-and-return. Once you submit an answer, you cannot return to that question.

If a child is stuck for more than 90 to 100 seconds, the right move is to eliminate what they can, commit to the strongest remaining choice, and move on. There is no upside in agonizing — the next question is waiting and depends on this answer.

ELA: review allowed only within a passage set

You can move between questions inside one passage set. Once you submit the set, it is locked.

Children should still read the whole passage carefully on first pass and answer in order, but it is now safe to mark a tough question, finish the rest of the set, and return to the marked one before submitting. They cannot, however, come back to the earlier set later in the test.

4. How scoring works on an adaptive test

The single biggest source of parent confusion right now: adaptive does not mean "harder questions are worth more." Per the SHSATlab scoring explainer, NYCPS has stated explicitly that "every scored question counts the same as one raw score point within each section, and that it does not matter which particular questions a student gets right or wrong within that section."

How the adaptive engine actually works:

  1. The first question is of average difficulty.
  2. After each response, the system updates its estimate of where the child's true score sits and serves a next question whose difficulty is calibrated to that estimate.
  3. By the end of the section, the system has converged on a raw score that is then converted to a scaled score.
  4. ELA scaled + Math scaled = composite. Rank-order seat assignment proceeds as in every prior year.

Practical consequence: accuracy on early questions matters disproportionately, because those first 8 to 12 items set the difficulty path for the rest of the section. A careless mistake in question 3 can route a child onto an easier path for the next 15 questions and cap their ceiling.

5. Technology-Enhanced Items: the new question types

Both the 2025 and 2026 digital SHSATs include Technology-Enhanced Items (TEIs) in addition to standard multiple choice. Children who have only practiced on paper or on older PDFs will be surprised. The four TEI families to drill on are:

  • Drag-and-drop. Reorder steps in a math solution, or arrange events in a passage.
  • Multiple-select. Two or three correct answers must be chosen — partial credit is not available.
  • Inline drop-downs. Choose the correct word or expression from a dropdown embedded in a sentence or equation.
  • Number-line and graph interactions. Click to place a point, drag a slider, or shade an inequality.

The official NYCPS SHSAT Online Practice Portal now offers two fully functional digital tests that mirror the real exam interface — the closest a family can get to test-day conditions without test day.

6. Five prep shifts every NYC family should make this summer

This is what we are advising every SOMATH SHSAT family right now. None of it is exotic; all of it is concrete.

  1. Move practice to the NYCPS Online Practice Portal. Paper practice still has a place for topic drills, but full-length runs should now happen on the official digital interface so the screen, tools, and navigation feel automatic by October.
  2. Train accuracy on the first 8 to 12 questions of each section. Because those questions disproportionately set the difficulty path, "warm up slow, finish strong" is no longer a viable strategy. The first ten minutes are the most important ten minutes of the test.
  3. Replace skip-and-return Math drills with a "commit and move" routine. Give kids a 90-second internal clock per Math question. At 90 seconds: eliminate what they can, pick the strongest remaining, move on. Practice this until it is reflex.
  4. Drill the four TEI types deliberately. Set a weekly TEI session — ten drag-and-drops, ten multiple-selects, ten inline drop-downs, ten number-line interactions. Get the muscle memory built before October.
  5. Do not abandon topic-by-topic content mastery. The skills being tested are unchanged. Algebraic fluency, geometry vocabulary, ratio/proportion reasoning, reading-comprehension stamina — these still drive 80% of the score. If a child has gaps in these areas, no amount of adaptive strategy will close them.

8. FAQ

Is the 2026 SHSAT really computer-adaptive?

Yes. NYCPS confirmed the move on June 4, 2026. Every student sees an item of average difficulty first, and subsequent questions adapt up or down based on the student's running response pattern. 50 ELA + 50 Math, 180 minutes.

Can my child still skip questions and come back?

Math is forward-only — no skip-and-return. ELA allows movement only within a single passage set before that set is submitted.

How is the new SHSAT scored?

Raw → scaled → composite. No penalty for wrong answers. Every scored question is worth one raw point within its section, regardless of difficulty.

Did the qualifying scores for Stuyvesant, Bronx Science, and Brooklyn Tech change?

No. The 2026 cutoffs remained 561 Stuyvesant, 525 Bronx Science, 506 Brooklyn Tech — the format changed, not the admissions math.

Does my child still need to learn the same content?

Yes. Pre-algebra, algebra, ratios and proportions, geometry, probability and statistics on Math; revising/editing plus reading comprehension on ELA. The standards are unchanged.

Should we still guess on hard questions?

Always. No penalty for wrong answers. Especially on Math, where you cannot come back — eliminate what you can, commit, and move on.

How should prep change for the adaptive SHSAT?

Move practice to the NYCPS portal, train accuracy on the first 10 questions of each section, drop skip-and-return Math habits, drill the four TEI types weekly, and keep doing topic-by-topic content mastery.
