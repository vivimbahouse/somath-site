The three-session syllabus

The three topics are sequenced deliberately. Each one builds on the previous session, and together they cover the conceptual foundation that early-elementary students need before they can do real reasoning with larger numbers, fractions, and multi-step problems later in the year.

Session 1 — Number Sense and Place Value (June 10)

90 minutes · Wednesday, June 10, 3:00–4:30 PM

Number sense is the single best predictor of how a child will do with every other piece of math they encounter through 8th grade. In this session students work on what numbers actually mean — not just how to read them. We cover place value (ones, tens, hundreds, and how the columns relate), comparing and ordering numbers, decomposing numbers into useful parts (a 47 is also a 40 + 7, or a 50 - 3, or two 20s plus a 7), and the patterns that make mental arithmetic possible later. Students who leave with strong place value rarely struggle with multi-digit addition and subtraction; students who don’t spend years compensating with memorized procedures that fall apart at the first non-standard problem.

Session 2 — Addition and Subtraction (June 17)

90 minutes · Wednesday, June 17, 3:00–4:30 PM

This session builds directly on the place value work from Session 1 and applies it to fluent, flexible addition and subtraction. We focus on strategies, not just the standard algorithm: making tens (8 + 5 = 8 + 2 + 3), counting up for subtraction (47 - 38 = 39 to 47 = 9), using known facts to derive unknown ones (if I know 6 + 6 = 12, then 6 + 7 must be 13), and regrouping with understanding rather than borrowing as a mystery rule. Students who learn addition and subtraction this way end up faster and more accurate than students who memorize a procedure, because they can check their work by trying a second strategy.

Session 3 — Word Problems and Mental Math (June 24)

90 minutes · Wednesday, June 24, 3:00–4:30 PM

The final session is where the foundations come together. Word problems are where most elementary students hit a wall, and the wall is rarely about arithmetic — it’s about reading mathematically. We teach a clear approach: read the problem twice, identify the question first, identify the relevant numbers second, decide whether the situation calls for addition or subtraction, and estimate before computing. Mental math runs throughout: not as speed drills, but as the habit of looking at a problem and noticing the easiest path. By the end of the session students will have worked through word problems aloud, in writing, and as a small group — the same way mathematicians actually work on problems.

Who these classes are for

The June series is calibrated for early-elementary learners building foundational number skills — roughly grades 1 through 3, with placement based on a brief diagnostic conversation rather than a strict grade cutoff. The cohort works best for:

  • Students entering 2nd or 3rd grade in the fall who would benefit from a focused refresh of number sense and addition/subtraction before the school year starts.
  • Advanced kindergarteners and 1st graders whose number sense is genuinely ready for the next level. Bring them by for a chat with Marcelo and we’ll confirm fit before enrolling.
  • 4th graders who are still building fluency with multi-digit addition/subtraction or who freeze on word problems. The foundational work here matters more than rushing them ahead.
  • UWS families who want to try SOMATH before committing to a fall course. Three weeks is a real preview — long enough to see how your child responds to small-group instruction, short enough that you’re not locked in.

If you’re not sure whether your child is the right fit, call us at (646) 668-6151 or book a free 60-minute evaluation. The written diagnostic that follows the evaluation tells you exactly where your child is, and we’ll be honest if a different group or a 1:1 session would serve them better.
