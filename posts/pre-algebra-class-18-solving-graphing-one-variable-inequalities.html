Understanding inequality symbols

An inequality compares two values that may not be equal. The symbol \(\lt\) means less than and \(\gt\) means greater than; the open side always faces the greater value and the pointed end faces the smaller value, so \(3 \lt 7\) and \(9 \gt 4\).

The symbols \(\le\) and \(\ge\) add the possibility of equality. The statement \(x \le 5\) means \(x\) can be 5 or any smaller number, and \(x \ge 2\) means \(x\) can be 2 or any larger number. Unlike an equation, an inequality usually has many solutions.

Practice: try these five questions

Q1

Write \(\lt\) or \(\gt\) in each blank: 12 __ 15, 20 __ 8, and −3 __ −5.

Answer

\(12 \lt 15\); \(20 \gt 8\); \(-3 \gt -5\)

  1. 12 is smaller than 15, so the pointed end faces 12: \(12 \lt 15\).

  2. 20 is larger than 8, so the open side faces 20: \(20 \gt 8\).

  3. On a number line −3 is to the right of −5, so −3 is the greater value: \(-3 \gt -5\).

Q2

Translate each sentence into symbols: “\(x\) is less than or equal to 9” and “\(y\) is greater than −2.”

Answer

\(x \le 9\) and \(y \gt -2\)

  1. “Less than or equal to” uses the symbol with the bar underneath: \(x \le 9\). The value 9 itself is allowed.

  2. “Greater than” without “or equal to” uses \(\gt\): \(y \gt -2\). The value −2 itself is not allowed.

Q3

Which of the values 1, 4, and 6 make the statement \(x \ge 4\) true?

Answer

4 and 6

  1. Replace \(x\) with each value. \(1 \ge 4\) is false; \(4 \ge 4\) is true because the symbol allows equality; \(6 \ge 4\) is true.

  2. So 4 and 6 are solutions. The boundary value 4 counts because of the “or equal to.”

Q4

Decide whether each statement is true or false, and explain: \(10 \le 10\) and \(10 \lt 10\).

Answer

\(10 \le 10\) is true; \(10 \lt 10\) is false

  1. \(10 \le 10\) says 10 is less than or equal to 10. The two values are equal, so the statement is true.

  2. \(10 \lt 10\) says 10 is strictly less than 10. A number is never less than itself, so the statement is false.

Q5

Write \(n \gt 15\) in words. Then give two numbers that make it true and one number that makes it false.

Answer

“\(n\) is greater than 15”; for example 16 and 20 are solutions, and 15 is not

  1. The open side faces \(n\), so \(n\) is the greater value: “\(n\) is greater than 15.”

  2. Any number larger than 15 works, such as 16, 20, or 15.5. The value 15 is not a solution because the symbol does not include equality, and smaller numbers such as 9 also fail.

Checking solutions

A value is a solution of an inequality when substituting it for the variable makes a true statement. Follow three steps: substitute the value, simplify the arithmetic, and decide whether the resulting comparison is true or false. For \(x + 3 \lt 9\), the value 4 gives \(7 \lt 9\), which is true, while 6 gives \(9 \lt 9\), which is false.

Watch the boundary. For \(x \le 5\), the value 5 works because \(5 \le 5\) is true; for \(x \lt 5\), the value 5 fails because \(5 \lt 5\) is false. An inequality can have many solutions, so checking one value tells you about that value only.

Q6

Is \(x = 5\) a solution of the inequality?

\[x + 4 \lt 10\]
Answer

Yes: \(x = 5\) is a solution

  1. Substitute: replace \(x\) with 5. (5) + 4 < 10

  2. Simplify the left side: 9 < 10.

  3. Decide: the statement is true, so \(x = 5\) is a solution.

Q7

Is \(x = 7\) a solution? Pay attention to the boundary.

\[3x \ge 21\]
Answer

Yes: \(x = 7\) is a solution

  1. Substitute: replace \(x\) with 7. 3(7) ≥ 21

  2. Simplify the left side: 21 ≥ 21.

  3. Decide: the statement is true, so \(x = 7\) is a solution.

  4. Because the symbol is \(\ge\), equality counts. The boundary value 7 is a solution.

Q8

Is \(x = 2\) a solution?

\[5x - 1 \gt 9\]
Answer

No: \(x = 2\) is not a solution

  1. Substitute: replace \(x\) with 2. 5(2) − 1 > 9

  2. Simplify the left side: 9 > 9.

  3. Decide: the statement is false, so \(x = 2\) is not a solution.

  4. The left side equals exactly 9, but the symbol \(\gt\) does not allow equality, so 2 is not a solution.

Q9

Which of the values \(x = 1\), \(x = 3\), and \(x = 5\) satisfy the inequality?

\[2x + 1 \le 7\]
Answer

\(x = 1\) and \(x = 3\)

  1. \(x = 1\): \(2(1) + 1 = 3\), and \(3 \le 7\) is true.

  2. \(x = 3\): \(2(3) + 1 = 7\), and \(7 \le 7\) is true because equality is allowed.

  3. \(x = 5\): \(2(5) + 1 = 11\), and \(11 \le 7\) is false.

  4. So 1 and 3 are solutions, and 5 is not.

Q10

Is \(x = -4\) a solution? Be careful with the negative numbers.

\[x - 2 \lt -5\]
Answer

Yes: \(x = -4\) is a solution

  1. Substitute: replace \(x\) with -4. (-4) − 2 < −5

  2. Simplify the left side: −6 < −5.

  3. Decide: the statement is true, so \(x = -4\) is a solution.

  4. On a number line −6 is to the left of −5, so −6 is less than −5 and the statement is true.

Graphing inequalities on a number line

A graph shows all the values that make an inequality true. Mark the boundary with a circle: an open circle for \(\lt\) or \(\gt\), because the boundary is not included, and a closed circle for \(\le\) or \(\ge\), because the boundary is included.

Then shade the solutions. Less than means shade to the left; greater than means shade to the right. The arrow at the end of the shading shows that the solutions continue without end. For example, \(x \lt 3\) has an open circle at 3 shaded left, and \(x \ge 3\) has a closed circle at 3 shaded right.

Open circle at 2; shade left

Solving inequalities using addition and subtraction

Isolate the variable by doing the same operation to both sides. Adding or subtracting the same number on both sides keeps the inequality sign unchanged. To undo addition, subtract: from \(x + 4 \lt 9\), subtracting 4 on both sides gives \(x \lt 5\). To undo subtraction, add: from \(x - 3 \ge 2\), adding 3 on both sides gives \(x \ge 5\).

After solving, graph the solution and check a value. For \(x \lt 5\), try \(x = 4\) in the original inequality: \(4 + 4 \lt 9\) gives \(8 \lt 9\), which is true. The graph has an open circle at 5 shaded left.

Solving inequalities using multiplication and division

Use inverse operations to isolate the variable. When you multiply or divide both sides by a positive number, the inequality sign stays the same. To undo multiplication, divide: from \(3x \lt 12\), dividing both sides by 3 gives \(x \lt 4\). To undo division, multiply: from \(\frac{x}{4} \ge 2\), multiplying both sides by 4 gives \(x \ge 8\).

Check with a value from the shaded region. For \(3x \lt 12\), try \(x = 2\): \(3(2) \lt 12\) gives \(6 \lt 12\), which is true. If the boundary is not a whole number, such as 4.5, place the circle between the tick marks.

Reversing the inequality sign

Multiplying or dividing both sides by a negative number reverses the inequality sign: \(\lt\) becomes \(\gt\) and \(\le\) becomes \(\ge\). The reason is that multiplying by a negative number flips the order on the number line. Start with \(2 \lt 5\); multiplying both sides by −1 gives \(-2\) and \(-5\), and \(-2 \gt -5\).

From \(-3x \lt 12\), divide both sides by −3 and reverse: \(x \gt -4\). From \(\frac{x}{-2} \ge 3\), multiply both sides by −2 and reverse: \(x \le -6\). Adding or subtracting a negative number does not reverse the sign: \(x - 3 \lt 5\) simply becomes \(x \lt 8\). Always check a value in the original inequality.

Solving two-step inequalities

Undo addition or subtraction first, then undo multiplication or division, doing the same operation to both sides each time. For \(2x + 3 \lt 11\), subtract 3 from both sides to get \(2x \lt 8\), then divide both sides by 2 to get \(x \lt 4\).

Reverse the inequality sign only when multiplying or dividing both sides by a negative number. For \(-3x + 2 \le 14\), subtracting 2 gives \(-3x \le 12\); dividing by −3 reverses the sign: \(x \ge -4\). Check with \(x = 0\): \(-3(0) + 2 \le 14\) gives \(2 \le 14\), which is true.

Inequality word problems

Translate the words, write an inequality, solve, and interpret. Common phrases: “at least” means \(\ge\), “at most” means \(\le\), “more than” means \(\gt\), and “less than” means \(\lt\). Define the variable first, for example let \(r\) be the number of rides.

Solve with the same steps as before, then explain what the answer means in the situation. If you have $26, admission is $8, and rides cost $3 each, then \(8 + 3r \le 26\) gives \(r \le 6\): you can afford at most 6 rides. When the variable counts objects, only whole numbers make sense, so a solution such as \(t \le 4.6\) means at most 4 whole tickets.

Q36

You have $30 to spend at an arcade. Entry costs $6 and each game costs $4. How many games can you play?

Answer

You can play at most 6 games, so g = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, or 6.

  1. Let \(g\) = the number of games.

  2. Write the inequality.

    \[6 + 4g \le 30\]

  3. Subtract 6 from both sides. Adding or subtracting the same number does not change the inequality sign.

    \[4g \le 24\]

  4. Divide both sides by 4. Dividing by a positive number keeps the sign unchanged.

    \[g \le 6\]

  5. Check the boundary value, g = 6: 6 + 4(6) ≤ 30 → 30 ≤ 30, which is true.

  6. Interpretation: You can play at most 6 games, so g = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, or 6.

You can buy at most 7 pencils.

W11

A diver is at −15 feet and descends 3 feet per minute. For safety, the diver must stay at or above −45 feet. For how many minutes can the diver keep descending?

Answer

The diver can descend for at most 10 minutes; after 10 minutes the depth is exactly −45 feet.

  1. Let \(m\) = the number of minutes.

  2. Write the inequality.

    \[-15 - 3m \ge -45\]

  3. Add 15 to both sides. Adding or subtracting the same number does not change the inequality sign.

  4. Divide both sides by −3. Dividing by a negative number reverses the inequality sign.

  5. Check the boundary value, m = 10: −15 − 3(10) ≥ −45 → −45 ≥ −45, which is true.

  6. Interpretation: The diver can descend for at most 10 minutes; after 10 minutes the depth is exactly −45 feet.

Quick questions and answers

What do the four inequality symbols mean?

\(\lt\) means less than, \(\gt\) means greater than, \(\le\) means less than or equal to, and \(\ge\) means greater than or equal to. The open side of the symbol faces the greater value.

How do I check whether a value is a solution?

Substitute the value for the variable, simplify, and decide whether the comparison is true. Boundary values are solutions only when the symbol includes “or equal to.”

When is the circle open or closed on a number-line graph?

Use an open circle for \(\lt\) and \(\gt\), because the boundary is not included, and a closed circle for \(\le\) and \(\ge\), because it is included. Shade left for less than and right for greater than.

Which operations keep the inequality sign the same?

Adding or subtracting any number on both sides, and multiplying or dividing both sides by a positive number, keep the sign unchanged.

When does the inequality sign reverse?

Only when you multiply or divide both sides by a negative number. Multiplying by a negative flips the order of numbers on the number line, so \(2 \lt 5\) becomes \(-2 \gt -5\).

How do I translate word problems into inequalities?

“At least” means \(\ge\), “at most” means \(\le\), “more than” means \(\gt\), and “less than” means \(\lt\). Define the variable, write the inequality, solve, and explain what the answer means, using whole numbers when the variable counts objects.
