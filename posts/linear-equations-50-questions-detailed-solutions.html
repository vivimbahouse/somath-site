Questions 1–10

One-step equations

Undo one operation at a time

An equation states that two expressions are equal. Solving means finding the value of \(x\) that makes the statement true. These equations are linear: after simplifying, the variable has power 1, with no products of variable terms.

Keep the equation balanced by doing the same operation to both sides. Addition and subtraction undo each other; multiplication and division by the same nonzero number undo each other. For \(x+5=12\), subtract 5 from both sides.

The sign belongs to the term: \(-x\) means \((-1)x\). If you reach \(-x=-11\), divide both sides by \(-1\).

Question 1

\[x + 5 = 12\]
Answer

Solution: \(x = 7\)

  1. Subtract 5 from both sides to undo adding 5.

    \[x = 12 - 5\]

  2. Simplify the right side.

    \[x = 7\]

Check in the original equation

Substitute \(x = 7\) and evaluate each side separately.

Left side

\[\left(7\right) + 5 = 12\]

Right side

\[12 = 12\]

Both sides equal \(12\), so \(x = 7\) is correct.

Questions 11–18

Two-step equations

Undo the outside operation first

For an equation such as \(2x+5=19\), subtract the constant term first, then divide by the coefficient of \(x\). This reverses the order of the operations that formed the expression.

A fraction like \(\frac{x}{3}\) means “\(x\) divided by 3.” Once that fraction is isolated, multiply both sides by 3. Keep negative signs attached to terms throughout the calculation.

Question 11

\[2x + 5 = 19\]
Answer

Solution: \(x = 7\)

  1. Subtract 5 from both sides before undoing the multiplication.

    \[2 x = 19 - 5\]

  2. Simplify.

    \[2 x = 14\]

  3. Divide both sides by 2.

    \[x = 7\]

Check in the original equation

Substitute \(x = 7\) and evaluate each side separately.

Left side

\[2\left(7\right) + 5 = 19\]

Right side

\[19 = 19\]

Both sides equal \(19\), so \(x = 7\) is correct.

Questions 19–20

Variables on both sides

Collect terms without changing the balance

Subtract a variable term from both sides to place all the \(x\)-terms on one side. Then use inverse operations to isolate \(x\). “Moving a term” is shorthand for adding or subtracting that term on both sides.

Combine like terms only: \(6x-2x=4x\), but \(4x+11\) cannot be combined into \(15x\). If variable terms ever cancel completely, a true statement means infinitely many solutions and a false statement means no solution. Each of these 50 questions has exactly one solution.

Question 19

\[6x + 11 = 2x + 35\]
Answer

Solution: \(x = 6\)

  1. Subtract 2x from both sides to collect the variable terms on the left.

    \[4 x + 11 = 35\]

  2. Subtract 11 from both sides.

    \[4 x = 24\]

  3. Divide both sides by 4.

    \[x = 6\]

Check in the original equation

Substitute \(x = 6\) and evaluate each side separately.

Left side

\[6\left(6\right) + 11 = 47\]

Right side

\[2\left(6\right) + 35 = 47\]

Both sides equal \(47\), so \(x = 6\) is correct.

Question 20

\[9x - 13 = 5x + 15\]
Answer

Solution: \(x = 7\)

  1. Subtract 5x from both sides.

    \[4 x - 13 = 15\]

  2. Add 13 to both sides.

    \[4 x = 28\]

  3. Divide both sides by 4.

    \[x = 7\]

Check in the original equation

Substitute \(x = 7\) and evaluate each side separately.

Left side

\[9\left(7\right) - 13 = 50\]

Right side

\[5\left(7\right) + 15 = 50\]

Both sides equal \(50\), so \(x = 7\) is correct.

Questions 21–30

Parentheses and distribution

Multiply every term in the group

The distributive property says \(a(b+c)=ab+ac\). Apply the factor to every term, including its sign. For example, \(-3(x-4)=-3x+12\), not \(-3x-12\).

You can sometimes avoid distributing: in \(3(x+4)=27\), divide both sides by 3 first. Choose a valid operation that makes the equation simpler, then combine like terms and isolate \(x\).

Question 21

\[3(x + 4) = 27\]
Answer

Solution: \(x = 5\)

  1. Divide both sides by 3 first; this removes the outside factor from the entire group.

    \[x + 4 = 9\]

  2. Subtract 4 from both sides.

    \[x = 5\]

Check in the original equation

Substitute \(x = 5\) and evaluate each side separately.

Left side

\[3(\left(5\right) + 4) = 27\]

Right side

\[27 = 27\]

Both sides equal \(27\), so \(x = 5\) is correct.

Questions 31–37

Multiple groups and nested brackets

Simplify carefully, from the inside out

Brackets and parentheses both group expressions. Simplify an inner group before distributing an outer factor, or remove an outside factor from both sides when convenient.

Write a separate line for distribution and for combining like terms. In \(5-2[3(x+1)-4]\), the factor outside the bracket is \(-2\); it multiplies the entire bracket.

Question 31

\[5(x - 2) - 3(x + 4) = 8\]
Answer

Solution: \(x = 15\)

  1. Distribute 5 and negative 3. In particular, negative 3 times positive 4 is negative 12.

    \[5 x - 10 - 3 x - 12 = 8\]

  2. Combine the x terms and the constants.

    \[2 x - 22 = 8\]

  3. Add 22 to both sides.

    \[2 x = 30\]

  4. Divide both sides by 2.

    \[x = 15\]

Check in the original equation

Substitute \(x = 15\) and evaluate each side separately.

Left side

\[5(\left(15\right) - 2) - 3(\left(15\right) + 4) = 8\]

Right side

\[8 = 8\]

Both sides equal \(8\), so \(x = 15\) is correct.

Question 32

\[8 - 3(2x - 5) = 2x - 17\]
Answer

    \[8 - 6 x + 15 = 2 x - 17\]

  2. Combine the constants on the left.

    \[23 - 6 x = 2 x - 17\]

  3. Add 6x to both sides.

    \[23 = 8 x - 17\]

  4. Add 17 to both sides.

    \[40 = 8 x\]

  5. Divide both sides by 8 and write x on the left.

    \[x = 5\]

Check in the original equation

Substitute \(x = 5\) and evaluate each side separately.

Left side

\[8 - 3(2\left(5\right) - 5) = -7\]

Right side

\[2\left(5\right) - 17 = -7\]

Both sides equal \(-7\), so \(x = 5\) is correct.

Questions 38–44

Equations with fractions

Clear denominators in every term

A fraction bar groups the entire numerator. Multiplying \(\frac{x+5}{3}=7\) by 3 gives \(x+5=21\), not \(x+\frac{5}{3}=21\).

With more than one denominator, multiply every term on both sides by the least common denominator (LCD). For denominators 3 and 4, use 12. Preserve numerator groups until you distribute, especially after a subtraction sign.

All denominators here are nonzero constants, so clearing them does not exclude any value of \(x\). Answers need not be whole numbers; keep fractions exact instead of rounding.

Question 38

\[\dfrac{x + 5}{3} = 7\]
Answer

Solution: \(x = 16\)

  1. Multiply both sides by 3. The whole numerator, x plus 5, remains.

    \[x + 5 = 21\]

  2. Subtract 5 from both sides.

    \[x = 16\]

Check in the original equation

Substitute \(x = 16\) and evaluate each side separately.

Left side

\[\dfrac{\left(16\right) + 5}{3} = 7\]

Right side

\[7 = 7\]

Both sides equal \(7\), so \(x = 16\) is correct.

Questions 45–50

Fraction challenges

Combine the methods, then verify

Identify every denominator, clear all fractions, simplify grouped expressions, collect variable terms, and isolate \(x\). Multiply standalone constants and every term on the other side by the LCD too.

Finally, substitute your answer into the original equation, not just your last simplified line. Calculate the left side and the right side independently. Matching values verify the solution.

Question 45

\[\dfrac{x + 3}{2} + \dfrac{2x - 1}{5} = x - 1\]
Answer

Solution: \(x = 23\)

  1. Multiply every term by 10. Multiply both terms on the right, not just x.

    \[5 \left(x + 3\right) + 2 \left(2 x - 1\right) = 10 \left(x - 1\right)\]

  2. Distribute all factors.

    \[5 x + 15 + 4 x - 2 = 10 x - 10\]

  3. Combine like terms on the left.

    \[9 x + 13 = 10 x - 10\]

  4. Subtract 9x from both sides.

    \[13 = x - 10\]

  5. Add 10 to both sides and write x on the left.

    \[x = 23\]

Check in the original equation

Substitute \(x = 23\) and evaluate each side separately.

Left side

\[\dfrac{\left(23\right) + 3}{2} + \dfrac{2\left(23\right) - 1}{5} = 22\]

Right side

\[\left(23\right) - 1 = 22\]

Both sides equal \(22\), so \(x = 23\) is correct.

Question 46

\[3 - \dfrac{2(x - 4)}{5} = \dfrac{x + 1}{2}\]
Answer

Solution: \(x = \frac{41}{9}\)

  1. Multiply every term by 10. The standalone 3 becomes 30. The second term becomes negative 4 times the group x minus 4.

    \[30 - 4 \left(x - 4\right) = 5 \left(x + 1\right)\]

  2. Distribute negative 4 and positive 5.

    \[30 - 4 x + 16 = 5 x + 5\]

  3. Combine constants on the left.

    \[46 - 4 x = 5 x + 5\]

  4. Add 4x to both sides.

    \[46 = 9 x + 5\]

  5. Subtract 5 from both sides.

    \[41 = 9 x\]

  6. Divide both sides by 9 and write the exact answer.

    \[x = \frac{41}{9}\]

Check in the original equation

Substitute \(x = \frac{41}{9}\) and evaluate each side separately.

Left side

\[3 - \dfrac{2(\left(\frac{41}{9}\right) - 4)}{5} = \frac{25}{9}\]

Right side

\[\dfrac{\left(\frac{41}{9}\right) + 1}{2} = \frac{25}{9}\]

Both sides equal \(\frac{25}{9}\), so \(x = \frac{41}{9}\) is correct.

Question 48

\[\dfrac{2(x + 3) - 5}{3} - \dfrac{x - 4}{2} = 4\]
Answer

Solution: \(x = 10\)

  1. Multiply every term by 6. Preserve the entire first numerator as a group.

    \[2 \left(2 \left(x + 3\right) - 5\right) - 3 \left(x - 4\right) = 24\]

  2. Inside the first group, distribute 2 and combine 6 minus 5 to get 1.

    \[2 \left(2 x + 1\right) - 3 \left(x - 4\right) = 24\]

  3. Distribute the outside factors.

    \[x + 14 = 24\]

  5. Subtract 14 from both sides.

    \[x = 10\]

Left side

\[\dfrac{2(\left(10\right) + 3) - 5}{3} - \dfrac{\left(10\right) - 4}{2} = 4\]

\[4 = 4\]

Question 49

\[\dfrac{3x - 2}{4} - \dfrac{2(x + 1)}{3} = \dfrac{x - 10}{6}\]
Answer

Solution: \(x = 6\)

  1. Multiply every term by 12. In the second term, 12 divided by 3 times the existing factor 2 gives 8.

    \[3 \left(3 x - 2\right) - 8 \left(x + 1\right) = 2 \left(x - 10\right)\]

  2. Distribute all factors, including the negative 8.

    \[9 x - 6 - 8 x - 8 = 2 x - 20\]

  3. Combine like terms on the left.

    \[x - 14 = 2 x - 20\]

  4. Subtract x from both sides.

    \[-14 = x - 20\]

  5. Add 20 to both sides and write x on the left.

    \[x = 6\]

Check in the original equation

Substitute \(x = 6\) and evaluate each side separately.

Left side

\[\dfrac{3\left(6\right) - 2}{4} - \dfrac{2(\left(6\right) + 1)}{3} = - \frac{2}{3}\]

Right side

\[\dfrac{\left(6\right) - 10}{6} = - \frac{2}{3}\]

Both sides equal \(- \frac{2}{3}\), so \(x = 6\) is correct.

Question 50

\[\dfrac{2[3(x - 1) + 4]}{5} - \dfrac{3(x + 2) - 1}{4} = \dfrac{x + 13}{10}\]
Answer

Solution: \(x = \frac{43}{7}\)

  1. The least common denominator of 5, 4, and 10 is 20. Multiply every term by 20. The first numerator already has a factor of 2, giving a total factor of 8.

    \[\begin{aligned}&8 \left(3 \left(x - 1\right) + 4\right) - 5 \left(3 \left(x + 2\right) - 1\right) \\&= 2 \left(x + 13\right)\end{aligned}\]

  2. Simplify inside each bracket: 3(x minus 1) plus 4 becomes 3x plus 1, and 3(x plus 2) minus 1 becomes 3x plus 5.

    \[8 \left(3 x + 1\right) - 5 \left(3 x + 5\right) = 2 \left(x + 13\right)\]

  3. Distribute 8, negative 5, and 2 to every term in their groups.

    \[24 x + 8 - 15 x - 25 = 2 x + 26\]

  4. Combine like terms on the left.

    \[9 x - 17 = 2 x + 26\]

  5. Subtract 2x from both sides.

    \[7 x - 17 = 26\]

  6. Add 17 to both sides.

    \[7 x = 43\]

  7. Divide both sides by 7 and keep the exact fraction.

    \[x = \frac{43}{7}\]

Check in the original equation

Substitute \(x = \frac{43}{7}\) and evaluate each side separately.

Left side

\[\begin{aligned}&\dfrac{2[3(\left(\frac{43}{7}\right) - 1) + 4]}{5} - \dfrac{3(\left(\frac{43}{7}\right) + 2) - 1}{4} \\&= \frac{67}{35}\end{aligned}\]

Right side

\[\dfrac{\left(\frac{43}{7}\right) + 13}{10} = \frac{67}{35}\]

Both sides equal \(\frac{67}{35}\), so \(x = \frac{43}{7}\) is correct.

Common questions

Why must I do the same thing to both sides?

An equation is a statement of equality. Adding or subtracting the same quantity on both sides, or multiplying or dividing both sides by the same nonzero constant, preserves that equality.

Why does a sign change when a term moves?

The term is not really moving by itself. Subtracting 5 from both sides removes a positive 5 from one side and introduces a subtraction of 5 on the other. Write the operation explicitly when you are unsure.

Can a correct answer be negative or a fraction?

Yes. The value only needs to make the original equation true. Keep fractional answers exact; a rounded decimal may fail an exact substitution check.

How do I avoid mistakes with a negative sign before parentheses?

Treat the sign and number as one factor. Multiply that factor by every term in the parentheses, including each sign. A negative factor times a negative term gives a positive term.

Is this lesson part of a numbered course?

No. This is a standalone lesson with 50 progressively more involved equations, detailed hidden solutions, and a check for every answer. No course enrollment or earlier numbered lesson is required.

A final habit: explain the operation

For each line, name what you did to both sides. For each final answer, substitute it into the original equation. This turns an answer into a solution you can justify.
