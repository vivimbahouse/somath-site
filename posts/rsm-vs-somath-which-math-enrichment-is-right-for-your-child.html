RSM vs SOMATH: Which Math Enrichment Is Right for Your Child?

RSM (Russian School of Math) vs SOMATH (School of Math): an honest, side-by-side comparison from an Upper West Side educator — class size, teaching method, pacing, pricing, and which child each is right for.

2026-05-19

Direct answer: RSM (Russian School of Math) and SOMATH (School of Math) both offer serious math instruction on the Upper West Side, but they're built for different children. RSM is best for families who want a structured, year-round curriculum delivered in a classroom of 8–14 students with a fixed scope and sequence. SOMATH is best for families who want small-group teaching (typically 4–6 students), a diagnostic-driven plan tailored to the individual child, and an explicit focus on SHSAT, AMC, SAT, and AP prep alongside grade-level enrichment. Read on for the full breakdown.

The honest one-paragraph version

If your child is on grade level, doing fine in school, and you want a steady year-round enrichment habit that builds depth in classical problem-solving — RSM is excellent and has earned its reputation over decades. If your child has a specific target (SHSAT in 8th grade, AMC8 in middle school, AP Calculus BC by 11th grade, recovering ground after a hard year, or accelerating into a competitive private school) — SOMATH's smaller groups and diagnostic-led approach are designed exactly for that. There is no "better" school; there is a better fit per child.

Class size and teacher attention

This is the single biggest practical difference.

  • RSM: Most RSM classes run 8 to 14 students. The classroom format is structured, lecture-with-practice, with a teacher leading the board and students working problems. Quality control across teachers is generally strong, and the curriculum is the same regardless of which RSM branch your child attends.
  • SOMATH: Small-group classes are capped at 6 students, and most run with 4 to 5. That means more talk time per student, more individualized correction, and a teacher who has actually read every piece of work your child produces in a given week. For students who learn by being challenged in conversation rather than by working in parallel in a larger room, this matters a lot.

Neither is "right" in the abstract. A confident, self-directed student often thrives in a larger RSM class because the bigger pool of peers is energizing. A student who's been quiet in class, who has gaps, or who needs to be pushed to articulate their thinking, gets more out of a 4–6 person group where it's harder to hide.

Curriculum and pacing

RSM uses a proprietary, sequenced curriculum that's been refined over more than 25 years. The Russian / Soviet school of math tradition emphasizes problem-solving depth and "olympiad-style" reasoning earlier than most American curricula. A student who starts RSM in 2nd grade and stays through 8th will have done thousands of structured problems in a deliberate order. That accumulation is real, and it shows up on tests.

SOMATH takes a different stance: every student starts with a free 60-minute evaluation and a written diagnostic delivered within 48 hours. From that, our instructors build an individualized plan that maps to the student's actual gaps, strengths, and target — whether that's SHSAT, AP Calculus, or just rebuilding confidence after a rough algebra year. We use a mix of our own problem sets, classical sources, and competition problem banks (AMC, MATHCOUNTS, SHSAT released forms). The pacing is set by the student, not by a national scope-and-sequence.

Two ways to think about it: RSM is like enrolling in a long-running music conservatory — the curriculum is the curriculum. SOMATH is like working with a private coach who happens to teach in small groups — the plan is built around your child.

Test prep focus

This is where the difference becomes most concrete for NYC families.

  • SHSAT: SOMATH runs dedicated SHSAT prep tracks starting in 7th grade. RSM students often perform well on the SHSAT because of their cumulative foundation, but SHSAT-specific prep is not the focus of an RSM classroom.
  • AMC / MATHCOUNTS: Both schools include competition material; RSM has a longer institutional history with it.
  • AP Calculus AB / BC, AP Statistics, AP Pre-Calculus: SOMATH offers AP-specific small groups with instructors who have taught the course and graded official-style FRQs. RSM's coverage of AP-level material varies by branch.
  • SAT / SAT Math II / Subject placement: SOMATH's high-school students typically take dedicated SAT math prep alongside enrichment.

If your primary goal is a specific test, ask the school directly: "What does the prep timeline look like, who will my child's teacher be for that specific track, and what's the realistic outcome based on starting point?" Both schools should give you a clear answer.

Diagnostic and onboarding

RSM places students by entrance assessment into the appropriate level. The assessment is competent and fair. SOMATH's free 60-minute evaluation is longer than a typical placement test because it's not only used to place — it's used to write a diagnostic. Parents receive a written document within 48 hours, with named strengths, named weaknesses, and a specific recommended track, whether or not they enroll. That document is useful on its own.

The difference is one of philosophy: RSM places students into the curriculum; SOMATH builds a plan from the diagnostic. If you're considering both, do both evaluations. They cost you nothing but time, and you'll learn something useful from each.

Pricing and logistics

We won't quote competitor prices in this post because they vary by branch and change. Generally:

  • RSM: Annual tuition is typically billed per year with a fixed weekly class commitment (usually 90 minutes once a week). Branch locations across NYC and the suburbs.
  • SOMATH: Tuition is structured around small-group sessions, with flexible scheduling. Located at 226 W 79th St on the Upper West Side. We're happy to send a current rate card when you book the free evaluation.

Don't choose based on price alone — choose based on what your child needs and how the schedule fits your family's actual life. A program your child can attend consistently beats a "better" program that becomes a Sunday battle.

Teacher quality

Both schools recruit seriously. RSM at scale relies on a hiring funnel and a teacher-training program that produces a relatively standardized classroom experience. SOMATH hand-picks every instructor; the two founders interview and evaluate everyone who teaches, and the instructor pool is small. That means at SOMATH, you generally know exactly who is teaching your child and that teacher will likely be with your child across the year.

If your child responds strongly to a particular teacher's style, the small-group model is an advantage. If your child is comfortable with any well-trained teacher, the large-school model is fine.

Which child is RSM right for?

  • Strong, self-directed students who like a structured classroom and steady cumulative practice.
  • Families committed to a long-term, year-over-year math habit (2nd grade through 12th).
  • Students who already enjoy math and want depth at grade level or one year ahead.
  • Families who want the same curriculum regardless of which branch they attend or move to.

Which child is SOMATH right for?

  • Students with a specific target — SHSAT, AMC, AP exams, private high school admissions, or competitive college math runway.
  • Students who have gaps and need a plan, not a fixed curriculum.
  • Students who do better when there are 4–6 people in the room and the teacher knows their name and their work.
  • Families who want a written diagnostic up front — so they can decide based on evidence, not marketing.
  • Families who specifically want Upper West Side proximity (226 W 79th St, between Broadway and Amsterdam).

Compare what you get — the placement letter from RSM and the written diagnostic from SOMATH. The documents themselves will tell you which school is paying more attention to your child specifically.

FAQ

Can my child attend both RSM and SOMATH?

Some families do, especially during SHSAT prep season. We'd generally recommend choosing one as the primary math home and supplementing thoughtfully — combining both at full intensity usually leads to overload and slower progress, not faster.

If RSM has been around longer, isn't it just better?

Longevity is real evidence — RSM has earned its reputation. But "better" is per child. A 12-student classroom is a different product than a 5-student classroom. Different parents value different things. There's no universal ranking.

Which school is better for SHSAT prep specifically?

For dedicated, time-bound SHSAT prep starting in 7th grade with a 6-month or 12-month runway, SOMATH's small-group structure is built specifically for that goal. Students enrolled in long-term RSM tracks often also test well, but it's a different model — cumulative depth vs targeted prep.

What about Mathnasium or Kumon vs both of these?

Mathnasium and Kumon are different categories. They're built around drill, individual self-paced workbooks, or computer programs — light touch on real teaching. RSM and SOMATH are both teacher-led, classroom-based instruction. If you're choosing between Mathnasium/Kumon and either of RSM/SOMATH, the more important question is whether you want teaching or worksheets.

How do I decide?

Three questions: (1) Does my child have a specific target, or do they need general enrichment? (2) Will my child thrive in a class of 12, or do they need a class of 5? (3) Do I want a written plan tailored to my child, or do I want a fixed, proven curriculum? Your answers will point clearly to one school or the other.
