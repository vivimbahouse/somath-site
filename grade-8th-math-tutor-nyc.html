The curriculum

What 8th graders learn in NYC public and private schools

Eighth grade math is exam year. General-track 8th graders take on functions, systems of linear equations, transformations, the Pythagorean theorem, and an introduction to statistics with two-variable data. SP-track students take Algebra 1 and typically sit for the Regents Algebra 1 exam in June, one year ahead of the standard sequence. Every 8th grader in NYC public school also takes the NY State Math Test in early May. The SHSAT is administered in fall (October). And 8th grade final grades are used for NYC high school admissions decisions.

Where kids get stuck

The three struggles we see most often in 8th grade math

1. The function concept transition

Eighth grade is the first time students formally see functions — inputs, outputs, function notation, graphs of linear functions. Students who thought of math as arithmetic on numbers now have to think about it as relationships between quantities. This transition is smoother when students have been drawing tape diagrams and equation models throughout middle school. Students who arrive in 8th grade with weak modeling skills struggle here more than they did with any single topic before.

2. SHSAT pacing pressure

The SHSAT allots 90 minutes for 57 math questions — about 95 seconds per question. Most 8th graders reach fall of 8th grade with the math content but without the pacing. The single most common SHSAT problem is running out of time on the last 10-15 questions, which is where the point differential typically lives. The fix is pacing practice under timed conditions starting in July, not November.

3. Regents Algebra 1 with weak middle-school foundation

SP-track students who reach 8th grade with any weakness in 7th-grade proportional reasoning or multi-step equations discover in October that the Regents Algebra 1 curriculum has no time to remediate. Every week adds a new topic that depends on the last. Students who reach Regents Algebra 1 with weak foundations often score in the low 80s (still 'passing') instead of the 90s that competitive high schools expect.

Milestones by month

The five 8th grade math milestones every NYC parent should watch for

  • By September: Baseline SHSAT practice test taken under timed conditions to identify weak strands.
  • By November: Systems of linear equations solved by substitution and elimination (general track), or first three units of Algebra 1 mastered (SP track).
  • By January: Function notation and linear function graphing fluent, quadratic functions introduced.
  • By March: Pythagorean theorem and geometric transformations applied to multi-step problems.
  • By April: NY State Math Test prep completed, Regents Algebra 1 review begun for SP students.

Common questions

8th grade math in NYC: parent FAQ

How do I know if my 8th grader is ready for the SHSAT?

Take a full-length practice test under timed conditions before Labor Day. If your child scores above the historical cutoff for their target school (roughly 500+ for Brooklyn Tech, 555+ for Bronx Science, 570+ for Stuyvesant), they're in reasonable range. If they're 30-50 points below cutoff, targeted work on weak strands over September-October is the right move. More than 60 points below cutoff usually means either moving down the target school list or accepting that a Screened-Only application is a stronger path.

What's the difference between SHSAT and NYC high school screened admissions?

The SHSAT determines admission to the eight specialized high schools (Stuyvesant, Bronx Science, Brooklyn Tech, Brooklyn Latin, HSAS at Lehman, HSMSE at CCNY, Queens HS for the Sciences at York, and Staten Island Tech). Screened admissions determines admission to schools like Beacon, Bard Manhattan, Bard Queens, LaGuardia, and dozens of others — these use 7th grade grades, state test scores (through 2025), and school-specific criteria. Most competitive 8th graders in NYC apply to both.

My 8th grader is in SP but doesn't want to take Algebra 1 Regents. Can they opt out?

In practice, at most SP schools (MS 54, MS 245, MS 334), Algebra 1 and its Regents exam are the SP curriculum. Opting out means dropping out of SP for the year, which has implications for high school course sequencing. That said, the Regents is not the pressure some parents make it out to be — most SP students who did the work through 7th grade score in the 80s and 90s. The bigger question is whether the workload is sustainable.

What's the SHSAT cutoff for Stuyvesant?

It varies year to year based on the test's total score distribution, but for the last several years the Stuyvesant cutoff has been in the 560-570 range out of 700. Bronx Science has hovered around 545-555. Brooklyn Tech around 495-505. These are helpful reference points but not guarantees — the actual cutoff is set based on how many students apply and score at each level.
