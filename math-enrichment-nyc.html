What we do

What math enrichment actually means

Math enrichment is what happens when a child gets more than what any single grade-level curriculum provides. Deeper reasoning. Harder problems. Better fluency. A stronger foundation than the school day alone can build. It is the answer for parents who look at their child's math and think: they can do more than this.

The word "enrichment" gets used loosely — sometimes for apps, sometimes for after-school clubs, sometimes for supplemental homework help. At SOMATH, math enrichment means something specific: a real classroom, a real teacher, a real curriculum, and small groups of six students working through problems together, once a week, for 90 minutes, year-round.

We built SOMATH because the alternatives on the Upper West Side did not fit what we would want for our own children. One-on-one tutoring is expensive and reactive. Kumon is worksheet-based and self-paced with no teacher in the loop. National franchises use rotating staff and generic curricula. We wanted a small school with its own curriculum, taught by educators who actually teach — so we built one.

The tracks

Who this is for

Which families come to SOMATH for math enrichment

Our families are usually not looking for tutoring. Their child is doing fine in school — often better than fine. But the school-day math is not enough. It moves at the pace of the middle of the class. Word problems are single-step. Fluency is assumed rather than built. These are the parents who come to us:

  • Parents of 6th–8th graders who see pre-algebra and algebra coming and want their child ahead of it, not chasing it.
  • Parents targeting SHSAT, Hunter, or private-school admissions who want a real curriculum rather than test-prep drilling in isolation.
  • Parents whose child likes math and needs harder problems to stay engaged.
  • Parents whose child is quietly falling behind at a fast-paced school and needs to consolidate before it becomes a crisis.

If any of that sounds like your family, the next step is the free evaluation. It is the only way we know whether we are a fit.

Common questions

Math enrichment in NYC — parent FAQ

What is math enrichment?

Math enrichment is math instruction that goes beyond what a child learns in school — deeper reasoning, harder problems, and a stronger foundation than what any single grade-level curriculum provides. Unlike tutoring, which is typically remedial and one-on-one, enrichment works with students who are on grade level or ahead and pushes them further. Unlike test prep, it is not tied to a single exam. At SOMATH, math enrichment means a 24-class year-round syllabus taught in small groups of six by trained teachers on the Upper West Side.

How is math enrichment different from tutoring?

Tutoring is reactive — a child struggles with a topic, a tutor is hired to help them catch up on that topic. It is usually one-on-one and short-term. Math enrichment is proactive — it assumes the child is doing fine in school and builds a stronger math brain for what comes next. It runs year-round on a curriculum, in a classroom setting, taught by a teacher rather than a tutor. Parents who want their child to move from B+ to A+ typically need enrichment, not tutoring.

How is SOMATH different from Kumon, Mathnasium, or Russian School of Math?

Kumon is worksheet-based and self-paced — the child works on packets, an adult checks the answers. There is no teaching in real time. Mathnasium runs small groups but relies heavily on workbooks and rotating instructors. Russian School of Math is closer to what SOMATH does but is a large franchise with locations across the country. SOMATH is a small, teacher-led school with a proprietary 24-class syllabus at every level, taught by Harvard- and Kellogg-trained educators in groups of six, in person on the Upper West Side. Every class is taught, not supervised.

Is math enrichment worth it if my child is already good at math?

Especially if they are already good at math. Strong students who only do grade-level work in school tend to plateau — they finish the assigned work quickly and rarely encounter problems that require more than one step of reasoning. Math enrichment is where they meet harder problems, learn to persist through frustration, and build the reasoning habits that pay off in SHSAT, SAT, AP exams, and college math. It is a compounding investment.
